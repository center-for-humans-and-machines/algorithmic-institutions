# The evaluation a language-model manager will be judged by

**Branch** `auto/llm-manager-eval`, from `dev` at `fddf37a`. Isolated remote dir `~/repros/ai-runs/llm-eval` on Raven, account `levinb` (delete when the PR closes). Nothing trained, nothing recalibrated; the four artifacts are the frontier stack #217 and #219 ran and their sha256s are in section 1.

**Scope.** The harness that runs a manager in the paired competing setting and produces the standard battery, built against a stub manager that returns fixed punishments so that it was finished and checked *before* the language model existed. Not the serving layer (agent A), not the prompt (agent B). The contract consumed is `predict(state, **_) -> (punishment int64 (B, A, 1), None)`, which `paired_rollout.rollout` already takes from any object.

**The objective is the group's undivided common pool.** Every table leads with it and the power calculation is sized on it. Total contribution and pool per member sit beside it as diagnostics and are never averaged with it.

Everything below is marked **measured** or **inferred**.

**The tie structure is not decoration.** `band16_p20` and `inv_thr7_p10` are both perfectly aimed *in the wrong direction* — each is the comonotone coupling of its own marginals — and their raw rank correlations differ by a factor of 1.7, 0.498 against 0.862, purely because the quiet one leaves 91% of its punishment column tied at zero and the loud one 59%. A language model told to punish sparingly will sit at the quiet end of exactly this effect.

## 4. The noise floor (measured, 6,144 episodes per control)

Both seats holding the same manager. Focal minus rival, paired bootstrap over episodes.

| control | quantity | seat bias | 95% interval | `sd_episode` | `sd_diff` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| clone vs clone | **pool** | +0.45 | [−1.25, +2.15] | **44.15** | 67.91 |
| never vs never | **pool** | −0.67 | [−2.17, +0.75] | **40.36** | 59.36 |
| thr9 vs thr9 | **pool** | −0.59 | [−2.59, +1.33] | **48.43** | 76.96 |
| clone vs clone | contribution | +0.35 | [−0.74, +1.45] | 27.02 | 43.37 |
| never vs never | contribution | −0.42 | [−1.36, +0.47] | 25.23 | 37.10 |
| thr9 vs thr9 | contribution | −0.22 | [−1.49, +1.00] | 28.05 | 48.73 |
| clone vs clone | members | +0.074 | [−0.009, +0.159] | 1.667 | 3.333 |
| never vs never | members | **−0.107** | **[−0.190, −0.026]** | 1.689 | 3.378 |
| thr9 vs thr9 | members | +0.006 | [−0.077, +0.089] | 1.684 | 3.368 |

**There is no detectable seat bias on the objective**, in any of the three worlds, at 6,144 episodes. This matters, because PR #217 reported `never_vs_never` at **−7.69 [−14.06, −1.31]** on the pool from 300 episodes and used it as a yardstick ("a pool margin under ~8 points is not read as the rule"). At twenty times the episodes the same control is **−0.67 [−2.17, +0.75]**. The 300-episode figure was sampling noise in a quantity whose per-episode spread is 40, where the 95% half-width is about 4.5; it should not be carried forward as a yardstick.

**There is a small real seat bias on membership in the silent world**: −0.107 [−0.190, −0.026] members for `never_vs_never`. It does not appear in the other two controls, it is a tenth of a member, and it is worth knowing about before anyone reads a membership difference of that size as a manager.

**`sd_diff` is larger than `sqrt(2) * sd_episode` on the objective**, not smaller. The two seats share one fixed set of eight players, so what one seat gains the other loses: `members` has `sd_diff` **exactly twice** `sd_episode` (perfect anticorrelation — the two headcounts sum to 8), and the pool's 67.91 exceeds `sqrt(2) * 44.15 = 62.4`. Only `pool_per_member` and `mean_punishment`, which partition nothing, come out positively correlated and cheaper within a run. **So the within-run contrast is the more expensive design on the pool**, which is the opposite of the usual intuition about paired measurement and is why both designs are reported.

## 5. What each episode budget buys (measured `sd`, inferred power)

Minimum detectable difference, `MDD = (z_{0.975} + z_{0.8}) * se`, two-sided, 80% power. **Unpaired** is arm A against arm B, two separate rollouts of `n` episodes each — the design for "does the language model beat `thr9_p10`". The `sd` is measured; the power calculation is the standard normal approximation and is *inferred*.

**On the pool, the objective:**

| episodes | never-world | clone-world | thr9-world |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | **22.6** | **24.7** | **27.1** |
| 200 | **11.3** | **12.4** | **13.6** |
| 500 | 7.2 | 7.8 | 8.6 |
| 1,000 | 5.1 | 5.5 | 6.1 |
| 3,000 | 2.9 | 3.2 | 3.5 |

**On total contribution**, the diagnostic beside it: 14.1 / 15.1 / 15.7 at 50 episodes, 7.1 / 7.6 / 7.9 at 200, 1.8 / 2.0 / 2.0 at 3,000. On `members`: 0.93 to 0.95 at 50, 0.47 at 200, 0.12 at 3,000.

Read the other way — how many episodes each effect this project has already measured would need, unpaired, against the clone-world spread:

| effect | quantity | size | episodes needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| inverted rule against never | pool | 32.75 | **29** |
| `thr9_p10` over never | contribution | 7.34 | **213** |
| `capped_sigmoid` over `thr9_p10` | pool | 5.00 | **1,225** |
| `thr9_p10` over never | pool | 0.51 | 117,664 |

(never-world spread: 24 / 186 / 1,023 / 98,327. thr9-world: 35 / 230 / 1,473 / 141,527.)

**This is the table that says what a language-model result means.** At 50 episodes only a catastrophe is visible: the harness can tell a deliberately inverted manager from never-punishing and nothing finer. At 200 episodes a contribution difference the size of the incumbent rule's becomes visible, but on the objective the floor is still 11–14 points, so a language model scoring anywhere between the clone and `capped_sigmoid` is indistinguishable from every one of them. **A meaningful pool result needs of the order of 1,000 episodes**, which the four-A100 budget can afford: a rollout is 24 sequential batched calls however wide the batch, and the harness side costs 6 ms per episode on CPU, so the cost is the language model's batch throughput and not the evaluation's.

And the last row is the one to hold on to: **the difference between punishing correctly and not punishing at all is 0.51 pool points and would need a hundred thousand episodes to resolve.** No budget reaches it. A language model landing at never-punishing on the pool has not been shown to have failed; it has been shown to be where the best hand-written threshold rule also is. What separates them is the contribution column, which needs 213 episodes, not 117,664.

## 6. Telemetry (measured, on the stub)

Wall clock is measured by the harness around each rollout. Token counts, the call count and the parse-failure count come through a manager's optional `telemetry()`; a manager that does not report is recorded as **NaN, never 0**, so "this rule has no tokens" cannot be read as "the language model spent none". `parse_failure_rate` is failures over episode-round prompts, and a failure falls back to zero punishment for that episode-round, which is what the plan specifies the client will do. All four behaviours are pinned by tests, including that turning the failure draw on does not disturb the environment's RNG stream — the stub draws from its own generator, so a run with failures and a run without face the same world.

## 7. What this does not show

1. **No language model was run.** Every number here is a rule, a clone, or a stub. The harness's claim is that it reproduces measurements made by other means; whether it measures a language model sensibly is not yet evidence.
2. **The power table is a normal approximation at a fixed effect size.** It assumes the per-episode spread under a new manager resembles one of the three controls. A manager that destabilises the group — the inverted rules shed members hard — will have a different spread, and the three worlds already differ by 20% on the pool, so the numbers are a guide and not a guarantee. A real run should read its own `sd` off its own episodes.
3. **`se_reference` in `validation.csv` is an estimate.** It uses this run's per-episode spread over the square root of the reference's episode count, because the references publish neither. It is labelled as such.
4. **The free-punishment fix is in the base here and was not in #217's.** `thr9_p10` punished every timed-out cell in that arm and punishes none here. The agreement in section 2 holds in spite of that difference, not in ignorance of it; #219 ran with the fix and reached the same place.
5. **Nothing here decomposes the membership channel.** `capped_sigmoid`'s pool margin is again mostly headcount (4.40 members against `thr9_p10`'s 3.83), and whether that is the graph channel or the migration channel is still #209's open item 4.
6. **The three symmetric controls are three worlds, not a survey of them.** The noise floor is a property of the world a manager makes, and the three measured here span 40.4 to 48.4 on the pool. A very loud or very destructive manager is outside that range.

## 8. Successor

1. **Re-measure the seat controls that other arms quote.** #217's `never_vs_never` pool yardstick of −7.69 is noise at its own episode count and is now contradicted by a twenty-times-larger measurement. Any arm carrying "a pool margin under ~8 points is not read as the rule" should be re-read against −0.67 [−2.17, +0.75].
2. **`rho_rel` should probably replace the bare rank wherever this project reports targeting.** The attenuation it corrects is a factor of 1.7 between two rules with identical aim, and it is correlated with exactly the quietness a well-behaved manager will have.
3. **Size the language model's first run at 1,000 episodes or more, and report the contribution column beside the pool.** At 200 it can only be said to be somewhere between the clone and the best known rule.
