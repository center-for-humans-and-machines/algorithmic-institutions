## Explorations

1. **Where does v3 stand?** Read v3's notes, rule, `landscapes.json` and
   `final/`, and re-ran scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-12/v1_final.py on
   v3's final. v3 is the best so far: 73.0 (se 1.1, 4.33 members) at thr
   9.8, peer 0.47, slope 11.9, decay 0.46, follow 0.78, against v2's 70.8.
   Its surface sits at the top edge of `slope` (12) and still rising
   (71.4 at 4.9, 73.3 at 9.2, 73.6 at 11.8); peer peaks inside (0.45-0.55),
   follow inside (0.7-0.9), decay at 0.42-0.49, thr at 9.5-10. At slope 12 a
   member 2.5 points below the bar already hits the cap of 30 at round 0, so
   the sweep is telling me round 0 wants something close to "cap anyone
   below the bar" - a step, not a slope. Per round of the final: round 0 is
   the most expensive round of the game (12.7 punishment per member, pool
   5.0), round 1 9.9 (pool 26.7); rounds 2-3 pool ~71; block 1's punished
   rounds 8.6/6.2; from round 12 on almost nothing. Contributions climb
   8.7 -> 11.2 (round 2) -> 12.2 (round 10+), the same end level as `ah`'s
   group (12.4); the edge is members (4.37-4.44 vs 3.56-3.63, set at
   decision 3) and the cheap late game. Next: per design point of all three
   sweeps, how block-0 punishment, members and late contributions trade.
2. **Per design point, what goes with a high pool?** Cached the v3 sweep
   (cache_sweep.py) and ran scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-12/v4_points.py
   on all three sweeps: per point and block, members, contribution, punishment
   per member and pool. Single points are noisy (v3's best single point 76.9
   vs its final 73.0). Two kinds of point win in v3: "keep pressing" (s765:
   punishment per member per round 4.2 -> 2.3 over blocks, contributions to
   14.2, 4.3 members) and "hit early, then stop" (s562: 4.7, 2.2, 0.7, 0.2,
   contributions flat at 11.2, 4.66 members) at the same pool (75-77). In

   punishment (+0.27) but not with block-2 punishment (0.00); block-0
   punishment costs block-1 members (-0.82). Next: how a member's response
   and leaving depend on the *size* of the round-0 punishment, up to the
   cap - is it linear, or does a step pay?
3. **How do response and leaving scale with the size of a round-0
   punishment?** Ran scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-12/v4_dose.py 0 on
   the v2 and v3 sweeps (members of the rule's group at round 0, by own
   contribution and punishment bin). For low contributors the later
   contribution (rounds 4-11, if they stay) rises almost linearly up to the
   cap while leaving barely moves: a 0-2 contributor goes 5.6 -> 10.0 from
   1-5 to 30 points, leaving 40% -> 49%; 3-5: 6.9 -> 11.6, 39% -> 46%.
   Mid contributors pay more in leaving: 9-11: 10.7 -> 14.2 but 29% -> 44%;
   11+ contributors gain nothing and leave more (24% -> 46%). So no
   saturation below the cap: that is why `slope` ran to its edge, and the
   gain per point is ~0.12-0.18 everywhere below the bar (observational,
   peers' mean rises with the dose too).
4. **What does the switch model actually see?** Read
   configs/training/artificial_humans/switch_predictor/joint_exodus.yml and
   environment.py: the switch model's inputs are each round's
   `common_good` = (1.6 sum c - sum p) / n_valid, i.e. the group's
   per-member pool *net of punishment*, the player's own punishment, its
   group and the round, through an RNN and an edge model. So every
   punishment point hurts every member's view of the group, not just the
   punished one's. Ran v4_cgflow.py: OLS of the rule group's net member gain
   at each decision on both groups' per-member common good in the block's 4
   rounds. At decision 3: +0.030, +0.047, +0.043, +0.061 members per point
   of own common good in rounds 0..3, and -0.031 ... -0.071 for `ah`'s
   group's; at decisions 7 and 11 about half that (0.015-0.038). So round 0
   is the cheapest round to hurt the common good in (half the weight of
   round 3), and the later decisions are less sensitive than the first.
5. **Is the harder opening worth having on its own?** Ran v4_open.py: a
   quadratic fit of the v3 sweep's pool on log block-0 slope (s0), log
   block-1 slope (s1 = slope*decay), thr, peer, follow. The two interact
   strongly (+8.0 on ls0*ls1): the good points lie on a ridge s0 ~ 1.5-2.2 x
   s1 that rises gently as both grow (s0 6/s1 3.5: 72.4; 12/5.5: 72.8;
   18/8: 72.3), with no gain from a harder opening at a fixed s1. The 20
   best single points (73.4-76.9, noise se ~2.5) are spread over slope
   3.5-11.5, decay 0.4-1.1, peer 0-0.9: a broad plateau at ~73-74. Another
   reshuffle of v3's dials will not move the score; I need a lever v3 does
   not have. Next: newcomers at the first punished round after a switch.
6. **Do newcomers need their own treatment?** Ran v4_newcomer.py on the v2
   and v3 sweeps: at rounds 4, 8, 12, newcomers vs stayers by contribution
   and punishment bin. Newcomers at round 4 leave less than stayers at every
   dose (e.g. 0-4 contributors punished 22-30: 33% vs 43%) and gain as much
   or a bit more later (8.6 vs 7.9); at rounds 8 and 12 they leave 1-6 pp
   more than stayers, with the same per-point slope. Dead end, as v3 found:
   no newcomer lever.
7. **Does the member cost of punishment depend on group size?** After
   exploration 4 I expected it to: the switch model sees the per-member
   common good, so a punishment of P points in a group of n lowers it by
   P/n for the n members (who may leave) *and* for the 8-n members of the
   other group (who may join). Members leaving scale as n*P/n = P, but
   joiners lost scale as (8-n)*P/n: a point is far dearer in a small group.
   Ran v4_size.py on the v3 sweep. Per person, at decisions 7-15, leaving
   the rule's group falls 0.013 per point of its block-mean common good,
   and leaving `ah`'s group (joining ours) rises 0.011 per point of ours:
   the two channels are about equal per head. At the group level, net
   member gain per 10 points of the rule group's total punishment in the
   block: -0.105 to -0.120 at sizes 1-3, -0.09 to -0.10 at size 4, -0.046 to
   -0.074 at sizes 5-8 - the cost halves from small to large groups, as
   the arithmetic predicts (1.9x at n=2, 0.7x at n=6 relative to n=4).
   Group size is far from 4 most of the time after the first switch: in
   blocks 1-4 of the v3 sweep it is 0-3 in ~36% of episodes and 6-8 in
   ~43% (18% hold all 8 in block 1). v3 punishes the same per shortfall
   point at every size. Next: does the gain side change with size too.
8. **Does the contribution gain per point depend on size?** Ran
   v4_size_gain.py (v2+v3 sweeps, rounds 4/8/12, members contributing <=10).
   Gain per punishment point over the next 7 rounds rises mildly with size:
   0-4 contributors 0.109 (alone), 0.133 (2), 0.145 (4), 0.157 (8); 5-10
   contributors 0.087, 0.122, 0.137, 0.149. The punished member's own
   leaving per point is ~0.40-0.49 pp at sizes 3-8 and nearly 0 at sizes
   1-2 (a lone or paired member stays put). So both sides point the same
   way: punishment buys a little more and costs about half as many members
   per point in a big group, and the small group's priority is to attract
   (its net gain per point of mean contribution is 0.13 vs 0.06-0.08 in big
   groups), which punishment works against.
9. **Drafted v4.** v3's rule plus one structural change: the punishment is
   multiplied by (n/4)^size, n the current size of the group. At 4 members
   (all of block 0) the factor is 1, so `slope` keeps its meaning and v3 is

   (v3's peak sat at the edge, 12, with the ridge of exploration 5 still
   rising) and lowered `decay`'s floor to 0.3 (a harder opening goes with a
   faster taper on that ridge).

## Key findings

1. v3 is the best so far (73.0, 4.33 members) but its surface is a broad
   plateau: the 20 best design points (73.4-76.9, se ~2.5) spread over
   slope 3.5-11.5, decay 0.4-1.1, peer 0-0.9, and the opening and the
   block-1 intensity lie on a ridge (s0 ~ 1.5-2.2 x s1) that rises only
   gently; the peak's `slope` sat at its upper edge, 12 (explorations 1, 2, 5).
2. Below the bar the response to a round-0 punishment does not saturate
   before the cap: a 0-2 contributor's later contribution goes 5.6 -> 10.0
   from 1-5 to 30 points while his leaving moves only 40% -> 49%
   (exploration 3).
3. The switch model sees each group's per-member common good net of
   punishment; at decision 3 a point of it weighs +0.030 (round 0) to
   +0.061 (round 3) members, about half that at later decisions
   (exploration 4).
4. Per head, leaving our group and joining it respond about equally to our
   common good (-0.013 and +0.011 per point), so a punishment point, which
   lowers the common good by 1/n, costs members roughly in proportion to
   (8-n)/n + const: net -0.105 to -0.120 members per 10 points at sizes
   1-3, -0.09 at 4, -0.046 to -0.074 at 5-8 (exploration 7).
5. Group size is rarely 4 after the first switch: 0-3 in ~36% and 6-8 in
   ~43% of episodes in blocks 1-4 (exploration 7).
6. The gain per punishment point rises mildly with size (0-4 contributors
   0.109 alone, 0.145 at 4, 0.157 at 8) and the punished member's own
   leaving is ~0 in groups of 1-2 (exploration 8).
7. Newcomers need no special treatment: they leave less (round 4) or about
   as often (rounds 8, 12) as stayers per dose and respond as much
   (exploration 6).

## Hypothesis

A good manager makes its group more generous than the other one early,
because members flow towards the higher per-member common good, and pays
as little in punishment and lost members as that takes. Since every
punishment point lowers the common good that both its own members and the
other group's players weigh at a switch, by 1/n, the same point is cheap in a
large group and dear in a small one, where the group's priority is to
attract joiners; so the manager should scale its punishment with its group's
size: press free riders hard when the group is large, go gently when it is
small. Otherwise it keeps v3's shape: hard at a block's first round, a
lighter follow-up in its second, quiet in the two rounds before each switch
and after the last one, tapering block by block, with a bar that blends a
fixed level with what the member's mates give.

The rule: `punishment = slope * decay^block * w_pos * (n/4)^size *
max(0, bar - c_t)`, `bar = (1-peer)*thr + peer*mates' mean`, `w_pos` 1 at a
block's first round, `follow` at its second, 0 at its last two and in rounds
20-23; n the group's current size.

- `thr` (float, [6, 14]): fixed bar. v3 peaked at 9.8 (within 0.5 of the top
  from 8.5 to 11); I expect 8.5-11.
- `peer` (float, [0, 1]): weight of mates' mean in the bar. v3 peaked at
  0.45-0.55; I expect 0.3-0.7.
- `slope` (float, [2, 20], log): punishment per shortfall point at round 0
  (4 members). v3's peak was at its edge 12; I expect 8-16, the range now
  reaching past the point where any shortfall above ~1.5 hits the cap.
- `decay` (float, [0.3, 1.2], log): per-block factor. v3 peaked at 0.42-0.49
  with slope at 12; a higher slope on the ridge wants a lower decay, so I
  expect 0.35-0.6.
- `follow` (float, [0, 1.5]): second-round punishment relative to the
  first. v3 peaked at 0.7-0.9; I expect the same.
- `size` (float, [-1, 3]): elasticity of the punishment to group size,
  (n/4)^size. 0 is v3. I expect 0.5-1.5 (cost per point roughly halves from
  n=2 to n=6, finding 4); the range allows the opposite sign and a steeper
  dependence.

### Justification

Finding 1 says v3's dials are spent: a lever v3 lacks is needed, and the
slope's edge needs room (hence [2, 20] and decay down to 0.3). Findings 3
and 4 give that lever: the switch model reads the per-member common good,
so a point of punishment costs about twice as many members in a group of 2
as in a group of 6, while finding 6 says it buys slightly more contribution
in a big group; both point to punishing harder in large groups and more
gently in small ones, and finding 5 says this situation is the common one
after the first switch, so the lever has room to matter. Size enters as a
power of n/4 so it is exactly 1 in block 0 (always four members), leaving
`slope` its v3 meaning and nesting v3 at size 0. Finding 2 supports keeping
a steep, near-step response at the opening; findings 3 and v3's own
results ground `follow` and the quiet rounds; finding 7 says not to add a
newcomer term. What would contradict the hypothesis: a best `size` at or
below 0 (the member cost by size is offset by something I have not seen,
e.g. a large group's free riders dragging others down less), or at the
top edge, 3 (size matters even more than the common-good arithmetic says).
Not modelled: the other group's size or common good separately from ours
(n already fixes the other group's size, 8-n, and we cannot act on its
common good), the exact recency weights within a block (positions 2-3 stay
quiet), and empty groups (no one to punish). The readings are
observational, from earlier sweeps' design points; the sweep is the test.
