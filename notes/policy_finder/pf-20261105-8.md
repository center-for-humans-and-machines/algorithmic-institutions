# pf-20261105-8

## Explorations

1. **What do the two reference managers score, and why?** Looked at
   `plots/simulation/29_EXP1_reference/` (aggregates.csv, per_round.parquet) with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-8/ref_response.py. Score (sum over 24 rounds of the
   mean valid payoff): zero 589.3 (se 2.1), humanlike 550.6 (se 3.4). Under zero the mean
   contribution decays steadily 11.6 -> 5.5; under humanlike it climbs to ~13.2 by round 8
   and holds. Humanlike gains roughly 0.6 * 4.5 * 24 ~ 65 points of contribution surplus
   but spends 2 * ~2.4 * 24 ~ 115 on punishment (each point costs the punished player and
   the pool). So the humanlike policy buys something real (a stable ~13) but pays about
   twice what it gets. The question is whether a much cheaper punishment buys most of the
   same stabilisation. Also noted: the exp1 clone artifacts (artifacts/behavioral_cloning)
   are not in this container, so I cannot simulate; I must reason from data and these sims.

2. **How does a player's next contribution respond to punishment (humanlike sim)?**
   Same script, change c(t+1)-c(t) binned by own contribution and punishment received.
   Low contributors (0-4): +0.8 with no punishment, +1.1 at p 1-2, +1.9 at p 4-6, +2.3 at
   6-9, +3.0 at 9-14, +4.9 at 15-30: roughly +0.2 per punishment point, concave-ish, and
   confounded by who humanlike chooses to punish. Mid contributors (10-14) barely move
   (+0.1..+1). High contributors (15-20) who get punished *drop* more (-1 to -3 vs -0.8):
   punishing cooperators backfires. Unpunished players whose groupmates are punished hard
   (>8 points) drop a little more at high contributions (-1.2 vs -0.5), no deterrence
   spillover visible. In the zero run the strongest driver is the gap to the group mean:
   players above the mean by >5 drop ~3.3 per round, those below by >5 rise ~1.4 (conditional
   cooperation, pulling everyone toward the mean, with a net downward drift ~-0.4/round).
   So: punish only the low contributors, never above-mean ones; a punishment point costs
   2 and buys ~0.2 contribution next round = 0.12 payoff per round it persists, so it only
   pays if the raise persists >~15 rounds, i.e. early in the game. Next: the human pilots,
   especially random_1 where punishment is closer to exogenous.
3. **Does the raise after a punishment persist, and does it spill over to the others?**
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-8/persistence.py: in the humanlike sim, low contributors
   (c<=6) at rounds 2-13, their change over t+1..t+8 by punishment received, with a
   regression on punishment-bin dummies controlling for own c, previous c, the others' mean
   and t. The raise does not decay: heavily punished (13-30) low contributors are +4.5 at
   t+1 and +7.9 at t+8, unpunished +0.5 / +1.1. With controls: 8-12 points gives own +1.4
   and others' mean +1.4..1.7 for t+1..t+8; 13-30 points gives own +2.3..2.9, others +1.3..1.9;
   1 point gives nothing; 2-3 points own +0.2..0.45, others +0.25..0.6. Linear fit: +0.12
   own and +0.06..0.08 for each other player, per punishment point, flat over 8 rounds.
   So one point buys ~0.12 + 3*0.07 ~ 0.33 extra contribution per round for the rest of the
   game, i.e. 0.2 group payoff per round against a one-off cost of 2: it pays when ~10+
   rounds remain, so punishment should be front-loaded and stop well before the end.
   (Confounded by humanlike's choices, the others' response especially; I treat these as
   upper-ish estimates.) Next: the human pilots.

4. **What did the human pilots do, and how did they respond?** scripts/policy_finder/
   pf-20261105-8/pilots.py. random_1 (16 rounds, rule manager, mean punishment 3.4):
   contributions rise 10.6 -> 15 by round 11 and hold to round 15 (14.6), no big end drop.
   trail_rounds_2 (8 rounds, human manager) flat ~12.5. Both managers punish
   zero-contributors ~10.5 points; humans also hit 15-19 contributors 1.7 points 43% of the
   time. Next-round response (random_1, closest to exogenous punishment):
   dc = ... + 0.21 p - 0.10 p*[c>=15]; trail: +0.13 p - 0.28 p*[c>=15]. Low contributors
   punished 12-30 rise +7.3 (random_1) / +5.4 (trail) vs +0.2/+1.0 unpunished; high
   contributors punished 3-12 points drop 2.5-7.8 (trail). Confirms: punish only the low,
   never the high. Also checked the clone's training config
   (configs/training/artificial_humans/contribution/script_21_no_grid.yml): inputs are
   prev_contribution, prev_punishment, prev_contribution_valid with node+edge+rnn, no round
   number, so the clones have no end-game knowledge; the only reason to stop punishing near
   the end is that the payoff horizon runs out. Next: a crude surrogate to size the ranges.
5. **A linear surrogate of the clones, to size ranges.** scripts/policy_finder/
   pf-20261105-8/surrogate.py: next c on own c, others' mean, own p, p*[c>=15], others' p,
   a decayed punishment history and t, fitted on both reference runs. Coefficients: c 0.72,
   others' mean 0.26, p +0.08, p*high -0.11, others' p +0.04, residual sd 3.65. Under
   zero it decays only to 9.3 (real 5.5) and scores 621 (real 589): a linear Markov fit
   misses the bimodal 0/20 contributions and the slow decay. Dead end for scoring; dropped.

6. **A nonparametric surrogate and a grid of rules.** surrogate2.py draws the next
   contribution from reference-sim rows matching (own c bin, others' mean bin, own p bin,
   round bin); try_rules.py plays "punish slope*(target-c)+ in rounds t<stop". Zero scores
   606.5 here (real 589.3), so it is optimistic about the no-punishment baseline and has no
   memory beyond one round (so it understates the persistence of entry 3). Results: the
   only rules that beat zero here are a high target with a strong slope stopped early:
   target 16, slope 1, stop 8 -> 621.8 (mean c 14.6-15.4 in rounds 4-8, mean p 1.1);
   the same until 24 -> 566; slope 2 at target 16 -> 446-568 (too costly); target 8 rules
   barely move contributions. Punishing until the end always loses ~20-50 vs stopping
   early. So the shape is: a clear standard around 14-17, roughly 1 point per point of
   shortfall, and an early stop; the true clones' persistence (entry 3) should push the best
   stop later than this memoryless surrogate's 8-12.

7. **Variants: relative standard, taper, cap, flat fee, credit for improving.**
   try_rules2.py / try_rules3.py on the same surrogate. Standard = min(target, others'
   mean) 609.7 vs absolute 621.8 (it lets an all-low group stay low); linear taper to 16
   607.0; cap 8 at slope 1.5 597.7; flat 3-point fee plus slope 0.5 595.5; credit for a
   rise since last round 620.5 vs 621.8. None helps on the surrogate, though the surrogate
   cannot really judge the cap (its punishment response is binned) and cannot see memory.
   Drafted the rule: absolute target, slope, a horizon after which no one is punished,
   and a per-player cap (the response is concave in entry 3, 0.15/point at 8-12 vs
   0.12/point at 13-30, and zero contributors would otherwise draw slope*target).
   Dropped the relative standard and the credit as not worth a parameter.
8. **Wrote and validated the rule.** configs/managers/rule_based/pf-20261105-8.yml:
   punishment = min(slope * max(target - c, 0), cap) for valid players in rounds
   t < horizon, else 0. validate-rule passes (256 distinct design points). Ranges chosen
   wide around the surrogate's best (target 16, slope 1, stop 8) because the surrogate is
   memoryless and optimistic about zero: target [6, 20], slope [0.2, 4] log, horizon
   int 1..24 (1 = punish only round 0, 24 = always; both ends kept so the sweep can tell
   "stop early" from "never stop"), cap [3, 30] log (30 = no cap).

## Key findings

1. Never punishing (589.3) beats the human-like manager (550.6): humanlike holds
   contributions near 13 instead of letting them decay to 5.5, worth ~65 points, but its
   ~2.4 points of punishment per player per round cost ~115 (entry 1).
2. Punishment raises the contributions of low contributors (+0.2 per point next round in
   the clones and in random_1 humans) but lowers those of high contributors (-0.1 to -0.3
   per point for c >= 15 in the pilots; -1 to -3 in the sim) (entries 2, 4).
3. In the clones the raise persists and spreads: per punishment point about +0.12 own and
   +0.07 for each other player, flat over 8 rounds, so a point repays its cost of 2 only
   with ~10 rounds left (entry 3).
4. The clones have no round-number input, so no end-game collapse; the only reason to stop
   punishing late is the shrinking horizon (entry 4).
5. On a crude nonparametric surrogate, only a high standard (~16) with ~1 point per point
   of shortfall, stopped early (round 8-12), beats zero (621.8 vs 606.5); punishing to the
   end loses 20-50; a relative standard, taper, flat fee or improvement credit did not help
   (entries 6, 7).

## Hypothesis

A good manager spends its punishment early, on players below a clear standard, in
proportion to their shortfall, and then stops: early punishment lifts the free riders,
which (through conditional cooperation) holds the whole group's contributions up for the
rest of the game, whereas punishment late in the game or on players already contributing
well only costs. The rule: punish each valid player min(slope * max(target - c, 0), cap)
in rounds t < horizon, nothing afterwards.
- `target` (float, [6, 20]): the contribution standard; expected best 13-17.
- `slope` (float, [0.2, 4], log): points per point of shortfall; expected best 0.6-1.5.
- `horizon` (int, 1..24): first round with no punishment; expected best 8-16.
- `cap` (float, [3, 30], log): per-player per-round ceiling; expected best 10-30 (likely
  not binding much), lower if large punishments are wasteful.

### Justification

Finding 1 says the value of punishment is real but its price must fall by more than half,
hence a proportional rule that charges nothing to compliant players and a horizon that
stops spending once the payback window is too short (findings 3, 4). Finding 2 grounds the
one-sided standard: punishment only below `target`, and the target range stops at 20 but
the expected best is below 15-19 where punished players react badly. Finding 3 (0.33 group
contribution per point per round, break-even ~10 rounds) grounds `horizon` at roughly 8-16;
the range keeps 1..24 so a sweep can find "stop almost immediately" or "never stop" if the
clones differ from my estimates. `slope` is centred on the surrogate's 1 (finding 5) with a
log range spanning ~4x either way. `cap` reflects the concave dose-response of finding 3;
its range reaches 30 so it can switch itself off. What would contradict this: a sweep in
which the best horizon sits at 24 (persistence weaker than lasting deterrence of a standing
threat), or at 1-2 (punishment never pays), or a best slope at the low edge (zero is best).
Not modelled: timeouts beyond excluding them (2% of rows, and a timed-out player cannot be
punished anyway); relative-to-group standards and credit for improvement (no gain on the
surrogate, entry 7); escalation for repeat offenders, which would need one more parameter
and which the data cannot separate from the clones' own memory.
