# pf-20261106-1

## Explorations

1. **What does the score depend on, and what is in the data?** Looked at the deduplicated human data (50 games, 9600 rows) with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-1/a1_overview.py. Mean contribution sits at 8-9.7 all game; human managers punished 3.4/player early, ~1.1 late. A group's mean pool per round is 51.9 (sd 36.8), and over the 100 group-games the pool correlates 0.99 with total contributions but only 0.06 with total punishment, and 0.53 with the group's final size. Groups range 0-8 members after round 4. So the score is mostly "how many people are in my group times how much each gives"; punishment is a direct cost. Next: what makes players leave or join.

2. **What drives switching?** Looked at the 2000 switch decisions (rounds 3,7,11,15,19) with a2_switch.py. Switching rate 28.6% (43% at the first decision, ~24% later). Switch rate rises steeply with own punishment in the decision round: 22% at 0, 29% at 1-2, 39% at 3-5, 58% at 6-10, 69% above 10; with block punishment (sum over the 4 rounds) 19% at 0 up to 67% above 40. Per-capita pool gap to the other group: 48% leave when own group is 10+ below, 11% when 10+ above. Mean punishment in the other group (pm_o) holds players in (logit -0.084/point), and own block punishment pushes out (+0.034/point) controlling for the pool gap. Big groups (8) lose only 14%. So punishment is a strong repellent, and a manager that punishes less than the other one gains members. The AH switch model sees common_good, own punishment, group and round (with an RNN over the rounds and edges to the others), so the same channels exist in simulation. Next: is punishment worth anything for contributions?
3. **Does punishment raise contributions, and whose?** Looked at next-round contribution changes of stayers by own contribution and punishment with a3_contrib.py. For zero contributors the next-round change is +1.0 unpunished, +3.4 after 6-10 points, +4.0 after 10+; for 1-5 contributors +0.1 unpunished, +1.9 after 6-10. For players at 15-20 any punishment is harmful (20-contributors drop 5-16 points after punishment vs 1.8 unpunished). OLS: next_c on punishment is -0.12/point overall but +0.09/point for players under 10. Human managers punished mostly by shortfall below the group mean (0.57 per point below the mean) and less over time (-0.1/round). Also checked (in the simulation code, manager/environment.py and generic/data.py) that the AH switch model reads common_good *per capita* of the group, so the per-capita gap I saw in 2 is the channel there too. So: punish only low contributors, scaled with how far below they are; never high ones. Next: does the effect last, and how harsh managers fared overall.

4. **Persistence, and how manager harshness played out.** a5_persist.py follows stayers 1-3 rounds after a block's first round: free riders (c<=3) punished 4-8 are +1.8/+2.7/+2.8 above their round-0 level vs +0.85/+0.7/+0.95 unpunished, punished 8+ are +3.9/+4.6/+3.3; mid-low contributors (4-8) punished just 1-3 points are ~+1.0 for three rounds vs -0.4 unpunished, which is a ~1.4 point * 1.6 = 2.2 pool gain per round for a 2-point cost. a4_mgr.py: group-games split by manager harshness (punishment per member-round): pool 57.8/58.9/63.9/50.8/28.1 over quintiles 0.24/0.97/1.51/2.54/5.24, and membership (member-rounds) falls from 113 to 71. Relative to the other group: being >1.3 points/member-round more lenient than the other manager gives pool 56 vs 32 and 128 vs 64 member-rounds. At decision rounds, players punished 4+ leave at 52-68% if low contributors and 23-45% if higher. So the best human managers punished lightly (~1-1.8 per member-round), concentrated on low contributors, and stayed below the other manager. Punishment in the last round (23) cannot pay back at all. Next: draft the rule.
5. **Drafted the rule.** Four parameters: `thresh` (contribution a member is expected to reach), `slope` (punishment per point of shortfall below it, so only low contributors are punished and free riders most), `dec_mult` (a multiplier in the switching-decision rounds 3/7/11/15/19, where 2 showed punishment drives members out; it may be below 1 to protect membership, or above 1 if pushing free riders into the other group pays), and `margin` (our mean punishment per member is scaled down to at most the other manager's mean per member over the last 4 rounds plus `margin`, the one handle on the opponent, from the "be the more lenient group" result in 2 and 4). Hard-coded: no punishment of timed-out players (charged 0 anyway, but they would distort the mean used for the cap) and none in round 23 (4: nothing left to pay back). validate-rule passes (256 distinct points).

6. **A torch quirk and a sanity check on human histories.** Running the rule standalone (t1_rule_on_humans.py) first failed with KeyError '__import__': in a fresh process, the first comparison of a tensor with a Python float (or th.clamp with a number) does a lazy import from C++, which fails inside the builtin-free scope. Any such op beforehand in the process warms it (validate-rule's random histories do; the simulation runs many before the rule), so it is a standalone-test artefact; my script warms it up first. On the 50 human histories (group 0's view) the rule with thresh 10, slope 0.5, dec_mult 0.5 deals 1.08 points per member-round with margin 6 and 0.78 with margin 1, against the human managers' 1.88: in the 0.97-1.51 band of the best human quintile in 4, with decision rounds near 0.5-0.6. A slope of 0.2 with thresh 6 gives 0.13, i.e. nearly a non-punisher, so the low end of the slope range covers "don't punish".

## Key findings

1. A group's pool is driven by how many members it holds and what they give: over 100 human group-games the mean pool correlates 0.99 with total contributions, 0.53 with final group size and 0.06 with total punishment (exploration 1).
2. Punishment drives members out at the switching decisions: 22% leave unpunished, 39% after 3-5 points, 58% after 6-10, 69% above 10; a per-capita pool 10+ points below the other group's makes 48% leave vs 11% when 10+ above; the other group's punishment holds members in (logit -0.084 per point) (exploration 2).
3. Punishment raises contributions only for low contributors and lastingly: free riders punished 4-8 are ~+1.8-2.8 above their level for three rounds vs ~+0.85 unpunished; contributors at 4-8 punished 1-3 points gain ~1.4 points per round; 20-contributors who are punished drop 5-16 points (explorations 3, 4).
4. The best human managers punished lightly: pool 63.9 in the quintile at 1.5 points per member-round vs 28.1 at 5.2 and 57.8 at 0.24; being >1.3 points/member-round more lenient than the other manager doubles member-rounds (128 vs 64) and gives pool 56 vs 32 (exploration 4).
5. Low contributors punished 4+ at a decision round leave at 52-68%, more than higher contributors (23-45%) (exploration 4).
6. On the human histories the rule at thresh 10, slope 0.5, dec_mult 0.5 deals 0.8-1.1 points per member-round, the best human band (exploration 6).

## Hypothesis

A good manager keeps its group large and contributing: it punishes only members who give clearly less than a modest norm, in proportion to the shortfall, so that the punishment buys lasting contributions from free riders while cooperators are never hit; it is careful at the switching decisions, where punishment and a lower per-capita pool push members to the other group; and it stays at most slightly harsher than the other manager, since the more lenient group gathers the players. Punishment in the last round is pure loss.

The rule (`configs/managers/rule_based/pf-20261106-1.yml`): punishment_i = slope * max(0, thresh - c_i) for valid members, times `dec_mult` in rounds 3/7/11/15/19, then all scaled down if the group's mean punishment per member exceeds the other manager's mean per member over the last 4 rounds plus `margin`; zero in round 23.

- `thresh` (float, [3, 15] uniform): the contribution a member is expected to reach. I expect 8-12: below 10 punishment raised contributions, at 10-15 it was neutral or harmful (finding 3); 15 is the far end where it would hit near-cooperators, 3 targets only outright free riders.
- `slope` (float, [0.05, 3] log): points per point of shortfall. I expect 0.3-1 (a free rider punished ~4-8, the band that paid in finding 3; human managers used ~0.57 per point below the mean). The low end floors to no punishment at all, so the sweep can find "don't punish" if the artificial humans say so; 3 lets a free rider get the maximum 30.
- `dec_mult` (float, [0, 1.5] uniform): multiplier in decision rounds. I expect 0-0.7 (finding 2), but leave room above 1 because expelled free riders (finding 5) cost little pool and lower the other group's per-capita pool.
- `margin` (float, [0.2, 6] log): how far our per-member punishment may exceed the other manager's. I expect 0.5-2 (finding 4: leniency relative to the other group is what holds members); at 6 the cap is rarely binding, at 0.2 the rule nearly copies a lenient opponent's level.

### Justification

Findings 1 and 4 say the pool is mostly membership times contribution and that heavy punishers lose both, so punishment must be light and targeted: hence a shortfall rule with a threshold (`thresh`) and a modest per-point rate (`slope`), whose ranges are set around the levels at which finding 3 shows punishment paying (and the low slope end allows no punishment). Finding 2 (punishment at the decision round raises leaving 22% -> 58-69%) and finding 5 justify `dec_mult` as a separate handle on the decision rounds. Finding 2's opponent coefficient and finding 4's relative-leniency split justify `margin`: it is the only parameter that uses the other manager's behaviour, which the rule can see in `p`, and it makes the rule soften against lenient opponents and keep its targeted punishment against harsh ones. Finding 6 checks that the central values give a human-plausible amount of punishment.

What would contradict it: a sweep peak at slope's low end with dec_mult and margin flat would say that the artificial humans hardly respond to punishment with contributions, and a non-punisher is best; a peak at high slope and high thresh would say the AH contribution model responds to punishment much more than the human data suggest. A margin profile that is flat would mean the opponents differ little in harshness or that relative leniency does not move the AH switch model.

Not modelled: a threshold relative to the group mean (humans punished below-mean contributions, but an absolute norm is simpler and does not let a collapsing group lower its own bar), punishment escalation for repeat offenders, a time trend beyond the last-round cut, and per-opponent identification beyond its punishment level. The data (50 games) cannot separate these effects reliably, and four parameters keep the 256-point sweep dense.
