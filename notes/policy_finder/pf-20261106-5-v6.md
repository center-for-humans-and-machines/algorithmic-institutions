The pool is ~4.1 members x ~11 contribution x 1.6 minus ~5 of punishment per round, so the candidates are members (switching) and the contribution level per unit of punishment. Next: what drives the artificial humans' switching in the simulated games, beyond punishment.
2. **What drives switching in the simulated games?** v5 sweep part 1 (episodes 0-11 of every run, 491k player-decisions), block-level logit of switching with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-5/v6_s2_switch.py. 34-51% of players switch at each decision (51% at the first). Per point of block mean: own group's punishment +0.18, other group's -0.03, own punishment -0.13 (given the group's), own group's mean contribution -0.20, other's +0.07, own contribution +0.13 (the group's high contributors are the mobile ones), and group size -0.42 per member (switch rate 0.62 / 0.51 / 0.39 / 0.30 at sizes 3 / 4 / 5 / 6). Same in payoff terms: own group's mean payoff -0.12, other's +0.06. So members follow the group's mean contribution about as strongly as its mean punishment, and a big group holds its members: a snowball. Next: the dose-response of a round-0 hit, especially small doses, to see whether the norm signal can be bought more cheaply.
3. **Do small doses buy the response?** v1 and v2 sweeps part 1 (which have many small doses), starting members, round-0 hit by c0, with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-5/v6_s3_smalldose.py. Confounded with the design point (points that hit one player lightly hit others too), so directions only. The round-1 response grows roughly linearly with the dose (c0 2-5: 5.1 / 5.5 / 5.6 / 6.4 / 7.0 / 7.7 for 0 / 1-2 / 3-5 / 5-9 / 10-14 / 15-20) and so does the staying cost (0.62 / 0.58 / 0.50 / 0.44 / 0.36 / 0.34); in rounds 9-11 a 1-2 point hit already goes with +1.6 at c0 8-11 (9.95 -> 11.6) at 0.05 staying, but that is mostly the group norm of the point, not the hit. No sign of a cheap threshold dose; the linear response is what v2-v5's slope already exploits. Dead end for a new lever. Next: does the best opening dose differ by opponent (pooled v3-v5 sweeps), since the other manager is the one thing the rule has never used successfully.
4. **Does the best dose differ by opponent?** v3, v4 and v5 sweeps pooled (768 points x 4 opponents, episodes 0-31 of each run), my group's punishment per member-round in rounds 0-1 ("opening") and 2-23 ("late") against each opponent's pool, with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-5/v6_s4_dose_opp.py. A late dose of 0.5-1 per member-round is 11-22 points per member over the game, i.e. 3-5 per member at each of 4 block starts, or less over more rounds.
5. **Does block-start punishment pay differently by group size?** v5 sweep (episodes 0-31), my group at rounds 4, 8, 12: members then, mean contribution then, punishment per member then, against the rest-of-game pool, with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-5/v6_s5_late_by_size.py. Group sizes spread widely (at round 4 a third of my groups have 6+ members, a fifth 1-2): the switching snowball of entry 2 makes the game largely about which group grows. Within size x contribution cells (rounds 4 and 8 pooled), a heavy hit (10-30 per member) costs 11-20% of the rest-of-game pool in groups of 3, 6-16% at 4-5 and 5% to +5% at 6+; a moderate one (2-10) is neutral to slightly negative at 2-4 members and +4-25% at 6+ (e.g. c 0-9: 885 -> 1100; c 9-11: 1694 -> 1819). The regression version (size and mean c as controls) is noisy and partly contradicts the cell table for small groups, so I trust only the direction: block-start punishment is cheaper in a big group (members there switch at 0.22-0.30 vs 0.5-0.6 in groups of 3-4, entry 2, and the norm reaches more players) and dearer in a small one, where it can empty the group. A fixed maintenance target averages these two, which may be why n_late and target_late looked flat in v5. This is the new lever I will try: the maintenance target rises with my group's size.

## Key findings

1. Five versions have reached 64.0 / 65.2 / 68.5 / 67.4 / 67.7; since v3 every re-tuning of "hard hit in rounds 0-1, little later" lands at 67.4-68.6, and v5's surface is noise-dominated (fit rmse 1.78 vs point se 2.1, contradictory effects of target_late and n_late) (exploration 1).
2. The artificial humans switch a lot (34-51% per decision) and toward the group with the higher mean contribution (-0.20 logit per point) and lower mean punishment (+0.18 per point), and a big group holds its members: -0.42 per member, switch rate 0.62 / 0.51 / 0.39 / 0.30 at 3 / 4 / 5 / 6 members (exploration 2).
3. Small doses are not a cheap way to the response: both the contribution response and the staying cost grow roughly linearly with the dose (exploration 3).
4. The best dose is the same against every opponent: 6-13 per member-round in rounds 0-1 and 0.5-1 per member-round afterwards (e.g. against opponent_4 53.5 -> 56.9 -> 52.1 across opening doses), with members falling with the opening dose against all four (exploration 4).
5. Block-start punishment pays by group size: a heavy hit costs 11-20% of the rest-of-game pool in groups of 3 but -5% to +5% at 6+, and a moderate one is neutral in groups of 2-4 and +4-25% at 6+ (exploration 5; direction only, the controls are noisy).
6. The two best rules so far (v3's peak, v4's s095, 68.5-68.6) punished two rounds at later block starts; the single-round peaks (v4, v5) got 67.4-67.7 (explorations 1 and 6).

## Hypothesis

Against these artificial humans a manager sets a contribution norm with a hard, early hit on whoever is below about 10 in rounds 0 and 1, and then maintains it lightly at the start of the next few blocks, when newcomers arrive and punishment costs least in members. Because members flow toward the group with the higher contribution and a big group holds them, the maintenance should depend on how big the group has become: a big group is held to a higher norm (punishment there is cheap in members and its norm effect reaches more players), a small group is spared (punishment there can empty it). Nothing is punished in rounds 2-3 of any block or from round 16 on, and never above a contribution of 14. The rule: a valid member of my group contributing `c` gets `slope * max(target - c, 0)` (the manager clamps to 30) with target = `target_open` in rounds 0-1, `min(max(target_late + size_gain * (members - 4), 0), 14)` in the first `window_late` rounds of blocks 1-3 (rounds 4, 8, 12 and, with 2, also 5, 9, 13), and no punishment otherwise.

- `target_open` (float, [7.5, 12.5]): the opening norm. I expect 9-11 (v3 8.8, v4 9.9, v4's s095 11.2, v5 10.7).
- `slope` (float, [2, 16], log): points per point of shortfall. I expect 4-7 (v3 6.3, v4 5.8, v5 4.5; v5's profile best at 3.3-5.5).
- `target_late` (float, [3, 12]): the maintenance norm for a group of four. I expect 7-10 (v3's 8.8 in rounds 4-5 and 8, s095's ~10-10.5, v5's 9.8-11).
- `size_gain` (float, [-1.5, 3]): change of the maintenance norm per member above four. I expect 0.5-2 (finding 5); negative values let the sweep say that big groups should be spared instead, 0 is v3's flat maintenance.
- `window_late` (int, [1, 2]): punishing rounds at each later block start. I expect 2 (finding 6).

### Justification

Findings 1 and 4 are why the opening is kept as it is (`target_open`, `slope`, rounds 0-1) and why the rule still ignores the other manager: the best doses are the same against all four, so there is nothing to adapt to, and the plateau since v3 says the opening is already tuned. Finding 3 is why the response stays linear in the shortfall rather than a flat token punishment. Findings 2 and 5 are the reason for `size_gain`: the switching snowball makes group size the largest source of variation in the pool, and the cost of punishing depends on it; a fixed maintenance target averages the cheap large-group case with the costly small-group one, which may be why v5's later-block parameters looked flat. Finding 6 is why `window_late` returns as 1 or 2, and finding 4's late optimum (0.5-1 per member-round) is why maintenance stops after block 3 and why `target_late` stays moderate. The cap at 14 comes from v3's finding that punishment backfires on players at 16-20. What would contradict this: `size_gain` best near 0 (size does not matter; the gain is then just v3's structure re-tuned on fewer dimensions) or at -1.5 (big groups should be spared, e.g. because a big group's high contributors leave when others are hit); `target_late` at 3 with window 1 (maintenance is pure cost). Not modelled: the other manager (finding 4), per-player histories and newcomer status (v2: newcomers respond like incumbents; the absolute target catches the low ones), a trigger on the group's contribution level (v5 entry 6 and my entry 5 regression gave contradictory signs), and the opening's dependence on size (always four members in rounds 0-1).
