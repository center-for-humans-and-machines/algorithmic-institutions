## Key findings

1. All three versions reach the same real ceiling. The finals are v1 648.8 and v2
   649.7, and v3's GP peak 645.7, though v3's other final points reach 649.7-651.6.
   A quadratic over the pooled v2+v3 sweeps leaves residuals of sd 3.7, the same
   as the per-point noise, and peaks at 649.8 (T 13.25, A 2.5, B 2.0, S 7) (1).
2. Give-up (N) does not pay on the real clones: the score is 647.6 at N=3 and
   649.8 at N=10. The landscape's effect sizes are T 16.5, B 14.6, A 4.4, S 1.8 and
   N 1.4, so v3's weak final came from the GP peak landing on noise in its flat
   dimensions (1).
3. The bill under the best rules is ~54 payoff points, spent mostly in rounds
   0-2. The fines in rounds 3-8 pay for themselves: cutting them (v3's GP peak)
   lowered the plateau from c 17.0 to 15.9 and cost 6 (2).
4. Round-0 dose-response from the real sweeps:
   - free riders keep answering bigger fines (c1 4.2 at p0 6-10, 10.3 at p0 20-30);
   - middling players saturate (c0 8-10 at p0 ~6-10; c0 11-13 at p0 3-6);
   - fining near-target players hard backfires (c0 14-16: 13.6 at p0 10-15,
     against 15.0 at 3-6).

   This matches a tap plus a linear price below a target of ~13-14 (3).
5. Later fines buy only +0.4-0.7 in the next round for fines of 2-9, against a
   cost of twice the fine. That fits the "silence after S" shape (3).
6. In mock sweeps on the pooled surface, v4's ranges put 20% of design points
   within 2 of the peak, against 8% for v3's and 1% for v2's (4).

## Hypothesis

A good manager sets up the cooperative regime in the first rounds with a
predictable price on falling short of a moderate standard. Every shortfall gets a
small tap, the price rises steeply with the shortfall, and the bulk of the fine
falls on free riders, who are the players that keep answering bigger fines. Once
the group has climbed (by round ~7-10), the manager goes silent. The clones then
carry on with only a slow decay, and late fines cannot repay their double cost.
The rule is v2's: `punishment = A + B*(T - c)` for c < T while t <= S, else 0.
This version searches it more densely, around the plateau all earlier sweeps share.
- `T`, the target: no fine at or above it. Sweep [11.5, 16.5], uniform.
- `A`, the tap any shortfall gets. Sweep [0.5, 4.5], uniform.
- `B`, the price per point of shortfall. Sweep [1.15, 3.0], log-uniform.
- `S`, the last punished round. Sweep int [5, 12].

### Justification

Findings 3-5 support the shape:
- Fines in rounds 0-8 build the climb from 11.5 to 17 (3).
- The response is flat near the target and steep for free riders (4), which is
  a tap plus a linear price.
- Late fines repay too little (5).

Findings 1-2 say the family's ceiling is ~650 on the real clones, and that the
extra dimension of v3 only made the search noisier. So I removed it and narrowed
the ranges (6):
- `T`: the pooled profile peaks at 13-13.5, and the real finals' best points sit
  at 14.3-14.5. Both lie inside [11.5, 16.5]. The profile is 642.5 at 11 and 645.7
  at 15, so the optimum is bracketed.
- `B`: the peak is ~2.0. The range [1.15, 3.0] is centred on it in log, with the
  drop on each side (638.6 at 1.0, 644.3 at 3.2) at or beyond its ends.
- `A`: the peak is 2-3. [0.5, 4.5] holds it with room on both sides.
- `S`: flat from 5 to 11 (1). [5, 12] keeps the whole flat stretch, and lets
  the sweep confirm that a later stop does not help.

What would contradict it: a v4 sweep whose best points sit at an edge (T at 16.5,
B at 1.15, or S at 12), or a final well below the plateau even with a well-sampled
peak. That would mean the surface is bumpier than the pooled quadratic suggests.

Not modelled:
- Give-up counts. The real clones show no gain (2).
- A saturating cap on the fine. It would cut the free riders' fines, which pay
  most (4).
- The vicarious effect of others' fines (+0.42 per point in a regression). It is
  confounded, and the tap already spreads a small fine over every shortfall.
- Late maintenance, ratchets, relative or rising targets, and per-round
  multipliers. v3's surrogate screens found all of them neutral or harmful.
- Timeouts. The rule cannot punish them, and the clones' high returns after a
  timeout are outside the manager's reach.

I expect v4 to land at ~649-652. It may tie v2 rather than beat it, because my
evidence shows no structural lever beyond this shape.
