## Key findings

1. v3's final pool was 67.4 (69.4 / 68.2 / 72.2 / 59.6 against opponents 1-4), +1.0 on v2. Its surface moved only in c0 (effect 8.9, best 10) and k (7.6, best 6.9); decay, follow and nblocks were nearly flat (0.2-1.5), and the best c0 and k were the same against every opponent (exploration 1).
2. About a quarter of my group is new each block (0.85-1.85 arrivals per decision), and newcomers contribute 1.5-3.7 less than stayers. Against the non-punishing opponent 4 they stay low (8.1-9.2) and my stayers sink from 11.75 to 10.5 once v3's punishment has decayed away (exploration 2).
3. A block-start punishment lifts a player never punished before by an extra +0.06 to +0.20 per sqrt point on a base of 0.27-0.46. Being a newcomer adds nothing to the lift (exploration 3).
4. The lift per sqrt point falls with the punishment already received (0.46-0.62 at 0-15 points received, 0.38-0.46 at 15-40, 0.20-0.33 at 40+), not with the round: a little-punished player responds as strongly in round 16 as in round 4. Exits per sqrt point are 0.022-0.035, somewhat higher for the never-punished (exploration 4).

## Hypothesis

A good manager punishes free riders in proportion to their shortfall, hard in round 0 and mainly at block starts after that, far from the switch decisions. It spends its punishment on players who have not yet been punished, because the simulated players' response saturates with the punishment they have received, not with time. A veteran who has already been corrected gets little more. A free rider who arrives later from a lenient group, and has never been punished, gets the full block-start treatment. Saturating on the punishment received from either manager (read from `p`) also adapts the rule to the other manager without identifying it. A punishing neighbour has already done the work on its emigrants, while a lenient one sends untreated free riders, who should be corrected.

The rule: punishment = k * max(0, c0 - c) * decay^block * pos * budget / (budget + received) for valid members of my group, where received is the punishment the player has received so far from either manager. pos is 1 in a block's first round, follow in its second, follow^2 in its third and 0 in its last (follow^2 in round 23).
- `c0` (int, [7, 15]): the expected contribution. I expect 9-12; v3's profile peaked at 10 and falls on both sides.
- `k` (float, [1.5, 20], log): points per point of shortfall for an unpunished player in round 0. I expect 5-10 (v3: 6.9, a free rider at 0-5 hits the 30 cap). The range runs from a near non-punisher to the cap for anyone below c0 - 1.5.
- `budget` (float, [3, 300], log): the saturation scale in points received. I expect 15-80, where the lift halves (finding 4: halved by 40+). At 3 only the first punishment counts. At 300 there is no saturation, and the rule is v3 without its block cut-off.
- `decay` (float, [0.2, 1]): the per-block shrink factor for the rounds left to pay back. Saturation now does part of v3's decay's job, so I expect 0.5-0.9, above v3's 0.42.
- `follow` (float, [0, 1]): the second round's share of a block's punishment. I expect 0.1-0.5 (v3: 0.35, flat).

### Justification

Finding 1 says the timing family is worked out, and that c0 and k carry the score. So both keep v3's form and ranges centred on v3's optimum (slightly narrowed for c0, where the edges lost 5-9 points). Findings 2-4 give the new parameter. The pool leaks through late, never-punished free riders, against the non-punisher in particular (the weakest opponent, 59.6). Those players respond to punishment about twice as well as veterans, so punishing by history beats punishing by the clock. `budget`'s range brackets the 15-40 point zone where the lift starts to fall (finding 4), and its top nests v3. `decay` stays because a lift still has fewer rounds left to pay back later in the game. `follow` and the zero in the decision round stay from v3 (exits rise towards the decision, v3 exploration 4). nblocks was dropped as flat (finding 1).

What would contradict it: a best budget at 300, meaning saturation buys nothing in the full game, or a best decay near 0.2, meaning late punishment of fresh free riders costs more in exits than it lifts. Also a pool against opponent 4 no higher than v3's.

Not modelled:
- A newcomer-specific term. Being new adds no lift and slightly more exit (finding 3).
- A threshold relative to the group mean.
- Any explicit conditioning on the other manager's level. The best c0 and k did not differ by opponent (finding 1), and the saturation already reads the other manager's punishment per player.
The response numbers are within-cell but observational, from simulated games of earlier versions, so the ranges are kept wide.
