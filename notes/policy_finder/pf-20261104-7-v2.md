# pf-20261104-7-v2

## Explorations

1. **What did version 1's sweep say?** Looked at plots/simulation/policy_finder/pf-20261104-7_sweep
   (sweep.json, landscapes.json, final/sweep.json). Version 1's final result: threshold 12.0,
   rate 2.29, t_stop 11, switch_factor 0.28 scored pool 70.5 (se 1.1, 2000 episodes) against `ah`,
   members 4.08; that is the bar (`zero` gets 60.5 in the reference sims). The surface contradicts
   my v1 hypothesis of light punishment: the fitted pool climbs monotonically with rate (60.5 at
   0.1 to 66.7 at 2.5, near the top of [0.05, 3]) and with threshold up to ~12 (60.7 at 2 to 66.7
   at 12), peaks for t_stop ~10-13 (64.1 at 23, 65.5 at 4), and switch_factor has no effect at all
   (length scale 100). Single points carry se ~2 at 500 episodes, so the top points (71.1, 70.5,
   69.6) are within noise of each other. So: hard, broad punishment early, then stopping, is what
   pays; members drop to ~4.1 (from zero's 4.57) yet the pool rises by 10. Next: look at the
   per-round dynamics of the best points: what punishment is actually dealt, when, and how
   contributions evolve after t_stop - does cooperation survive when punishment stops?

2. **What happens round by round under v1's best points?** Looked at v1's final per_round.parquet
   against the reference `zero`/`ah` (members, contribution, punishment, pool of my group per
   round) with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-7/v1_dynamics.py, and a per-point block summary of
   all 256 sweep points with v1_sweep_summary.py. Found the key mechanism: contributions climb while
   punishing (gp: 8.7 in round 0 to 13.2 by round 11) and then *stay* there after punishment stops
   (13.0-12.6 over rounds 12-23), while members, which fell to 3.8 at the first switch, come back
   to 4.4 once punishment stops (newcomers from `ah` conform to the high level). The price is paid
   early: gp spends 8.2 per member in round 0 and its pool there is 22.9 vs zero's 55.7. Under `zero`
   contributions sit at ~8.2 throughout and members at 4.6-4.9. Across the sweep, late
   contribution correlates 0.97 with total punishment and members -0.89; pool 0.5 with punishment.
   Two regimes reach ~69-71: heavy-and-short (s216: ~7 per member for 9 rounds, late c 12.7,
   members ~4.1-4.3) and light-and-short (s036: ~1.8 per member for 9 rounds, late c 10.3,
   members 4.5-4.7). So punishment is an investment in a persistent contribution level; the
   question for v2 is how to buy that level more cheaply and with fewer members lost. Next: the
   individual-level response in the sweep data - how a player's contribution reacts to the size of
   a punishment (does it saturate, so 27 points to a 0-contributor is waste?), and who leaves.

3. **How does a single player respond to the size of a punishment, and who leaves?** Looked at
   v1's sweep per-round data (part 1, 150 episodes per point, my group only) with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-7/sweep_response.py: next-round and t+3 change in contribution
   by contribution level x punishment, split by first-time vs repeat punished, and the leave rate at
   decision rounds. Found (next-round change, first-time punished, vs unpunished baseline):
   0-contributors +4.1 unpunished (regression to the mean), +4.8 at 1-3 points, +5.3 at 8-12, +7.0
   at 21-30; 1-4 contributors +0.8 / +2.5 / +4.1 / +6.2; 5-9 +0.15 / +1.2 / +2.3 / +3.2 (13-20);
   10-12 -0.3 unpunished vs +0.5 at 1-3 and +0.9 at 4-12; 13-15 -1.2 vs -0.65 at 1-3. So the
   response keeps rising with size but is strongly concave: the first 1-3 points buy 0.4-0.8
   contribution per point, 8-12 points ~0.25 per point, 21-30 ~0.1-0.2 per point. It even works on
   10-15 contributors if light. The t+3 change has the same size as t+1 (e.g. 1-4 contributors
   +6.2 at 21-30, +3.4 at 8-12 vs +0.7 unpunished), so the gain persists: a point of contribution
   gained in round 2 is worth ~1.6*20 pool points over the game, which is why heavy early punishment
   paid in v1 despite the poor per-point ratio. Repeat-punished players respond about half as much
   (1-4 contributors +2.3 at 8-12 vs +0.5 baseline). Leaving at a decision rises with that round's
   punishment: 23% unpunished, 35% at 1-3, ~50% at 4-7, 60-70% above 8, nearly independent of
   contribution level; yet v1's switch_factor had no effect on the pool (stayers are pushed up,
   leavers are replaced later). Hard-core free riders exist: 0-contributors who already took 50+
   points stay at 0 next round 63% of the time (37% for unpunished ones).
   So: (a) the shape should be concave - a fixed "base" amount for any shortfall plus a
   proportional part, rather than v1's purely proportional one which gives 27 to a 0-contributor
   and ~1 to an 11-contributor; (b) because the gain persists and the remaining horizon shrinks, the
   intensity should fall with the round rather than switch off abruptly - the value of a point of
   punishment is roughly response x rounds left, so a tapering intensity is the principled form;
   (c) the switch-round multiplier can go: it had no measurable effect in 256 points.

4. **Drafted v2's rule.** For a valid member of my group with c < threshold:
   punishment = (base + rate * (threshold - c)) * time_weight, where time_weight =
   1 - taper * t / t_end for t < t_end and 0 from t_end on. taper = 0 is v1's hard stop, taper = 1
   a linear ramp down to zero at t_end. Five parameters: threshold, rate, base, t_end (int), taper.
   Dropped switch_factor (exploration 1: length scale 100, effect 0.0002). v1's peak sits at
   threshold 12 and rate 2.3, with rate's profile still rising at 2.5 (range end 3), so I widen
   rate upward; with base taking up the flat part, the best rate may be lower than v1's.

5. **Validated the draft and checked the spending it implies.** `validate-rule` passes (256
   distinct design points). With scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-7/cost_check_v2.py I applied
   candidates statically to the contributions seen under v1's final point (no feedback). v1's own
   values (threshold 12, base 0, rate 2.29, t_end 11, taper 0) give 2.46 per member-round (v1's
   actual 2.15, the gap being its switch-round discount), 22% of rows punished, 11.2 points per
   punished row: v2 nests v1's winner. A concave, tapered candidate 12/3/1.0/14/0.7 spends 1.43,
   punishes 26% of rows at 5.4 points each: more players, smaller amounts, over more rounds, for
   ~40% less money - what exploration 3 says should buy more contribution per point. 14/2/0.8/16/1
   spends 1.27 (32% punished, 4.0 each); 16/6/3/20/1 7.3 (far beyond `ah`), 6/0/0.2/6/1 0.01
   (practically `zero`). So the design spans from `zero` to very heavy, with v1's peak inside.

## Key findings

1. v1's final point (threshold 12, rate 2.29, t_stop 11) scored pool 70.5 (se 1.1) against `ah`,
   10 above `zero`'s 60.5; its surface rises with rate and threshold up to ~12 and peaks at
   t_stop ~10-13; the switch-round multiplier had no effect (exploration 1).
2. Punishment buys a persistent contribution level: under v1's best, contributions rise from 8.7 to
   13.2 while punishing and stay at 12.6-13.0 for the 12 rounds after it stops, and members recover
   from 3.8 to 4.4 once punishment stops; the cost is concentrated early (8.2 per member in round 0)
   (exploration 2).
3. The individual response is concave in the size of the punishment: the first 1-3 points buy
   0.4-0.8 contribution per point, 8-12 points ~0.25 per point, 21-30 ~0.1-0.2; it also works,
   lightly, on 10-15 contributors (+0.8 to +0.6 vs unpunished), and the t+3 gain equals the t+1
   gain (exploration 3).
4. A decision round's punishment raises leaving from 23% (none) to 35% (1-3 points) and 60-70%
   (8+), nearly regardless of contribution, but this did not show up in the pool (exploration 3,
   finding 1).
5. Repeat-punished players respond about half as strongly as first-time ones (exploration 3).

## Hypothesis

A good manager on this stack invests in cooperation early and then stops. Because the artificial
humans' contribution gains persist for the rest of the game and newcomers conform to the group's
level, a point of contribution bought in the first rounds is worth ~1.6 pool points in every round
left, while the punishment's cost is paid once. The response per point is concave, so the money
goes further spread as a modest punishment over every member below a fairly high bar than as a
maximal one on the worst free riders; and since the rounds left to collect the gain shrink, the
punishment should taper off and end well before the game does, after which the unpunished group
also draws players back from `ah`.

The rule, for a valid member of my group contributing c < threshold in round t:
punishment = (base + rate * (threshold - c)) * max(1 - taper * t / t_end, 0), and 0 from t_end on.

- `threshold` (float, [6, 18]): the bar below which a member is punished. Expected best ~11-15:
  v1 peaked at 12, and light punishment still helps 10-15 contributors.
- `base` (float, [0, 8]): flat points for any shortfall, the cheap high-yield first points.
  Expected ~1-4; 0 recovers v1's purely proportional shape.
- `rate` (float, [0.2, 6], log): extra points per point of shortfall. Expected ~0.7-2.5, lower
  than v1's 2.3 if base takes over the flat part; widened above v1's range end (3) because v1's
  profile was still rising there.
- `t_end` (int, [6, 22]): first round with no punishment. Expected ~10-16; later with a strong
  taper, since the late rounds then carry little punishment anyway.
- `taper` (float, [0, 1]): how far the punishment ramps down towards t_end (0: v1's hard stop;
  1: linear to zero). Expected ~0.5-1.

### Justification

Finding 2 (gains persist after punishment stops, members come back) is the core of the
hypothesis and grounds `t_end`; together with the falling horizon it grounds `taper`: the value of
a punishment point is roughly its response times the rounds left, so the intensity should fall
with t, and a linear ramp is the simplest form of that while taper = 0 still allows v1's hard stop.
Finding 3 (concave response, also at 10-15) grounds `base` (cheap first points for everyone below
the bar) and a `threshold` range reaching 18; finding 1 grounds keeping `rate` and a threshold
around v1's peak inside the ranges, and widening `rate` upward. Finding 1 and 4 are why I dropped
the switch-round multiplier. What would contradict it: a peak at base ~0 and taper ~0 (the
concavity and horizon arguments do not carry over to the group level, e.g. because conformity to
the group mean makes a few big punishments the efficient lever), or a peak at t_end at 22 (gains do
not persist after all). Not modelled: giving up on hard-core free riders (finding 5: they respond
less but still respond, and a design dimension for it would thin the sweep), thresholds relative to
the group mean (an absolute bar lets spending fall naturally as contributions rise), punishing
newcomers after t_end (v1's groups held their level with newcomers), and the other group's state.
