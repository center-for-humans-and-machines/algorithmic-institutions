## Key findings

1. Five versions have plateaued at 68.3-68.7 (v3 68.72 best, v5 68.58). v5's opponent-stance lever kappa was flat on its surface (effect 0.96, length scale 4.1) (entry 1).
2. After the first switch my group's size is spread almost uniformly over 0..8, and it is empty 7-13% of the time. The score is mostly E[size] x contribution per head, so group size is the largest source of variation in the pool (entry 2).
3. Switching responds to punishment per head, not to its total. The size change per point of my block punishment per head is ~-0.03 to -0.05 members at every group size. So a point dealt to one member costs ~0.03/n members: about 3x cheaper in a group of 8 than in a group of 2-3 (entry 3).
4. Measured with decay as an exogenous lever, within size bins at round 4, more later severity lowers the future pool in small groups and raises it in large ones. The slopes per unit log(decay) are -1.33 (size 1), -0.92 (2-3), ~0 (4-7) and +1.04 (8). From rounds 8 and 12 the gradient is steeper, up to +4.9 and +5.8 at sizes 6-8 (entry 4).

## Hypothesis

In this simulator a good manager disciplines early and in proportion to how far a member falls below the others' mean. It stays silent in the two rounds before each switch, lets the end fade, and scales its discipline with the size of its group. Group membership is very volatile, and switchers respond to the punishment per head of a group. In a large group, the punishment a shirker needs is diluted across many members, so it costs few members while it raises the shirker's and the bystanders' contributions. In a small group the same punishment is a big per-head signal that empties the group. The rule therefore punishes harder in large groups and more gently in small ones, keeping the v3-v5 timing. The rule: punishment = rate * decay^b * (n/4)^sigma * max(0, others' mean + delta - c) * w_pos * min(1, (23 - t)/3). Here b = floor(t/4), w_pos = 1, mid, 0, 0 by position in blocks 0-4 (1 in block 5), and n = my group's members this round.
- `rate` (float, [3, 20] log): points per point of shortfall in block 0 (group of four). I expect 6-13.
- `delta` (float, [-3, 0.8]): tolerance relative to the others' mean. I expect -2 to 0.
- `decay` (float, [0.15, 0.85]): severity multiplier per block. I expect 0.4-0.65.
- `mid` (float, [0.2, 1.8]): weight of a block's second round. Its surface is flat, and I expect 0.6-1.3.
- `sigma` (float, [-0.5, 2.0]): elasticity of severity in group size. I expect 0.4-1.2; 0 is v4's rule.

### Justification

Finding 1 says the old levers are exhausted, so v6 adds one new axis and keeps the core's ranges where the v4/v5 peaks were interior. Findings 2 and 3 give the mechanism for sigma. Group size varies over a wide range after round 4, and the membership cost of a point of punishment falls as 1/n because the switching model reads per-head punishment. Finding 4 is the direct, mostly causal evidence: decay cannot have shaped the round-4 size, and the slope of the future pool on later severity goes from negative in groups of 1-3 to positive in groups of 8. My expected sigma (0.4-1.2) comes from setting those slopes against the pool's curvature in log severity (~2.5, from the v5 decay profile). Normalising at n = 4 leaves block 0 (always four members) exactly as before, so the sweep can find the v4/v5 optimum again at sigma = 0.

What would contradict the hypothesis:
- A best sigma at or below 0. That would mean the size-bin slopes came from composition (large groups at round 4 holding better contributors) rather than from cheaper punishment.
- A best sigma at the upper edge. That would mean the size effect is stronger than the round-4 estimate, as the later block starts suggest.

What I chose not to model:
- The opponent's stance (kappa, flat in v5).
- Targets from the other group's contributions.
- A separate size effect on the tolerance delta.
- Grace for newcomers (no support in v5's entry 8).
I expect a modest gain, ~+0.5-1.5 over the 68.7 plateau, because the size factor only changes punishment after round 4, where most punishment has already been dealt.
