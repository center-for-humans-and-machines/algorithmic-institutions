# pf-20261112-2-v4

## Explorations

1. **Where do versions 1-3 leave me?** Read their notes, rules and `final/sweep.json`. All three finals landed on "never punish" (v3 at fine 0) and scored the identical +16.76 +- 1.42. v3's targeted rule (fine low positive givers in the first rounds of blocks 1-4) lost like the others: in its sweep the 32 non-punishing points average 15.18, punishing points 13.0-14.5. New and important: the finals re-run 6 points with batch seeds `seed + batch index`, one batch per point and seat, so the `gp` slot always gets the same seeds (1000, 1001). The never-punish policy scores 16.76 on the `gp` seeds but 14.81 and 14.79 on two other slots (v1 s245/s093 and v3 s007/s245, all of which floor to no fine). So the bar 16.76 is never-punish *plus about +1.8 of seed luck*. Since a batch draws its randomness element-wise for 1000 episodes at once, an episode in which my rule never fines should replay the never-punish episode exactly; an episode in which it fines diverges and loses that luck. Consequence for v4: a rule that fines in every game must gain ~+2 in truth to beat the bar; a rule that fines only in rare states keeps the luck in the other games and needs only a positive effect where it fires. Next: what the never-punish games look like round by round.

2. **What does the race look like under never-punish?** Looked at the `gp` games of v3's final (2000 games, both seats; per round sizes, pools, ah's spend) with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-2/v4_gp_state.py. Lead by round: 15.7 -> 7.9 over rounds 0-3 (ah fines 15 -> 11.5 a round, which is what gives me the lead while its per-capita contributions are higher, 10.4 vs ~8), jumps to 27 at round 4 (members 4.00 -> 4.98), then decays steadily to 10.8 by round 23 as members drift back (4.98 -> 4.82 -> 4.72 -> 4.67 -> 4.60). Per-capita contributions in ah's group stay 0.5-2 above mine all game; per-capita pools are about equal after round 4 (13.6 vs 13.4 at round 4; 13.5 vs 14.1 at round 12), and the drift back follows that. The per-game lead is dominated by the final size of my group: -67 when my group empties, +67 when it holds all 8 (each size step ~ +20). So membership is everything, and the drift after round 4 is the loss I could try to stop. Next: how v3's spend related to its score, point by point.

10. **Drafted the rule: "fine the shirker relative to his own group".** A valid member of my group is fined a flat `fine` if the other valid members' mean contribution this round exceeds his by at least `gap`, my group has at least `n_min` members, and the round lies in blocks `start` .. `last_block - 1` (t from 4*start to 4*last_block - 1). ## Key findings

1. The bar of +16.76 is never-punish on lucky seeds: the finals give each slot fixed batch seeds, and the never-punish policy scores 16.76 on the `gp` slot but 14.81 and 14.79 on two others; games in which a rule never fines replay exactly (max |diff| 0.0000), so finals and sweeps are paired experiments against never-punish (explorations 1, 5, 6).
2. In paired comparisons every earlier rule family loses about three quarters of what it spends: -0.033, -0.031, -0.032 per point per game for v1, v2, v3 (pure cost is -0.042); only 9 of 101 paired points are more than 1 SE above zero, about chance; the unpaired "-0.85 for starting to fine" was seed noise (explorations 3, 6).
3. Splitting the paired effect by the state at the later fines is fooled by post-treatment selection (my group being large at a fine looked like +0.57 per point, but that state is reached by games the earlier fines did not hurt); at each game's first fine, where both arms are identical, the size effect largely vanishes (-0.44 to -1.06 per point by size) (explorations 7, 8).
4. What separates a costly fine from a break-even one is the target's shortfall below the rest of his group at the first fine: -1.29 per point when he is at or above the others' mean, -1.02 at 0-4 below, -0.56 at 4-8, -0.22 at 8-12, +0.20 +- 0.4 at 12-14, -0.42 at 14-20; his absolute contribution separates much less (-0.56 to -1.19) (exploration 8).
5. Large shortfalls are rare under never-punish: 5.1% / 2.7% / 1.4% of member-rounds at 8 / 10 / 12 points below the others, 2-5 fines a game in rounds 0-19, targets giving 2-4 on average; the rate is flat across blocks and higher in small groups (exploration 9).
6. Membership decides the game: the per-game lead runs from -67 (my group empties) to +67 (all 8), about +20 per member at the end; my lead is built in block 0 (ah fines 15 a round) and the first switch (4.00 -> 4.98 members), then decays as members drift back to ah's sorted, higher-giving group (explorations 2, 4).
7. Fines in block 0 cost far more than themselves in the event regression (-4.05 per point), but not visibly more than later blocks in the clean big-shortfall slices; endgame fines were pure cost in v3 (explorations 7, 8).
8. In the rule engine, `sum(..., keepdim=True)` fails in a fresh process (a lazy import), which `validate-rule` does not catch (exploration 11).

## Hypothesis

Against this stack, fines nearly never pay: contributions respond, but members respond more, and members are the score. Punishing at all is only defensible where both levers point the same way, which is a member far below the rest of his own group: his fine raises his giving the most (a conditional cooperator pulled toward a generous group), and if he leaves instead, my per-capita pool, which steers everyone's switching, goes up while ah's goes down. Fining a member close to the group's mean (which absolute thresholds keep doing in stingy groups) reads as arbitrary and pushes ordinary givers out. So a good manager here stays out of the way almost always and fines only clear relative shirkers, in groups big enough that an exit cannot start a collapse, and not when fines can no longer buy anything. I expect this to be about break-even to slightly positive; never-punish stays in the design as a fallback.

The rule (`configs/managers/rule_based/pf-20261112-2-v4.yml`): a valid member of my group is fined `fine` if the other valid members' mean contribution this round exceeds his by at least `gap`, my group has at least `n_min` members, and 4*start <= t <= 4*last_block - 1; otherwise 0.
- `gap` (float, [5, 15]): the shortfall line, in points below the other members' mean. Expect 9-13.
- `fine` (float, [0, 10]): flat fine; below 1 floors to no punishment, so never-punish is 10% of the design. Expect 2-6.
- `n_min` (int, [2, 6]): smallest group size at which to fine; 2 means almost always. Expect 4-5.
- `start` (int, [0, 2]): first fining block (0 = from round 0). Expect 0-1.
- `last_block` (int, [3, 6]): fines stop after round 4*last_block - 1 (5 = at the last switch decision, 6 = never stop). Expect 5.

### Justification

Finding 4 is the core and sets `gap`: the per-point return climbs from -1.3 to about 0 as the first target's shortfall goes from 0 to 12+, so the range [5, 15] runs from the costly side (5, about -0.6) across the break-even zone (8-14) to where shortfalls are so rare (finding 5: 0.7% of member-rounds at 14) that the rule is nearly off; the best should be inside. The slices behind finding 4 come from rules that also fined smaller shortfalls later in the same games, so a rule that fines only large shortfalls should do at least as well as they show. `fine` [0, 10]: the clean slices are at the earlier versions' fines of 1-8; the bottom tenth floors to never-punish (findings 1-2: the honest fallback), and the top lets the sweep test whether a larger fine pushes the shirker out to good effect. `n_min` follows finding 6 (an emptied group costs ~-67) and the faint size gradient of finding 3 (-0.44 per point at 7-8 members vs -0.9 to -1.06 below); [2, 6] includes "no size condition" as the contrast. `start` [0, 2] and `last_block` [3, 6] leave findings 7's two open questions to the sweep, with the expected values (start 0-1, stop at round 19) inside and both "fine from round 0" and "fine to the end" reachable. Contradicting evidence would be a peak at fine < 1 (relative targeting does not rescue punishment either, and never-punish is simply best here), a peak at gap = 5 (the shortfall gradient was an artefact), or at n_min = 2 with start = 0 and last_block = 6 (no timing or size structure at all). A caution for whoever reads the result: by finding 1 the earlier finals' 16.76 contains about +1.8 of seed luck on the `gp` slot; a paired comparison against never-punish on the same seeds is the fair test of this rule, and the same pairing makes the final's comparison less noisy than its 1.42 SE suggests. What I chose not to model: a fine that scales with the shortfall (the flat fine plus `gap` already selects the regime, and a slope would thin the 256-point design), conditioning on ah's group size (ah-empty states looked best, -0.25 per point, but are rare and partly the same as my group being large), phase within the block (the clean slices had too few games per phase once restricted to large shortfalls), and repeat-offender memory (the shortfall is measured on the current round, which is what the analysis conditioned on).
