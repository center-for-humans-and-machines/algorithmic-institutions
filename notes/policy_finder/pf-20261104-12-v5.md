## Explorations

1. **Where does v4 stand?** Read v4's notes, rule, `landscapes.json` and
   `final/sweep.json`. v4's final is 72.9 (se 1.15, 4.31 members) at thr
   9.0, peer 0.54, slope 20, decay 0.31, follow 0.70, size 3.0 - three of
   six parameters at an edge (slope, decay, size), and no better than v3's
   73.0, which stays the mark to beat. The final's runners-up range 69.1-73.7
   (s206 at size 2.96, slope 9.4, decay 0.52: 73.7 on 2000 episodes); the
   only point with negative size is the worst (69.1). The fitted surface's
   effects: decay 8.6, slope 6.6, thr 5.0, peer 2.6, size 2.0, follow 1.7.
   So the size lever is real but small (a couple of points at most), and
   the extreme corner (slope 20, decay 0.31) says: hit very hard in block
   0, almost nothing after. Four versions now sit on the same 71-74
   plateau. Also re-read the switch model's config (joint_exodus.yml): its
   inputs are only the per-member common good net of punishment, own
   punishment, group and round - *not* own contribution - and the
   simulation's artifacts (model weights) are absent here, so I can only
   learn from the earlier sweeps' per-round data. Cached v2, v3, v4 sweeps
   (cache_sweep.py) to /tmp. Next: where in the game does the rule's group
   still lose pool against what is possible - per round of v4's final and

2. **Where does the pool go, round by round, against `ah` and `zero`?** Ran
   v1_final.py on v4's final and the same table on the reference
   `zero_vs_ah` sims. v4's group: pool 4.5 / 25.1 at rounds 0-1 (12.9 and
   10.3 punishment per member), ~71 at rounds 2-3, 30.5 / 45.5 at rounds 4-5
   (9.9 / 7.9 per member), 55.6 / 66.1 at rounds 8-9, ~88 from round 14 on.
   The punished rounds 0,1,4,5,8,9 cost ~260 pool points against their
   neighbours, ~11 per round of the game. Members are set at the first
   decision (4.36) and hardly move after. Contributions end at 12.6 - but
   `ah`'s group, facing v4, ends at 12.4 too, while facing `zero` it ends at
   9.4: players carry their contributions across groups, so part of what
   the punishment buys leaks to the rival. `zero` itself gets 4.90 members
   at the first decision (we get 4.36) with flat contributions ~8.0.
   So the trade is: ~0.5 member and ~11 pool per round of punishment bill
   for +4.6 contribution per member. Next: human managers whose groups
   reached high contributions - how did they punish?
3. **What did the human managers of high-contribution groups do?** Ran
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-12/v5_human_high.py (one copy per game,
   100 group-episodes, quartiles of rounds 12-23 mean contribution). The top
   quartile reaches 16.0 late (pool 85.9), the bottom 3.2. The top quartile's
   managers punished members giving 0-5 at 10.8 per such member-round,
   5-12 at 5.0 and 12+ at 0.4, and kept doing it: 3.4 per member in block
   0, 2.3, 2.3, 1.4, 1.2, 1.1 after - a taper, but not a stop. Across group
   games, late contribution correlates +0.66 with the punishment of
   low contributors and -0.30 with that of high ones; the pool correlates
   -0.30 with block-0 punishment per member. So the humans' good managers
   punish low givers consistently, not just once at the start, and spare
   the high ones fully. Our sims say the opposite about time (decay to
   0.3), so the sim's contribution model may forget punishment slower than
   humans, or the member cost dominates. Next: who v4 still punishes in
   rounds 4-9 - newcomers or stayers, and at what contribution.
4. **Who does v4 still punish after round 1?** Ran
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-12/v5_who.py on v4's final. Contributions
   are bimodal: at round 0 0.63 members per episode give 0-2 and 0.83 give
   15-20. A core of 0-2 stayers (0.30-0.35 per episode) stays at 0-2 through
   rounds 4-13 despite ~18-20 points every punished round; at round 4 the
   newcomers carry two thirds of the bill (28.8 of 43 per episode), since
   the joiners from `ah`'s group are disproportionately low givers (`ah`
   punishes them, and the switch model reads own punishment). Re-reading
   the switch model's inputs (common good, own punishment, group, round;
   not own contribution) gives a thought: who leaves depends on his
   contribution only through his punishment. So a punished free rider's
   exit is (given his punishment and the group's history) a random draw -
   which lets me ask what a free rider's exit is worth. Side check
   (v5_cg0.py, v3+v4 sweeps, 1.4M episode records): the member gain at
   decision 3 is close to linear in round 0's and round 1's per-member common
   good from -40 to +15 (about +0.04 and +0.06 members per point) and
   flattens above 15; `ah`'s block-0 common good -0.22 per point of its

5. **What is a low giver's exit worth?** First try (v5_exit.py, low givers
   of block 0 who leave vs stay, within punishment bins) is muddled by the
   joint exodus head: when one leaves, others tend to leave with him
   (n_rest -1.24 per exit). So I moved to the group level
   (scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-12/v5_exit2.py, decision 3, v3+v4
   sweeps, 256k episodes, within design point): the rule group's pool per
   round over rounds 4-23 on the counts of leavers and joiners by their
   block-0 mean contribution (low <= 5, mid 5-12, high > 12), controlling
   for the counts and the punishment received of each band in both groups
   and both groups' block common good. Per exit, against staying:
   a low giver leaving *raises* the later pool by +4.4 per round (se 0.16)
   and costs only -0.18 members over the rest of the game, and raises the
   remaining members' contribution by +0.37; a mid giver leaving -5.7, a high
   giver -20.3 (-0.71 members). A low joiner costs -3.0 per round, a high
   joiner brings +15.3. So members are not all worth having: a free rider
   is worth less than nothing to the pool (he gives ~0 and drags the
   herd down), and his exit is soon refilled. That is a lever none of my
   versions used on purpose: they punish free riders to *raise* them, at
   the block's first rounds, when punishment barely moves anyone's switch
   decision. Next: can punishment push free riders out cheaply - how does
   own punishment at each block position move one's own leaving, and what
   does it do to the others' leaving, in the sweeps that punished late in
   the block (v1, and v2's quiet 1)?
6. **Can punishment push free riders out cheaply?** Cached v1's sweep (it
   has quiet 0 and 1, so punishment at block positions 2-3) and ran
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-12/v5_pos3.py 0: logit of a rule-group
   member's leaving at decision 3 on his own punishment at each block
   position and on his mates' mean punishment at each position, with
   contributions and `ah`'s common good as controls. Own and mates'
   punishment move leaving by the same amount per point: +0.017, +0.028,
   +0.041, +0.052 log-odds at positions 0-3 (own) and +0.017, +0.029, +0.037,
   +0.053 (mates' mean); `ah`'s members' joining falls -0.018 ... -0.049 per
   point of our per-member punishment. Worked example: 30 points on a free
   rider at round 3 in a group of four raises his leaving ~+37 pp (worth
   ~+1.6 pool/round at exploration 5's +4.4) but every mate's by ~+13 pp
   (0.39 leavers, mostly mid/high givers, ~-4.7) and cuts joiners by ~0.37
   (~-2.2). Net clearly negative: since the switch model sees punishment
   through the common good, a point aimed at one member pushes all members
   out alike. Dead end - expulsion cannot be targeted. Next: on the
   contribution side, whether repeat punishment of the stubborn 0-2 core

7. **Do hardened free riders stop answering?** Two looks. (a)
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-12/v5_repeat.py (v3+v4): of the

8. **Taking stock.** Every lever I tried this round is closed: expulsion
   (exploration 6: punishment pushes all members out alike), forgiveness
   (7), a common-good floor (4: member flows are linear in the early
   common good). What the data keep confirming is v4's shape: costs and
   gains of a point are linear, so the best rule is near bang-bang per
   cell (who, which round), and the cells that pay are low givers at a
   block's first two rounds, early in the game, in large groups. The one
   unfinished business is v4's surface: its fitted peak sat on three edges
   at once (slope 20, decay 0.31, size 3), with slope and size profiles
   still rising (+0.2 per last step) and decay flat at its floor - the
   sweep could not see where the optimum is. Meanwhile thr, peer and
   follow peaked well inside their ranges in both v3 and v4, at nearly the
   same values (thr 9-10, peer 0.45-0.55, follow 0.7-0.8), so their ranges

## Key findings

1. v4's final (72.9, 4.31 members) did not beat v3's (73.0); its fitted
   peak lay on three edges (slope 20, decay 0.31, size 3), but the raw
   design points show a broad plateau (best 15 at 74.4-76.6 spread over
   slope 3.3-19, decay 0.31-0.97, size -0.7-3.0) (explorations 1, 9).
2. v4's group pays ~11 pool points per round of the game for its
   punishment, all in rounds 0-1, 4-5 and 8-9; members are set at the
   first decision (4.36 vs `zero`'s 4.90) and contributions end at 12.6 -
   with `ah`'s group, fed by movers, at 12.4 instead of the 9.4 it reaches
   against `zero` (exploration 2).
3. Human managers of the top-quartile groups (late contribution 16.0)
   punished 0-5 givers at 10.8 per member-round, 12+ givers at 0.4, with a
   taper (3.4 -> 1.1 per member by block) but no stop (exploration 3).
4. Member flows at the first decision are linear in rounds 0-1's
   per-member common good from -40 to +15 (+0.04, +0.06 members per point)
   and flatten above ~15 (exploration 4).
5. A low giver's exit raises the group's later pool by +4.4 per round and
   a high giver's costs -20.3; a low joiner costs -3.0, a high joiner
   brings +15.3 (exploration 5).
6. Own and mates' punishment raise a member's leaving by the same amount
   per point (+0.017 / +0.017 at position 0 up to +0.052 / +0.053 at
   position 3), so punishment cannot push free riders out without pushing
   everyone out (exploration 6).
7. The answer to a punishment does not shrink with the punishment a
   member has already taken (dose x cum +0.0004); hardened members rise
   less at any dose (exploration 7).

## Hypothesis

A good manager in this stack makes its group visibly more generous than
the other early, when member flows are decided, and then lets herding
carry the level, paying as little as it can in common good, the quantity
every player of both groups weighs when deciding whether to switch. Since
gains and member costs are linear per point and punishment cannot target
who leaves, the best manager is close to bang-bang: it caps the clearly
low givers at the first round of a block, follows up more lightly in its
second, stays quiet before each switch and after the last one, tapers
block by block, and presses harder when its group is large (a point is
spread over more members and seen by fewer outsiders) and gently when it
is small. v5 keeps v4's rule and gives the sweep room where v4's surface
was cut off.

The rule: `punishment = slope * decay^block * w_pos * (n/4)^size *
max(0, bar - c_t)`, `bar = (1-peer)*thr + peer*mates' mean`, `w_pos` 1 at a
block's first round, `follow` at its second, 0 at its last two and in rounds
20-23; n the group's current size.

- `thr` (float, [7, 12]): fixed part of the bar. v3 and v4 peaked at 9-10
  (within 0.5 of the top from 7.6 to 10.4); I expect 8.5-10.
- `peer` (float, [0.15, 0.9]): weight of mates' mean in the bar. Both
  peaked at 0.45-0.55; I expect 0.35-0.65.
- `slope` (float, [5, 50], log): punishment per shortfall point at round 0.
  v4's profile still rose at its edge 20; I expect 15-35, i.e. an opening
  that is nearly a step at the bar.
- `decay` (float, [0.15, 0.8], log): per-block factor. v4's profile was
  flat at its floor 0.3; I expect 0.2-0.4.
- `follow` (float, [0.2, 1.3]): second-round punishment relative to the
  first. Both peaked at 0.7-0.8; I expect the same.
- `size` (float, [0, 4]): elasticity to group size, (n/4)^size. v4's
  profile rose to its edge 3; I expect 2.5-4.

### Justification

Finding 1 says the shape is right and the dials are flat, with three
dials cut off at an edge: hence the same rule with room past those edges
(slope to 50, decay down to 0.15, size up to 4) and tighter ranges where
v3 and v4 agree (thr, peer, follow), so the 256 points are spent where the
surface is still unknown. Findings 2 and 4 support the hard, early,
tapering opening: the common good of rounds 0-1 weighs least in members
per point, members are set at the first decision, and the late game is
then nearly free. Finding 3 (humans never fully stop) is the one piece of
evidence for a higher decay; the range keeps 0.8 in reach. Findings 5 and
6 explain why the size term should be positive and steep rather than a
targeting term: low givers are worth losing, but punishment pushes every
member out alike, so the only member-side control is how much common good
to spend, which costs least where the group is large. Finding 7 is why I
did not add a forgiveness term. What would contradict the hypothesis: a
peak back inside v4's ranges with no gain (the edges were noise, as
finding 1's raw bins hint), or best values at the new edges again (slope
50 or size 4 would say a pure step that ignores small groups entirely is
better; decay 0.15 would say block 0 alone matters). Not modelled: the
other group's state (its size is 8-n, its common good I cannot move), the
identity of joiners (findings 5-6: not reachable by punishment), and
punishment at block positions 2-3 (finding 6: it costs 2-3x as many
members per point as position 0).
