# pf-20261104-5-v7

1. **Where did v6 end up?** Read every version's `final/sweep.json` and
   v6's `landscapes.json`. v6's peak (k=7.9, thr=10.3, w=0.48, t_stop=9,

   close race costs more lift than it saves members. The bar is still v4

   versions sit on 67-72.7 with zero at ~60.5. Next: look in v6's sweep
   for something other than the dose that separates good games, in
   particular what happens *after* the first switch, when the group size
   is known.
2. **Per-block table of v6's sweep.** Built
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v7_blocks.py (both parts, 128k
   games, group 0 ours): per game and block our size at the block start,
   our and ah's punishment, contribution per member, pool. Means by block:
   our punishment 37/38/23/15/9/4 per block against ah's 53/30/24/19/16/13;
   our pool per round 49 -> 60 -> 66 -> 69 -> 70 -> 71, ah's 44 -> 60. So
   in v6's sweep we already punish less than ah in block 0 and more in

   we hold then, which the earlier versions never conditioned on.
3. **Does a later block's punishment pay, and does it depend on our
   size?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v7_late_value.py. A
   natural experiment on t_stop: arm A stops just before block b
   (t_stop in [4b-4, 4b-1]), arm B punishes block b and stops at its end
   (t_stop in [4b, 4b+3]). The other parameters are balanced by the
   design, and the size at block b's start is nearly the same in both
   arms. B - A in our pool over rounds 4b..23 (se), by size n at block
   start:
   - block 1: n 1-2 +0.5 (1.0), 3-4 -1.7 (1.5), 5-6 +4.0 (1.6),
     7 +2.5 (1.9), 8 +4.3 (1.8); members lost at the next decision
     -0.1/-0.2/-0.3/-0.1/-0.35; contribution per member over the rest
     +0.1/+0.1/+0.7/+0.5/+1.2.
   - block 2: n 1-2 -0.3, 3-4 +1.3, 5-6 +2.7, 7 +3.7, 8 +4.8 (se
     0.7-1.6).
   - block 3: about 0 at n <= 7, +6.6 (1.7) at n = 8.
   - block 4: -0.8 to -1.9 everywhere, i.e. nothing.
   So after the first switch the push pays in proportion to how many
   members it reaches. A big group has many low givers to lift and little
   to lose, since the other group is small and its pull weak. In a small
   group the lift is ~0.1 per member and the members lost cost a lot.
   A v4-v6 dose with a fixed t_stop is too hard on small groups and too
   soft on big ones. Next: the dose response per point by size.
4. **Dose response by size (a dead end).**
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v7_late_dose.py regressed the
   pool from block b on a design-level dose proxy (fitted from log k,
   thr and w) within each size bin, in both arms. The A arm, which has no
   block-b punishment, shows slopes as large as the B arm in blocks 2-3
   (e.g. block 3, n 5-6: B 5.9, A 4.9). So the proxy mostly measures the
   legacy of the design's earlier dose, and I cannot read a marginal
   dose response from it. Block 1's B - A slope difference is positive
   in every bin (+0.2 at n 1-2 up to +3.9 at n 7), which agrees with 3
   in sign only. I keep the t_stop experiment of 3 as the evidence.
5. **Drafted v7: a size-elastic dose, guard dropped.** v6's guard went to
   its off end (exploration 1), so I removed m. I kept v4-v6's graded,
   block-timed dose. Its points per contribution point now scale with our
   current size n: k * (n/4)^s, so the dose is
   min(30, k (n/4)^s relu(thr - c_i)) * w^(t mod 4) for valid members
   while t <= t_stop. In rounds 0-3 n is always 4, so the block-0 race of
   v6's explorations is untouched and k keeps its old meaning there.
   After the first switch, s > 0 pushes harder in a big group and softer
   in a small one, which is the pattern of exploration 3; s = 0 is v6
   without its guard, so the plateau's best rule stays inside the design.
   (The code first used `.sum(-1, keepdim=True)`, which fails with a
   KeyError '__import__' in the builtin-free scope; switched to
   `.sum(-1).unsqueeze(-1)`.) Checked with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/test_rule_v7.py: with k=6, thr=10,
   w=0.5, a member giving 8 gets 12 at n=4 for any s, 12 at n=8 with s=0
   and 30 with s=1.5; a 0-giver in a pair gets 21 instead of 30 at s=1.5;

6. **Ranges and validation.** s: [-0.5, 3]. Exploration 3's gain per
   block rises roughly linearly from about 0 at n<=4 to +4-5 at n=8. The
   members lost per block barely change with n (-0.1 to -0.35). So I
   expect s at 0.5-2: at s=1 a group of 8 gets twice the per-point dose
   and a pair half; at s=2, 4x and 1/4. The 30 cap limits the top end
   anyway. -0.5 leaves room below 0, in case I misread the sign. k
   [2, 20] log, around v4-v6's best 5.4-12.2. thr [7.5, 13] around
   10-10.9. w [0, 1] (peaks 0.33-0.48). t_stop [3, 23]: v6 peaked at 9
   and v4 at 10-11, and with s > 0 the late rounds of a big group may
   pay (block 3 at n=8, +6.6), so t_stop could move up. The validator
   passes on all 256 points.

## Key findings

1. v6's share guard did not help. m's fitted peak sat at its off end
   (-13.9 of [-15, 5]) and the final scored 70.6 (se 1.1). The bar is
   still v4's 71.3-72.7, and zero gets ~60.5 (exploration 1).
2. Punishing a block after the first switch pays in proportion to our
   size at that block's start. These are t_stop natural-experiment
   effects on our pool from block b on (exploration 3):
   - block 1: about 0 at n <= 4, +2.5 to +4.3 at n >= 5;
   - block 2: rising from -0.3 at n 1-2 to +4.8 at n = 8;
   - block 3: only n = 8 gains, +6.6;
   - block 4: nothing.
3. The lift behind 2 is larger per member in big groups: +0.7 to +1.2
   contribution per member over the rest of the game at n >= 5, against
   +0.1 at n <= 4. The membership cost of a punished block is small at
   every size, -0.1 to -0.35 members (exploration 3).
4. A design-level dose proxy cannot separate a block's marginal dose
   from the design's earlier dose. The arm without block-b punishment
   shows the same slopes (exploration 4).

## Hypothesis

Punishment lifts contributions for good, and its price is members who
leave and the points it takes from the pool. Once the first switch has
sorted the players, that trade depends on how many members the group
holds. A big group has many low givers to lift, and the small other
group pulls few of them away. A small group gains little and loses
members it cannot spare. So a good manager keeps the graded, block-timed
push that wins the plateau in rounds 0-3. After that it scales the push
with its size: harder when it holds more than its 4 starting members,
softer when it holds fewer. The rule's dose is
min(30, k (n/4)^s relu(thr - c_i)) * w^(t mod 4) for valid members while
t <= t_stop.

- `k` (float, [2, 20], log): punishment points per contribution point
  below thr at the starting size 4, before the 30 cap. I expect 5-12.
- `thr` (float, [7.5, 13]): the contribution from which a member is
  safe. I expect 9.5-11.
- `w` (float, [0, 1]): the weight per round of distance from the
  block's first round. I expect 0.3-0.5.
- `t_stop` (int, [3, 23]): the last punishing round. I expect 9-15.
- `s` (float, [-0.5, 3]): the size elasticity of the dose. I expect
  0.5-2.

### Justification

Finding 1 retires the guard, and with it m. Finding 2 is the reason for
s:
- the value of a punished block rises with size in blocks 1-3, and is
  about nil at n <= 4;
- a size-blind dose is therefore too hard in small groups and too soft
  in big ones;
- (n/4)^s leaves block 0 (n = 4) exactly as before and nests the old
  rule at s = 0.

Finding 3 says the mechanism is the lift, not membership. A big group
can take a harder push without losing more members, which is why I
expect s > 0 and not merely a size-dependent stop. k, thr and w keep the
ranges centred on the plateau's interior peaks. t_stop keeps its full
range because finding 2 (block 3 at n = 8) says late rounds may pay once
the dose is size-scaled.

The hypothesis is contradicted in two cases:
- the sweep's best s sits near 0 or below;
- the final stays at 70-72.
Either would mean the size pattern in finding 2 is the 30 cap and the
group's mix of givers rather than the size itself.

I chose not to model these:
- A separate size threshold. (n/4)^s with a large s already all but
  switches off small groups.
- A size-dependent t_stop. With s it is mostly redundant, and finding 4
  gives no dose response to fit it to.
- Any reading of the other group's state beyond what n implies.
