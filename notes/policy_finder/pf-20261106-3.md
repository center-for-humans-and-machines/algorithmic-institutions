# pf-20261106-3

## Explorations

1. **What does the game look like, and what drives switching?** Looked at the
   deduplicated human data (50 games, 9600 rows) with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-3/a1_overview.py and a2_switch.py.
   Mean contribution is flat around 9 (8.2-9.7) across rounds; human managers
   punish hard early (3.7 mean in round 0) and less later (~1.1). Group sizes
   spread widely (1 to 8 members), so group size is a large lever on the
   pool, which is a group total. 28.6% of players switch at a decision (43%
   at the first). Quintiles of (own per-capita pool - other's) give switch
   rates 0.47, 0.51, 0.31, 0.16, 0.09; quintiles of own punishment received
   in the block give 0.18 ... 0.50. Logit: own per-capita pool -0.125/pt,
   other's +0.055, the player's own punishment +0.17/pt, the *other* group's
   mean punishment -0.20/pt (others punishing keeps people home), own group
   size -0.13. So punishment drives people out, a high per-capita pool keeps
   them, and a harsh rival manager pushes people toward us. Next: does
   punishment raise contributions enough to pay for itself and its exodus?

2. **Does punishment pay for itself in contributions?** Looked at round-to-round
   change in contribution of players who stayed, by previous contribution and
   previous punishment, with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-3/a3_contrib.py.
   OLS: contribution_t = 1.39 + 0.82 c_{t-1} + 0.153 p_{t-1} - 0.003 p_{t-1}^2.
   For low contributors (0-4) the rise is +1.5 unpunished vs +1.0/+1.7/+2.3/+3.7
   for punishment bins 1-2/3-5/6-10/11-30: roughly +0.15-0.3 contribution per
   point, i.e. +0.25-0.5 pool next round per point that costs 1 pool point now.
   Punished high contributors (15-20) drop sharply (-8 to -16 at p>2). Two
   rounds on, punishment adds only 0.045/pt beyond the carried contribution.
   Human managers punished by deviation below the group mean (7.1 at dev<-8,
   0.85 near the mean, 0.7-0.8 above). So punishment is at best roughly
   break-even in the short run, must never hit high contributors, and costs
   members (entry 1). Next: which managers ended up with large pools in the
   human games, and how much is it group size vs per-capita?

3. **Which human managers ended with big pools?** Per game and group, with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-3/a4_managers.py. Episode pool is
   driven by group size (corr 0.66) and per-capita contribution (0.78);
   pool ~ 12.5*n + 6.7*cmean - 0.8*pmean. By quintile of mean punishment per
   player-round: 0.24 -> pool 57.8 (n 4.7, cmean 7.6); 0.97 -> 58.9;
   1.51 -> 63.9 (n 4.0, cmean 10.2); 2.54 -> 50.8; 5.24 -> 28.1 (n 3.0).
   By punishment relative to the rival: -3.5 -> 56.0 (n 5.4), -0.9 -> 63.8,
   0 -> 55.9, +0.9 -> 51.5, +3.5 -> 32.4 (n 2.7). Rounds 0-3 punishment
   moves later group size by -0.20 members per point of mean punishment,
   symmetric with the rival's +0.20. So the best human managers punish
   *lightly* (about 1-1.5 per player-round) and a little less than the
   rival: enough to lift contributions by ~2.5, not enough to lose members.
   Next: how is switching timed - does the punishment just before a
   decision matter more than earlier in the block?

4. **Is the switch decided by the last round of the block?** Logit of switching
   on each block round's own punishment and per-capita pool, with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-3/a5_timing.py. Punishment coefficients
   by block position 0..3: 0.045, 0.028, 0.045, 0.068; per-capita pool:
   0.001, -0.015, -0.028, -0.036. The decision round (rounds 3,7,11,15,19)
   weighs most on both counts, and punishing in it also cuts that round's
   pool, which is the comparison players act on. Rival's punishment in the
   block's first round keeps people (-0.083). So: go easier in decision
   rounds. Next: does late-game punishment still pay (few rounds left to
   reap contribution gains, no more switching after 19)?

5. **Late-game returns.** Low contributors (<10) who stayed, contribution on
   previous contribution and punishment, by phase, with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-3/a6_late.py: rounds 1-7 +0.080/pt
   (se 0.018), 8-15 +0.155, 16-23 +0.161, persistence 0.69-0.79. A point of
   punishment returns ~0.16*1.6 = 0.26 pool next round and, carried by the
   0.75 persistence, ~1 pool over a long horizon: break-even at best, and
   only if many rounds remain. The early response is weakest of all. So
   punishment cannot be the main lever; membership is. Punishment should be
   light, aimed at the lowest contributors, off in decision rounds, and
   stop some rounds before the end (round 23 punishment is a pure loss).

6. **Drafted the rule.** punishment = slope * (1 + alpha * rival_mean_p) *
   max(0, thr - c_i), times m_dec in decision rounds (3,7,11,15,19), and 0
   after round t_stop. rival_mean_p is the mean punishment the other manager
   charged its own (valid) members over all past rounds, read from `p` and
   `in_group`. The idea from entries 1 and 3: the rival's punishment pushes
   players toward us (+0.20 members per point), so the harsher the rival,
   the more enforcement we can afford without losing the size race; against
   a lenient rival we must stay lenient. Absolute threshold rather than
   group-mean deviation, because the artificial humans react to their own
   punishment level, and an absolute bar never touches players above it
   (entry 2: punished high contributors collapse).

7. **Validated, and checked the ranges' scale.** validate-rule passes (256
   distinct points). With scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-3/a7_scale.py I
   mapped (thr, slope) to the mean punishment per player-round it would deal
   on the human contribution distribution (after flooring): thr 10 gives
   0.00 / 0.12 / 0.46 / 1.08 / 2.33 / 6.23 at slope 0.05 / 0.1 / 0.2 / 0.4 /
   0.8 / 2; thr 12 gives 1.40 at slope 0.4. So the slope range runs from
   effectively no punishment (floors to 0) through the human sweet spot of
   ~1-1.5 (entry 3) to clearly harsh, and the best is likely mid-range
   (slope 0.2-0.8). Open question I cannot settle here: whether the
   artificial humans' contribution response is strong enough that any
   punishment pays; the low end of slope covers the case where it does not.

## Key findings

1. Group size is the dominant lever on the pool: episode pool ~ 12.5 per
   member + 6.7 per point of per-capita contribution - 0.8 per point of mean
   punishment, and groups range 1-8 members (entries 1, 3).
2. Punishment drives members out and the rival's punishment drives them in:
   switching logit +0.17 per point of own punishment, -0.20 per point of the
   rival's mean punishment; rounds 0-3 punishment moves later group size by
   -0.20 members per point, the rival's by +0.20 (entries 1, 3).
3. Punishment lifts a low contributor's next contribution by only
   0.08-0.16 per point (more later in the game), with persistence ~0.75, so
   it is roughly break-even in pool and needs many remaining rounds to pay
   (entries 2, 5).
4. Punished high contributors (15-20) cut their contribution by 8-16 points;
   only below-average contributors respond positively (entry 2).
5. Human managers who punished lightly (~1.5 per player-round) and slightly
   less than their rival had the largest pools (63.9 and 63.8 vs 28-32 for
   the harshest) (entry 3).
6. The decision round (3, 7, 11, 15, 19) weighs most on switching: own
   punishment 0.068 vs ~0.04 in other block rounds, per-capita pool -0.036 vs
   ~0 (entry 4).

## Hypothesis

A good manager wins the size race first and enforces second: it punishes
only free riders, lightly and in proportion to how far they fall short of a
bar, never anyone above the bar; it lets up in the rounds players judge the
groups by (the switch-decision rounds) and stops punishing once too few
rounds remain for contributions to repay it; and it scales its enforcement
to the rival: against a harsh rival players flow in anyway and some
enforcement is affordable, against a lenient rival any harshness costs
members. The rule: punishment_i = slope * (1 + alpha * rival_mean_p) *
max(0, thr - c_i), multiplied by m_dec in decision rounds and set to 0
after round t_stop.

- `thr` (float, [4, 16]): the contribution bar; expected best 8-12, around
  the mean contribution of ~9, so that free riders are hit and the
  cooperative half never is.
- `slope` (float, [0.05, 2.0], log): punishment per point of shortfall
  against a non-punishing rival; expected 0.2-0.8 (mean punishment ~0.5-2);
  the low end is effectively "never punish".
- `alpha` (float, [0, 1.5]): relative increase in slope per point of the
  rival's mean punishment of its members so far; expected 0.2-0.7 (rival
  means of 1-3 then raise our slope by 20-200%); 0 means ignore the rival.
- `m_dec` (float, [0, 1]): multiplier in decision rounds; expected low,
  0-0.5.
- `t_stop` (int, [10, 23]): last punishing round; expected 15-20.

### Justification

Findings 1 and 2 make membership the main lever and punishment its main
threat, so the rule punishes lightly and only shortfalls (thr, slope), and
finding 5 places the sweet spot at a light level the slope range is
centred on (entry 7). Finding 4 is why punishment is tied to an absolute
bar and never reaches contributors above it. Finding 3 says punishment pays
only with time left to repay it, hence t_stop; the range starts at 10
because rounds 8-23 show the strongest response and switching ends after
19. Finding 6 motivates m_dec. Finding 2's rival coefficient motivates
alpha: the rule's only window on the other manager is `p`, and the rival's
harshness is what changes how much our own punishment costs us in members.
It would be contradicted if the sweep puts slope at its floor (punishment
never pays in the artificial humans), or alpha at 0 (the rival's harshness
does not change what we can afford), or m_dec near 1. Not modelled: the
group-mean-relative bar human managers used (an absolute bar is simpler
and the artificial humans react to their own punishment level), special
treatment of newcomers, of group size, or of the rival's per-capita pool,
and timeouts (a timed-out player is charged 0 anyway); each would cost a
parameter the five above need more.
