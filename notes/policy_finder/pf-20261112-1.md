# pf-20261112-1

## Explorations

1. **How does `zero` (never punish) fare against `ah` in the reference sims, on the real score?** Looked at `plots/simulation/25_LEVIN_run1_ah_zero_pairings_batched/per_round.parquet` with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-1/ref_sims.py and ref_score.py (the score uses the group sum of 1.6*c - p per round, via `pool_scores.against_anchor`; `common_good` in the parquet is the per-member share, which I first misread as the pool). Found zero leads ah by 13.97 (se 1.44) pool points per round over 2000 games: own 60.5 vs 46.5, with 4.57 vs 3.43 members on average. Almost all of the gap opens at the first switch (round 4: 4.90 vs 3.10 members) and holds. Contributions per member are lower under zero (8.06 vs 8.97), and ah punishes ~1.84 per member per round. So the baseline to beat is "never punish": members matter more than the per-member contribution bump. Next: what moves members in the human data and in the switching model, and whether any punishment pays for itself.

2. **Does punishment raise human contributions, and what did human managers do?** Looked at the human data (one copy per game, 50 games, 9600 rows) with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-1/human.py: next-round contribution change by this round's contribution bin x punishment bin, for players who stay. Found a real but modest effect on low contributors: a player giving 1-5 who gets 0 punishment changes by +0.08 next round, by +1.25 with 3-5 points, +1.93 with 6-10; for 6-10 givers 0 -> -0.40, 3-5 -> +1.15. Punishing high contributors (>15) backfires hard (-5 to -16). Human managers punished mostly low givers (3.3 on zero givers, 0.26 on 20s) and much more in rounds 0-3 (3.1-3.7 per member) than later (~1-1.7). So a point of punishment on a low giver buys maybe 0.3 points of contribution next round (~0.5 pool points), i.e. it does not pay back within a round; it would need to persist several rounds, and only if it does not drive the player or others out. Next: what drives switching.

3. **What makes players switch groups?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-1/human_switch.py, per player and 4-round window before each decision. Found switching rises with one's own punishment in the window (0 -> 18.9%, 1-4 -> 23.7%, 5-10 -> 34.8%, 11-20 -> 43.8%, >20 -> 54.5%) and with the group's mean punishment relative to the other's (other group punishes >2 less per member: 55% leave; >2 more: 12.8%). Logit: own window punishment +0.041/pt, own-group mean punishment +0.21, other group's -0.24, own-group mean contribution -0.20, other's +0.085, own contribution +0.073 (high givers leave more readily). The first decision has the most switching (43.5%). So members flow toward the group that punishes less and where others give more; relative punishment is a lever on members, and the first decision (after round 3) is the biggest one, which is exactly where zero gains its 0.9 members. Next: how the artificial humans (contribution and switching models) encode this, since they are what my rule meets.

4. **Can I run the artificial humans here?** No: `artifacts/` holds only the copula json of the contribution model and the linear baselines, not the GNN weights, and there is no GPU. So everything below reads the AH stack's behaviour off the reference sims (ah-managed groups supply the punishment variation).

5. **How do the artificial humans' contributions respond to punishment?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-1/sim_response.py on the ah-managed groups of the reference sims (players who stay). Found the same shape as the humans: for 1-5 givers, next-round change +0.61 at p=0, +1.27 at p 3-5, +1.89 at p 6-10, +2.51 at p>10; for zero givers 1.65 -> 2.56 (3-5) -> 3.05; for 6-10 givers +0.4 at 3-5; for >10 givers punishment lowers contributions (20-givers -1.74 at 0 vs -2.55 at 6-10, -4.55 at >10). Measured from a window's first round, the extra over p=0 persists and grows a little to lag 3 (1-5 givers, p 3-5: +0.64, +0.76, +0.81). So ~4 points on a low giver buys ~0.7-0.8 contribution per round for at least 3 rounds, ~2.3 contributions = ~3.7 pool points: about break-even inside a window, positive only if it persists longer and nobody leaves because of it. (Correlational: ah's punishment is itself random, which helps, but not clean.) ah itself punishes 3.75 on zero givers, 0.36 on 20s, falling from 3.75/member in round 0 to ~1 at the end. Next: the sims' switching response, which I expect to dominate.

6. **What drives switching in the sims?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-1/sim_switch.py: per player and window, logit of switching on own window punishment, own punishment in the window's last round, group mean punishment and contribution, the other group's, own group size, window dummies. Found own punishment +0.017/pt plus +0.031/pt extra if dealt in the decision round itself (recency), own-group mean punishment per member-round +0.113, other's -0.109, own-group mean contribution -0.155, other's +0.132 (the largest z by far), own group size -0.21 per member (bigger groups hold on). Switching rate by own window punishment: 0 -> ~22%, 1-4 -> ~28%, 5-10 -> ~37%, 11-20 -> ~48%, >20 -> ~63%. The first decision moves most (46% leave ah groups vs 31% leave zero groups). So: members go where contributions are high and punishment low, and a group that is already bigger keeps them. Rough sum for punishing one low giver 4 points at a window's start: pool cost 4, contribution gain ~3.7 pool points (entry 5), group-mean punishment +0.25 -> logit +0.03 on every member, their own +0.07, partly offset by group mean contribution +0.15 -> -0.02. Near zero, a little negative before a switch, worse late in a window. Next: does punishment spill over onto others' contributions (the contribution model has herding), and what about the last window, which no switch follows.

7. **Spillover, dose-response at exact contributions, and the trend over rounds.** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-1/sim_spill.py on ah-managed stayers. OLS of next contribution: own contribution 0.72, others' mean contribution 0.255 (herding), own punishment +0.061/pt if the player gave <=10, -0.041/pt if >10, others' mean punishment +0.020/pt. Own persistence plus herding sum to ~0.98, so a bump in a group's contributions lasts nearly the rest of the game: 1 point on a low giver ~ 0.06 contribution per later round, ~0.1 pool per later round, break-even after ~10 rounds, so early is worth more. At exact contributions the dose-response is concave: c=0 next round 1.71 at p=0, 2.40 at 1-2, 2.66 at 3-5, 3.08 at 6-10, 3.14 at >10; c=2: 2.89, 3.23, 3.48, 4.01; c=5: 5.26, 5.64, 5.81, 6.36; c=10: flat. The first 1-2 points buy the most per point. The surprise: under zero contributions slide from 9.2 (round 0) to 8.1-8.2 (rounds 7-23), under ah they climb from 9.3 to 10.3. By the late game ah's groups give ~2 more per member, ~3.2 pool points, against ~1 point of punishment per member: late punishment of ah's kind pays per member; early it is expensive (3-3.75 per member) and costs members at the first decision. So my working hypothesis: never punish before the first switch (that is where zero's 0.9-member lead comes from), then deal small punishments, scaled by the shortfall, to players giving below a bar, never to high givers, and nothing in round 23 (no later round to benefit). Open: what to do in decision rounds (3, 7, ..., 19): punishment there adds to leaving through recency (+0.031/pt), but the one most pushed to leave is the low giver himself, whose leaving costs our pool little and lifts our mean contribution, which attracts others. I cannot sign that from here, so it gets a multiplier with a range on both sides of 1.

8. **Drafted the rule.** punishment = min(cap, k * max(0, thr - c)) for members, 0 before round t_start and in round 23, times dec_w in rounds 3, 7, 11, 15, 19. Five params: thr (the bar), k (points per point of shortfall), cap (most points per player-round, because the dose-response is concave and big punishments drive leaving), t_start, dec_w. Zero is (nearly) nested: t_start = 22 leaves one punished round, small k with thr low floors to 0.

9. **Validated, and checked how much the range punishes.** validate-rule passes (256 distinct points). scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-1/dose.py applies the rule's formula to the contributions of zero-managed groups (rounds 4-22): thr 5, k 0.5, cap 3 deals 0.55 per member-round (35% of players punished); thr 8, k 0.5, cap 5: 1.29; thr 10, k 0.3, cap 3: 0.97; thr 10, k 1, cap 5: 2.68; thr 14, k 1, cap 10: 5.98; the far corner (16, 3, 15): 11.5. ah deals ~1-2.3 per member-round after round 4. So the design spans from near-zero to far harsher than ah; I expect the best around 0.5-1.5 per member-round, i.e. thr 6-12, k 0.3-1, cap 2-6, t_start 4-8, and kept the ranges wider than that because these are guesses from correlational sim data I could not re-simulate.

## Key findings

1. Never punishing beats ah by 13.97 pool points per round (se 1.44): 60.5 vs 46.5, mostly from members (4.57 vs 3.43), gained almost entirely at the first switch (4.90 vs 3.10 members from round 4) (exploration 1).
2. In the sims members leave a group for its punishment (own window punishment +0.017/pt, +0.031/pt more if dealt in the decision round; group mean punishment +0.113 vs other's -0.109) and for its low contributions (group mean -0.155, other's +0.132), and a bigger group holds on (-0.21 per member); the humans show the same signs (explorations 3, 6).
3. Punishment raises a low giver's next contribution, concavely: a zero giver goes to 1.71 / 2.40 / 2.66 / 3.08 next round at 0 / 1-2 / 3-5 / 6-10 points; for givers above 10 it lowers contributions (-0.041/pt, 20-givers -4.55 at >10 points) (explorations 2, 5, 7).
4. Contribution effects last: own persistence 0.72 plus herding 0.255 on others' mean, and the bump from punishment persists to at least lag 3 (explorations 5, 7).
5. Under zero, contributions slide from 9.2 to ~8.1 by round 7 and stay there; under ah they rise to 10.3, a late gap of ~3 pool points per member against ~1 point of ah punishment per member (exploration 7).

## Hypothesis

A good manager first wins members, then lifts their contributions cheaply. Before and around the first switch it should not punish at all, since the zero manager's whole lead comes from members won at the first decision from ah's heavily punishing group. Afterwards, contributions in an unpunished group slide down, and small punishments aimed only at low givers lift them for the rest of the game at a cost below what they bring. So it should punish below a contribution bar, in proportion to the shortfall, capped low (the first points buy the most and big punishments drive players out), never punish high givers, and never punish in round 23. The rule: `thr` (int, [2, 16]), the contribution bar; `k` (float, [0.1, 3] log), points per point of shortfall; `cap` (int, [1, 15]), most points per player-round; `t_start` (int, [0, 22]), the first round with any punishment; `dec_w` (float, [0, 2]), a multiplier on punishment in the rounds that end with a switch decision (3, 7, 11, 15, 19). punishment = min(cap, k*max(0, thr - c)) * (dec_w in decision rounds) for members, when t_start <= t < 23.

### Justification

Finding 1 sets the bar: anything that costs members at the first decision gives up the zero baseline's lead, hence `t_start`, which I expect at 4-8 (after the first decision, or after the second when the switching rates of finding 2 have dropped to ~25%); its range runs to 22 so that the sweep can find "almost never punish" if punishment never pays. Findings 3 and 4 back punishing low givers only, by shortfall: `thr` around 6-12 (above 10 punishment turns harmful, finding 3), `k` around 0.3-1 and `cap` around 2-6 because the response is concave, with wider ranges as hedge since the response estimates are correlational (ah's punishments are random given the state, but not an experiment). Finding 5 says what punishment buys late: ~2 contributions per member, so the rule keeps punishing up to round 22. Finding 2 makes decision rounds special in both directions: the recency term says spare them (dec_w < 1), but the player most pushed to leave is the low giver whose departure raises our mean contribution, which attracts others, so `dec_w` ranges 0-2 and I do not predict its side. Contradicting evidence would be a sweep peak at large t_start or near-zero punishment volume (punishment never pays on this stack), or at the high corners (harsh punishment pays, against findings 1-3). Not modelled: relative bars (shortfall from the group mean, which herding could favour), targeting newcomers differently, reacting to the other group's state or to group size, and tapering punishment as the remaining rounds shrink; each would add a parameter for an effect I could not size from the reference sims without being able to re-simulate.
