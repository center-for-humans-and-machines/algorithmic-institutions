## Key findings

1. The opening family is saturated: finals 65.6 / 68.0 / 68.7 / 68.6 / 68.9 / 68.5 for v1-v6;
   in v6's sweep kn, nb and s0 move the pool by under 1 point, only k1 (65.2 -> 67.1) and k0
   (65.6 -> 66.7) by more, and against opponent 4 every parameter quartile sits at 57-59
   (exploration 1).
2. A round-1 punishment point on a member already punished in round 0 returns ~0.66 pool points
   (own +0.18, each groupmate +0.16) against ~0.87 for a first-timer (+0.18, +0.23 per
   groupmate); per point a repeat lifts its own later contribution 0.030 vs 0.050 and lowers
   staying 0.0041 vs 0.0065 (explorations 2, 3).
3. The game's outcome is set mostly by the group's round-0 mean: pool 36-43 at m0 ~5, 58-61 at
   ~8.5, 95-102 at ~12 (exploration 6); neither a budget scaled to the game's total shortfall nor
   abstaining in low groups is supported: k0 is flat in the lowest m0 sextile and positive
   elsewhere, k1 positive in every sextile (+0.14 to +1.21 pool per log unit) (explorations 5,
   7, 8).

## Hypothesis

A good manager runs a short initiation and then stays out of the way: in each member's first
two rounds with the group (the originals at rounds 0-1, newcomers arriving at rounds 4 and 8 in
their first two rounds) it punishes shortfalls below the group's mean, hard in round 0, more
gently later, and quiet in the two rounds before each switch decision and after round 9.
In the artificial humans a first punishment lifts the member and its groupmates for the rest of
the game, while the pool and members it costs are paid once; a second punishment of the same
member in the next round buys less for the same price, so it should be discounted. The rule
reads only our own members' histories, so it carries over to any other manager.

The rule: punishment = slope x rep x max(0, mean_c - c_i - s0) over valid members whose tenure
with us is under 4 rounds, in rounds 4b and 4b+1 before round 10 (clamped to 30 by the manager);
slope k0 in round 0, k1 later; rep = r if we punished the member in the previous round, else 1.
- `k0` (float, [6, 30], log): round-0 slope; I expect 12-20 (v5 flat 8-23, v6 peak 16.7).
- `k1` (float, [1.5, 20], log): slope in round 1 and for newcomers; I expect 4-10 (v5 3.3-4.5,
  v6 4.6, but with repeats discounted the first-timer slope can go higher, and log k1 was still
  positive in every group type).
- `s0` (float, [-1, 2]): tolerance below the mean; I expect 0-1 (v5 1.1, v6 -0.2).
- `r` (float, [0, 1.5]): repeat factor; I expect 0.3-0.8 (a repeat returns ~3/4 of a first
  point per point and the round-1 return is near the 1-point cost).

### Justification

Finding 1 says the remaining gains are small and spread thinly over the dimensions, so v7
returns to v5's best structure with four parameters, fixes the flat ones (the newcomer slope is
k1 again, the newcomer horizon nb = 2) and narrows k0 and s0 where all sweeps agreed. Finding 2
is the reason for `r`: the round-1 and newcomer-second-round punishment falls mostly on members
punished the round before, whose return per point is about 0.2 pool points below a first-timer's,
which is the difference between breaking even and losing on a near-break-even instrument; r = 1
recovers v5. Finding 3 widens k1's range upwards and argues against conditioning the dose on the
group's level or total shortfall. The hypothesis would be contradicted if r's best sat at or
above 1 (repeats are as good as first punishments, so the spillover regression misled me) or if
k1's best fell below v5's ~4 with r small (then round 1 does not pay even on first-timers). I did
not model the other manager (v5's licence was flat), group-type dependence (exploration 8 found
none worth a dimension), a separate newcomer slope or horizon (flat in v6), or late maintenance
punishment (earlier sweeps lost with it). The expected gain over v5 is small (a few tenths of a
point), within the final's standard error.
