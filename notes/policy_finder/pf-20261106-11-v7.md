Block-0 punishment drives both: it lifts contributions but pushes members out. Next: is there a cheaper way to get the same lift? 3. **Momentum: does a member who just cut its contribution respond more to punishment?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-11/l2_momentum.py on the v3-v6 sweeps (4 x 256 points x 4 opponents, ~5.9M member-rounds). At rounds 1, 5 and 9 I took my members below the others' mean and split them by their own drop from the previous round (rose / flat / drop 1-3 / drop 3+). Within shortfall bins, I regressed the change to the decision round and leaving on p, controlling for shortfall, own c and last round's p. The rule's dose varies across design points through rate, delta and decay, so p is mostly exogenous within a bin. Members who dropped 3+ respond about twice as much per point (0.05-0.08 against 0.02-0.04 for rose/flat at shortfall 1-8). Their exit cost per point is only slightly higher (0.0056-0.0071 against 0.0031-0.0070). This is surprising and new: no rule so far has looked at a member's own history. Next: check it at block starts too, and over a longer horizon. A transient bounce would not be worth paying for.
4. **Momentum, at block starts and to the next block's end.** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-11/l3_momentum2.py: same sweeps, members with shortfall >= 1 at t = 1, 4, 5 and 8, one regression per round with p x drop-bin slopes. So a point spent on a member who just fell sharply buys ~1.5-3x the lasting contribution of a point spent on a member who is low but steady or rising, at the same exit cost. The v3-v6 rule spends by shortfall alone. Plan: keep the v3 core (the best final), and add a momentum term mu * max(0, own drop - theta) to the shortfall of members who are below the target. 5. **Drafted and checked the v7 rule.** I went back to the v3 core, the best final (68.72): rate * decay^b * shortfall * w_pos * end-fade, with w_pos = 1, mid, 0, 0 by position in blocks 0-4. I dropped v6's size elasticity (flat, entry 1). The shortfall is now an effective shortfall: max(0, others' mean + delta - c) + mu * max(0, c_prev - c - theta), where the momentum term counts only for members below the target. Punishing at or above the norm backfires (v1 finding 4), so a member who fell but is still above the norm stays unpunished. c_prev is the member's own contribution in the previous round, which may have been in the other group. In round 0 there is no previous round, so the drop is 0. A timed-out previous round is recorded as 0, so it gives no drop. I narrowed the core ranges around the region of the v3-v6 peaks: rate [3, 16] log (peaks 4.0-12.1), delta [-2.8, 0.8] (-1.84 to +0.40), decay [0.25, 0.8] (0.40-0.64) and mid [0.3, 1.6] (0.2-1.22, flat). That leaves sweep density for the two new parameters: mu [0, 3] and theta [0, 6]. On sizing mu: entry 4 says the drop3+ members are worth ~2x the dose. Their typical drop is ~5-6 with shortfall ~6, so doubling their dose needs mu*(drop - theta) ~ shortfall, i.e. mu ~1-2 at theta ~2. I expect mu 0.6-2 and theta 1.5-3.5. The range includes mu = 0 (the v3 rule) so the sweep can reject the idea. scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-11/m1_check_v7.py confirms v7 == v3 at mu = 0 in every round, with random memberships and timeouts. In a hand case at rate 1, mu 1, theta 2, a member falling 12 -> 6 gets 9.3 against 5.3 for a member steady at 6. validate-rule passes (256 distinct points).

## Key findings

1. Six versions of the shortfall rule have plateaued at 68.0-68.7 (v3 68.72 best, v6 68.05). v6's size elasticity was flat on its surface (effect 0.42), and the fitted peaks of v3-v6 wander (rate 4-12, delta -1.8 to +0.4, decay 0.4-0.64) with no gain (entry 1).
2. At the best final (v3), the deduction is ~34 in each of rounds 0 and 1, and ~4.9 per round over the game. Against the never-punishing opponent my group loses ~0.5 member at the first switch, and contribution per head stalls at ~10.5 against opponents 2 and 4 (12-13 against 1 and 3). The block-0 dose is the main expense, so its efficiency matters most (entry 2).
3. At the same shortfall, members who just cut their own contribution by 3 or more respond to punishment ~1.5-3x as strongly. The lasting slopes, to the next block's decision round, are 0.08-0.12 per point against 0.05-0.10 for steady or rising members, and to this block's end 0.07-0.11 against 0.02-0.07. The exit cost per point is the same (0.003-0.007) in every drop bin. Drops of 1-3 behave like no drop, and drop3+ members are 16-26% of members below the norm (entries 3, 4).

## Hypothesis

A good manager in this simulator disciplines early and in proportion to how far a member falls below the others' mean. It stays silent in the two rounds before each switch and lets the end fade. Within that budget, it puts more of its punishment on members who have just cut their own contribution sharply. A sharp cut is a lapse the artificial humans correct strongly under punishment. A steady low contributor moves less per point, and either pays the same exit cost per point. Shifting the dose towards fresh lapses buys more contribution per member lost. The rule: punishment = rate * decay^b * [max(0, others' mean + delta - c) + mu * max(0, c_prev - c - theta) * 1(below target)] * w_pos * min(1, (23 - t)/3), with b = floor(t/4) and w_pos = 1, mid, 0, 0 by position in blocks 0-4 (1 in block 5).
- `rate` (float, [3, 16] log): points per point of effective shortfall in block 0. I expect 5-10, perhaps a little lower than v3's 6.6 because the momentum term adds dose.
- `delta` (float, [-2.8, 0.8]): target offset from the others' mean. I expect -2 to 0.
- `decay` (float, [0.25, 0.8]): severity multiplier per block. I expect 0.4-0.65.
- `mid` (float, [0.3, 1.6]): weight of a block's second round. Its surface is flat, and I expect 0.7-1.3.
- `mu` (float, [0, 3]): extra effective shortfall per point of own drop beyond theta. I expect 0.6-2; 0 is the v3 rule.
- `theta` (float, [0, 6]): the drop tolerated before momentum counts. I expect 1.5-3.5.

### Justification

Finding 1 says the shortfall-only levers are exhausted, so v7 keeps the best core (v3) and narrows its ranges to where four sweeps put their peaks. That frees design density for the new axis. Finding 2 says the early dose is the main cost, so a better allocation of that dose is where gains can come from. Finding 3 is the evidence for the momentum term. Across ~3M member-rounds of exogenous dose variation (rate, delta and decay vary over the designs), drop3+ members return about twice the lasting contribution per point at an unchanged exit cost per point. That supports a positive mu. That drops of 1-3 look like no drop supports a theta of ~2-3, and theta's range [0, 6] lets the sweep find a threshold anywhere from "any drop" to "only large cuts". The 1(below target) gate keeps the v1 finding: punishing members at or above the norm backfires.

What would contradict it:
- A best mu at or near 0. That would mean the drop3+ slopes come from nonlinear reversion that my linear shortfall control did not absorb, not from a stronger response to punishment.
- A best theta at 6 with a small mu. That would mean only extreme cuts are worth the extra dose.
- A final no better than ~68.7. That would mean the extra dose's exit cost, which compounds across a member's punishments, outweighs the response.

What I chose not to model:
- Size scaling (v6, flat), opponent-stance scaling (v5, flat), and grace for newcomers (v5 finding 7).
- Lighter punishment for members who are rising. Their slopes are no smaller at block starts (0.067-0.070 at t = 4 and 8), so there is no evidence for it.
