# pf-20261104-5-v5

## Explorations

1. **Where did v4 end up, and where does its pool go?** Read v4's
   `final/sweep.json` and `landscapes.json`, and profiled the final runs
   per round with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v5_profile.py
   (group 0 = ours; also the ah/zero reference sims).
   - v4's surface peak (k=5.4, thr=10.0, w=0.45, budget=300, t_stop=10)
     scored 71.3 (se 1.07) on fresh seeds, the best of v1-v4 (67.3, 67.6,
     68.3). Its best final point s168 (k=12.2, thr=10.9, w=0.33,
     budget=299, t_stop=11) scored 72.7 (se 1.15). So the bar is ~71-73.
   - Profiles: k peaks at ~5.5 (62.7 at 1, 68.3 at 15); thr peaks at 10
     (65.7 at 5, 64.6 at 14); t_stop peaks at 10 (66.5 at 0, 65.5 at 23);
     w is flat (70.0 at 0, 70.5 at 0.45, 69.8 at 1); budget rises to its
     top edge 300, i.e. *no* per-player taper is best. The taper idea of
     v3 is dead.
   - Per round in v4's peak: punishment per member is 10.7/4.7/1.8/0.7 in
     block 0, 7.8/3.1/1.2/0.5 in block 1, 6.3/2.5/1.0/0 in block 2, then
     0. Contribution per member rises 8.7 -> 10.4 (r3) -> 11.3 (r7) ->
     11.8 (r11) and stays at 11.5-11.8 to the end with no punishment at
     all. Members 4.32-4.51 vs ah's 3.5-3.7. The pool is 13 in round 0,
     38 in round 4, 51 in round 8, against 82-84 in unpunished late
     rounds. s168 (harder) reaches cm 13.0 by r11, 12.2 at the end, pool
     87-90 late, but -6 in round 0.
   - Reference: zero vs ah keeps 4.6-4.9 members at cm ~8 (score 59.1);
     ah vs ah ~54-58.
   So the lift bought in rounds 0-11 is almost fully persistent: the late
   rounds are free. The whole game is a one-off investment: how much
   contribution level can be bought in the first three blocks, at what
   punishment cost and member loss. Next: across v4's 256 points, which
   punishment (by block, block position, contribution class) pays.
2. **Which punishment pays across v4's sweep?** Summarised all 256 v4
   points with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v5_points.py: the
   punishment per game on our members split by block (0, 1, 2, rounds
   12+), block start vs the other three rounds, and the member's class at
   that round (0-4, 5-8, 9-11, 12+); regressed the score, late members and
   late contribution per member on them (R2 0.54 / 0.77 / 0.94). Score per
   punishment point:
   - Block starts on 0-4 givers: round 0 +0.23 (se 0.05), round 4 +0.12
     (se 0.06), round 8 +0.01 (se 0.08). The value of the push falls
     block by block.
   - Round 0 on 5-8 givers +0.01, on 9-11 givers -0.14 (se 0.12).
   - Rounds 1-3 on 0-4 givers -0.04; the other within-block cells are
     noisy (se 0.1-2) because few points punish there.
   - Rounds 12+: -0.05 (se 0.009) on the score, +0.007 cm_late, -0.002
     members. Late punishment is pure cost, as in v3.
   Round 0 is the cleanest cell: c0 is set before any punishment, so the
   round-0 sums vary only with the design. So the first push is the most
   valuable, and every later block start is worth less than the previous.
   v4's rule hits blocks 0, 1 and 2 with the same k and thr; its k peak of
   ~5.5 (with s168 at k=12 doing as well) is then a compromise between a
   round 0 that wants more and a block 2 that wants less. So next: a
   per-block decay of the dose, letting round 0 be hard and later block
   starts lighter, and dropping the taper `budget`, which ended at its
   no-taper edge.
3. **Player-level value of the round-0 push.** With
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v5_round0.py (v4 sweep part 1,
   group 0) I used round 0 as a natural experiment: c0 is set before any
   punishment, so a player's round-0 dose depends only on the design
   point. Per c0 class I regressed the mean contribution in rounds 1-3,
   staying at the round-3 decision and the player's own net pool
   contribution over rounds 1-23 (1.6 c - p while with us) on the round-0
   dose and the rounds 1-3 dose. Per round-0 point:
   - 0-2 givers: +0.15 contribution in rounds 1-3, -0.10pp stay, +2.6
     net over the game. 3-5: +0.13, -0.20pp, +2.4. 6-8: +0.11, -0.26pp,
     +1.9. 9-11: +0.10, -0.37pp, +1.5. 12+: -0.01, -0.52pp, -1.7.
   - So a round-0 point returns 1.5-2.6 times its cost on anyone under
     ~11, and the leaving it causes is small (0.1-0.4pp per point).
   - By dose bin, the return is concave: 0-5 givers gain most up to
     20-30 points (3-5 givers: net 142 at 0, 163 at 10-19, 182 at 20-30);
     6-8 givers peak at 20-29 (214 vs 192 at 0) and fall at 30; 9-11
     givers gain only at 1-9 points (252 vs 246) and lose at 20+ (221).
   - The rounds 1-3 dose is negative for every class (-3.5 to -7 net per
     point, -0.8 to -1.3pp stay). That part is endogenous (it lands on
     those who did not move), but it agrees with v4's exploration 3 that
     the later in a block, the more leaving per point.
   So the first round deserves a hard push graded by the shortfall (about
   30 for 0-5, 15-25 for 6-8, a few points for 9-11, nothing above), which
   is roughly v4's peak shape (k=5.4, thr=10). The point-level fall in
   value from round 0 to rounds 4 and 8 (exploration 2) then says the
   same shape should get weaker at later block starts. I now think the
   lever left is the profile over blocks, not the shape within a round.
4. **Drafted v5.** Kept v4's base k * relu(thr - c_i) for valid members,
   clamped at 30 by the manager, and its within-block weight w^(t mod 4).
   Changes:
   - Added `d`, a per-block decay: the dose is multiplied by
     d^floor(t/4), so round 0 gets the full shape, round 4 d times it,
     round 8 d^2 times it. d=1 is v4. With d<1 a hard k can serve round
     0 without overcharging block 2, and t_stop can reach further with
     ever lighter reminders.
   - Dropped `budget`: v4's peak sat at its no-taper edge (300) and its
     effect was 1.7 pool points.
   - Kept `t_stop`, now [3, 23]: values 0-2 scored 66.5-67.7 in v4's
     profile, far below the peak at 10, so the sweep's points go where
     they can matter.
5. **Moved the 30 cap before the timing factors, and checked the rule.**
   In the first draft the manager's clamp came after w and d. With a hard
   k (say 10) a 0-giver's raw dose of 100 stays above 30 even at d^2 = 0.36,
   so d would only lighten moderate shortfalls, and the 0-4 givers whose
   block-2 points exploration 2 values at ~0 would still get 30. Now the
   dose is capped at 30 first (written as raw - relu(raw - 30): `th.clamp`
   with `max=` raised a KeyError '__import__' under `run_rule` in my test,
   although the validator accepted it). w and d then scale the capped
   dose, so they read as fractions of the round-0 dose. This changes w's
   meaning slightly from v4 (v4's w scaled the uncapped dose), so d=1 is
   v4 without the taper only up to that. Tested with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/test_rule_v5.py on contributions
   0/0/4/7/11 (player 5 timed out, players 6-7 in the other group):
   - k=3, thr=8, w=0.5, d=0.5, t_stop=12: round 0 gives 24/24/12/3/0;
     rounds 1 and 3 are 0.5 and 0.125 of that; round 4 is 0.5 of it,
     round 8 0.25, round 12 0.125, and round 13 on nothing.
   - k=10, thr=10, w=0, d=0.6, t_stop=23: 30 to everyone under 7 in round
     0, 18 in round 4, 10.8 in round 8, 6.5 in round 12, 3.9 in round 16,
     nothing in between.
   - Timed-out and other-group players get 0 throughout.
   The validator passes on all 256 design points.

## Key findings

1. v4 is the best so far: its peak scored 71.3 (se 1.07) on fresh seeds
   and its best final point 72.7 (se 1.15), against 67.3-68.3 for v1-v3.
   Its budget taper ended at the no-taper edge, w was flat (0.7 effect),
   and t_stop peaked at 10, i.e. pushes in blocks 0-2 (exploration 1).
2. The lift bought in rounds 0-11 lasts: under v4's peak, contribution
   per member stays at 11.5-11.8 through rounds 12-23 with no punishment,
   and the late pool is 82-84 against 13/38/51 in the punishing rounds 0,
   4 and 8 (exploration 1).
3. Across v4's points, each block-start point on 0-4 givers is worth
   +0.23 score in round 0, +0.12 in round 4 and +0.01 in round 8; any
   punishment in rounds 12+ costs 0.05 per point (exploration 2).
4. A round-0 point returns 1.5-2.6 net pool points over the game on
   givers under 12 (2.6 for 0-2, 1.9 for 6-8, 1.5 for 9-11) and loses 1.7
   on 12+ givers, with only 0.1-0.4pp extra leaving. The return is
   concave: 20-30 points for 0-5 givers, 15-25 for 6-8, a few for 9-11
   (exploration 3).
5. Punishment later in a block costs 0.8-1.3pp of staying per point
   against 0.1-0.5pp at round 0 (exploration 3, endogenous), in line with
   v4's finding of 2-3 times more leaving per point late in a block.

## Hypothesis

In this simulation, punishment works as a one-off investment. A firm push
graded by the shortfall early in the game raises contributions for good,
and the late rounds then run free. The push is worth most in round 0. A
point there returns about twice its cost and drives few players away.
Each later push is worth less: the remaining low givers respond less,
and the group has more to lose to leaving. So a good manager front-loads.
It hits hard in round 0, comes back more lightly at the next block
starts (where newcomers arrive and leaving is cheapest), and then stops.
The rule charges each valid member
min(30, k * max(0, thr - c_i)) * w^(t mod 4) * d^floor(t/4) while
t <= t_stop, and nothing after.

- `k` (float, [2, 30], log): punishment points per contribution point
  below the threshold, before the cap of 30. I expect 4-12. It was 5.4 at
  v4's peak and 12 at its best final point, and it may rise now that d can
  spare the later blocks.
- `thr` (float, [6, 14]): the contribution from which a member is safe. I
  expect 9.5-11.5. v4 peaked at 10, and finding 4 still pays 9-11 givers
  for a few points.
- `w` (float, [0, 1]): the fraction kept per round of distance from the
  block's first round. I expect 0.2-0.6, as in v4, and a weak effect.
- `d` (float, [0.15, 1.3]): the fraction of the dose kept per block. I
  expect 0.4-0.8. d=1 is v4's constant dose, and d>1 lets the sweep test
  escalation.
- `t_stop` (int, [3, 23]): the last punishing round. I expect 8-16, a
  little later than v4's 10 if d is small.

### Justification

Finding 2 makes the game an investment problem: the payoff of the early
push is collected for free in rounds 12-23, and finding 3's -0.05 per
late point says nothing more should be spent there. Finding 3's falling
value per point from round 0 to rounds 4 and 8 is the direct basis of
`d`. v4 had to use one dose for all three blocks, so its k peak (~5.5,
with k=12 doing as well) was a compromise. d lets round 0 take the
graded 20-30 point push that finding 4 says pays. Block 2 then gets a
fraction of it.
- `k` and `thr` keep the graded shape that finding 4 supports: concave
  returns, full doses below ~5, partial at 6-8, small at 9-11, none at
  12+. Capping before the timing factors makes w and d act on the doses
  actually charged.
- `w` stays because finding 5 still prices late-block points high. Its
  flat profile in v4 makes it cheap to keep.
- `t_stop` keeps the sweep able to end the reminders. Its range starts at
  3 because 0-2 scored ~3-4 points below v4's peak.
- `budget` is dropped by finding 1.

The hypothesis is contradicted if the sweep's best d sits at 1 or above.
Then later pushes are worth as much as the first, and exploration 2's
falling values came from who was left to punish, not from timing. It is
also contradicted if the score stays at v4's ~71.

I chose not to model these:
- Separate thresholds per block.
- A threshold relative to the group's mean, since there is no evidence
  that punishing 12+ givers ever pays.
- Newcomer-specific doses, since v4's taper, which partly did this, was
  flat.
- Anything about the other group.
