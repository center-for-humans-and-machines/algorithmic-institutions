# pf-20261106-10

## Explorations

1. **What do the rules and the simulation reward?** Read docs/reports/basics_exp2_multi.md
   and the AH training configs (configs/training/artificial_humans). The score is the
   group's pool per round, an empty group counting 0, so group size enters linearly:
   every extra member is ~1.6*c more pool. Punishment is subtracted from the pool 1:1.
   The contribution model sees prev_contribution, prev_punishment and group (plus edges
   to the others); the switch model sees prev_common_good, prev_punishment and round.
   So punishment acts on the AHs through two channels: own next contribution and own
   switch decision (and via the pool, everyone's switch decision). Next: how humans
   respond to punishment.

2. **How does next contribution respond to punishment?** Looked at contribution,
   punishment, next contribution (stayers only) with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-10/a2_response.py. Low contributors (0-4) rise by
   +0.5 unpunished, +1.6 with 3-5 points, +2.3 with 6-10, +3.7 with 11-30. Mid (5-9):
   -0.3 / +1.1 / +1.5. But 15-20 contributors punished drop sharply (20 punished 3-5:
   -9.4 vs -1.8 unpunished). Relative to group mean the same: below-mean punished rise.
   No spillover visible: unpunished players' change does not depend on others'
   punishment. So: punish only clear low contributors, never the high ones. A 5-point
   punishment buys ~+1 contribution = +1.6 pool next round at a cost of 5 now, so
   one-round arithmetic says punishment barely pays unless the effect persists or it
   keeps members. Next: persistence, and switching.

3. **What drives switching?** Looked at the switch decisions after rounds 3,7,11,15,19
   (group_id vs next round's) against the block's punishment and contributions, with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-10/a3_switch.py. Switch rate 0.29 overall (0.44 at
   the first decision). Players punished >10 points in the block switch 0.50 vs 0.19
   unpunished. The group-level contrast is even stronger: when one's group punishes
   >2 points per member-round more than the other group, 0.59 leave; >2 less, 0.13.
   A group whose mean contribution is >4 above the other's loses 0.12, >4 below 0.43.
   Logit: +0.24 per point of mean punishment gap, -0.10 per point of contribution gap,
   +0.03 per point of own block punishment, -0.16 per member (big groups hold).
   So punishing is doubly costly: pool loss and exodus, and the exodus is driven by the
   *relative* punishment level against the other manager. A manager that punishes less
   than the other group collects members. Next: what did human managers do, and which
   groups ended big with large pools.

4. **What did human managers do, and what came of it?** Per (game, group) means, with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-10/a4_managers.py. Human managers punished by
   shortfall below the group mean: 7.1 on average when >8 below, 0.85 near the mean,
   ~0.7 above. Within a game, the group with the lower punishment *per member* ended
   larger (4.75 vs 3.25 members) and with the larger pool (59.8 vs 44.1, in 56% of
   games), though the harsher group had higher mean contributions (9.9 vs 8.4). (By
   total punishment the sign flips, but that is just size: big groups sum more.) Six
   near-zero punishers averaged pool 37.7, size 3.5, c 5.7: no punishment at all is
   not the answer either. So the trade-off is contributions (punish) against members
   (don't); size enters the pool linearly, so a mild, targeted punisher should win.
   Next: can I run the simulation here, and is there a model for the AHs?

5. **No simulator here.** artifacts/ holds only copula calibration files, no model
   weights, and plots/ is empty (version 1), so I cannot simulate; the rule has to
   come from the data and the structure of the AH models.

6. **Does the punishment effect persist, and when does punishment hurt switching
   most?** OLS of c_{t+k}-c_t on p_t x (below group mean - 1), controlling for c_t and
   group mean, stayers, clustered by game, with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-10/a5_persist.py. For below-mean players each
   point of punishment adds about +0.09 / +0.10 / +0.10 / +0.14 contribution at k=1..4
   (p + p*low), roughly 0.4-0.5 contribution summed over four rounds, i.e. ~0.7 pool
   points per point of punishment, which itself costs 1 pool point now. For at/above
   mean players the effect is ~0 or negative. The table in 2 suggests concavity:
   1-5 points buy ~0.27 contribution per point next round, 11-30 points ~0.18.
   Switching logit on own punishment by lag before the decision: 0.088 per point in the
   decision round itself, 0.038 / 0.021 / 0.027 for the three rounds before.
   So in the human data punishment is close to break-even at best, only small doses on
   clear free-riders are efficient, and the decision rounds (3,7,11,15,19) are where
   punishment costs members. In the last rounds there is no future to buy: round 23
   punishment is pure loss. Next: does the pool level itself (which punishment lowers)
   keep members, as the AH switch model's prev_common_good input suggests?

7. **Pool, contributions and the other manager in the switch decision.** Logit of
   switching in decision rounds on own/other group's state, with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-10/a6_pool_switch.py. Own punishment +0.08 to
   +0.11 per point, own group's mean punishment +0.09, the *other* group's mean
   punishment -0.08 to -0.11 (symmetric: what counts is the gap), own mean contribution
   -0.14 per point, other's +0.06, own pool per member -0.056, size -0.16 per member.
   Switchers contribute like stayers (9.5 vs 9.3 next round), so every member gained is
   worth a full average contribution. Rough arithmetic from 6-7: a point of punishment
   in a decision round raises the chance of losing that player by ~0.02, which at
   ~15 pool per member-round over the next block costs more than the ~0.7 pool the
   point buys. In other rounds the costs and gains are of the same order.
   So my hypothesis is taking shape: punish only clear free-riders, mildly and capped,
   never in decision rounds or the last round, and keep the group's punishment level
   tied to (below) what the other manager does, which is visible in `p` for the
   players of the other group. Next: draft the rule.

8. **Drafted the rule.** Five params. Per player in my group this round:
   raw = clamp(slope * (group mean - tol - c), 0, cap), group mean over my valid
   members this round. Then a budget per member-round, base + ratio * (other manager's
   mean punishment per member-round over the last 4 rounds), scales raw down
   proportionally when its group mean exceeds it. Punishment is 0 in the switch-decision
   rounds 3,7,11,15,19 (fixed: 6 shows the decision round is ~3x as costly in exodus)
   and in round 23 (fixed: nothing left to buy). The 4-round window matches the
   switch block. I keep the low ends of slope, cap and base near zero so that the
   sweep can find "barely punish" if the AHs agree with the break-even in 6.

9. **Validated and replayed the draft.** validate-rule passes (256 distinct points).
   Replayed the code on the 50 human histories in group 0's seat with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-10/t1_replay.py (other group's punishments as the
   humans dealt them, ~1.9 per member-round). At slope 0.7, tol 2, cap 6, base 1,
   ratio 0.6 the rule punishes 0.38 per member-round, 16% of member-rounds, at most 6;
   at slope 3 it rises to 0.71 (19%), still well under the human manager's 1.88, and
   exactly 0 in rounds 3,7,11,15,19 and 23. So the design spans "barely punish" to
   "human-like on free-riders", and with cap 20 and a large budget it can go harsher;
   the sweep decides. I keep the draft unchanged.

## Key findings

1. Punished low contributors raise their next contribution (0-4 contributors: +0.5
   unpunished, +1.6 at 3-5 points, +3.7 at 11-30), while punished high contributors
   collapse (20-contributors punished 3-5: -9.4 vs -1.8). (Exploration 2)
2. Each point of punishment on a below-mean player buys only ~0.4-0.5 contribution
   summed over four rounds (~0.7 pool for 1 pool point spent), and small doses are the
   most efficient per point (~0.27 vs ~0.18 per point next round). (Explorations 2, 6)
3. Players leave the harsher group: a mean-punishment gap of >2 points per member-round
   makes 0.59 leave vs 0.13 when the own group is >2 milder; the logit is about +0.09
   per point of own group's mean punishment and -0.08 per point of the other group's.
   (Explorations 3, 7)
4. Punishment in the switch-decision round itself weighs ~3x as much on leaving as the
   rounds before it (0.088 vs 0.02-0.04 logit per point). (Exploration 6)
5. Within a game the group milder per member ended larger (4.75 vs 3.25) and with the
   larger pool (59.8 vs 44.1), though the harsher group had higher contributions
   (9.9 vs 8.4); never punishing at all was also poor (pool 37.7, 6 groups).
   (Exploration 4)
6. Higher own mean contribution keeps members (-0.14 logit per point) and switchers
   contribute like stayers (9.5 vs 9.3), so a member gained is worth a full average
   contribution, ~15 pool per round. (Exploration 7)

## Hypothesis

Group size is the biggest lever on the pool, and punishment is close to break-even in
contributions while it pushes members out, above all when it exceeds the other group's
and when it falls on the decision round. A good manager therefore punishes only clear
free-riders, in small capped doses proportional to their shortfall below the group's
mean, keeps its group's overall punishment tied to (and typically below) the other
manager's observed level, and stays silent in the rounds right before a switch
decision (3, 7, 11, 15, 19) and in the last round (23), where punishment buys nothing.

The rule (five parameters):
- `slope` [0.1, 3, log]: points per point of shortfall below (group mean - tol).
  Humans used ~0.7-1; I expect 0.2-1.
- `tol` [0, 8]: tolerated shortfall below the group mean. Expect 1-4.
- `cap` [1, 20, log]: per-player maximum per round. Expect 3-8 (concave response).
- `base` [0, 4]: punishment budget per member-round when the other manager punishes
  nothing. Expect 0.3-1.5 (humans ~1.7 overall).
- `ratio` [0, 2]: budget added per point of the other manager's mean punishment per
  member-round over the last 4 rounds. Expect 0.3-0.8, i.e. milder than the opponent.
Fixed in code: silence in rounds 3, 7, 11, 15, 19 and 23; the 4-round window; the
group mean over valid members of this round; the budget scales all punishments
proportionally and is rounded.

### Justification

Findings 1 and 2 justify punishing only below-mean players, proportionally to the
shortfall (`slope`, `tol`), and the concavity in 2 justifies `cap`. Findings 3, 5 and 6
say members are worth more than the contributions punishment buys and that members
follow the relative punishment level, which is why the budget is anchored to the other
manager's observed punishment (`ratio`) with an allowance for a non-punishing opponent
(`base`); this is how the rule adapts to whichever manager runs the other group.
Finding 4 justifies the fixed silence in decision rounds; round 23 has no future round
to influence. What would contradict it: a sweep optimum at high `slope`, high `cap`
and `ratio` well above 1 (the AHs reward harshness more than the humans did), or at the
low edges of `slope`/`base`/`ratio` (punishment never pays in simulation; the silence
rules would then matter most). Not modelled: individual histories (repeat offenders,
a player's trend), punishment of players who just switched in, and the decline of
the punishment's value over rounds 20-22; I left them out to keep five readable
parameters and because the data gives no clear signal on them beyond the above.
