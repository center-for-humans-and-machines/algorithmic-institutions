## Key findings

1. v3's surface peak (F 29.7, curvature 2.18, window 7, net c<=2 at ~25
   points to round 16) scored 656.0 +- 1.2, the bar; its landscape is
   interior on every axis (curvature effect 25, window 9.7, F 9.3) (exploration 1).
2. Under it the group settles at a norm of ~17 by round 7 (45% at 20,
   everyone's type converging to 16-17) and slides only slowly after the
   net stops (16.3 -> 14.9 over rounds 16-23, ~4 payoff); the opening
   costs ~46 payoff and buys most of the gain (exploration 2).
3. The response to a fine depends on the player's own contribution and
   the fine size, hardly on the group around them (+3.1/+3.6/+4.1 by t+3
   for c 10-14 fined 5-9 with low/mid/high peers) or on whether they were
   already rising (+3.0 vs +2.8 over the rest); a fine last round damps
   the immediate response but less the lasting one (explorations 4, 5).
4. After the opening, a big fine on a c<=2 player is worth +17 (round 6),
   +10-13 (rounds 8-12), +6.4 (14), +1.4 (16), -3.6 (18) counting the
   peers' slide it stops; most of the late value is the peers'. Fines on
   c 3-6 are worth about zero (explorations 6, 7).
5. With the net fine as an absolute amount, v3's real sweep scores peak
   at a net fine of ~20-25 (8 -> 645, 30 -> 653) (exploration 8).
6. On a surrogate trained on all three sweeps (v3's peak 654.7 vs real
   656.0), every structural addition tried loses or ties: constant vs
   scaled net within 0.3, tapered opening -1 to -4, mid net tier +-0.2,
   drop net -2 to -7, group trigger -4 to -9, first-round boost -0.4 to
   -5; a weaker first fine (x0.6) loses 4.8 (explorations 9, 10).
7. The narrowed v4 design averages 3.4 above v3's design on that
   surrogate (651.0 vs 647.6) (exploration 11).

## Hypothesis

A good manager raises the group with a short, hard opening and then only
guards it against defectors. In the first ~7 rounds it fines every player
by a convex function of the shortfall below 20, so free riders get close to
the 30-point cap and near-full contributors nothing; this moves the group
from ~11.5 to a norm of ~17, and the first fine matters most. After that
the decline comes from players dropping to near zero and dragging their
peers down, so the manager fines only those defectors, with a fixed fine of
~20-25, until about round 16, when too few rounds remain for the defector's
and the peers' response to repay it; then it stops. v4 is v3's structure,
with the net fine made its own absolute amount and every range narrowed
around v3's real peak.
punishment = F x (max(0, 20-c)/20)^curvature x [t < window]
+ net_fine x [window <= t < net_end] x [c <= net_floor].
- `free_rider_fine` (float, 22-42): the opening fine of a player
  contributing 0 (before the cap).
- `curvature` (float, 1.6-3.0, log): convexity of the opening fine in the
  shortfall.
- `window` (int, 4-10): rounds of the opening.
- `net_floor` (int, 0-5): after the opening only players at or below it
  are fined.
- `net_end` (int, 11-21): round at which the net stops.
- `net_fine` (float, 10-30): the fixed fine of a defector caught by the net.

### Justification

Finding 1 sets the centres: v3's real profiles peak at F ~30 (falling by 3
at 24 and by 4 at 38), curvature ~2.2 (falling by 6 at 1.65 and 4 at 2.7),
window 6-7, floor 2-3, end 15-17; the narrowed ranges keep each of these
well inside (F 22-42, curvature 1.6-3.0 log, centre 2.19; window 4-10;
floor 0-5; end 11-21), and finding 7 says the narrowing puts more of the
design where the scores are. I expect F 27-35, curvature 2.0-2.5, window
5-8, floor 1-3, end 14-17. Finding 4 is the net's reason and its end: the
value of a defector fine crosses zero near round 17, and fines above c=2-3
buy nothing, hence floor at most 5. Finding 5 sets net_fine's range: the
scores peak at 20-25, with 10 and 30 as ends the sweep can test (30 is the
cap, so a peak there is the physical limit, not a short range). Findings 3
and 6 are why v4 adds nothing new: neither the group context, nor the
player's trend, nor a taper, drop net, group trigger or first-round boost
paid on the data or the surrogate, and finding 2 says the late slide left
by stopping the net is worth only ~4 points, less than what guarding it
costs. The hypothesis is contradicted if the sweep's peak sits at window 4
or 10, net_end 11 or 21, or net_fine at 10 (the net not worth its fines),
or if v4's final falls clearly below v3's 656. Not modelled: timeouts
(cannot be fined, ~2% of rows, no rule lever), the relative position in the
group and own trends (finding 3), and rewards, which the game lacks.
