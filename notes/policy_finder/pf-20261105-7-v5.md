## Key findings

1. v4's final (653.9 +- 1.3) did not beat v3's 656.0; both sit on the
   same profile (c 11.5 -> 17 by round 6 -> 15 at round 22, ~25-29
   fines per player), and the v4 landscape is flat in window (1.2) and
   net floor (1.3): six-parameter tuning of this family has run out
   (exploration 1).
2. Persistent free riders still answer big fines (rest-of-game mean
   12-14 fined 20-30 vs 4.5-8 unfined), so the defector net stays
   (exploration 2).
3. Token fines for near-full contributors (16-19), which flooring leaves
   untouched in v2-v4, look attractive in raw comparisons (+1 point of
   own c over the rest) but lose on the surrogate in every form (-0.8 to
   -20) (explorations 3, 5).
4. Fining only the lowest contributor (-5.1) or tapering the net (-0.2
   to -0.7) loses; gating the opening on the group, fining only players
   below the group mean + m, gains +0.9 (m 0) to +1.5 (m 1) at v3's
   schedule and +2.3-2.4 with a harder schedule (F 35-40 or curvature
   1.8), while the same harder schedules ungated gain at most +0.4
   (exploration 6).
5. The raw real-data comparison by position in the group is confounded
   by the window (peers fined too), and the surrogate reproduces it
   (above-mean own +1.1 vs real +1.3; below +0.4 vs +0.6) while still
   finding the gate pays (explorations 7, 9).
6. The gate gains on a second surrogate trained only on v1+v2 (+1.3 at
   m 1, +1.7 at F 40 / m 1, +0.1 at m 6) as on sur3_all3 (+1.0, +2.0,
   +0.1) (exploration 9).
7. In a 400-point surrogate search of the gated family the good region
   is F 30-45, curvature 2.0-2.4, window 5-7, m -2..2, floor 1-2, end
   13-16, net fine 20-28 (exploration 8); v5's design averages 1.6 above
   v4's on the surrogate (exploration 10).

## Hypothesis

A good manager still raises the group with a short, hard, convex opening
and then only guards against defectors until about round 16, but in the
opening it fines only the players who lag their group. A player already
above the round's group mean is not shirking: under the herding
dynamics they are being pulled down toward the others, and a fine on them
buys little; sparing them saves fines, and the saved budget lets the
schedule be harder on the laggards, who answer it. The rule:
punishment = F x (max(0, 20-c)/20)^curvature x [c < mean(c valid) + gate_margin] x [t < window]
+ net_fine x [window <= t < net_end] x [c <= net_floor].
- `free_rider_fine` (float, 24-50): opening fine of a player at 0.
- `curvature` (float, 1.6-3.0, log): convexity of the opening fine.
- `window` (int, 4-9): rounds of the opening.
- `gate_margin` (float, -2..8): the opening fines only players below the
  group mean plus this margin; ~8 switches the gate off.
- `net_floor` (int, 0-4): after the opening only players at or below it
  are fined.
- `net_end` (int, 12-20): round at which the net stops.
- `net_fine` (float, 12-30): the fixed fine for a defector in the net.

### Justification

Finding 1 is why v5 adds a structural element rather than retuning, and
it sets the shared ranges around v3/v4's real peaks (F ~30, curvature
~2.2, window 6-7, end 16-17, net fine ~22), widened where the gate moves
the optimum: finding 4 and 7 say a gated schedule wants a harder F
(35-45), hence F up to 50; I expect F 32-42, curvature 2.0-2.4, window
5-7, floor 1-2, end 14-17, net fine 20-28. Finding 2 keeps the net and
its big fine; finding 3 is why the flooring at 16-19 is left alone.
Findings 4-6 are the gate's case: it gains on two differently trained
surrogates, and the surrogate is consistent with the real data that
seemed to argue against it. I expect gate_margin at 0-3; its range runs
from -2 (a strict gate, which the surrogate says starts to cost: -5.8 at
-3) to 8 (effectively off), so the sweep compares gated and ungated
rules directly. The hypothesis is contradicted if the sweep's peak puts
gate_margin at 6-8 (the gate buys nothing on the real artificial
humans) or if v5's final does not reach v3's 656.0; a peak at -2 would
mean the gate should be stricter. The gain I expect (+1.5-2) is about
1.5 final standard errors, so one final cannot settle it. Not modelled:
timeouts beyond excluding them from the group mean (no lever, ~2%),
small fines at 16-19 (finding 3), the player's own trend and earlier
fines (v4's findings: no gain), a gate on the net (defectors at c<=4 are
below the mean anyway), and rewards, which the game lacks.
