5. **Is the fade about the round or about habituation?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-11/f5_fade.py on half the v3 games, for members more than 1 below the norm, split by whether they had been punished before (by any manager). The positive block-1 coefficient in entry 2 then has to come from somewhere else: from composition (punished low contributors leaving) or from endogeneity. I keep decay's range wide down to 0 and let the sweep tell.

7. **Drafted the v4 rule: v3's form, re-ranged where v3 stopped short.** I considered a separate block-1 multiplier, a concave power on the shortfall, a per-player cap, and reacting to the opponent's punishments. None has support strong enough to spend sweep points on. Block 1 and blocks 2+ are both in the fading part of the response (entry 5), so a geometric tail is enough. Response grows linearly with the gap for big gaps (v3 entry 2). `kappa` failed against every opponent (v3 entry 4). So the form stays: punishment = rate * decay^b * max(0, others' mean + delta - c) * w_pos * fade, with w_pos 1 / mid / 0 / 0 by position in blocks 0-4, and fade min(1, (23 - t)/3). The changes are all in the ranges.
   - `decay` [0.4, 1.2] -> [0.0, 0.8]. v3's peak was at its low edge, and entries 4-5 say the response window closes in block 1. 0 now means "punish only in rounds 0-1", and the constraint is now decay >= 0 (th.pow(0, 0) = 1 keeps block 0 intact).
   - `rate` [2, 20] -> [2.5, 25] log. With less punishment later, the sweep may want the first block harsher. Its profile at 2 was 64.2, far below the peak.
   - `delta` [-5, 0.5] -> [-3.5, 1.0]. The peak moved from -2.0 (v2) to -1.3 (v3), with 63.7 at -5 and 67.9 at 0.5, so I shifted the range towards it and allowed slightly above the norm.
   - `mid` [0, 1.5] -> [0.2, 2.0]. The profile was flat from 1.05 to 1.5 and low at 0. Round 1's response is close to round 0's (0.050-0.059 vs 0.072-0.079), but it costs ~2x in exits (entries 4, 6), so the best value could lie on either side of the old upper edge.
   v3's final point (6.6, -1.29, 0.4, 1.22) is inside the new space, so the sweep can find it again.

## Key findings

1. Version 3 is the best so far at 68.7 (v2 66.9, v1 65.1). Its peak sits at the low edge of `decay` (0.4), whose profile falls from 69.7 at 0.4 to 63.0 at 1.2. Rate peaks at ~6.5, delta at ~-1.2, and mid at ~1.2, flat to 1.5 (entry 1).
2. A quadratic over v3's design points puts the decay optimum between 0.2 (opponents 1 and 4, extrapolated) and 0.55 (opponents 2 and 3), 0.45 overall (entry 2).
3. In the simulator, a point of punishment raises the next-round contribution of a member below the norm by 0.07-0.08 at round 0 and 0.05-0.06 at round 1, but only 0.01-0.03 at round 4 and ~0 from round 8. This holds whether or not the player was punished before, and in the other managers' groups too, so the fade is about the round, not habituation (entries 4, 5).
4. The exit cost of a point does not fade: +0.003-0.006 leave probability at every round. Within a block it rises towards the switch (v2: 0.007, 0.009 and 0.012 at rounds 0, 1 and 2), which keeps rounds 2-3 silent (entries 4, 6).
5. At v3's final point the deduction is concentrated in rounds 0-1 (~68 pool points) and 4-5 (~35). Against the never-punishing opponent 4, my group still loses half a member at the first switch and scores 60.0 against its 60.8 (entry 3).

## Hypothesis

In this simulator punishment works as a short opening shock. The artificial humans adjust their contributions to it in the first few rounds and hardly at all later, while every point dealt still costs the pool that point and some chance of losing the member. A good manager therefore sets the norm hard at the start. In rounds 0 and 1 it punishes members who fall clearly below the others' mean, in proportion to the shortfall. It then quickly winds down to little or nothing and lets the group's own regression to its norm carry the contributions. It never punishes just before a switch. Version 4 keeps v3's form and moves the ranges to where v3 and the response analysis point: decay's range now reaches 0 (block-0-only discipline).

The rule: punishment = rate * decay^b * max(0, others' mean + delta - c) * w_pos * min(1, (23 - t)/3), b = floor(t/4); w_pos = 1, mid, 0, 0 by position in blocks 0-4 and 1 in block 5.
- `rate` (float, [2.5, 25] log): punishment per point of shortfall in block 0. I expect 6-12, a bit higher than v3's 6.6 if decay goes lower.
- `delta` (float, [-3.5, 1.0]): tolerance relative to the others' mean. I expect -2 to -0.5, around v3's -1.3.
- `decay` (float, [0.0, 0.8]): severity multiplier per block. I expect 0.1-0.45; 0 is block-0-only.
- `mid` (float, [0.2, 2.0]): weight of a block's second round relative to its first. I expect 0.8-1.6.

### Justification

Findings 1 and 2 put v3's best decay at or below its old range's edge, and finding 3 explains why. Punishment after block 1 buys almost no contribution, and finding 4 says it still costs exits, so the range now extends to 0. The upper end of 0.8 still covers the region where v3 scored 67.5 and below, so the sweep can see the slope. Finding 1 centres `rate`, `delta` and `mid`. Finding 3 (round 1's response is close to round 0's) against finding 4 (round 1's exit cost is ~2x) leaves `mid` open on both sides of 1. Finding 4 keeps the hard zeros in rounds 2-3 of each block.

These sweep results would contradict the hypothesis:
- A best decay at 0.6-0.8 would mean the later punishment pays through composition (pushing low contributors out) rather than through behaviour. Entry 2's block-1 coefficient (+0.30) hints at that, so it is a live alternative.
- A best rate at the low end would mean the opening shock is too costly after all.

What I chose not to model:
- Reacting to the other manager (v3 entry 4: kappa lowered the pool against every opponent).
- Grace for newcomers, and group-size scaling (v3 entries 4, 6: no interaction).
- A concave shortfall or a per-player cap (the response is linear in the gap for big gaps).
- A separate block-1 multiplier: the geometric tail spans it, and an extra dimension would thin the 256-point design.

Opponent 4 stays the weak pairing. A shorter, sharper opening is the only lever here that touches it, by dealing less punishment before the later switches.
