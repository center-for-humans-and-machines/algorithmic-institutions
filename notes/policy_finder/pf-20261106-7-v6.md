## Key findings

1. v5's relative target paid. Its final pool was 68.9 (71.6 / 69.2 / 74.1 / 60.5 against opponents 1-4), +1.5 on v3, and w's profile ran from 66.3 at 0 to 69.0 at 0.5-0.6. Two very different points scored the same: v5's proportional peak (k 5 per point) and s092 (c0 12, w 0.8, k 20.5, follow 0.16, 69.2), which is close to a flat fine at the cap. So the surface is a ridge in the shape of the punishment, not a single peak (exploration 1).
2. The flat-fine point punished later block starts 2-5x more (11.5, 8.0, 5.1 per member at rounds 4, 8, 12 vs 4.9, 2.2, 1.1). It bought contributions that kept climbing to 13.7-14.3 vs 12.7-13.7, at the cost of deep pool dips in those rounds. Against the non-punishing opponent 4 both modes lose 0.6-0.7 members at the first decision and score 60.5 (exploration 2).
3. The other manager shifts the base exit rate (0.456 next to the non-punisher vs 0.378-0.420), not the exits per point of my punishment (~0.005 / 0.01 / 0.03 per sqrt point in a block's rounds 0 / 1 / 2, against every opponent). Within cells, a block's third round buys about twice the lift of the second at three times the exits (explorations 3, 4).
4. In round 0 the lift per sqrt point climbs steeply with the shortfall from the target, from 0.48 at 0-1.5 to 1.90 at 7.5-10, with exits flat at 0.031-0.039. In round 4 it climbs only from 0.47 to 0.71. So the best punishment, which scales like the square of that slope, grows about linearly with the shortfall in round 0 and like shortfall^0.3 later (exploration 5).

## Hypothesis

A good manager punishes the members below a partly relative target hard in the first round, again at block starts with a shrinking size, and never right before a switch decision. The total amount and the timing are settled by five versions. What v6 adds is the shape: how the punishment grows with a member's shortfall. The simulated players respond more strongly the further below the target they are, so the punishment should grow with the shortfall, but the cap at 30 and the milder gradient after round 0 argue for something flatter than v5's straight line. One exponent lets the sweep pick between a near-flat fine, proportional, and steeper. That separates "how steep" from "how much", which v5's single k mixed (its two equal modes).

The rule: punishment = k * decay^block * pos * (shortfall / 5)^gamma for valid members of my group, capped at 30. shortfall = max(0, (1 - w) * c0 + w * others' mean - c), and pos = 1 / follow / follow^2 / 0 over a block's four rounds (follow^2 in round 23).
- `c0` (int, [6, 14]): absolute part of the target; expected 9-11 (v5 peak 10).
- `w` (float, [0, 1]): weight of the group's own norm in the target; expected 0.4-0.8 (v5 0.54, s092 0.8).
- `k` (float, [8, 100], log): round-0 punishment at a shortfall of 5; expected 20-40 (v5's peak is 25 at gamma 1; a flatter gamma needs about the cap).
- `gamma` (float, [0.1, 1.6]): steepness in the shortfall; expected 0.4-1.0 (exploration 5: ~1 in round 0, ~0.3 later; v5 was 1).
- `decay` (float, [0.1, 1], log): per-block shrink; expected 0.4-0.7 (both v5 modes 0.55).
- `follow` (float, [0, 1]): second-round share of a block's punishment; expected 0.15-0.7 (flat in v5; the two modes sat at 0.16 and 0.61).

### Justification

Finding 1 says the timing family and the relative target are worth keeping, so c0, w, decay and follow keep their meanings, with ranges centred on v5's optima. follow's and decay's ranges move where v5's profiles were flat at an edge (finding 3 confirms follow has no strong optimum). Findings 1 and 4 give gamma. The data's best punishment rises with the shortfall, but more gently after round 0 than v5's linear form imposes, and v5's two equal modes differ mainly in this shape (finding 2). Re-anchoring k at a shortfall of 5 keeps it the "how much" parameter at any gamma, so the sweep can move gamma without having to retune k along a curved ridge. gamma = 1 with k = 25 reproduces v5's peak exactly (exploration 6), so v6 contains v5 and can fall back to it.

What would contradict the hypothesis: a flat gamma profile, meaning the shape does not matter at the game level (the per-band slopes of finding 4 are observational, from earlier rules' random parameters). A best gamma at either edge would mean the range stopped short. A final pool not above v5's 68.9 within about two standard errors (~1.1) would say the same.

Not modelled:
- Conditioning on the other manager's punishment. Finding 3: it changes the base exit rate, not the price of my punishment, and v1's relative cap failed.
- Separate exponents for round 0 and later blocks. Finding 4 suggests they differ, but later punishment is small at decay ~0.5, and one more parameter would thin the 256-point design.
- Separate weights for a block's second and third rounds. Finding 3's prices offset each other in value, and follow was flat.
- Per-player history. v4's saturation was flat.
