# pf-20261112-1-v2

## Explorations

1. **What did version 1's sweep say?** Looked at plots/simulation/policy_finder/pf-20261112-1_sweep (sweep.json, landscapes.json, final/) with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-1/v1_sweep.py, adding each point's punishment per member-round from the per-round parquets. Found that v1's final point (thr 2, k 0.1, t_start 22) floors every punishment to 0: it is the zero manager, and scored 16.76 (se 1.42) against the reference zero's 13.97 (se 1.44), i.e. the same rule on different seeds. Over the 256 points the score falls monotonically with punishment volume: 15.06 for the 40 points that punish <0.05 per member-round, 13.5 at 0.05-0.3, 11.9 at 0.3-0.7, 10.6 at 0.7-1.5, 4.2 at 1.5-3, -4.5 above 3; OLS pool = 14.65 - 1.6*pun_early - 4.0*pun_late (per member-round). Members fall with volume (corr -0.90 with punishment) while contributions per member rise (8.2 -> 10.7): the contribution gain is real but the member loss dominates. The landscape's peak sits at the edges k=0.1 and t_start=22. So on this stack, v1's family (punish below a bar) has nothing better than not punishing; next I want to know whether any time or target of punishment pays, starting with the rounds after the last switch, where members cannot react.

2. **Does punishment pay after the last switch (rounds 20-22, membership fixed)?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-1/v1_endgame.py on the 67 v1 points with t_start >= 17 (zero before then), per point: punishment per member-round in rounds 20-22, members' mean contribution in round 20 and 23, own pool per member in rounds 20-23. Found the contribution gain from round 20 to 23 is -0.17 + 0.14 per unit of punishment per member-round (t 19), but the pool per member over rounds 20-23 falls by 0.23 per unit (t -3.8): a point of punishment buys ~0.14 contribution, ~0.2 pool points, over the remaining rounds. Even where nobody can leave, punishment does not pay back on this stack. So the contribution channel is closed; any gain must come through members, i.e. through who sits in which group. Next: the score's arithmetic of members, and whether pushing particular players (free riders) to ah's group could pay.

3. **How do members of the rule's group react, at a switch decision, to punishment in v1's sweep?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-1/v1_switch.py and v1_switch2.py: one row per player and decision (rounds 3-19, 2.87M decisions in the rule's group), logit of switching. v1's punishment is set by the design point, so it varies exogenously given contributions. Found (per point of punishment): own punishment in the decision round +0.038, own punishment earlier in the window +0.018; the group's mean punishment of the others earlier in the window +0.063 per point per member-round (contagion), in the decision round -0.025 but on top of the share effect; the decision round's per-member pool share -0.057 per point, the other group's share +0.049, own group size -0.14 per member. Leaving rates of zero givers: 0.29 unpunished, 0.33 at 1-3 points, 0.43 at 4-8, 0.52 at 9-15. ah's group loses 50% of its members at the first decision vs 28% of the rule's. A back-of-envelope for pushing a zero giver out with 15 points in a decision round: +0.16 chance he leaves (worth about nothing: he gives ~1.5, ah would punish him ~3.5 per round), against ~0.09 of the other members lost (worth ~27 lead points per round each, since a member who moves counts on both sides). So the member channel says no to punishing as well; members follow the per-member share, which punishment can only lower. The only remaining hope I can see is the steep first step of the contribution response (exploration 7 of v1: a zero giver goes 1.71 -> 2.40 at 1-2 points), i.e. one-point nudges. Next: v1 points with cap 1-2.

4. **Do one-point nudges pay?** Regressed v1's point scores on punishment volume within cap groups (inline, on the table v1_sweep.py writes). Found the slope is negative at every cap: cap 1: -8.1 per unit of punishment per member-round (se 4.8, 17 points), cap 2: -5.3 (2.1), cap 3-4: -4.0 (1.3), cap 5-15: -5.1 (0.24). No sign that small doses pay. So the contribution channel is closed at every dose (this and 2), and the member channel only punishes punishment (3).

5. **Hypothesis and draft.** On this stack the best manager I can support is the one that does not punish: members follow the per-member share, punishment lowers it, and the contribution it buys is ~0.14 per point. v1's result (16.76) is zero on lucky seeds. The one mechanism left that the v1 family could not express is moving a free rider to ah's group: a zero giver is worth nothing in our pool but lowers our share (which drives our members out and keeps ah's members from coming), while in ah's group ah punishes him (~3.5 per round, which counts for us) and he lowers ah's share. My back-of-envelope in 3 says this loses (bystanders leave), but it rests on regression coefficients (share vs group size nearly offset each other) that I cannot trust to the decimal, and it is the only lever with a plausible sign. So version 2 is a free-rider dump with "never punish" nested: in a decision round only (3, 7, 11, 15, 19), a member whose mean contribution over the current window is at most `bar` gets a fixed `amount`; only from decision `first_dec` on. Nobody else is ever punished, and nothing outside decision rounds (early-window punishment is the most contagious, +0.063 in 3). bar < 0 or first_dec = 5 is the zero manager, so about a third of the design is zero and the sweep can land there if dumping loses.

6. **How often would the dump fire, and does the code do what I mean?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-1/v2_dose.py on the zero-managed groups of the reference sims: share of members whose window-mean contribution is at most the bar at a decision: bar 0: 4%, 1: 9%, 2: 16%, 3: 25%, 5: 37%, 8: 55% (rising over the game, e.g. bar 3: 15% in window 0, 33% in window 4). Above 5 the "free rider" label covers a third to half of the group and the rule would become v1's costly volume punishment, so I narrowed `bar` from [-2, 8] to [-2, 5]; bar -2 and -1 still give the zero manager (a quarter of the bar values), and first_dec = 5 another sixth of the design. A hand-made game checks the code: a member giving 1, 1, 2, 0 in window 1 gets 12 at round 7 with bar 1, nothing at round 6 or 23 or when first_dec = 2; a member giving 3 each round is spared; non-members are never punished. validate-rule passes (256 distinct points).

## Key findings

1. Version 1's final point is the zero manager (every punishment floors to 0) and its 16.76 (se 1.42) is the zero manager's score on lucky seeds; the reference zero scored 13.97 (se 1.44) and v1's 40 near-zero design points averaged 15.06 (exploration 1).
2. Across v1's 256 points the score falls with punishment volume, about -4 pool points per point of punishment per member-round after round 4 and -1.6 in rounds 0-3, through members (corr -0.90), while contributions per member rise from 8.2 to 10.7 (exploration 1).
3. Even after the last switch, where nobody can leave, punishment buys only ~0.14 contribution per point and the pool per member falls 0.23 per unit (exploration 2); one-point doses do no better (slope -8.1, se 4.8, at cap 1; exploration 4).
4. Members switch on the per-member pool share (-0.057 per point of own share, +0.049 per point of the other's), on their own punishment (+0.038 per point in the decision round, +0.018 earlier) and on the punishment of others earlier in the window (+0.063 per point per member-round); ah's group loses 50% of its members at the first decision vs 28% of the rule's (exploration 3).
5. Zero givers leave at 0.29 unpunished, 0.43 at 4-8 points and 0.52 at 9-15 points in the decision round; window-mean free riders (<=3) are 25% of members at a decision under the zero manager, rising over the game (explorations 3, 6).

## Hypothesis

On this stack punishment does not raise contributions enough to pay for itself and it drives members away, while members (each worth ~14 pool points per round, and twice that in the lead when one moves between groups) are what the score is made of; so a good manager does not punish its contributors. The only use of punishment I cannot rule out is relocating free riders: a member who gives (almost) nothing adds nothing to our pool but lowers our per-member share, which pushes our contributors out and keeps ah's from coming over, while in ah's group ah punishes him, which counts for us and lowers ah's share. So the rule punishes nobody, except, in the last round before a switch decision, members whose mean contribution in the current window is at most a bar, with a fixed amount meant to make them leave. Parameters: `bar` (int, [-2, 5]), the window-mean contribution at or below which a member is a free rider, negative meaning nobody (the zero manager); `amount` (int, [1, 30]), the punishment each free rider gets in a decision round; `first_dec` (int, [0, 5]), the first switch decision (after round 3+4*first_dec) at which free riders are pushed, 5 meaning never.

### Justification

Findings 1-3 say the contribution channel never pays and that volume costs members, so the rule punishes nobody outside decision rounds and nobody above a low bar; the zero manager is nested (bar < 0 or first_dec = 5, about a third of the design), and I expect the sweep's best region to be there or at a low bar (0-2), where the rule fires on 4-16% of members (finding 5). Finding 4 places the push in the decision round, not earlier: own punishment works more strongly on the target there (+0.038 vs +0.018) and is less contagious to bystanders than punishment earlier in the window (+0.063); finding 5 says it takes a large dose to move a free rider (0.29 -> 0.52 at 9-15 points), so `amount` spans 1-30 uniformly; I expect any gain at 10-25 if at all. `first_dec` tests whether the first decision, where ah's group bleeds half its members and our share is what they compare, should be spared (finding 4); I expect 1-3 if dumping pays. My back-of-envelope in exploration 3 says dumping loses (bystanders leave because our share drops in the decision round, and the zero giver is worth nothing anywhere); a sweep peak at bar < 0 or first_dec = 5 would confirm that and leave the zero manager as the answer, while a peak at a bar of 0-2 with a large amount would support the relocation idea. Not modelled: anything that punishes contributors (findings 1-3 rule it out on this stack), dependence on group sizes or on the other group's state (the switch regressions' share and size terms nearly offset, and I could not size a conditional effect without re-simulating), and timeouts (a timed-out player is charged nothing).
