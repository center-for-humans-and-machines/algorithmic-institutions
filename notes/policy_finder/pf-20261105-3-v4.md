2. **Fitting a surrogate of the clones.** Wrote scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-3/v4_surrogate.py: a 3-layer MLP (192 units) with a 22-way softmax (contribution 0-20 at t+1, or timeout) on 30 per-player features of the history up to t (own c, p for the last 3 rounds, own running mean c, cumulative and max own p, number of times punished, round-0 c; the others' mean / min / max c now and last round, their mean and max p now, their cumulative p, valid count; position vs the group mean; t). ## Key findings

1. Three earlier rule shapes all reach 650 +- 3 on fresh seeds (v1 649.7, v2 651.9, v3 648.3 at its fitted peak, 650.9-651.1 at its nudge-free points); `zero` scores 585.9. v3's standing nudge on near-cooperators did not pay (exploration 1).
2. A surrogate of the clones fitted on the three sweeps (768 rules x 500 games) reproduces their scores with Pearson 0.987 and rmse 4.25, the noise level of the two estimates, and reads the 18 earlier finals 1-4 points high in nearly the same order (explorations 2-3).
3. On the surrogate v2's convex-shock family is saturated at ~653.5; seven history-dependent refinements (forgiveness, budgets, group-relative standards, punishing drops, round-0 scaling, running-mean shortfall) gain at most ~0.5, and each cell of a free dose table is within +-0.5 of neutral (explorations 4-6).
4. The one lever that clears the noise is a late phase for relapsing free riders: keep big doses for c <= 2-3 only after the shock, until round ~15. On 3 seeds x 4000 games it adds +2.0 to +2.5 to the base on every seed, and searched jointly 657.2-657.3 vs 653.4-653.8 (+3.5), with the shock then ending earlier (horizon 6 vs 10) (explorations 7-8).
5. In the clones' own data a late free rider (c <= 3, rounds 9-15, others averaging 12+) left unpunished rises +2.0 in three rounds while the others fall -2.77; with a 20-30 dose the free rider rises +13.1 and the others fall only -0.62. That repays a dose in ~4-5 rounds (exploration 8).
6. The late phase must stay narrow and big: on the surrogate, a late line of c < 7 costs 3.8, c < 9 costs 7.4, a late dose of 8 costs 6.7; it is flat for stop rounds 12-18 and costs 3.6 if it runs to the end. Giving free riders their own shock dose instead of a separate late dose costs only ~0.15 (explorations 8-9).

## Hypothesis

A good manager on these clones does two things. It shocks the group early: for the first ~6 rounds, a convex dose on everyone below a high standard (about 30 for free riders, ~10 for c 8, a point or none near 18-20), which lifts contributions from ~11 to ~17 and is what v1 and v2 found. Then, instead of stopping everything at once, it keeps policing only relapses to free riding with the same big dose until ~8 rounds before the end, and lets everyone else alone. Relapses are rare once the group cooperates, so this costs little, but each one left alone starts the herding decay that ate the earlier versions' second half; a big dose stops both the free rider and the others' slide. Small doses for near-cooperators (v3's nudge) and anything in the last ~8 rounds do not repay their cost. The rule: `dose = min(30, pmax ((thr - c)/thr)^gamma)` for c < 20, given to every valid player below thr in rounds t < horizon, to players with c < lthr in rounds horizon <= t < lstop, and to nobody after.
- `thr` (int, [18, 24]): the shock standard and the scale of the dose; I expect 20-22 (surrogate 21; 19 costs 3, 23 costs 1).
- `pmax` (float, [24, 40]): the dose for a zero contributor before the clamp at 30; I expect 28-34 (surrogate 31).
- `gamma` (float, [1.6, 3.2]): curvature of the dose; I expect 2.0-2.6 (surrogate 2.3; 1.5 and 3.0 cost 6-10 at fixed thr and pmax, but the three trade off along a ridge).
- `horizon` (int, [3, 10]): first round of the late phase; I expect 5-7 (surrogate 6; 10 recovers v2's shock length).
- `lthr` (int, [0, 8]): the late phase's free-rider line (c < lthr); I expect 2-4; 0 switches the late phase off and recovers v2.
- `lstop` (int, [8, 22]): first round with no punishment; I expect 14-17 (flat 12-18 on the surrogate).

### Justification

Finding 1 says another reshaping of the early dose will not move the score, and finding 3 confirms it on the surrogate, where every history-dependent refinement and every table cell is neutral within the surrogate's error (finding 2); so thr, pmax and gamma keep v2's convex shape with ranges centred on its optimum (v2 thr 20 at the edge, surrogate 21; pmax 30 at the edge, surrogate 31). Finding 4 is the reason for the new phase and for the shorter horizon range (3-10 instead of v2's 9); finding 5 shows the mechanism in the clones' own games rather than only in the surrogate, and its payback time (~4-5 rounds) is why lstop should land ~8 rounds before the end; finding 6 sets lthr low (the line must catch free riders only) and justifies reusing the shock dose (20-30 for c 0-3) instead of a seventh parameter. lthr 0 and horizon 10 are inside the design, so the sweep can fall back to v2 if the late phase is a surrogate artefact. It would be contradicted if the sweep put lthr at 0-1 or lstop at or below horizon (no late phase), or lthr at the top (the late line should be broad). Not modelled: an early nudge for c 16-19 (+0.3-0.8 on the surrogate, inside its error), forgiveness, budgets and group-relative standards (finding 3), and timeouts (a timed-out player cannot be punished, and the clones' timeouts showed no lever). The surrogate is fitted only on memoryless rules like this one and reads 1-4 points high, so I expect ~654-655 on the clones, a gain of 2-3 over v2's 651.9, not the surrogate's 657.
