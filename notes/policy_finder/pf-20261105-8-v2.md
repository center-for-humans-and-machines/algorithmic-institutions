# pf-20261105-8-v2

## Explorations

1. **Where did version 1 end up, and where does its best rule spend?** Read v1's notes,
   rule, sweep.json, landscapes.json and final/ with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-8/v2_best_dynamics.py. v1's final (target 13.75,
   slope 2.23, horizon 12, cap 30) scored 648.2 (se 1.3) vs zero 589.3, the bar to beat.
   The landscape put cap at its top edge (no cap is best), slope peaks ~2.1-2.5, target
   ~13.5, horizon ~11-12 (horizon 1 = 624, 24 = 626). Per round under the best rule: round
   0 costs 8.6 points of punishment per player (59% punished), payoff 9.7 vs 26.9 under zero;
   round 1 5.2; from round 4 to 11 a steady ~1.1-1.2 per player (15% punished). Mean c rises
   11.5 -> 17 by round 8 and, after punishment stops at 12, decays only ~0.2/round to 14.6
   at round 23 (zero decays 11.4 -> 5.3). So the gain is mostly bought in rounds 0-3 and is
   very persistent; the steady 1.2/round of rounds 4-11 (~20 payoff points in total) is the
   part to question. Next: who gets punished in rounds 4-11.
2. **Who keeps getting punished in rounds 4-11 under v1's best rule?**
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-8/v2_who_punished.py. Players punished 7-12 times
   (12% of players) take 35% of all punishment points and still average 12.7 in rounds
   12-23; players punished 0-1 times (45%) take 10%. Next-round rise after a punishment
   falls with the number of earlier punishments: +7.1 for a first punishment (these were
   cooperators at 16.9 who dipped to 8), +4.3 at the 2nd, +2.7 at 3-4, +1.6 at 5-8, +0.7
   beyond 8 (they sit at ~10.5 and stay there). Round-1 change by punishment received:
   -0.9 at 0, +1.3 at 1-5, +2.7 at 5-10, +4.9 at 10-20, +9.9 at 20-30, steep and not
   concave. Hypothesis forming: repeat punishments of the same player are poor value, a
   "forgive after k" or decaying-with-history term could save a chunk of the 35%. But this
   is not causal (regression to the mean; a forgiven chronic low contributor may drift to 0
   and drag the group). Next: cached the v1 sweep (128k games of 256 rules,
   v2_cache.py) to estimate the response with rule-driven variation in p.
3. **Dose-response from v1's sweep, with rule-driven variation in punishment.**
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-8/v2_dose.py and v2_dose_ctrl.py (the latter adds the
   rule's parameters as controls; same answers). Rows: valid player-rounds with c < 16,
   change in own c and in the others' mean c k rounds later regressed on this round's p.
   Per punishment point: own +0.17 next round growing to +0.24 at k=10, others' mean +0.04
   -> +0.15 (so the spillover to the group is as large as the own effect). The effect
   depends strongly on who is punished: c 0-5 own +0.40 and others' mean +0.34 at k=10
   (i.e. ~1.4 group contribution units per round per point!), c 5-10 +0.35/+0.14, c 10-13
   +0.25/+0.08, c 13-16 +0.09/-0.01. It fades with the round (own at k=10: 0.30 at t 1-2,
   0.13 at t 10-19) and with the number of earlier punishments (own 0.37 first time, 0.17
   after 7+), but the others' spillover does not fade with the count (0.13-0.17). The raw
   next-round rise is linear in the dose: +0.5 at p 1-3, +1.6 at 4-8, +3.2 at 9-15, +6.2 at
   16-30: no saturation, which is why v1's cap wanted to be 30. So "forgive repeat
   offenders" is not clearly worth it (they still pay through the group); dropped that idea.
4. **Net value of a punishment point by (own c, round).** v2_value_grid.py: regress the
   group's whole future net surplus sum_{s>t} sum_i (0.6 c - 2 p) on p(t), state and rule
   controls, per cell; a point pays if b > 2. Result (b):
   c 0-1: 23 at t0, 20 at t1-2, 18 at t3-5, 12 at t6-9, 7.5 at t10-13, 4.4 at t14-17, 1.6 at
   t18-21; c 1-5 about the same; c 5-10: 13, 11, 7.5, 3.8, 0.9, 0.6, 0.1; c 10-13: -3.8, 0.1,
   0.8, -3, -4.6, -4, -2; c 13-16: -19, -11.5, -6, -4.7, -4.3, -2.6, -1.6.
   This is the main new insight: punishing players at 10-13 never pays, while punishing
   free riders (c<5) pays until about round 17-18, and moderate shirkers (5-10) until about
   round 9. v1's linear rule with target 13.75 and a single horizon at 12 punishes the
   10-13 group (waste) and stops punishing free riders 5-6 rounds too early. Since the
   response is linear in the dose, the right dose where a point pays is "a lot" and
   elsewhere zero: a high slope from a lower standard, and a standard that falls over the
   game so that late only the deep free riders are hit. Caveat: observational within the
   sweep; the controls are linear. Next: an empirical surrogate built on the sweep, checked

5. **An empirical surrogate built on v1's sweep, checked against the 256 true scores.**

## Key findings

1. v1's best rule (target 13.75, slope 2.23, horizon 12, no cap) scores 648.2 vs zero
   589.3; it buys a rise from 11.5 to 17 by round 8 that decays only to 14.6 by round 23
   after punishment stops; its cost is front-loaded (8.6 points per player in round 0)
   (exploration 1).
2. Per punishment point, the clones raise their own contribution ~0.2-0.4 and the others'
   mean ~0.15 for 10+ rounds, linearly in the dose; the effect is largest for low
   contributors (c<5: own +0.40, others +0.34) and nearly nil for 13-16 (exploration 3).
3. The net value of a punishment point is high for free riders early (b ~20 vs cost 2 in
   rounds 0-5) and falls with the round and with the punished player's contribution
   (exploration 4); the clones also respond to the punishment the others receive
   (exploration 5).
4. An empirical surrogate built from v1's sweep reproduces its true scores (corr 0.91) and
   the shape of its landscape, with ~80% of the gain (exploration 5).
5. On that surrogate every early round of punishment pays (round 0 most: -12.6 if dropped)
   and a late free-rider tail, a falling standard, a relative standard, escalation,
   forgiveness and credit for rising do not help (exploration 6).
6. A punishment convex in the shortfall (dose 30 for a zero contributor, exponent 1.5-2,
   standard 17-19) beats v1's linear rule by ~7 points on the surrogate (635.5 vs 628.3,
   3 seeds) at the same total punishment (exploration 7).

## Hypothesis

A good manager front-loads its punishment and stops around round 10-12, and within that
window hits free riders with (nearly) the maximum while giving players just short of the
standard only a light touch: a punishment point buys most from the lowest contributors
(own and group-wide response) and least from those already near the standard, and the
clones' response is linear in the dose, so it pays to concentrate points where they buy
most. The rule: punishment = dose * (max(standard - c, 0) / standard)^curvature for valid
players in rounds t < horizon, nothing afterwards.
- `standard` (float, [11, 23]): contributions below it are punished; expected best 15-20.
- `dose` (float, [16, 40]): the punishment of a zero contributor (clamped at 30); expected
  best 26-34.
- `curvature` (float, [0.7, 3.5], log): 1 is v1's linear rule, above 1 concentrates the
  punishment on free riders; expected best 1.3-2.5.
- `horizon` (int, 4..20): first round with no punishment; expected best 9-13.

### Justification

Findings 2 and 3 say a punishment point is worth much more on a free rider than on a
near-cooperator, and finding 2 that the response does not saturate, which together argue
for a convex dose (`curvature` > 1, `dose` ~30); finding 6 shows that convexity gains on
the surrogate that finding 4 makes credible. Findings 1 and 5 keep v1's front-loading and
`horizon`: every early round pays, and v1's true landscape put the horizon at 11-12. The
`standard` rises with the curvature along the surrogate's ridge (finding 6), so its range
extends to 23; it also reaches down to 11 so the sweep can recover v1's 13.75 with
curvature 1. What would contradict this: a sweep whose best curvature is at or below 1
(convexity was a surrogate artifact, e.g. of its others'-punishment feature), or a best
dose well below 30. Not modelled: a late tail of punishment for free riders (the value
grid favoured it, the surrogate did not), group-relative standards, escalation or
forgiveness, credit for improvement (all no gain in exploration 6), and timeouts beyond
excluding them.
