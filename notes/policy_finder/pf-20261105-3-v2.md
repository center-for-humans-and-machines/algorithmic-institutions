6. **Is there a spillover, and is late punishment worth it?** v2_spill.py and v2_later.py. Unpunished high contributors (c>=15) next round hardly react to the others' summed punishment (+0.03 per point), but five rounds later they do (+0.19 per point for the first 10, +0.16 to 30): with others at mean c<=7 they fall -8.2 when nobody was punished and only -2.0 when the others got 30+. This is herding, not deterrence: the punished free rider rises and the conditional cooperators follow. Late in the game the own response is unchanged (c 0-3, 8+ points: +0.49 per point in rounds 12-17, still +0.6 five rounds later), but players already punished >= 10 before ignore the first 8 points (+0.0-0.1) and answer only the big doses (+0.45). Players never punished before answer even 1-3 points strongly (+0.5-0.85 per point), though that comparison is selected (a never-punished low contributor has just dropped). Economics: a big point on a free rider buys ~0.33-0.45 own contribution next round, lasting and growing, plus the herding; at 0.6 per contribution point per remaining round against a cost of 2, it pays with roughly 6-10 rounds left. So the horizon should stay, and big doses late are waste.

## Key findings

1. v1's final result is 649.7 (se 1.3) at thr 15, slope 1.96, cap 30 (top edge), horizon 11, taper 0.29, against 589.4 for `zero` and 550.5 for `humanlike`; weak punishment (slope < 0.5 or cap < 5) is the worst region (539-560), and the surface is flat in horizon 6-14 and in taper (exploration 1).
2. The best v1 rules pay a large entry fee (round 0 punishment ~8.9 per player, payoff 9.2 vs 26.9 under zero), lift contributions 11.5 -> 17.1 by round 8-10, and then decay to ~14 by round 23 once punishment stops (exploration 1).
3. Free riders respond convexly to punishment: round 0 -> 1, c=0 gains +2.0 at p 1-2, +3.7 at 7-10, +8.6 at 15-30; per point in rounds 1-10, c 0-3: +0.06 (first 3 points), +0.16 (3-8), +0.45 (8-15), +0.44 (15-30); the gain grows over the following rounds (c<=2 at p 25-30: +10.2 next round, +15.4 six rounds later) (exploration 5).
4. Middle contributors saturate and high contributors want at most a nudge: c 9-12 +0.2-0.27 per point up to ~8 points and nothing beyond; c 13-15 +0.29 for the first 3 points, then negative; c 18-19 lose 1.5 per point beyond 3 (exploration 5).
5. Punishment spills over by herding, not deterrence: unpunished high contributors barely react next round (+0.03 per point of the others' punishment) but five rounds later fall -8.2 when free-riding others were not punished vs -2.0 when they got 30+ (+0.19 per point) (exploration 6).
6. Late in the game the free-rider response to big doses is unchanged (+0.49 per point in rounds 12-17), but the value of a contribution point is 0.6 per remaining round against a cost of 2 per punishment point, so big doses pay only with roughly 6-10 rounds left (exploration 6).
7. Entries 2-3 of my trail were computed on scrambled players (a loader bug) and are superseded by 5-6 (exploration 4).

## Hypothesis

A good manager on these clones punishes hard and early, and spends the punishment where the response is: near-maximal doses on free riders (whose response is convex and lasting, and who drag the conditional cooperators down with them), moderate doses (~8-10) on middling contributors whose response saturates, a nudge or nothing just below the standard, never anything on full contributors, and nothing in the last rounds, when too few rounds are left to repay a cost of 2 per point. v1 had the free-rider end right but its linear dose overdoses the middle. The rule: `p_i = min(30, pmax * (s_i/thr)^gamma + warn * [s_i > 0]) * ((horizon - t)/horizon)^taper` for t < horizon, else 0, s_i = max(0, thr - c_i).
- `thr` (int, [12, 20]): the contribution standard; I expect 15-17 (v1's 15, perhaps a bit higher now that the dose near the standard is small).
- `pmax` (float, [12, 30]): the dose for a zero contributor at full strength; I expect 25-30 (finding 3; v1's cap sat at 30).
- `gamma` (float, [0.6, 2.6]): curvature of the dose in the shortfall; I expect 1.2-1.8 (findings 3-4: 30 for c=0 and ~10 for c=8 needs ~1.5); 1 reproduces v1.
- `warn` (float, [0, 4]): fixed extra dose for anyone below the standard; I expect 0-2 (finding 4: the first 1-3 points help c 13-15).
- `horizon` (int, [6, 22]): the first round without punishment; I expect 10-16 (findings 1 and 6).
- `taper` (float, [0, 2]): how fast strength fades toward the horizon; I expect 0-1 (flat in v1, finding 1).

### Justification

Findings 1 and 3 say the free-rider dose must be big, so pmax ranges from 12 to the game's 30 and the expected best is near 30. Findings 3 and 4 say the response is convex for free riders and saturating for the middle, which a linear shortfall cannot fit at both ends: gamma bends the dose so free riders keep ~30 while c 5-10 get ~8-15 instead of v1's 10-20, saving payoff on doses that buy nothing; warn tests the small nudge findings 4 and 6 favour just below the standard, which a convex dose alone would floor to 0. Finding 5 (herding) is why a free rider's big dose is worth more than its own response, and finding 6 together with finding 1 is why the horizon and taper stay. Since v1's best point is inside this family (gamma 1, warn 0), the sweep can fall back to it. It would be contradicted if the sweep put gamma at or below 1 (the middle needs as much as the linear rule gives) or warn at 4 (the near-standard players want more than a nudge). Not modelled: a maintenance phase after the horizon (v1's longer-horizon points held contributions at ~17.6 but paid as much as they gained), doses that depend on a player's punishment history (repeat offenders ignore small doses, but a convex dose already gives them big ones), standards relative to the group, and punishing full contributors, which no rule in v1's sweep did, so the clones' response there is unknown.
