# pf-20261105-7-v7

## Explorations

1. **Where does v6 leave me?** Read v6's notes, rule, sweep.json,
   landscapes.json and the finals of v3-v6. v6's consolidation worked on
   the sweep (mean of its 256 points 652.5, against ~648 for v4/v5) but not
   on the final: its six final points scored 651.8-653.5 (surface peak
   predicted 656.4, got 653.54), all below v3's 656.05, the bar. Its
   landscape is interior on every axis, mid_fine effect 4.8, curvature 3.5,
   gate 2.3, end 1.1, net fine 0.9, window 0.3, floor 0.0. v3's finals
   held two points at 655.6-656.05 (both ungated, net to round 16-18),
   v6's six were all gated with nets ending at 12-16. So either v3's 656
   was a lucky draw (a max of six draws at se 1.25 carries ~+1.5 of luck)
   and the family's true top is ~654, or something v3 had (no gate, a
   longer net) is worth ~2 and the v4-v6 ranges drifted away from it.
   Next: check whether finals share seeds (paired comparisons), then pool
   all six sweeps in one parameterisation.
2. **Do the finals share seeds?** 74_final_pairing.py, 77_final_seed_groups.py:
   episode scores of every final run of v1-v6 correlated by episode. The
   finals come in six seed blocks, by position in the final (the _gp run,
   then the five sweep points in order): runs in the same block correlate
   0.74-0.95 across versions, runs in different blocks ~0. So the 36 final
   runs are six blocks of six rules played on common random numbers, and
   differences within a block have se ~0.4-0.7 instead of ~1.8. Paired, in
   the _gp block, v3_gp beats v4_gp by 2.19 +- 0.40, v6_gp by 2.50 +- 0.50
   and v5_gp by 3.20 +- 0.51: v3's lead is not luck against its
   successors. And v7's _gp final will be played on the same seeds, so it
   is effectively paired against v3_gp's 656.05. This is the most precise
   evidence in the repository, and nobody has used it yet.
3. **Where does v3_gp gain? Paired per-round differences.**
   75_gp_paired.py, _gp block. v4 (window 10 vs 7, net floor 0, constant
   net fine): rounds 7-9 of extra opening cost 5.6 payoff (+1.0 fine per
   player per round) and buy +0.25-0.4 contribution afterwards (~+3.4
   payoff): the opening's 8th-10th rounds return about half their cost.
   v6 (harder rounds 0-1, +0.75 / +0.37 fine per player; window 8; net to
   12): rounds 0-1 cost 2.2 and buy ~+0.1 c (~1); the 8th opening round
   costs 1.9; stopping the net at 12 instead of 16 saves 3.3 in rounds
   12-15 and loses ~0.3-0.45 c over rounds 15-23 (~1.9). v5 (net floor 4
   at fine 30 in rounds 7-12) pays +0.15-0.2 per player per round there
   and gets slightly *less* contribution (-0.1). So v3_gp's margin is
   cheapness: a lighter first round, a 7-round opening, a moderate net on
   c <= 2 only. Next: table all 36 finals against their seed block and
   look for the profile features that the block-adjusted scores follow.
4. **All 36 finals against their blocks, and a pooled GP with block
   effects.** 78_final_table.py tabulates every final with its score
   minus its block's mean and its per-phase fines and contributions;
   79_pooled_gp_blocks.py fits a GP to the v3-v6 sweeps (1024 points)
   plus the 24 v3-v6 finals with their block effects removed (block
   effects relative to the sweep surface: -0.6, -1.25, +1.74, -0.91,
   -1.66, +0.14; the _gp block is slightly unlucky, block 2 lucky).
   Block-adjusted, v3_gp is the best real rule seen (656.65), then v6_s242
   (655.1), v4_gp, v5_s177, v3_s181 (654.3-654.5); the best in each block
   are ungated or nearly so (gate >= 6) with windows 5-7, nets to 13-18
   with fines 17-25, 24.6-26.8 fines per player in total. The GP's peak
   is 655.9-656.0 at mid_fine ~7, curvature 2.2, window 6, gate 3, floor
   2-3, end 14-15, net fine 22-24, essentially v3_gp. Profiles are broad:
   window 5-7, gate 2-8, floor 1-4, end 13-17 and net fine 20-26 all within
   0.5 of the peak; mid_fine (6-8.6) and curvature (2.0-2.6) the only
   sharp axes. So within the v3-v6 family nothing beats v3_gp by more than
   a few tenths: retuning cannot clear the bar, which on the _gp seeds is
   a paired comparison against v3_gp itself.
5. **What does a point of fine buy in each phase? (paired finals)**
   80_phase_returns.py: within-block regression over the 36 finals of the
   total contribution (sum of round means) on fines per player by phase.
   A fine point in rounds 1-2 goes with +6.4 +- 1.3 contribution-rounds
   (worth 0.6 x 6.4 - 2 = +1.9 payoff), in rounds 3-6 with +2.5-3.6
   (-0.5 to +0.2), in the net rounds 7-15 with +2.8-3.3 (~0); round 0
   is collinear with rounds 1-2 and cannot be separated. These are
   endogenous (fines follow contributions), but they say what the paired
   v4/v6 comparisons said: at v3's optimum the marginal fine after round 2
   just about pays for itself, so the existing levers are exhausted. A
   gain must come from a fine the family never gives.
6. **Small fines on near-full contributors, in the clones' training
   data.** 81_pilot_small_fines.py, the pilots: next-round change (d1) and
   mean over the rest of the game minus now (drest) by own band and fine,
   valid rounds with an active manager. Unfined players at 15-17 drift
   down (drest -0.88 rule manager / -1.57 human manager), at 18-19 -1.33
   / -1.19, at 20 -1.20 / -1.37. Fined 1-2 at 15-17: drest +0.26 / +0.79
   (n 127 / 44); at 18-19: -0.18 / +0.11 (n 55 / 20); fined 3-5 at 15-17
   +0.20 / +1.86. A full contributor fined anything crashes (-5.5 to
   -11.5, n 34 in the human data). So in the data the clones learnt
   from, a token fine of 1-2 to a player at 15-19 turns a ~1.2-point
   slide into a hold, about +1.2-2.4 over the rest of the game, worth
   ~0.15 x 1.5 x 12 = 2.7 payoff per token costing 0.75; and the family
   never gives it (fines at 15-19 floor to 0 in the opening, the net only
   catches c <= 2-4). v5 tested such nudges on its surrogate (sur3_all3)
   and found losses (-0.8 to -9), but that surrogate was trained on sweeps
   with very few such fines and its own notes say it is unreliable on
   the fine's shape. Under v3_gp the decline from 17.1 (round 7) to 14.9
   (round 23) costs ~12 payoff, which is the room a nudge could take.
   Next: check the real sweeps for the same contrast, and whether a
   repeated token keeps working.
7. **The token fine in the real clones: a dead end.** 82_last_round_token.py,
   real sweeps v2-v6 (694k cases): players at c 15-19 in the last opening
   round (t = window-1), fined 0, 1 or 2-3 there by the schedule (which
   one depends on the rule's parameters) and unfined for the next three
   rounds. Raw, the fined ones gain +1.2-1.5 more over the next 8 rounds
   (own -1.06 unfined, +0.11 fined 1, +0.48 fined 2-3), as in the pilots.
   But the fined ones sit lower (c_t 15.4 vs 17.1) and regress up; with
   c_t, own last c, the others' mean, version, round and past fines as
   controls the effect is +0.23 +- 0.01 for a fine of 1 and +0.46 for 2-3,
   peers +0.0. Worth ~0.15 x 0.23 x 12 = 0.4 against a cost of 0.5 for
   a fine of 1, and 0.8 against 1.2 for 2-3: it does not pay. Entry 6's
   pilot contrast was mostly mean reversion, and v5's surrogate verdict
   stands. So I drop the nudge.
8. **Designs on the block-adjusted surface.** 83_fit_save_gp.py pickles
   entry 4's GP; 84_design_check_v7.py scores Sobol designs on it, with
   the true value of the five sweep points a noisy sweep (se 2.5) would
   send to the final. The surface is calibrated: it gives v6's design a
   mean of 652.70, v6's real sweep mean was 652.52, and v3_gp 655.64
   (real, block-adjusted 656.65, so if anything it underrates the v3_gp

   pooled peak and v3_gp: B (mid_fine 6-8.5, curvature 1.95-2.6, window
   5-7, gate 2.5-8, floor 2, end 13-17, net fine 19-27) 655.0; B2 (mid_fine
   6-8.2, curvature 2.0-2.55, window 6-7, gate 2.5-8, floor 2, end 14-17,
   net fine 20-26) 655.3, 62% of its points >= 655; C (tighter still)
   655.4. Freeing the floor (1-3) or fixing the gate off changes < 0.1.
   This matters more than it looks because of entry 2: the finals are

   655.61 at a block-adjusted 653.9); the _gp point is paired with
   v3_gp's 656.05. So the final is won by a design whose every point is
   truly near the peak, not by one with a few lucky outliers. I take B2:

9. **Drafted v7 and validated it.** The rule is v6's code unchanged
   (opening mid_fine x (shortfall/10)^curvature behind the group gate for
   `window` rounds, then a fixed net fine on c <= net_floor until
   net_end); what changes are the ranges, set by entries 4 and 8 from the
   seed-paired finals: mid_fine 6-8.2 (log), curvature 2.0-2.55 (log),
   window 6-7, gate 2.5-8, net_floor fixed at 2 (flat in every fit, 2 in

   fine for players at 15-19 (entry 6) and dropped it after entry 7. I
   expect a flat v7 landscape (true spread ~0.5 against sweep noise 2.5),

   every candidate the final picks should be within ~1 of the family's
   best. validate-rule passes with 256 distinct points.

## Key findings

1. The finals of v1-v6 are six seed blocks (by position in the final)
   played on common random numbers: within a block runs correlate
   0.74-0.95 and differences have se ~0.4-0.7. Paired, v3_gp beats v4_gp
   by 2.19 +- 0.40, v6_gp by 2.50 +- 0.50, v5_gp by 3.20 +- 0.51; v7's
   _gp final is paired with v3_gp's 656.05 (explorations 2, 3).
2. The paired per-round differences show v3_gp wins by being cheap: the
   8th-10th opening rounds return about half their cost, harder rounds
   0-1 (v6) cost 2.2 for ~1 back, a net on c <= 4 at 30 (v5) buys
   nothing (exploration 3); within blocks a fine point after round 2
   returns about its cost (0.6 x 2.5-3.3 - 2 ~ 0), rounds 1-2 more
   (exploration 5).
3. A GP over the v3-v6 sweeps plus the block-adjusted finals peaks at
   ~656.0 (mid_fine ~7, curvature 2.2-2.3, window 6-7, gate ~3 or off,
   floor 2-3, end 14-15, net fine 22-24), essentially v3_gp (block-adjusted
   656.65, the best real rule); only mid_fine and curvature are sharp
   (exploration 4).
4. Token fines on near-full contributors look strong in the pilots
   (+1.2-2.4 over the rest of the game) but in the real clones, with
   controls, a fine of 1 buys +0.23 own contribution over 8 rounds and
   2-3 buys +0.46, short of their cost (explorations 6, 7).
5. On that surface v6's design is worth 652.7 (real sweep mean 652.5) and
   its top-5 picks 654.1; v7's design 655.1 and 655.3, with 62% of the
   points >= 655 (exploration 8).

## Hypothesis

A good manager raises the group with a short, convex opening and then
only guards against defectors, and it wins by being cheap: a player at 10
pays about 7 in the opening, a free rider the cap, players at 16-19
nothing, for 6-7 rounds; afterwards only players at 0-2 are fined, ~23
points, until round 14-17; then nothing. Every further fine, on mid
contributors late, on near-full contributors as a token, or harder in
the first round, returns no more than its double cost in the clones.
Six versions put this family's ceiling at ~656 (v3_gp); v7 does not add
structure but places all 256 sweep points within ~1 of that ceiling, so
that the final's picks, played on seed blocks that are paired with
earlier finals, are the family's best rather than its luckiest. The rule
(v6's code): punishment = mid_fine x (max(0, 20-c)/10)^curvature x
[c < mean(c valid) + gate_margin] x [t < window] + net_fine x
[window <= t < net_end] x [c <= net_floor], capped at 30 by the manager.
- `mid_fine` (float, 6-8.2, log): opening fine of a player at 10.
- `curvature` (float, 2.0-2.55, log): convexity of the opening fine.
- `window` (int, 6-7): rounds of the opening.
- `gate_margin` (float, 2.5-8): the opening spares players at or above the
  group mean plus this margin; ~8 is off.
- `net_floor` (int, fixed 2): after the opening only players at or below
  it are fined.
- `net_end` (int, 14-17): round at which the net stops.
- `net_fine` (float, 20-26): the fixed fine of a defector in the net.

### Justification

Findings 1 and 3 say the family's best is v3_gp's neighbourhood and that
retuning cannot beat it by much; finding 4 closes the one new lever I
found (token fines), and finding 2 says the remaining levers (longer or
harder openings, wider or harder nets) all lose on paired seeds. So the
ranges follow finding 3: mid_fine 6-8.2 around the peak 6.5-7.4 (the
pooled profile drops ~0.8 at 6.0 and ~1.1 at 8.6), curvature 2.0-2.55
around 2.2-2.3 (drops ~0.3-0.8 at either end), window 6-7 (5 and 8 each
~0.4-0.7 lower), gate 2.5-8 so the sweep still compares a gate near 3
(the GP's choice, +0.9) with an ungated opening (what the paired finals
favour), net_floor fixed at 2 (flat from 1 to 4 within 0.4), net_end
14-17 and net_fine 20-26 (both flat within 0.3 there). Finding 5 is why
narrow: v6's wider ranges sent 654-level points to the final. I expect
the v7 landscape flat, peak anywhere inside, with mid_fine around 7 and
curvature around 2.2-2.3. The hypothesis is contradicted if v7's _gp
final (paired with v3_gp) falls more than ~1 below 656.05, which would
mean the pooled surface mislocates the peak, or if the landscape puts
mid_fine or curvature at an edge with a slope of more than ~1. Not
modelled: timeouts beyond excluding them from the group mean (~2%, no
lever), token fines (finding 4), round-specific fine scales (rounds 0
and 1-2 cannot be separated in the data, and the surrogate tests of v4
lost), and the free rider's fine, which the cap makes moot.
