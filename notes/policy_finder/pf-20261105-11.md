# pf-20261105-11

## Explorations

1. **What do the pilots look like, and what do the reference sims say?** Looked at
   the pilot csv (contribution, punishment, payoff by round and experiment) with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-11/a1_overview.py and at
   plots/simulation/29_EXP1_reference/aggregates.csv. The mean payoff is
   `20 + 0.6*mean(c) - 2*mean(p)`, so one point of punishment costs as much as 3.3
   points of contribution earn. In the sims `zero` starts at c=11.6 and decays to
   5.5 by round 23 (payoff 26.6 -> 23.0, about 586 summed), while `humanlike`
   climbs to c~13.2 but punishes ~2-3 per player per round (payoff ~21-24, about
   554 summed): `zero` wins. Human managers punish mostly the low contributors
   (c=0: mean 10.4, c=20: 0.26). Timeouts are rare (2%). So a good rule has to
   get humanlike's contribution lift at a fraction of its punishment; next I want
   to see how contributions respond to punishment.
2. **How does next-round contribution respond to punishment, by contribution
   level (pilots)?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-11/a2_response.py. For
   c<=4 the next-round change is +0.9 unpunished, +3.8 at p 6-10, +6.4 at p>10;
   for c 5-9: +0.1 vs +4.1. For c 10-14 the effect is small (-1.0 vs +0.5..+1.1)
   and for 15-19 nearly none; punishing a full contributor collapses them (-11).
   Regression: dc ~ 0.15*p + 0.26*(others mean - own c). So punishment works
   mainly on low contributors, and players also herd toward the group mean.
   A punishment of 15 costs 30 payoff points summed and needs ~50 contribution
   points later to repay itself - only plausible if the lift persists and
   spreads. Next: the same response in the artificial humans (humanlike sims),
   which is what the rule will face.
3. **The same response in the artificial humans (humanlike reference sims).**
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-11/a3_sim_response.py. Weaker than in the
   pilots but the same shape: a c=0 player gains +1.4 next round unpunished,
   +2.8 at p 6-10, +5.3 at p>10; c 10-14 barely moves (+0.3 vs -0.75); a
   punished 19-20 contributor drops (-2.7 at p>10 vs -0.75). The climate matters too:
   unpunished high contributors decay -0.75/round under humanlike vs -1.7 under zero.
   Timeout rate 1.9% in both. The contribution model (configs/training/.../
   script_21_no_grid.yml) takes only prev contribution, prev punishment and
   validity, with edges to the other players and an RNN (hidden 5), and no round
   number, so it has no built-in end game; the decay under zero is its own
   dynamics. Next: does the effect persist over later rounds and spill over?
4. **Persistence and spillover (humanlike sims).**
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-11/a4_persistence.py, regression of c_{t+k}
   on c_t, p_t, others' punishments, others' mean c, lagged c and p. One unit of
   punishment raises the player's own contribution ~0.075-0.09 at every lag k=1..4
   (no decay), ~0.12-0.13 for c<=5, ~0 for c>=15; each unit given to someone else adds
   0.017-0.035 per other player, rising with k. So one unit buys ~0.16 contribution
   per later round over the group: payoff 0.6*0.16 = 0.1 per round against a cost of 2,
   break-even after ~20 rounds if linear. Taken at face value, only early punishment
   pays, and none near the end. But the regression is local (humanlike's
   punishment levels) and does not see the regime effect. Next: the pilots' random rules give
   variation in rule strength across episodes - which strength worked?
5. **Rule strength vs outcome in the random_1 pilots.**
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-11/a5_random1_episodes.py; per episode, slope
   of punishment on deficit (20-c). Split into quartiles by slope: 0.37 -> late
   (rounds>=10) c 9.9, late p 2.8, late payoff 20.5; 0.77 -> 16.4/2.4/25.2;
   1.03 -> 16.5/2.2/25.5; 1.33 -> 17.7/1.5/27.7. Controlling for round-0
   contribution, late payoff rises 6.9 per unit of slope. Steep rules cost about
   as much over the game (p 3.65 vs 2.8) but cooperation rises, so the punishment
   bill shrinks over time. Weak punishment is the worst of both: it costs and does not work.
   So my working hypothesis: punish deficits steeply and predictably, early,
   so players learn that a deficit costs more than it saves (slope > 1 means a
   deficit point costs the free rider more than the 0.6 they keep... actually
   keeping a point gains 1-0.4=0.6, so any slope > 0.6 makes free riding a loss).
   Next: check whether the artificial humans reproduce this regime effect in the
   humanlike sims across episodes.
6. **Does rule strength matter for the artificial humans too?**
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-11/a6_sim_episodes.py: per humanlike-sim episode, slope of
   punishment on deficit in rounds 0-7. By slope quintile (0.07 -> 1.01): late
   (round>=12) c 9.4 -> 16.6, late punishment 1.8 -> 1.8 (not higher), summed payoff
   542 -> 581. Regression with round-0 contribution as control: +7.9 late c and
   +40 summed payoff per unit slope. So the AH reproduce the regime effect: steep
   early deficit punishment raises cooperation without raising the later bill. Yet even
   the steepest quintile (581) only matches `zero` (586 average, and 583 for
   zero episodes with similar c0). Humanlike's punishment is noisy (it also hits
   19-20 contributors, which collapses them) and its early bill is high (4.4 per
   player-round). A clean rule should keep the steep slope and drop the waste:
   no punishment at or above a target, none in the last rounds. Next: the
   fine shape of the response for low contributors (is it convex in p?), and what
   a threshold rule would cost in round 0.
7. **Shape of the response: is a big punishment worth more per point?**
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-11/a7_shape.py, humanlike sims, gain in
   contribution over unpunished players of the same contribution bin, 1 and 3
   rounds ahead, by punishment bin. Strongly concave: for c 3-8 a punishment of 1-3
   gives +1.9 at k=3 (1.0 per point), 12-15 gives +4.0 (0.28 per point); for c
   8-12, 1-3 gives +1.6 (0.84 per point) and 12-15 only +2.5 (0.17 per point). For c 0-3
   the response is more linear (0.27-0.31 per point, up to +6 at p>20). For c 16-20
   punishment does nothing or harms (-0.9 at p 15-20). So the signal
   of being punished does most of the work; the size adds a little, mostly for
   near-zero contributors. This changes my draft: rather than a steep linear slope,
   a concave punishment of the shortfall below a target - a modest bill for a
   small shortfall, more for free riders - and never above the target.
8. **What would the drafted family cost in round 0?**
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-11/a8_bill.py, rule
   `p = P * (max(0, T-c)/T)^g` on the zero sims' round-0 contributions (median 11,
   35% below 10, 22% at 20), floored as the manager does. The bill ranges from
   p 0.4/player (T=10, P=3, g=1) to 9 (T=20, P=15, g=0.3), i.e. a payoff cost of
   0.8-18 per round. Humanlike spends ~2.3/player-round (cost ~4.6) and loses ~32
   summed payoff to zero, so a winning rule probably sits at a bill of about 1 or
   less, through a moderate target and a moderate P: the concave
   response (7) says small punishments carry most of the lift. Flooring gives a natural
   dead zone: with g>=1 small shortfalls round down to 0.
   End game: the clones see no round number, but a punishment in round 23 has no
   future to work in, and per (4) and (7) a small punishment needs ~4+ rounds to
   repay itself, so punishment should stop some rounds before the end; I let
   the sweep choose how many.
9. **Drafted the rule** (configs/managers/rule_based/pf-20261105-11.yml): four
   parameters, target T [6, 20], punishment of a full free rider P [1, 30] log,
   curvature g [0.25, 2.5] log, and E, the number of final rounds without
   punishment, int [1, 10]. I considered a target relative to the others' mean
   (herding, coefficient ~0.2-0.3 in 3-4) but kept it absolute: a relative target
   lets a group drifting down drag the standard with it, which is what the zero
   manager's decay looks like, and a fifth parameter would thin the 256-point design.
10. **Checked the rule by hand and found an import trap.**
    scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-11/t1_rule_check.py runs the code through
    load_rule/run_rule. In a fresh process `th.clamp` on a tensor raised
    `KeyError: '__import__'` (torch imports lazily on first use, and the rule
    scope has no builtins), although validate-rule had passed, probably because
    its earlier calls had already done that import. I replaced it with `th.relu(T - c)`,
    which needs no import. With T=15, P=8, g=0.5, E=3: c=0 -> 8, 5 -> 6.5, 10 -> 4.6,
    14 -> 2.1, 15 and 20 -> 0; round 20 punished, round 21 not. validate-rule passes
    on all 256 design points.

## Key findings

1. Mean payoff per round is `20 + 0.6*mean(c) - 2*mean(p)`. One point of punishment
   costs as much as 3.3 contribution points earn. `humanlike` (summed ~554) loses
   to `zero` (~586) in the reference sims, because it spends ~2.3 per player-round
   for a lift from ~8 to ~13 mean contribution (1).
2. Without punishment the artificial humans decay from c=11.6 to 5.5 over 24 rounds.
   Unpunished high contributors fall 1.7/round under zero but only 0.75 under
   humanlike, so the punishment climate holds them up (1, 3).
3. Punishment works on low contributors and not on high ones. A c<=4 player
   gains several points next round when punished hard (pilots +6.4 vs +0.9; sims
   +5.3 vs +1.4). A 19-20 contributor who is punished drops (pilots -11, sims -2.7)
   (2, 3, 7).
4. The effect persists and spreads: per punishment point the player's
   contribution is ~0.08 higher at every lag 1-4 (0.12-0.13 for c<=5), and each
   other player's is 0.02-0.035 higher (4).
5. The response is concave in punishment size. For c 3-12, a punishment of 1-3
   gives +1.6 to +1.9 three rounds later (0.8-1.0 per point), while 12-15 gives
   +2.5 to +4.0 (0.17-0.28 per point) (7).
6. Consistent deficit punishment raises cooperation in both the pilots and the
   clones. Pilots: steepest-slope quartile late c 17.7 and late payoff 27.7, against
   9.9/20.5 for the weakest. Clones (humanlike episodes): +7.9 late c and +40 summed
   payoff per unit slope, with no rise in the late punishment bill (5, 6).
7. The clones see no round number. A punishment needs several rounds to repay
   itself, so punishment in the last rounds is pure cost (4, 8).

## Hypothesis

A good manager makes contributing below a standard reliably costly, to the
contributor alone, from the first round. The aim is to set the cooperative climate that
stops the clones' decay. It does this as cheaply as possible: high contributors
are never touched, and a small shortfall gets a small punishment, because being
punished at all carries most of the effect. Free riders get more, since only near-zero
contributors respond in proportion. Once the group contributes above the standard
the bill falls to nothing by itself. The manager stops punishing in the final rounds,
where there is no future left for a punishment to pay off in. The rule:
`punishment = P * (max(0, T - c)/T)^g` while `t <= 23 - E`, else 0.
- `T`, target contribution: at or above it a player is never punished. Sweep
  [6, 20], uniform.
- `P`, punishment of a full free rider (c=0). Sweep [1, 30], log-uniform.
- `g`, curvature in the shortfall: g<1 is a near-flat signal for any shortfall,
  and g>1 spares small shortfalls (flooring then rounds them to 0). Sweep [0.25, 2.5],
  log-uniform.
- `E`, final rounds without punishment. Sweep int 1..10.

### Justification

Findings 1 and 6 show that steady deficit punishment raises contributions but
that humanlike pays too much for it. That is why the rule has an explicit target
(`T`) and never punishes above it. Finding 3 also argues against T near 20.
I expect T around 10-16: the round-0 median is 11, and the clones respond weakly above
12. The range [6, 20] covers lenient through demanding standards. Finding 5
(concavity) and 3 (free riders respond linearly) motivate `g`. I expect g below 1
(0.4-0.9), but g>1 is the cheaper bill, so the log range [0.25, 2.5] spans both
sides of 1 evenly. Finding 1's budget and 8's round-0 bill motivate `P`. Humanlike's
~2.3/player-round loses, so I expect P around 3-12, with a bill near or below
1/player-round. The lower end, P near 1, is almost the zero manager, a safe
fallback. The upper end, 30, is the cap, and pilots' steep rules did best (6).
Finding 7 motivates `E`. A point needs ~4 rounds to repay (from 4-5), so I expect
E around 2-5. E=1 is the logical minimum, since round 23 can never pay, and 10 allows an
early stop if late punishment does not pay at all.

What would contradict it: if the best sweep points sit at P near 1 (any
punishment loses to doing nothing on these clones), or at T=20 with large P (a
harsh regime beats a cheap one, despite finding 3).

Not modelled: targets relative to the group mean (herding, 0.2-0.3). I kept
the target absolute, since a relative one follows a decaying group down. History-based
escalation for repeat offenders is not modelled either: the clones' per-point effect does
not grow with past punishment (p_prev coefficient ~0 in 4), and it would cost a
parameter. Timeouts are left out because the manager cannot punish a timed-out
player and they are rare (1.9%).
