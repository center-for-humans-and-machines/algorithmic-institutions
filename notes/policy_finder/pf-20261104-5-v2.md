# pf-20261104-5-v2

## Explorations

1. **What did version 1's sweep find?** Read
   `plots/simulation/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5_sweep/` (`sweep.json`,
   `landscapes.json`, `final/sweep.json`). Found the opposite of v1's
   hypothesis: the peak is a fairly hard punisher, k=1.75 per point short of
   thr=11.6, cap=14.8, t_stop=10, which scores 67.3 (se 1.06, 2000 episodes)
   with 4.11 members, against ~60.5 for `zero` in the reference sims. The
   fitted profiles rise with k (60.4 at 0.05 to 65.1 at 1.8, flat to 3), with
   cap (61.7 at 1 to 65.1 at 14), peak in thr at 11-12 (61.2 at 1, 64.2 at
   14.4) and in t_stop at 8-11 (63.2 at 0, 60.8 at 23); `quiet` has no effect
   (0.0002 points). The lightest points of the sweep (cap 1-2, thr 4-5) are
   its worst (56-58), below zero. So the group pays fewer members (4.1 vs
   ~4.5 for light punishers) for higher contributions, and on net gains;
   v1's per-point estimates (0.045 contribution points per punishment point)
   understated what punishment does here. Next: from the sweep's per-round
   data, find where the gain comes from (contributions by round, who is
   punished, who leaves, who arrives).
2. **Where does v1's gain come from, by round?** Looked at the v1 final
   points (`final/per_round.parquet`, our group is group 0) against the
   zero/ah reference sims, per 4-round block, with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v1_mechanism.py. Found that the peak
   (gp) pays in the first block (pool 43.7 vs zero's 54.2, punishment 4.3 per
   member and round) and collects later: members give 10.8 in rounds 4-7 and
   11.4-11.6 from round 8 to the end, against zero's 8.0-8.4, while it keeps
   4.4-4.7 members (zero 5.0-5.2). The striking part is that the gain
   survives the stop: from round 11 gp punishes nothing, yet its pool is
   84.8-88.6 per round in rounds 12-23 against zero's ~65. A light punisher
   (s036, 0.5-0.75 per member) gives zero-like contributions (8.5-8.9). So
   punishment here works as an early, hard, one-off investment whose
   returns last; the artificial humans' contribution model is recurrent
   (artifacts config: node+edge+rnn), which fits a lasting memory. Next:
   separate how much of the late gain is the same players behaving better
   (memory) and how much is selection (free riders gone, cooperators come).
3. **Memory or selection?** Typed every player by its round-0 contribution
   (before any punishment) and looked at rounds 12-23 of the v1 peak against
   zero, with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v1_selection.py. Found it is
   not selection: the group's late mix of types is the same (0-4 types 17.1%
   of member-rounds vs 17.4% under zero, 15-20 types 22.5% vs 21.3%), but
   every type gives ~3.7 points more (0-4: 9.3 vs 5.4; 15-20: 14.9 vs 11.1).
   Starting free riders spend about as much of the late game with us (48%
   vs 52%) as under zero.
4. **A group norm or a personal memory?** Split late contributions in our
   group by how long the player was with us before round 12 and how much we
   punished it, with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v1_norm.py. Found the
   rise is population-wide: under the v1 peak *ah's* group also gives 11.7
   late (9.6 in the zero/ah reference), and players who never were in our
   group before round 12 give 11.6 with us (9.4 under zero). So the early
   punishment lifts the whole population's level of contribution, which then
   travels with players between groups and holds without further
   punishment; our group's edge over ah's late is that it stops punishing
   and so holds more members (4.7 vs ~3.3). Next: across v1's 256 points,
   how the late level depends on the dose in each early block, to see
   whether v1's best dose is still on the rise.
5. **How does the late level depend on the dose per block?** Summarised
   every v1 sweep point by block (rounds 0-3, 4-7, 8-11, 12-23; punishment
   per member-round `pm`, member contribution `cm`, members `n`, pool) with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v1_points.py and regressed with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v1_dose.py. Found that each point of
   punishment per member-round in any of the first three blocks lifts the
   late (12-23) member contribution by 0.31-0.38 (R2 0.89), with no sign of
   saturation (squared terms -0.03), and costs only 0.04-0.19 late members;
   on the score each early point is worth +0.6, while each point in rounds
   12-23 costs 2.9. The 9 points with the heaviest early dose (4.4 per
   member-round in rounds 0-3) average 64.4 and are the only region near the
   peak, but v1's log ranges put most of the design at light doses (median
   k 0.39, median cap 4.5; 178 of 256 points under 0.5 per member-round in
   rounds 0-3). So the dose was still rising at v1's best points and the
   sweep sampled that region thinly; v2 should sample heavier early
   punishment and keep the late phase free (or nearly). Next: at the player
   level, who should carry the dose: does punishing moderate contributors
   backfire, and does a punishment change the punished player more than the
   others?
6. **Personal or shared?** Compared late (12-23) contributions anywhere by
   round-0 type and starting group, and regressed a player's late
   contribution on its round-0 contribution, the punishment it got in rounds
   0-11 and the punishment the others in its group got, with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v1_personal.py. Found that under the v1
   peak the players who started in ah's group end up nearly as high as ours
   (0-4 types 8.9 vs 9.6; 15-20 types 14.7 vs 14.8), and that before the
   first switch ah's group does not move (9.3 in round 2 in both the v1 peak
   and the reference) but rises after players mix. The per-player
   regression is confounded (low contributors are the ones punished; own
   punishment -0.68, others' -0.99), so it cannot say who should carry the
   dose. My reading: the punished raise their giving, carry it across the
   switch, and herding spreads it to whoever they play with. Whom to punish

   cooperators (64.2 at 14.4), which matches the humans cutting back when
   punished at 15-20 (v1 finding 2).
7. **Drafted v2.** Kept v1's shape, min(cap, k*max(0, thr - c_i)) for every
   valid member during a punishing phase that ends at `t_stop`, and dropped
   `quiet` (no effect at all in v1's surface). Added `decay`: the strength is
   multiplied by decay^t, so the sweep can choose between a short sharp start
   (decay < 1) and steady or rising pressure up to the stop (decay >= 1) at
   the same `t_stop`. The reason is the trade-off visible in exploration 5's
   heavy points: the heaviest of them (s120: cap 19, thr 13.5) reaches a
   late contribution of 13.3 but pays a first-block pool of 34.6, against
   46.1 for the best point (s192). I moved the ranges to the heavy region
   where v1's best points sat. Checked with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v2_design.py: at full strength the
   median design point now charges a zero contributor 11.1 points (v1 1.7)
   and a 7-contributor 5.5 (v1 0.2), and 57% of points charge a zero
   contributor 10 or more (v1 5%). The v1 peak (14 and 8) sits inside the
   design. Ran the rule in a fresh process with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/test_rule_v2.py (k=2, thr=12, cap=15,
   decay=0.9, t_stop=9; contributions 0/4/7/11/20): 15/15/10/2/0 in round 0,
   x0.9 a round after that, 0 from round 10, and 0 for timed-out and
   other-group players. The validator passes on all 256 points.

## Key findings

1. Version 1's sweep turned down its own hypothesis. Its peak is a hard,
   early punisher (k=1.75, thr=11.6, cap=14.8, punishing until round 10),
   which scores 67.3 (se 1.1) against ~60.5 for `zero`. Light punishers are
   the worst points (56-58), and `quiet` had no effect (exploration 1).
2. The gain comes after the punishing stops. With no punishment from round
   11 on, the v1 peak's members give 11.4-11.6 against zero's 8.0-8.4, and
   its pool is 85-89 a round against ~65 (exploration 2).
3. The lift is behavioural and spread across the population, not
   selection. The group's mix of types is the same as under zero, but every
   type gives ~3.7 more; ah's group rises to 11.7 too (9.6 in the
   reference); and players who were never with us before round 12 give 11.6
   (explorations 3, 4, 6).
4. Across v1's 256 points, each punishment point per member-round in
   rounds 0-3, 4-7 or 8-11 lifts the late contribution by 0.31-0.38, with
   no sign of saturation, and is worth +0.6 on the score. Each point in
   rounds 12-23 costs 2.9. v1's design put 70% of its points under 0.5
   points per member-round, so the heavy region near its peak was sampled
   thinly (exploration 5).
5. The heaviest early doses pay a lot up front (first-block pool 34.6 for
   s120 against 46.1 for the best point), so how the dose is spread over
   the punishing phase is a real trade-off (exploration 7).

## Hypothesis

In this simulation a good manager invests early and then gets out of the
way. For the first rounds it punishes low contributors hard. That raises
their giving, they carry it with them when they switch groups, and herding
spreads it, so the whole population settles at a higher level that holds
without further punishment. After that the manager punishes nothing. This
makes its group the cheaper one to be in, which holds members against `ah`'s
steady punishing, and the higher level is collected over the rest of the
game. The rule punishes each valid member i by
min(cap, k * max(0, thr - c_i)) * decay^t in rounds t <= t_stop, and by 0
afterwards:

- `k` (float, [0.5, 5], log): punishment points per contribution point
  below the threshold, at full strength. I expect 1.3-3.
- `thr` (float, [7, 16]): the contribution from which a member is safe. I
  expect 10-13.
- `cap` (float, [4, 30]): the most a member gets in one round, at full
  strength. I expect 10-20.
- `decay` (float, [0.75, 1.15]): how the strength changes from round to
  round during the punishing phase. Below 1 front-loads the punishment,
  1 is v1. I expect 0.85-1.0.
- `t_stop` (int, [2, 19]): the last round of the punishing phase. I expect
  6-12.

### Justification

Findings 1-3 say punishment is an investment whose return is a lasting rise
in the population's contributions, and finding 4 says rounds 12-23 should
stay free of punishment. That is the reason for a punishing phase with a
hard `t_stop`. Its range, [2, 19], is centred on v1's optimum of 8-11 but is
wide on both sides: heavier doses may reach the new level sooner, and
`decay` may move the best stop. Finding 4 shows the dose was still rising at
v1's best points, and those points sat at the top of v1's log ranges. So `k`
and `cap` now span the heavy region. `k` is still log-ranged, because what
matters is whether a member a few points short is charged a little or a lot.
`cap` runs uniformly up to the game's maximum of 30, since v1's profile rose
until about 14 and was still flat at 18. `thr` keeps v1's peak of 11-12 near
the middle of its range. It stops at 16 because v1's surface fell once the
threshold reached the cooperators, and punished high contributors cut back
(v1 finding 2). Finding 5 motivates `decay`: it is the one new idea. It tests
whether a sharp start does the job more cheaply than steady pressure, and its
range includes 1 (v1's shape) and mildly rising pressure (1.15^10 = 4, which
`cap` then bounds). I dropped `quiet` because v1 found it inert.

The hypothesis would be contradicted if v2's best points sit at low `k` or
low `cap` (heavy punishment was a lucky draw in v1), or at `t_stop` near 19
(the late phase should punish after all). If the best points sit at
`decay` 0.75 with a late `t_stop`, the useful phase is just the first few
rounds and the stop no longer matters.

I chose not to model several things:
- The other group's punishments (`p` of ah's members). The lift reaches
  ah's group anyway.
- Newcomers. They arrive at the raised level (finding 3).
- Reactions to a player's own history, such as repeated free riding or a
  drop from its previous contribution. The per-player regression could not
  separate whom punishment moves (exploration 6), and a threshold on the
  current contribution is what v1's surface supports.
- Timing around switch decisions. It was inert in v1.
