3. **Do fines after the group has complied still pay? (graduation).**
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-11/e2_graduation.py uses v4's sweep, where S is

   rounds r0+1..23 across S (T 12-15, B 1.3-2.6).
   - Graduated by round 3 (48% of groups): 585.7 at S=5, 588.0-589.6 at S=6-11,

   - Graduated vs not differ by ~30 points.

   So later fines repay themselves almost exactly, in both kinds of group. A
   "stop once the group complies" switch cannot gain more than ~1-2. Payoff is
   decided by whether the group locks in during rounds 0-3. Next: what makes a
   group fail to lock in, and does anything in the rule's reach change it?
4. **Why groups fail to lock in.** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-11/e3_lockin.py,

   So the gap is set by who is in the group at round 0, which no rule controls.
   Entry 3 already showed that fines on slow risers repay themselves, so they are
   not waste to cut. Next: the vicarious channel. If a fine on others moves a
   player, the total opening fine matters, not just each player's own.
5. **Vicarious response, cleanly at round 0.** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-11/e4_vicarious.py:
   1.55M player-games from v1-v4 and the reference. I took players with own p0 = 0,

   varies only with the rule's parameters.

## Key findings

1. The four versions' finals tie: v1 648.8, v2 649.7, v3 645.7 (GP peak; other
   points up to 651.6) and v4 649.9, each with se ~1.3 (1).
2. A quadratic over the 539 v2-v4 real sweep points leaves residuals of sd 3.6,
   the per-point noise. Its peak is 649.0 at T 12.75, A 3.25, B 1.98, S 7, with
   every profile interior, and the top raw points (651-655) are noise (2).
3. Fines after round ~4 repay themselves almost exactly. With S drawn
   independently, the payoff from round 4 on is flat in S from 6 to 11, both in
   groups that complied by round 3 (585.7-589.6) and in those that did not
   (555.9-560.0) (3).
4. Whether a group locks in by round 3 decides ~30 points, and it depends mainly
   on the round-0 lineup: lock-in is 38% when the lowest opener gives 0-3, and 88%
   when the lowest gives 12+. Non-locked groups have a slow riser (4.1 -> 7.5 by
   round 3) (4).
5. The vicarious effect is large. An unfined middle player (c0 10-13) goes to
   c1 9.2 when the others' total round-0 fine is <10, against 12.6 at 30-45. For
   top players it saturates at a total of ~20-30, about what the plateau rule
   hands out (5).
6. A cap-level fine for free riders is about neutral (its rough gain of ~3.7
   matches its cost of 3) (6).
7. Fixing S and narrowing T, A and B raises the true value at the fitted peak in
   mock sweeps: 648.47 against 648.31 for v4's box. The p10 is 647.94 against
   647.53 (7).

## Hypothesis

A good manager makes the group lock in during rounds 0-3, with a predictable price
on contributing below a moderate standard. Every shortfall gets a tap, and the
price rises steeply with the shortfall, so free riders feel it most. The fines also
show the middle and top players that shortfalls are not tolerated, which keeps
them from sliding. After the opening rounds the fines only repay themselves, so
the manager stops after round 7, and the clones carry on with a slow decay. The
rule is unchanged from v2/v4: `punishment = A + B*(T - c)` for c < T while
t <= S, else 0. This version sweeps it with S fixed and a box tight around the
pooled real peak:
- `T`, the target: no fine at or above it. Sweep [11.5, 15.0], uniform.
- `A`, the tap any shortfall gets. Sweep [1.0, 5.0], uniform.
- `B`, the price per point of shortfall. Sweep [1.3, 3.0], log-uniform.
- `S`, the last punished round. Fixed at 7.

### Justification

- The shape:
  - Finding 4 says the payoff is decided in the opening.
  - Finding 5 says the opening fine works on onlookers too, and that its total
    at the plateau sits where that effect saturates.
  - Finding 3 says nothing is lost or won by fining later, which is why a stop
    exists and why its exact round does not matter.
- `S` fixed at 7: the pooled S profile is within 0.5 over 5-10 (2), and the
  graduation split shows the same flatness (3). Fixing S removes a dimension that
  only adds noise to the GP's peak (7).
- `T` [11.5, 15]: the pooled profile is 644.7 at 11, 648.9 at 13 and 642.0 at 15
  (2). v4's final best sits at 13.3, so the peak is bracketed.
- `B` [1.3, 3.0] log: the profile peaks at 2.0, and falls to 644.7 at 1.3 and
  643.5 at 3.2 (2). Both drops are at the ends.
- `A` [1, 5]: the profile peaks at ~3, within 0.7 from 2 to 4, and is 643.5 at 0
  (2). The tap stays on.

What would contradict it: a best point at an edge of T or B, which would mean the
real surface has moved; or a final well below ~648 with an interior peak, which
would mean the plateau is narrower than the pooled quadratic says.

Not modelled:
- Give-up counts. v3/v4 showed no real gain.
- A post-compliance switch-off. It can be worth at most ~1-2 (3).
- A free-rider surcharge. It is neutral (6), and v1's exponent found the same.
- Late maintenance, ratchets, relative or rising targets, and per-round
  multipliers. v3's screens found all of them neutral or harmful, and finding 3
  says late fines only repay themselves.
- Timeouts. They are exogenous to the rule (the valid count is identical across
  every final), and a timed-out player cannot be punished.

I expect a tie with v4 (~649-650), not a gain. Four versions and the real data
show this family's ceiling, and no lever outside it.
