# pf-20261112-6-v2

## Explorations

1. **What did version 1's sweep find?** Read plots/simulation/policy_finder/pf-20261112-6_sweep (sweep.json,
   landscapes.json, final/). The surface's peak is thr 0.5, k 0.18, t_start 0, w 4: k*thr = 0.09 floors to 0,
   so the final is the `zero` policy. Its final score 16.76 (se 1.42, 2000 games, 4.63 members) is the bar;
   the reference `zero` vs `ah` gave 13.97 (se 1.44), so part of the 16.76 is seed luck. In the sweep the 41
   zero-equivalent points average 14.80, the 215 punishing ones 11.93; the best punishing points (19.6,
   19.5, 19.0, se ~2.8 each) are what the maximum of 215 noisy draws looks like. So v1 says: in its family
   every kind of punishment costs. To beat 16.76 I need a lever that pays, not a re-tuning of v1.
2. **Where does the cost of punishment come from, by phase?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-6/
   v1_sweep_points.py (per design point: punishment summed per game by phase: first round of a block b0,
   second b1, last two b23, rounds 20-23 late; and punishment of members contributing 5+ `on_coop`;
   regressed the point's score, members, own and other pools on them; the parquets must be read as arrays,
   the container has a 4 GB limit, so load_sweep.py reads them without pandas sorting). Per point of
   punishment summed over a game, which costs 1/24 = 0.042 of the round-averaged pool directly: b0 -0.012,
   b1 -0.011, b23 -0.065, late -0.033, plus -0.014 extra when it hits a 5+ contributor. Members: b23 -0.0038
   per point, b0/b1 ~-0.001. So first-of-block punishment recovers about 70% of its cost (via contributions)
   and still loses; punishment near a decision costs about 1.5x its direct cost (via members going to ah,
   `other` +0.027/pt); late punishment recovers only ~20%. Nothing in v1's family has a positive slope.
3. **Is a single punishment worth what it costs, member by member?** The v1 sweep holds a natural
   experiment: before a point's first punishable round its group is run exactly like `zero`, so at that round
   groups that start punishing and groups that start later are exchangeable. scripts/policy_finder/
   pf-20261112-6/first_round_effect.py, first_round_matched.py (matched on exact contribution) and
   single_pulse.py (w=1 points: one pulse at a block start, nothing for three rounds). Next-round response of
   the punished member: a 0-contributor gains -0.08 at p=1, -0.03 at 4-7, +0.43 at 8-30; a 1-4 contributor
   +0.03 / +0.17 / +0.97; a 5-9 contributor +0.07 / +0.55 / +1.13; a 10-14 contributor +0.43 (se 0.08) at
   p=1. The single pulse keeps its effect over three rounds (p 8+: +0.86, +0.69, +0.67), raises leaving at the
   block's decision by +0.07, and costs the group 2-5 pool points per round over the block. So roughly 0.05-0.1
   contribution per punishment point per round: it would take 6-12 rounds of full persistence to repay the
   point, before counting members.
4. **What does punishment do to the members who are not punished?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-6/
   spillover.py: unpunished members of a group that just started punishing, matched on exact contribution
   against control groups. All of them cut their next contribution: 0-4 contributors -0.23 to -0.55,
   10-14 -0.28 to -0.68, 15-20 contributors -0.75 (group punished 1-4 points in total) to -1.35 (31+). The
   artificial contributors read a punished group as a worse group (its common good drops), and the best
   contributors react most. This spillover alone outweighs the punished member's gain at every size. I also
   checked timeouts (scripts inline, /tmp arrays): 1.7% of player-rounds, unmoved by punishment (1.64% after
   0, 1.66% after 11-30), and a timed-out player's punishment is charged 0 in the environment, so no lever
   there. So far every channel says punishment loses in this simulation; next I want the long-run view: does
   punishment at round 0, with the longest horizon to persist, pay at the point level?
5. **Is there any round group where punishment pays at the point level?** scripts/policy_finder/
   pf-20261112-6/point_regression_rounds.py: the v1 sweep's lead per point regressed on punishment of
   0-4 contributors summed per game by round group, plus punishment of 5+ contributors (direct cost 0.042 per
   point). Rounds 1-3: -0.13 (se 0.07); mid-block rounds: -0.031 (0.012); decision rounds 7/11/15/19: -0.097
   (0.026); rounds 20-23: -0.038 (0.010); any punishment of a 5+ contributor: -0.046 (0.011); round 0: +0.10
   (0.28, almost never used, untested). The single exception is the first round of blocks 2-5 (rounds 4, 8,
   12, 16): +0.004 (0.015), i.e. there punishment of low contributors repays its direct cost and no more.
   Cross-check by design point: points with k*thr >= 1 average 9.4-14.4 in every (w, t_start) cell, the 31
   zero-equivalent ones 15.1 (sd 2.8). So the evidence keeps pointing at `zero`; the only place left where a
   positive effect is not excluded (upper end of the interval about +0.03 per point) is low contributors at a
   block start, far from the decision and with the most rounds left for the effect to persist; and
   punishment of transient dips is wasted, since unpunished 0-contributors already rise by +2.2 next round on
   their own (v1_response.py), so a persistent free rider is the better target.
6. **Drafted the v2 rule.** I drop everything v1 showed to lose (late rounds, decision and mid-block rounds,
   any punishment of contributors above a low threshold) and keep one narrow probe of the remaining niche,
   with `zero` nested twice so the sweep can confirm it: punish a flat `amt` points, only at block starts
   t = 0, 4, 8, 12, 16 with t < 4*`n_blocks` (n_blocks = 0: never), only members whose contribution has been
   <= `thr` in each of the last `streak` rounds (persistent free riders, not dips that revert anyway), and
   only valid members. Flat rather than proportional because the response at low contributions is about
   the size of the hit, not the shortfall, and a flat amount is easier to read.
7. **Checked the draft and its design.** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-6/design_check_v2.py: a toy
   history (one permanent 0-contributor, one 0 only this round, one 10-contributor) gives the intended
   punishments (block starts only, streak respected, nothing at 3 or 20). My first draft made n_blocks = 1
   with streak >= 2 silently zero (no history at round 0), which would have added ~27 more zero points; I
   changed the streak to count only the available rounds at round 0, so n_blocks = 1 is a real round-0 probe.
   The design now has 60 of 256 zero-equivalent points (n_blocks 0 or amt < 1), about 40 per n_blocks value
   1-5, about 85 per streak. validate-rule passes with 256 distinct points. I accept that, if my reading is
   right, the sweep's peak will be the zero corner again; this design makes that a clear answer, and gives the
   one untested niche (round 0, and low contributors at early block starts) enough points to show a gain if
   there is one.

## Key findings

1. Version 1's sweep peak and final are the `zero` policy (k*thr = 0.09 floors to 0); its 16.76 (se 1.42) is
   above the reference zero-vs-ah 13.97 (se 1.44) by seed luck, and in the sweep the zero-equivalent points
   average 14.8-15.1 against 11.9 for punishing ones (explorations 1, 5).
2. In the natural experiment of a group's first punishment round, the punished member's next contribution
   rises only 0.05-0.1 per punishment point (0-contributor +0.43 at 8-30 points, 5-9 contributor +0.55 at
   4-7), the effect persists over three rounds, and leaving rises +0.07 (exploration 3).
3. Unpunished members of a group that just punished cut their next contribution, by -0.23 (low contributors,
   1-4 points dealt) to -1.35 (15-20 contributors, 31+ points dealt): punishment's spillover is negative
   and largest on the best contributors (exploration 4).
4. Per point of punishment per game (direct cost 0.042), the lead moves -0.13 in rounds 1-3, -0.031
   mid-block, -0.097 at decision rounds, -0.038 in rounds 20-23, -0.046 extra on 5+ contributors; only low
   contributors at the block starts 4/8/12/16 break even (+0.004, se 0.015), and round 0 is untested
   (exploration 5).
5. Unpunished 0-contributors rise +2.2 by themselves the next round, so punishing one-round dips is wasted
   (exploration 5, from v1_response.py); timeouts (1.7%) do not respond to punishment and a timed-out
   player's punishment is charged 0 (exploration 4).

## Hypothesis

In this simulation a good manager is essentially a non-punisher. Every punishment point is lost from the
pool, lowers the group's common good, which makes the other members contribute less (most of all the best
contributors) and makes leaving at the next decision likelier, while the punished member gives back only
0.05-0.1 points per punishment point per round. The membership lead that `zero` earns against `ah`
(4.6 vs 3.4 members) is the whole score, and the manager keeps it by not spending the group's common good.
If any punishment pays, it is small, dealt to persistent free riders only (dips revert by themselves), at
the start of a block (the round farthest from the next switch decision, where it breaks even), and early,
when its effect has the most rounds left to persist. The rule: punishment = amt for valid members whose
contribution was <= thr in each of the last streak rounds, only in rounds t = 0, 4, 8, 12, 16 with
t < 4*n_blocks; nothing in any other round.
- `n_blocks` (int, 0-5): how many block starts from round 0 may punish; 0 is `zero`. I expect 0, or 1-2 if
  the early niche pays. The whole range, since 5 (every block start) is the only way to see that the
  effect does not decay with the horizon.
- `thr` (int, 0-8): free-riding threshold; I expect 0-3 (true free riders; punishing 5+ contributors is
  clearly negative), with 8 as the upper end so the sweep can see the slope turn.
- `streak` (int, 1-3): rounds of persistent free riding required; I expect 2-3 (skip dips that revert).
- `amt` (float, log 0.8-12): flat points per persistent free rider; I expect amt < 1 (no punishment) or 2-6;
  the response only reached +0.4 for a 0-contributor at 8+ points, so 12 bounds it, and the log scale
  puts more points at the small end, where the spillover is smallest.

### Justification

Finding 1 says the bar is a lucky `zero`, so a rule that does not nest `zero` would lose in expectation; the
v2 rule nests it twice (n_blocks = 0, amt < 1; 60 of 256 points) so the sweep can confirm it. Findings 2 and
3 together are why I expect zero to stay best: the punished member's gain (0.05-0.1 per point per round) is
smaller than the spillover loss on the others, and both come on top of the direct cost. Finding 4 is why the
rule is confined to block starts (the only round group not significantly negative) and why round 0 is
included (untested, longest horizon); finding 4's -0.046 on 5+ contributors is why `thr` stops at 8; finding
5 is why `streak` exists. What would contradict the hypothesis: a sweep peak with n_blocks >= 1 and amt >= 2
that holds up on fresh seeds, which would mean early, targeted punishment of persistent free riders repays
its spillover. Not modelled: punishing cooperators with token amounts (the +0.43 for a 10-14 contributor at
1 point is from 1,569 cases and outweighed by the spillover of finding 3), expelling free riders at decision
rounds (decision-round punishment is the most negative, -0.097 per point, and expelled players contribute in
`ah`'s group), and conditioning on group sizes or on the other group (I found no channel through which a
punisher could raise its group's attractiveness, which is what membership responds to).
