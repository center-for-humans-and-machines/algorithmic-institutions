## Explorations

1. **Where do versions 1 and 2 leave me?** Read both notes and both sweeps (sweep.json, landscapes.json, final/sweep.json). Both final points are the zero manager in disguise (v1: k 0.1 floors to 0 at t_start 22; v2: bar -1) and both scored exactly 16.757 (se 1.42), i.e. the final run uses the same seed (1000) and the zero manager replays the same games bit for bit. In v2's sweep the 96 zero-equivalent points average 14.68 with sd 2.46 across points (different seeds per point), and the 160 punishing points 13.32: own pool 59.3 vs 60.3, ah's pool 46.0 vs 45.6, members 4.45 vs 4.59. So 16.76 is the zero manager on lucky seeds and the true zero is ~14.7-15. What follows from the shared final seed: a rule that equals zero except in rare situations should replay most final games identically (the samplers draw fixed-shape random tensors), so its final score would be 16.76 plus the effect of its deviations only. To beat the bar I need a deviation with a positive true effect, and the rule must nest zero exactly. Next: look for a lever the earlier versions did not see, starting with the opponent `ah` itself.

2. **What does `ah` read, and can my punishment reach into ah's group?** Read src/aimanager/manager/linear_opponent.py and simulation/linear_ah.py and loaded the bundle artifacts/baselines/punishment_multinomial_timeout_severity_copula.joblib with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-1/v3_ah_prevp.py. ah is a multinomial logit on the player's own contribution, contribution==20, validity, previous contribution, **previous punishment** and the round, with a group-shared severity copula (rho 0.43). `prev_punishment` is the player's punishment in the previous round whoever dealt it, so a player who joins ah's group carries the punishment I dealt him in the decision round. Its effect is large: a zero giver in round 4 gets 2.7 from ah in expectation with prev_p 0, 9.5 with 10, 18.6 with 30 (P(no punishment) 0.57 -> 0.08 -> 0.00), a 15-giver 0.8 -> 4.2 -> 14.5. And it feeds itself (ah's punishment is the next round's prev_p): mean-field over rounds 4-7, a zero giver arriving with prev_p 30 collects ~53 from ah instead of ~18. So punishment I deal to a player who then leaves becomes a stream of ah punishment, which counts against ah's pool. This is a lever v2 used without knowing it (it punished free riders in decision rounds). Next: does the cascade show up in the sims?

3. **Does the cascade show in v2's sweep?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-1/v3_cascade.py (with a memory-light loader v3_load.py; the full parquet does not fit in the 4 GB here): every player who left the rule's group at decisions 3-15, by contribution and by the punishment I dealt him in the decision round. Zero givers: ah charges them 8.3 over the next 4 rounds if unpunished, 11.2 / 18.5 / 25.8 / 32.9 after 1-5 / 6-12 / 13-20 / 21-30 points from me; about one point of ah punishment per point I dealt. Their contributions in ah's group over those rounds are lower too (4.8 unpunished vs 2.6-3.0), for higher givers (6-20) 11.9 vs ~6 (but punished movers are selected differently from voluntary ones). The share coming back to my group at the next decision barely moves (0.30 -> 0.35). Yet in aggregate (entry 1) v2's punishing points did not lower ah's pool (46.0 vs 45.6) because my group lost members (4.45 vs 4.59): pushing free riders out also pushed bystanders out. So the transfer is real but roughly 1:1 per mover, times the chance he moves, which is ~0.3-0.5; a lever that pays only when I hit players who are leaving anyway, and without lowering my group's share in the round members decide. Next: can I see who is about to leave?

4. **Who leaves a group that never punishes?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-1/v3_leavers.py on the rule's group at v2's 96 zero-equivalent points (1.1M decision rows). Leaving is 22.7% overall (27% at the first decision), and the strongest predictor I can see from inside the rule is the group's size: 46% leave a group of 1, 39% of 2-3, 20% of 6, 15% of 8. Own contribution matters little (zero givers 27%, 20-givers 18%), relative contribution and last-round changes hardly at all; ah's punishment in its own group keeps players with me (-0.11 logit per point per member-round). A logit on all of it ranks rows into a top 10% that leaves at ~0.6, the rest at 0.13-0.3. So I cannot pick out players who are "leaving anyway" with the near-certainty the cascade would need (at ~1 point of ah punishment per point I deal, it pays only if q*(1 + their lost contribution) > 1). Next: measure the push's net effect directly, per decision, from v2's exogenous variation.

5. **Does a decision-round push pay, net, in v2's games?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-1/v3_push_effect.py: one row per episode and decision of v2's sweep (all 256 points), OLS of the lead (my pool minus ah's, per round) over the rest of the game on the punishment T I dealt in the decision round, controlling for both sizes, the counts of members with window means <= 1, 3, 5, both groups' window contributions, ah's window punishment and the lead so far (the latter with T added back; my first try put the already-punished window lead in the controls, which absorbed T's own cost and gave a spurious +0.1 to +0.2 per point). So the cascade into ah's group is real but never repays the point, at any decision or size. 6. **Does punishment pay in some group size, outside decision rounds?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-1/v3_window_effect.py on v1's sweep, per episode and window, the lead from the window on (my own punishments added back) on my in-window punishment split by my group's size at the window's start. 7. **The push by my group's exact size.** Same rows as entry 5 (now also own and ah's pools after the decision, members at the end), scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-1/v3_full_group.py, with the decision-round punishment T in bins (0, 1-9, 10-30, 31-60, 61+) per size. 8. **Hypothesis and draft.** The zero manager stays the backbone (it is what wins the members, findings of v1/v2), plus one exception: at a switch decision (rounds 7, 11, 15, optionally 19), only when my group holds at least `n_full` members (7 or 8; 9 means never, which nests the zero manager in a third of the design), punish the `n_push` members with the lowest mean contribution in the current window, if that mean is at most `bar`, with `amount` points each, so that they move to ah's (near-)empty group and become ah's liability there. Parameters: `n_full` int [7, 9]; `bar` int [0, 6] (data mostly from bars 0-5; I expect 0-3 best, a higher bar starts pushing contributors whose loss costs my pool); `amount` int [5, 30] (best measured bin 10-30, mean 20; ah's cascade grows with the dose, the target's leaving too); `n_push` int [1, 3] (one pushed is best measured, two still positive at 8, three or more negative); `last_dec` int [1, 4], the last decision (after round 3+4*last_dec) with a push, since the return needs rounds left to accumulate (decision 19 measured negative, so I expect 2-3).

## Key findings

1. Versions 1 and 2 both ended at the zero manager, and both final runs scored exactly 16.757 (se 1.42): the final seed is shared and replays the zero manager's games bit for bit, while across sweep points zero averages 14.68 (sd 2.46), so 16.76 is zero on lucky seeds and a rule that deviates from zero only rarely changes only those games (exploration 1).
2. ah punishes on the player's previous-round punishment whoever dealt it: a zero giver arriving in ah's group with prev_punishment 0 / 10 / 30 gets 2.7 / 9.5 / 18.6 from ah in expectation, and this feeds itself round after round (~53 vs ~18 over a window, mean-field) (exploration 2); in v2's games ah charged pushed movers ~1 point per point I dealt (8.3 -> 32.9 over 4 rounds at 21-30 points) (exploration 3).
3. In general a decision-round push does not pay: per point dealt, the later lead moves -0.033 to -0.064 per round at decisions 7-15 and +0.025 at 19, and each point costs 0.015-0.018 members, because bystanders leave with the pushed (exploration 5); leaving is driven mostly by group size (46% leave a group of 1, 15% a group of 8), so leavers cannot be picked out in advance (exploration 4).
4. The exception is a full group: with my group at all 8 members (ah's group empty), one push of ~20 points at decisions 7-15 raises the lead summed over the rest of the game by +41.3 (se 10.9; my pool +19.6, ah's -21.7), about +21 net of the points dealt; at 7 members +25.3 (se 11.1, about break-even), at 6 -3.3, at 5 -26.9; two pushes at 8: +26.2 (15.7), three or more negative; at decision 19 the return is about half the cost; members at the end do not fall (5.61 vs 5.53) (exploration 7).
5. Under the zero manager my group holds all 8 at 13-18% of decisions, and a low-bar push fires in ~4.5% of games per decision, so the lever is rare and worth ~+0.1 to +0.3 on the score (explorations 7, 9).
6. Regressions that condition on group sizes produced after the punishment mislead (+2.5 to +3.7 per point in small groups, an artifact of decision-19 pushes) (exploration 6).

## Hypothesis

On this stack the members are the score, and punishing my own contributors only drives them to ah's group, so the backbone stays the zero manager. The one punishment that pays is a push into an empty opponent group: when my group holds (nearly) all eight players, a free rider pushed out at a switch decision lands alone in ah's group carrying my punishment as his previous punishment, ah punishes him there round after round, so ah's group pool sinks to zero or below and its share deters anyone from following him, while my group loses only a non-contributor (who tends to come back). The rule: never punish, except in the decision rounds 7, 11, 15 (and 19 if `last_dec` allows), when my group has at least `n_full` members, deal `amount` points to each of the `n_push` lowest window-mean contributors whose mean over the current window is at most `bar`. Parameters and ranges: `n_full` (int, [7, 9]; 9 = never punish, the zero manager), `bar` (int, [0, 6]), `amount` (int, [5, 30]), `n_push` (int, [1, 3]), `last_dec` (int, [1, 4]).

### Justification

Finding 1 makes zero the floor to keep, so the rule is zero in all games but those with a full group (finding 5), and `n_full` = 9 nests zero in a third of the design. Finding 4 sets the trigger: positive at 8 members, about even at 7, negative below, hence `n_full` in [7, 9]; I expect 8. Finding 2 is the mechanism, and its dose-response (ah's punishment and its persistence grow with prev_punishment) argues for a large `amount`; finding 4's best bin is 10-30 (mean 20) and the target's own leaving also rises with the dose, so the range is [5, 30] and I expect 15-30. Finding 4 says one push is best and three or more lose (a group of several in ah's group starts to function and bystanders follow), hence `n_push` in [1, 3], expected 1. `bar` decides who is a free rider; the evidence comes from bars 0-5, and pushing a real contributor costs my pool his contributions, so [0, 6], expected 0-3. `last_dec` follows from the return needing rounds to accumulate (decision 19 returns about half the cost, finding 4): [1, 4], expected 2-3. What would contradict it: a sweep peak at `n_full` 9 (the push does not pay in the zero manager's own games, whose full groups may differ from v2's mixed ones), or a peak at 3 pushes or at 7 members (then the bystander channel is weaker than I measured). Not modelled: pushes when ah's group is small but not empty beyond the 7-member case (measured negative at 6), punishment of contributors anywhere (findings 3 and v1/v2), the first decision (groups start at 4, never full), reacting to ah's group's contributions or punishments (I could not size a conditional effect beyond group size from the data), and pushes outside decision rounds (a pushed player must move at the next decision for the carried punishment to reach ah).
