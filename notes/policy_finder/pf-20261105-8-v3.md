## Key findings

1. v2's family is saturated: its landscape peaks inside every range and its re-checked top
   points all score 647.5-651.6 (final 651.0); the bar for v3 is 651.0 (exploration 1).
2. The empirical surrogate generalises poorly across rule families (built on v1, scored on
   v2: corr 0.90 but bias -35; built on v2, scored on v1: corr 0.22), so I used it only for
   small departures from v2 and leaned on model-free regressions over the 512 true sweep
   rules (exploration 2).
3. The clones' response to a punishment is hump-shaped in the dose, with the hump lower the
   higher the contribution: free riders want 13-30 points (1-3 points is worse than none,
   group -2 per point), 8-12 contributors 4-12, 12-16 contributors 1-3, 16+ none at all
   (others' mean drops 0.3-1.8 per point) (explorations 3, 5, 6). v2's convex curve
   already matches this profile.
4. The break-even round of a punishment point depends on whom it hits: mid and
   near-standard contributors stop paying at about round 9 (net per point at rounds 9-13:
   -0.6 for 8-12, +0.1 for 12-16), free riders pay until round ~14-18 (+3.9 at rounds 9-13,
   +0.9 at 14-18), 3-7 contributors until ~13 (+1.0 at 9-13) (exploration 6).
5. A short late tail for free riders only (floor 3-5, to round ~14) is the one change that
   gained on the surrogate (+1.4 over v2's final, which the surrogate scores at 642.7),
   holding contributions after round 9 at ~16 instead of 15.25 for ~0.2 points per player
   per round; longer or wider tails lose (exploration 7).
6. Late punishment pays on players who have just dropped (net +2.1 per point if never
   punished before) and is neutral on chronic free riders (0.0 after 41+ earlier
   points); after v2's horizon most low contributors are new droppers (exploration 8).

## Hypothesis

A good manager spends heavily early and stops in stages. In the opening rounds it
punishes everyone below a high standard with a dose convex in the shortfall (the maximum
for a free rider, a light touch just below the standard, nothing at 16+), because the
clones' response is hump-shaped and persistent and an early point repays several times
over. Around round 9 the touches on mid and near-standard contributors stop repaying, but
a free rider (mostly a cooperator who has just collapsed) still lifts himself and the
group enough to repay a large punishment for another 4-8 rounds; so after the main
horizon the manager keeps punishing only players below a low floor, for a short tail,
and then stops entirely. The rule: punishment = dose * (max(standard - c, 0) /
standard)^curvature for valid players in rounds t < horizon, the same for players with
c < floor in rounds horizon <= t < horizon + tail, otherwise 0.
- `standard` (float, [14, 24]): contributions below it are punished in the main phase;
  expected best 18-21.
- `dose` (float, [22, 40]): the punishment of a zero contributor (clamped at 30);
  expected best 28-36.
- `curvature` (float, [1.2, 3.6], log): concentration of the punishment on free riders;
  expected best 1.8-2.6.
- `horizon` (int, 5..13): end of the main phase; expected best 8-10.
- `floor` (float, [1, 10]): in the tail only players below it are punished; expected best
  3-7.
- `tail` (int, 0..12): length of the tail phase (0 = v2); expected best 4-8.

### Justification

Finding 1 says v2's shape has no more to give, so v3 keeps it (standard, dose, curvature,
horizon, with ranges narrowed around v2's interior peak) and adds one structural change.
Finding 3 grounds the convex curve and why it is not the place to change: v2's curve
already gives each contribution class a dose near the top of its hump. Finding 4 is the
reason for the change: a single horizon must stop at the mid contributors' break-even
(round ~9), which forgoes the free riders' returns for another 5-9 rounds; `floor` and
`tail` let the punishment for free riders outlive the rest, and their expected values
come from the classes and rounds where finding 4's net returns turn negative (floor
between the 3-7 and 8-12 classes, tail to round ~14-18). Finding 6 says the tail mostly
pays because it hits new droppers, and finding 5 that a short, low tail is what the
surrogate prefers; `tail` 0 is inside the range, so the sweep can tell whether the tail
helps at all. The gain I expect is modest (a few points; the surrogate shows +1.4 and
understates persistence). What would contradict this: a sweep in which tail and floor
have no effect, or the best tail is 0-1, or the best floor is at 1 (the tail does not
pay), or a best floor near 10 with a long tail (then the issue is the horizon itself, not
whom it covers). Not modelled: forgiveness of chronic free riders in the tail (neutral by
finding 6, not worth a parameter), a separate round-0 standard (the surrogate and the
regressions disagreed, exploration 4), group-relative standards, escalation and credit
for rising (no gain in v2's tests), and timeouts beyond excluding them.
