# pf-20261106-9-v2

## Explorations

1. **How did version 1 do, and against whom?** Read v1's notes, rule, `landscapes.json` and
   `final/sweep.json`, and tabulated the final run per opponent and round with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-9/v2_explore1.py. v1's final (k=1.95, s0=0.71, quiet=2,
   t_end=14, w=0.46) scored 65.6 (68.3 / 67.3 / 70.2 / 56.8 against opponents 1-4). The
   surface: k wants to be large (at the upper edge 2.0; 58.1 at k=0.05 -> 62.6 at k=2), s0
   small (62.5 at 0 -> 59.0 at 8), quiet=2 best (60.5 at 0), t_end and w do nothing (effects
   0.0001 and 0.02). So my v1 prior "punish little" was wrong for the artificial humans:
   targeted, harsh-ish punishment in the first two rounds of each block pays. Opponents: 1 punishes
   hard early (10.8 per head in round 0) and decays to 0 by round 15; 2 punishes ~2-4 per head
   steadily; 3 punishes ~10 in rounds 0-1 and in rounds 0-1 of each block, nothing in rounds 2-3
   (a block-timed style like mine); 4 never punishes. Group sizes barely move (3.4-4.6 on
   average): members matter less in the AH than in the human data. Against opponent 4 we stay
   ~4 members with contributions ~9.5 vs 8.5, but our punishment there is only ~2.5 per head
   (w had nothing to scale), and our score there is the weakest (56.8). Our contributions climb
   8.8 -> 12.5-13.4 against opponents 1-3, only 8.8 -> 9.5 against 4. Next: from the sweep's
   per-round data (256 points x 4 opponents x 128 games), how the AH contribution responds to
   our punishment, how long it lasts, and what drives switching.

2. **How does the AH contribution respond to our punishment?** Converted v1's sweep per-round
   files to arrays (scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-9/v2_load.py) and looked at our members
   who stay in the group, with v2_explore2.py and v2_explore3.py (9.6M player-rounds). Raw
   means suggest a big first-point jump (zero contributors +1.5 unpunished vs +3.2 after 1-2
   points), but that is selection: holding own c, rounded group mean, round and previous
   punishment fixed (cells where the sweep's parameters make punishment vary), the response is
   roughly linear: per punishment point next-round contribution rises ~0.08-0.10 (c 0-4: +0.14 at
   2 points, +0.54 at 8-12, +2.1 at 21-30; c 10-14: +0.31 at 2, +1.30 at 8-12). High contributors
   (15-20) respond too (+0.18 at 1 point, +0.39 at 2) but saturate at ~+0.6 beyond 5 points:
   unlike the humans (v1 entry 2), punished AH high contributors do not collapse. The effect
   persists and even grows: for low contributors the coefficient of p[t] on c[t+h] is 0.086 at
   h=1, 0.11 at h=4, 0.14 at h=8 (part of that may be repeat punishment). And unpunished members
   rise when their groupmates are punished (+0.21 when others got 3-6 on average, +0.76 at >10):
   a spillover. Rough budget: one point early costs 1 pool point once and buys ~0.1 x 1.6 = 0.16
   pool points per later round for ~20 rounds, i.e. ~3 points, plus spillover; late, with few
   rounds left, it cannot repay. So front-loaded punishment pays in the AH. Next: what it costs
   in members (switching).

3. **What does punishment cost in members in the AH?** Switch decisions of players who spent
   the whole block on one side, with v2_explore4.py. 25% leave our group per decision overall;
   by own block punishment: 25% (0), 26% (4-10), 30% (10-20), 35% (20-40), 45% (>40). Pool per
   head at the decision round is the strongest driver: 51% leave when ours is 8-20 lower than
   the other group's, 14% when it is 8-20 higher. Linear probability: +0.0034 per own block
   punishment point, +0.0049 per point in the decision round, +0.014 per point of our per-head
   punishment above theirs, -0.18 per 20 points of per-head pool lead, -0.029 per member we
   already have. So the member cost of punishment is modest and mostly about the decision
   round itself (it lowers the pool per head the switchers compare): quiet rounds before a
   decision stay, and a higher-contributing group attracts players back. Next: which amount of
   punishment, and when, went with the best pool across v1's design points.

4. **How much punishment, and when?** Per design point and opponent, our punishment per head by
   phase vs our pool, with v2_explore5.py, and v1's effective scale k*(1+w*op) (op read at
   round 8) for points with s0<3 and quiet 1-2, with v2_explore7.py. Most of v1's design was
   very mild (half the points punished <0.1 per head per round). Pool rises with early
   punishment all the way up: OLS over all points, +1.6 per unit of per-head punishment in
   rounds 0-1 and +4.4 in rounds 2-7, but -0.4 in rounds 8-15 and -1.1 in 16-23. By effective
   scale (pool vs opponents 1/2/3): 59.8/59.7/62.5 at 0.1-0.3, 63.5/63.9/67.7 at 1-2,
   65.2/64.5/67.0 at 2-4, 67.5/68.5/69.3 at 4-8, then 57.3/-/66.1 at 8-30 (1-2 points, noisy;
   members fall to 3.5-3.8). Late contributions follow: 8.5 -> 11.4 against opponent 1. Against
   opponent 4 (never punishes) v1's scale never exceeded 2 (w had nothing to multiply), which
   is why it scored only 51-52 there in the sweep and 56.8 in the final. So the best scale is
   ~4-8 points per point of shortfall, independent of the opponent: v1's w was a detour that
   happened to supply that harshness only against harsh opponents.

5. **What do the opponents' own groups show?** Their groups' per-round outcome over all of v1's
   sweep, with v2_explore6.py. Opponent 3 punishes 29-30 to contributors <=4, 17 to 4-8, ~0
   above 12, only in rounds 0-1 of each block and nothing from round 16; opponent 1 is similar
   (22-25 to <=4) but decays; opponent 2 punishes everyone mildly (8 to zero contributors, 1 to
   full ones) all game; 4 never. Their groups' mean pools: 59.2, 46.9, 62.9, 50.8. Opponent 2's
   untargeted mild punishment is worst (contributions 8.7 -> 8.1, members 3.4), and the
   no-punishment group decays 8.7 -> 6.8. The harsh, targeted, block-timed styles win, and v1's
   final with its mean-relative target pushed contributions further (13.4 vs 10 against opponent
   3): a target that moves with the group mean keeps lifting the bottom as the group improves.
   So v2: drop w, make k's range reach well past 8, keep the mean-relative shortfall, quiet and
   t_end, and add a per-player cap so the sweep can also find opponent-3-like flat maximal
   punishment of anyone below the target.

6. **Drafted v2.** Five parameters: punishment = min(cap, k * max(0, group mean - c_i - s0)) for
   valid members, nobody in the `quiet` rounds before each decision (rounds < 20), nobody from
   round `t_end` on. Changes from v1: w is gone (entry 4: the good scale is ~4-8 whatever the
   opponent, and w left us soft against the non-punisher, our weakest pairing); k's range moves
   to [0.5, 30] log so that 4-8 sits in the middle rather than past the edge; s0 may go
   negative (entry 2: AH high contributors respond positively to punishment, so punishing
   at-mean members may pay; v1's s0 profile was flat from 0 to 1.6, on its lower edge); a
   per-member `cap` is new (entry 5: the best opponents deal a flat near-maximum to free riders;
   entry 3: leaving rises with own punishment above ~20 per block). quiet and t_end are kept:
   quiet=2 was v1's clear best (entry 3: the decision-round pool drives switching), and t_end,
   flat for v1's mild scales, should matter once punishment is harsh (entry 4: rounds 16-23
   punishment went with -1.1 pool per unit). Checked on a hand-made history with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-9/test_rule_v2.py (mean 7.5, k=5, s0=1, cap=20: 20 to a
   zero contributor, 7.5 to a 5, none in rounds 2-3 with quiet=2 or from t_end=16), and
   validate-rule passes on all 256 design points.

## Key findings

1. In the AH, punishment raises the punished member's next contribution by ~0.08-0.10 per
   point at all contribution levels, holding own contribution, group mean and round fixed (low
   contributors +2.1 after 21-30 points; high contributors +0.4 at 2 points, saturating ~+0.6),
   and the effect persists (coefficient 0.086 at one round, 0.14 at eight); unpunished members
   also rise when groupmates are punished (+0.76 at >10 per head) (exploration 2).
2. The member cost is modest: leaving our group goes from 25% (no own punishment in the block)
   to 35% (20-40 points); the per-head pool gap at the decision round dominates (-0.18 leaving
   probability per 20 points of lead) (exploration 3).
3. Across v1's design, pool rose with the effective punishment scale up to 4-8 points per point
   of shortfall (67.5/68.5/69.3 against opponents 1-3 vs ~60-62 at 0.1-0.3); v1 reached that
   scale only through w against harsh opponents and stayed at <=2 against the non-punisher,
   its weakest pairing (56.8 in the final) (explorations 1, 4).
4. Early punishment pays and late does not: +1.6 and +4.4 pool per unit per-head punishment in
   rounds 0-1 and 2-7, -0.4 and -1.1 in rounds 8-15 and 16-23 (exploration 4).
5. Among the opponents' groups, harsh targeted block-timed punishment (opponent 3: 29-30 to
   contributors <=4, rounds 0-1 of each block) did best (62.9), mild untargeted punishment worst
   (46.9), none in between (50.8, contributions decaying 8.7 -> 6.8); v1's mean-relative target
   lifted contributions further (13.4 vs 10 against opponent 3) (explorations 1, 5).

## Hypothesis

In this AH population, punishment is an investment that pays early: each point buys a lasting
rise in the punished member's contribution and a spillover on groupmates, while the members it
drives away are few as long as the group's pool per head at the decision round stays high. A
good manager therefore punishes shortfalls from the group's own mean harshly (several points per
point of shortfall, up to near the maximum for clear free riders), so the target rises as the
group improves; stays quiet in the rounds before each switch decision; and stops when too few
rounds are left to repay the cost. This should hold against any other manager, since the
benefit comes from our own members' response, not from the comparison.

The rule: punishment = min(cap, k * max(0, mean_c - c_i - s0)), valid members only, zero in the
`quiet` rounds before each decision (rounds < 20) and from round `t_end` on.
- `k` (float, [0.5, 30], log): points per point of shortfall; I expect 3-10 (finding 3), the
  log range puts that in its middle and covers v1's mild regime and the near-flat maximum.
- `s0` (float, [-3, 5]): tolerance below the mean; I expect -1 to 2 (v1 peaked at 0.7 on a flat
  0-1.6 stretch; finding 1 says at-mean members may be worth a little punishment too).
- `cap` (float, [4, 30]): per-member maximum per round; I expect 15-30 (finding 1: low
  contributors keep responding up to 21-30; finding 2: leaving rises with heavy punishment).
- `quiet` (int, 0-3): silent rounds before each decision; I expect 1-2 (v1's best was 2).
- `t_end` (int, 4-24): first round with no punishment; I expect 12-20 (finding 4).

### Justification

Findings 1 and 3 say harsher punishment than v1 used pays, and 3 says the best scale (4-8) is
the same against opponents 1-3, so the scale `k` is a plain constant whose range now centres on
it, and v1's opponent-dependent w is dropped; that also fixes v1's weakest pairing against the
non-punisher. Finding 5 supports the mean-relative target and the cap (the flat maximal
punishment of opponent 3 is the corner of a high k and a cap). Finding 2 supports `quiet`
(the decision-round pool drives switching) and bounds how harsh `cap` should be; finding 4
supports `t_end`. It would be contradicted if the sweep's best k sat below 2 (the scale results
of v1 were confounded by which opponent supplied the harshness) or if the high-k points lost
members fast enough to lose pool (v1's 8-30 bin had 3.5 members, but only 1-2 points). I did not
model the other manager's behaviour (finding 3 showed the good scale does not depend on it),
the treatment of newcomers, or history-based targeting of repeat free riders: they add
dimensions to a 256-point sweep without evidence that the AH distinguishes them.
