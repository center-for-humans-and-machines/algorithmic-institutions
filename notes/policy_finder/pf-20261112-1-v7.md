## Key findings

1. v6's final point scored 17.70 on seed 1000 (+0.94 over the zero manager's 16.76 on the same games); over v6's sweep the paired gain is +0.97 per game (se 0.045), with gap_full best near 2-4 (quadratic peak 2.7) and gap_six slightly better low (6-12) than high, both effects small (exploration 1).
2. The gain comes mostly from the punished free rider leaving my group: at decision 7 the touched games where he left gain +6.5 per game, those where he stayed +0.6 (decision 11: +4.8 vs -0.4; 15: +2.6 vs -0.8); after he leaves ah's pool drops ~10 at once and stays 3-6 lower, and my group ends +0.28 members up (exploration 2).
3. Punishment raises the target's leave rate from 16% (twin) to 42% at dose 20, and higher doses raise it further (50% at 28-30), but the gain per touched game is flat in the dose (2.0-2.4 from 10 to 30), so the dose stays at 20 (exploration 3).
4. A second target pays when he is himself clearly below the group: in v4's randomized n_push, two targets vs one changes the lead by -2.00 (se 0.62) + 0.57 (se 0.19) per point of his gap, break-even near 3.5; +1.28 (se 0.63) at gaps >= 4, +2.32 (1.34) at >= 6; a third target behaves alike (break-even near 4, noisy) (exploration 4).
5. At v6's final thresholds a further target clearing gap_more 4-5 arises at decision 7 in 6-12% of games, so the new lever is worth roughly +0.1 to +0.2 on the score (exploration 6).

## Hypothesis

The zero manager's members-first backbone stays, and punishment is used only to push clear free riders out of a large group at a switch decision. A hard punishment there more than doubles the chance that the free rider leaves; he lands in ah's small group, where ah keeps punishing him on his past punishment and the group's contributions sag, while my cooperative members are rid of him, so my pool rises and ah's falls for the rest of the game. That is worth it only for a member far below the rest of a cooperative group, and the same holds for every such member, not only the lowest: each further clear free rider punished is another chance to export one. The rule: at decisions 7, 11 and 15, with my group at 7-8 members, punish by `amount` the lowest valid member (window mean at most `bar`) if his gap to the others' window mean is at least `gap_full`, and the second and third lowest if their gaps are also at least `gap_more`; with my group at 6, the same with `gap_six` in place of `gap_full`. Swept: `gap_full` (float, [0, 7], expected best 2-4), `gap_six` (float, [6, 18], expected best 8-12; near 18 means never), `gap_more` (float, [2, 14], expected best 4-6; near 14 means one target, i.e. v6). Fixed: `n_full` 7, `bar` 12, `amount` 20, `n_push` 3, `last_dec` 3.

### Justification

Finding 2 recasts the mechanism as exporting a free rider, and finding 4 follows from it: the randomized n_push comparison in v4 shows that the extra target loses only when he is not clearly below the group, so a gap threshold for further targets (`gap_more`) turns v5's "more targets lose" into a lever; its range [2, 14] runs from below the break-even (3.5-4) to "never", so the sweep can both find the best and fall back to v6. `gap_full` and `gap_six` keep v6's ranges where finding 1 places their best values inside (2.7 and 6-12), gap_six's top cut to 18 since 14-20 was flat and below. Finding 3 fixes the dose at 20, and v5/v6's findings fix the size floor (7, with 6 by gap_six), the bar (12) and the last decision (15). Expected result: v6's +0.97 sweep gain plus about +0.1 to +0.2, i.e. a final near 17.8-17.9 on the shared seed, within the noise of one final run (se ~0.37 on the paired difference). What would contradict it: a sweep best at gap_more near 14 (further targets do not pay once the first target is chosen by gap and a bar of 12 lets in members giving up to 12), or a fall of the paired gain below v6's at moderate gap_more. Not modelled: a dose that depends on the gap (the dose effect is flat), punishing over several rounds of a window to raise the leave rate (no randomized evidence, and each extra 20 points must buy about 9 points of leave probability), pushes at decision 19 or sizes 5 and below (negative at every gap in earlier versions), and a separate threshold for the third target (too few cases to set it apart from the second).
