4. **Is a free rider worth keeping? (herding and bad apples).** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-6/herding.py
   and bad_apple.py on the reference zero-vs-zero / zero-vs-ah sims. Herding is strong: next contribution
   = 0.68 own + 0.27 mates' mean (+0.03 other group's mean). Low contributors (<= 3 two rounds running) are

   give +0.5 more next block, but the rest-of-game lead still falls (-3.9 per round, se 2.2; -12.9 at round 3),
   because the leaver gives 3-4 in `ah`'s group and the group shrinks. So expelling is no lever, and the
   round-3 decision is pivotal (membership cascades).
5. **The block-start natural experiment, by block.** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-6/blockstart_effect.py:

9. **Drafted the v3 rule.** v2's family plus the one state variable that changes the sign: the own group's
   size. punishment = amt to each valid member who gave <= thr in each of the last streak rounds, only at
   block starts 4, 8, ..., 4*last_b (round 0 excluded: -0.138 per point in 3; 5 adds round 20), and only when

   last_b, and kept streak because of 8. No explicit zero corner: n_min = 8 with last_b = 1 punishes in a
   small minority of games and is close to zero, and zero has been measured twice already. n_min starts at 5

   design has 64 points per n_min, 50-52 per last_b, 85-86 per streak, 86 with amt < 4, 24-27 per thr.

   ~9 points dealt per qualifying block start, +0.1 lead per point, two early block starts: about +0.6 over
   zero; small against the sweep's se of ~2.85 per point, so the GP's pooling across points carries it.

## Key findings

1. The bar (v2's final 17.01) is `zero` + 0.25 on the same random streams (the peak always plays the first
   slot of seed 1000, where zero scored 16.76); zero's true value is ~14-15 (exploration 1).
2. Punishment's cost is membership: a point dealt in round 0 raises the group's contributions (+0.08-0.11 per
   round for three rounds) but costs 0.016 members at round 3 and -0.13 lead (exploration 3); each point at a
   later block start costs ~0.006 members (exploration 5); decision and mid-block rounds cost most (7).
3. Herding is strong (next c = 0.68 own + 0.27 mates' mean), low contributors persist in unpunished groups
   (16% -> 31% of players), but expelling them loses lead (-3.9 per round) because they contribute in the other
   group and the round-3 decision cascades (exploration 4).
4. The membership cost depends on the own group's size: at block starts 4/8 the rest-of-game lead per point is
   -0.137 (se 0.092) with <= 4 members and +0.105 (0.047) with >= 7, where members do not leave (-0.001 per point
   vs -0.017) and own contributions rise +0.105 per round per point; at block starts 12/16 even big groups only
   break even (-0.010, 0.018) (exploration 6). Groups of >= 7 are common: 35% of games at round 4 (6).
5. In big groups late rounds 20-22 also nearly break even (-0.011 per point vs -0.044 in small groups) (7).
6. Within big groups, persistent low contributors (streak 2-3: +0.093 vs +0.062), a real hit (amt >= 4: +0.099
   vs -0.067) and thr 4-8 (+0.086 vs +0.037) do better; noisy but same sign (exploration 8).

## Hypothesis

Membership is the score, and a punished member leaves when the other group is a real alternative. When the
other group has shrunk to 0-1 members (its common good, which the switch model reads, is tiny), punished
members stay, and then punishment of persistent low contributors raises their contributions, which herding
spreads to the rest of a large group over the remaining rounds; this pays most at the early block starts
(rounds 4, 8), far from the next decision and with the most rounds left. So a good manager never punishes
while the groups are contested, and punishes persistent free riders at early block starts only once its group
dominates. The rule: amt to each valid member who gave <= thr in each of the last streak rounds, at rounds 4,
8, ..., 4*last_b, when the own group has >= n_min members.
- `n_min` (int, [5, 8]): minimum own group size. I expect 7 (6-8); 5 lets the sweep see the cost return as the
  other group becomes a real option; 8 is the natural maximum.
- `last_b` (int, [1, 5]): last block start that may punish (5 adds round 20). I expect 2 (rounds 4 and 8),
  possibly 5 if late punishment in big groups pays (finding 5).
- `thr` (int, [1, 10]): low-contribution threshold. I expect 4-8; 10 to see whether hitting average givers
  turns negative.
- `streak` (int, [1, 3]): rounds of low giving required. I expect 2-3.
- `amt` (float, log [2, 16]): flat points per persistent low contributor. I expect 4-10; v2 tested up to 12, and
  with no exodus the only cost of a bigger hit is direct, so 16 bounds it.

### Justification

Findings 2 and 3 say every unconditional punishment loses through membership, which is why v1 and v2 ended at
or near zero (finding 1). Finding 4 is the new lever and supports `n_min` directly: the sign of the per-point
effect flips between small and big groups, with the member loss vanishing in big groups; it also supports
`last_b` low (4/8 pay, 12/16 do not), and finding 5 is why the range reaches round 20. Finding 6 supports
`streak`, `thr` in the middle of its range and `amt` >= 4. Round 0 is excluded by finding 2. What would
contradict it: a sweep whose surface is flat or falling in n_min (the size dependence was an artefact of
v2's selection of games), or whose peak sits at n_min = 8 with last_b = 1 and small amt, i.e. back at zero.
Not modelled: conditioning on the pool gap rather than size (size is the simpler, more robust proxy of the
other group's attractiveness; the two move together), mid-block punishment in big groups (v1 shows a
membership cost even there), and reacting to who just joined; each would add a dimension the 256 noisy points
cannot resolve.
