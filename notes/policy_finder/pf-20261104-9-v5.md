6. **The gap round by round.** v5_gap_rounds.py on v3's final run and on
   zero_vs_ah. A rule can see, before punishing, its own per-capita pool
   1.6 x mean c and estimate the other's as 1.6 x mean c minus its mean
   punishment last round. Under v3 that seen gap is about 0 in round 0
   and +3.5 to +5.7 in rounds 1-11. It falls to +1.3 by round 23, positive
   in only 50-70% of episodes. After v3's punishment the realised gap is

   the seen gap cannot know (no punishment history yet). So v3

   that my per-capita pool, after punishing, stays no more than

## Key findings

1. The threshold-and-linear-dose family is at its ceiling. v2-v4's finals
   are 71.9, 72.9 and 71.3, and the best rechecked points are all
   72-73. v4's convex dose was rejected (power peak 0.79), its `mid` went
   to the top edge 1, and `last_round` was flat over 4..14 (exploration
   1).
2. The sim's lift from punishment depends on where a member sits
   relative to its group, not on its absolute level. Laggards below
   0.8x gain +2 for 22-30 points, at-mean members nothing, and
   unpunished high contributors drift down (-1.9 over 3 rounds at
   18-21). Late contribution per member never exceeds 12.9 in any of
   the 1024 rules, so headcount is the margin left: 4.4-4.7 members late
   in the best rules against 4.9-5.1 under zero (explorations 2, 3).
3. Per point, the lift and the exit cost are balanced and not
   state-dependent in the ways I tried. Improvers and non-improvers,
   group sizes 2-6, and positions 0-2 all give a similar lift (+0.07-0.13
   per point) and exit cost (0.005-0.013 per point). Herding adds
   +0.010-0.017 per point to each unpunished member (exploration 4).
4. The gap between my group's per-capita pool and the other group's
   moves leaving fourfold: 0.49 when behind by 4+, 0.12 when ahead by 4+,
   with no punishment. It also sets the price of a punishment: 16-40
   points add +0.075 to leaving when my group leads by 4+, and +0.18-0.26
   when the groups are level or my group is behind (exploration 5).
5. v3 punishes blind to this. Its realised gap is -5.8 in round 0 (behind
   in 74% of episodes), -3.2 in round 4 and -2.5 in round 8. Its
   first exodus runs 0.64 / 0.44 / 0.31 / 0.18 across episodes whose
   round-0 seen gap is < -4 / -4..0 / 0..4 / > 4 (explorations 6, 7).

## Hypothesis

A good manager on this stack still sets its group's norm early: it
punishes laggards, relative to their own group, in proportion to their
shortfall, hardest right after a switch decision, never just before one,
and then stops. But it watches the other group. Players leave a group
whose per-capita pool is below the other group's, and a punished player
leaves far more readily when its group is not clearly the better one. So
the manager rations its punishment by the comparison. When its group
leads, the lift comes cheap and it punishes fully. When its group is
level or behind, it holds back, so that its own punishment does not
push the group's per-capita pool further below the other's just before
players compare them.

The rule: shortfall = max(0, `rel` x mean c of the other valid members
- c); dose = min(30, `slope` x shortfall) at positions 0 (rounds 0, 4,
8, ...), x `mid` at positions 1-2, 0 at position 3 and after
`last_round`. Budget = my members x max(0, seen gap - `margin`), where the
seen gap = 1.6 x my mean c - (1.6 x the other group's mean c - its mean
punishment last round). If the doses sum to more than the budget, all
are scaled by budget / sum.

- `rel` (float, [0.65, 1.0]): the laggard threshold as a fraction of
  the others' mean. I expect 0.78-0.9 (v3 0.84, v4 0.82).
- `slope` (float, [3, 30] log): fresh-round points per point of
  shortfall. I expect 7-14 (v3's best is 9.7). It may go higher if the
  budget does the limiting.
- `mid` (float, [0, 1.2]): the within-phase dose as a share of the
  fresh-round dose. I expect 0.3-0.9 (v3 0.37, v4 at its edge 1).
- `last_round` (int, 6..20): the last round with punishment. I expect
  9-14, or later if the budget makes late punishment pay.
- `margin` (float, [-20, 4]): how far behind the other group my
  per-capita pool may fall after my punishment (negative = behind). I
  expect -10 to -4. At -20 the budget is almost never binding, which is
  v3.

### Justification

Finding 1 is why v5 adds a new dimension rather than reshaping the
dose, and why the dose is v3's best shape (linear, with the clamp at
30) with ranges centred on v3's and v4's peaks. Findings 2 and 3 say
that the per-member margins are exhausted and that headcount is where the
remaining pool is. Finding 4 is the one state variable I found that
changes the price of a punishment, and by a factor of 2-3, which is
what `margin` acts on. Finding 5 says that v3 spends its heaviest
punishment exactly where that price is highest: round 0, in episodes
where my group starts behind. The seen gap uses only what the rule can
see. The other group's punishment this round is unknown, so last
round's stands in, and in round 0 it is taken as 0, which `margin`
absorbs (ah deals ~3.4 per member in round 0). The budget scales all
doses together, so that the targeting stays v3's.

What would contradict it: `margin` peaking at its low end (-20) would
mean the lift is worth most exactly in the groups that start behind,
since they have the most laggards, and that the exits are the price of
it. Finding 5's link between punishment and the first exodus would then
be mostly mechanical (low groups have more laggards) rather than the
switch model's comparison. A peak near 0 or above would mean the
comparison dominates, and that punishing while level is already too
dear.

Not modelled: a boost when my group leads by a lot (the dose already
saturates at 30 for deep laggards, and the fresh-round dose is near the
clamp), a phase-level rather than round-level budget (the decision
regression weighs the phase mean and the pre-decision round, and the
round-level budget is the simplest proxy), a prior for ah's round-0
punishment (`margin` absorbs it), and the dead ends of exploration 4
(improvement, group size). All my estimates are correlational, from
rules that chose whom to punish, which is why `margin`'s range reaches
the non-binding end.
