## Key findings

1. Four versions of the same family plateau at a true ~653-655. Finals were 648.2, 651.0,
   651.8 and 650.6, but the re-checked top points of v3 and v4 average 652.8 and reach
   656.2. The losses happened at the final step: v4's fitted peak landed in a corner
   (standard 17, horizon 10) and scored 650.6 (exploration 1).
2. One GP over v2-v4's 768 sweep points (residual sd 3.0, about the noise of one point)
   puts the family's optimum inside every range. That optimum is standard ~23, rate
   ~0.11, dose ~29, horizon 6-7 and end ~14-15, with the floor flat over 1-3. The fitted
   peak is 657, about 3 points above what re-checks find (exploration 2).
3. A separate round-0 standard adds nothing on the pooled surface (657.6 vs 657.7 with
   opening = standard). The floor moves the fit by less than 1 point over 1-3
   (exploration 4).
4. Under every good rule the losses are the same: 54-66 points from contributions below
   20 and 49-60 from punishment, ~70% of it in rounds 0-5. The good rules differ by only
   2-5 points, so no large untouched loss is left for a new element to reach
   (exploration 3).
5. Within a 5-d box set from the pooled surface, the fitted mean is 651.7 and the 10th
   percentile 646.2, against 641.7 and 629.8 in v4's 7-d box. Every profile through the
   peak falls 3-9 points toward each edge (exploration 4).

## Hypothesis

A good manager makes an expensive first impression and then stays cheap. For about the
first 7 rounds it punishes every player below a high standard (~23). The punishment
falls steeply with the contribution: about 28 for a free rider, a few points just below
the standard, nothing for full contributors. The clones' raise is persistent and spreads
to the group, so these early points repay several times over. After that, it punishes
only players who give (almost) nothing, until about round 14: their raise still repays a
full punishment, and lifting the group's lowest player slows the others' downward drift.
After round 14 it punishes nobody, because no raise has enough rounds left to repay.
After four versions, the structure is settled. What v5 changes is how reliably the sweep
lands on its plateau: fewer swept dimensions, with ranges centred on the optimum of all
earlier sweeps pooled. The rule: base = dose * (max(standard - c, 0) / standard)^(rate *
standard). Every valid player gets it in rounds t < horizon; only players with c < floor
get it in rounds horizon <= t < end; nobody gets it afterwards.
- `standard` (float, [18, 30]): the opening-phase cut-off. I expect 21-25.
- `rate` (float, [0.088, 0.15], log): steepness, the fraction of the dose lost per
  contribution point near zero. I expect 0.10-0.12.
- `dose` (float, [23, 36]): the punishment of a zero contributor, clamped at 30. I expect
  27-31.
- `horizon` (int, 4..10): end of the opening phase. I expect 6-8.
- `end` (int, 11..20): first round with no punishment. I expect 13-16.
- `floor` (fixed at 2): the late phase hits only the 0 and 1 contributors.

### Justification

Findings 1 and 4 say the family is saturated and that its weak point is the last step
(fitted peak, then re-check), not the shape. So I keep the shape and tighten the search.
Finding 2 places every range: each is centred on the pooled optimum, with edges 3-9
fitted points below it (finding 5). The design therefore samples the plateau about 4x
more densely per dimension than v4's did, and a fitted peak cannot end up in a poor
corner. Finding 3 is why the opening is dropped and the floor fixed: both cost a
dimension and gain less than 1 point. The structure itself rests on the earlier
versions' findings: a convex, hump-matched dose (v3 finding 3), early front-loading with
a stage-wise stop (v3 finding 4), and a free-rider-only tail because the late drift comes
from high contributors who must not be punished (v4 finding 2).

What would contradict this:
- a v5 landscape peaking at an edge, especially rate or horizon (the pooled surface
  mislocated the optimum);
- a final clearly below ~652 (the plateau is lower than the re-checks suggest, and the
  earlier top points were luck).

Not modelled:
- a separate round-0 standard and a swept floor (finding 3);
- relative standards, credit for rising and escalation (no gain in v1/v2's tests);
- a cap on repeated late hits (v4 finding 4: about 1 point at most);
- timeouts, beyond excluding timed-out players.
