## Key findings

1. v4's final (17.90, seed 1000) is `zero` + 1.14 on the same streams. Its peak hits 0-4 givers with 13 points at
   round 4 only. The v4 sweep's own surface is mostly noise (500 games per point, se 2.8), so the pooled natural
   experiment is the only precise guide (exploration 1).
2. At round 4 the payoff depends on whom the hit lands on. Hitting one 3-5 giver gains +1.89 (se 0.56) rest-of-game
   lead at 13 points and +2.33 (0.91) at 16. Hitting a 0-2 giver gains -0.26 (0.44), and a 6+ giver loses
   (explorations 2, 4, 5).
3. 3-5 givers raise their own giving by +1.7 per round for the rest of the game after 13 points and stay (members
   at round 8 -0.04 to +0.04). 0-2 givers raise theirs by only +0.2 to +0.7 and leave more (-0.07 to -0.12 members)
   (explorations 2, 4, 5).
4. The 3-5 dose curve still rises at 16, the largest dose either sweep tried, by about +0.15 lead per extra point
   (exploration 5).
5. The 3-5 effect needs no history condition (steady low givers +1.96, givers who dropped +1.81). It is larger
   when the target's mates give 10 or more (+3.71 vs +1.62), but positive either way (exploration 5).
6. Punishing at round 8 (after round 4's hit) is negative (-0.19 per point on 2-4 givers, -0.022 members per
   point). At round 0 the same 3-5 band loses members at the first decision (-0.12 at 9 points; lead -0.28)
   (explorations 3, 6).

## Hypothesis

Membership is the score, so a good manager punishes almost never, but one hit pays. At round 4, right after the
first switch decision and with 19 rounds left, it hits the members who give a little but not nothing (about 3-5
points) once and hard. They raise their giving for the rest of the game, herding pulls the group up with them, and
they do not leave. Hard-core free riders (0-2) and middling or high givers are left alone: the first barely
respond and leave, the second cut back. The rule: at round 4 only, every valid member who gave between lo and hi
points gets amt points.
- `lo` (int, [0, 4]): lowest contribution punished. I expect 2-3. 0 nests v4's peak (0-4 givers hit), and 4
  narrows the band to its strongest values.
- `hi` (int, [4, 7]): highest contribution punished. I expect 5. 6-7 lets the sweep see where the band ends.
- `amt` (float, uniform [8, 28]): the one-off hit in points. I expect 14-22. The measured gain still rises at 16,
  and 28 is near the cap.

### Justification

Finding 1 sets the bar and the method: v4's gain came from its round-4 hit, and the pooled experiment shows which
part of that hit earns it. Finding 2 supports the band. Its lower end `lo` is the main change from v4, because
v4 spent about half its hits on 0-2 givers, who earn nothing. Its upper end `hi` sits around 5, where the effect
turns negative. Finding 3 is the reason the band works: those givers respond and do not leave. Finding 4 is why
`amt`'s range runs past v4's 16. Finding 6 is why round 4 is the only round. Finding 5 is why there is no streak
or history condition. Expected gain over v4's peak: +0.3 to +0.5 (dropping the 0-2 hits, giving 5-givers the full
dose), plus whatever a dose above 16 adds.

What would contradict it: a sweep surface rising toward lo = 0 or hi = 7, or falling in amt above 16. The first two
would mean the band effect was a noisy artefact of per-value bins. The third would mean big hits cost members in a
way the 8-16 data did not show.

Not modelled:
- Scaling the dose with how far the target lags its mates (finding 5). It is positive in both halves, and the
  sweep's noise could not resolve a fourth parameter.
- Round 12, where zero-givers showed +0.20 per point. That is one bin at 2 se, and it contradicts earlier versions.
- Conditioning on group size. Both size halves have the same sign pattern.
