# pf-20261104-7-v3

## Explorations

1. **Where did versions 1 and 2 end up?** Read both notes, rules and v2's sweep
   (plots/simulation/policy_finder/pf-20261104-7-v2_sweep: final/sweep.json, landscapes.json).
   v2's final point (threshold 9.7, base 2.9, rate 6.0, t_end 8, taper 1.0) scored 69.6 (se 1.1),
   just below v1's 70.5 (se 1.1): the bar is still 70.5. v2's surface: threshold has by far the
   largest effect (25 points) with a sharp peak at ~9.8 (67.9 at 6, 71.7 at 9.8, 62.7 at 13.7,
   47.9 at 17.5); rate climbs monotonically to its range end (63.7 at 0.2, 71.4 at 5.2, at_edge),
   taper climbs to its end 1 (69.9 at 0 to 71.6), t_end is flat 6-12 and falls after; base has a
   small hump (70.4 at 0, 71.7 at ~3). So again "hard, front-loaded punishment of everyone below
   ~10, then stop" - and the concave `base` idea of v2 bought nothing. Two versions with different
   shapes landing at the same ~70 suggests a ceiling set by something neither rule touches.
   Next: compare the round-by-round dynamics of v1's and v2's finals to find that something.

2. **What do v1's and v2's finals look like per round?** Looked at both finals and the reference
   with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-7/v2_dynamics.py. Same pattern for all top points:
   contributions climb from 8.7 to 11.8-13.7 while punishing and stay flat afterwards; the
   points that punish longer (v2_s157, t_end 17) reach 13.7 and a late pool of 93/round, but pay
   more early (pool 3.3 in round 0). Members: all drop to 3.4-3.8 after the first switch (zero
   rises to 4.9 there) and recover to 4.2-4.4. The pool is lost in rounds 0-7 (v2 gp: 8.6 in round
   0, ~50 in rounds 3-7 vs zero's 55-65) and won from round 8 (77-83 vs 60).
   Next: the contribution model's inputs (configs/training/artificial_humans), to understand why
   gains persist, and a spillover check.

3. **Why do gains persist? (model inputs + spillover)** The contribution model
   (group_switching_contribution_50ep_vnode_stimulus_skip config) sees only previous contribution,
   previous punishment and group membership, with a graph over the group's members and an RNN, no
   round number; the switch model (switch_predictor/joint_exodus.yml) sees common_good (the
   group's 1.6*sum(c) - sum(p) per member, checked in the data), punishment, group and round
   number, and draws leaver counts jointly. With scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-7/spillover.py
   (v2 sweep, my group, 80 episodes per point) an OLS of the next-round change gives own
   contribution -0.365 and others' mean contribution +0.327 (se 0.001): strong conformity whose
   two terms nearly cancel over the group, so the group total is nearly conserved from round to
   round - a level bought by punishment stays, and there is no end-game decay since the model does
   not know the round. Spillover of others' punishment is small (+0.038 per point of others' mean
   punishment). So a contribution point bought in round t is worth ~1.6 x members x (24-t) pool;
   the limit has to be the members lost. Next: what moves the group size.

4. **What moves my group's size at a switch decision?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-7/
   members_flow.py, v1+v2 sweeps, 100 episodes per point, 210k group-decisions. OLS of the size
   change: my per-member common_good +0.099, the other group's -0.098, my mean punishment in the
   three rounds before the decision -0.124 per point, my own size -0.365 (strong reversion). By
   table, the first decision (round 3) is the sensitive one: with heavy punishment (8-30 mean per
   member) in round 3 my group changes by -2.6 members on average (+1.3 with none), while at
   later decisions heavy punishment costs only -0.5 to -0.8 (vs +0.1 to +0.5). Within the first
   decision, at the same punishment in rounds 0-2 (3-6 per member), 0 in round 3 gives +1.4 and
   4-8 gives -1.3 - but that is confounded (no punishment in round 3 means everybody was already
   above the threshold).

5. **Is the collapse after the first switch what caps the pool?** scripts/policy_finder/
   pf-20261104-7/collapse.py, finals. Yes, and dramatically: the joint exodus model moves groups
   wholesale. After round 3 my group is empty in 16% (v1) / 19% (v2) of episodes and has <=1
   member in 31-34%, vs 6.5% / 14% under `zero`. Pool per episode by the size at round 4:
   v2 gp 30.6 (0 members), 38 (1), 58 (2) ... 107 (7), 108 (8); `zero` 30, 37, 38 ... 74, 78. So
   given the size after the first switch, punishment adds ~+20-30 pool in the groups that keep
   their members; the loss is the third of episodes where the group empties at round 4.

6. **Is the decision-round effect causal? (v1's randomised switch_factor)**
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-7/switch_factor_check.py. Among v1 points that punish in
   earnest (rate*threshold > 8, t_stop > 4; 59 points), switch_factor 0 -> 1 raises round-3
   punishment from 0.3 to 3.0 per member and cuts the round-4 size by 0.43 (se 0.07); pool -1.3
   (se 1.1). So the decision round's own punishment matters, but only modestly; most of the
   round-4 loss must come from rounds 0-2.

7. **Where in time does punishment pay? (point-level spend profile)**
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-7/spend_profile.py, all 512 v1+v2 points: mean punishment per
   member in rounds 0-3 (pe), 4-11 (pm), 12-23 (pl). Round-4 size: -0.246 per point of pe (se
   0.011), R2 0.94. Late contribution (rounds 12-23): +0.50 per point of pe and +0.50 per point of
   pm (pm spans twice as many rounds, so per total point early buys twice as much). Pool (R2 0.61):
   +1.5 pe - 0.16 pe^2 - 0.79 pm - 0.43 pm^2 + 0.33 pe*pm; the quadratic peaks at pe ~6, pm ~1.5.
   Top points all spend 5-10 per member in rounds 0-3 and 2-4 in 4-11. But no point of either
   sweep punished little early and much later: pe and pm move together in both rules, so the
   option "wait for the first switch, then punish" is untested. The first decision is the most
   sensitive one (exploration 4), and rounds 0-3 cost 0.25 members per point, so I suspect a
   delayed or softened start could keep the group intact through round 4 and still buy the level
   in rounds 4-11, with 20 rounds left to collect it.

8. **Drafted v3's rule.** For a valid member of my group contributing c < threshold in round t:
   punishment = min(rate * (threshold - c), 30) * weight(t), with weight = early_factor in rounds
   0-3, (1 - (t-4)/(t_end-4))^shape in rounds 4..t_end-1, and 0 from t_end on. Five parameters:
   threshold, rate, early_factor, t_end (int), shape. What changed from v2 and why: (a) the new
   `early_factor` separates the first block from the rest, because the first switch is where the
   group empties (explorations 4-7) and no earlier design point could punish little before it and
   much after it; (b) `base` is dropped (v2's effect 1.3 pool points, and with a high rate it is
   nearly irrelevant), freeing a design dimension for (a); (c) the cap at 30 is applied before the
   time weight, so a fade actually lowers saturated punishments (in v2 a 0.5 weight on a 60-point
   raw punishment still gave 30); (d) rate's range moves up to [1, 30] (log) since v2's surface
   was still rising at its end 6, with 6 now in the middle; (e) `taper`, which sat at its edge 1,
   becomes `shape`, an exponent that can go past linear (front-loading right after the first
   switch) or down to 0 (v1's hard stop). The fade starts at round 4 so that early_factor = 1,
   shape ~1, t_end ~8 roughly reproduces v2's winner.

9. **Validated the draft and checked the spending it implies.** `validate-rule` passes (256
   distinct design points). scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-7/cost_check_v3.py applies
   candidates statically to the contributions under v2's final point (no feedback) by block
   (rounds 0-3 / 4-11 / 12-23 mean punishment per member-round). v2 gp's actual: 7.84 / 1.20 / 0;
   v3 at 9.7/6/1/8/1: 7.88 / 1.61 / 0, so v3 nests v2's winner. Delayed start 9.7/6/0/12/1:
   0 / 2.92 / 0 (in reality more in 4-11, since contributions would still be at ~8.4 in round 4);
   soft start 10/10/0.3/12/1: 2.83 / 3.56 / 0; corners: 8/1.5/0/6/0 spends 0.32 in round 4 only
   (practically `zero`), 14/30/1/20/0 spends 20 / 18 / 11 (far beyond `ah`). So the design spans
   from `zero` to brutal, with v1's and v2's winners inside and the untested "wait for the first
   switch" profiles well covered (early_factor < 0.3 in 30% of points).

## Key findings

1. The bar is v1's final 70.5 (se 1.1); v2 reached 69.6 (se 1.1) with a different shape. Both
   surfaces say: punish everyone below ~10 hard (v2's threshold peak 9.8 with a 25-point effect,
   rate rising to its range end 6) early, then stop (exploration 1).
2. In the sim, a contribution level bought by punishment persists: the contribution model has no
   round input and its conformity terms (-0.365 own, +0.327 others' mean) nearly conserve the
   group's total; contributions stay flat after punishment stops (11.8-13.7) (explorations 2, 3).
3. The pool is capped by the group collapsing at the first switch: under v1/v2's finals my group
   is empty after round 3 in 16-19% of episodes (6.5% under `zero`), and episodes that keep 7-8
   members score 102-108 against 74-78 under `zero` (exploration 5).
4. Punishment in rounds 0-3 costs 0.25 members at round 4 per point of mean punishment per member
   (R2 0.94 over 512 points); the first decision is far more sensitive to punishment than later
   ones (-2.6 vs -0.5 to -0.8 members with heavy punishment); the decision round's own share is
   causal but modest (v1's randomised switch_factor: -0.43 members, pool -1.3, se 1.1)
   (explorations 4, 6, 7).
5. Early and mid-game punishment buy late contributions at the same rate per member-round point
   (+0.50 each), and no earlier design point punished little before the first switch and much
   after it, so that profile is untested (exploration 7).

## Hypothesis

A good manager on this stack buys a high contribution level with hard punishment of everyone
below the group's typical contribution, because the artificial humans keep a level once it is
reached and newcomers conform to it, and then stops punishing. Its main cost is not the points
spent but the members lost, and those are lost mostly at the first switch decision, where a
heavily punished group empties wholesale; so the manager should weigh how hard to punish before
the first switch separately from after it, and may do best by punishing softly, or not at all,
until the group has survived the first switch and then punishing hard for a few rounds, with 20
rounds left to collect the gain.

The rule, for a valid member of my group contributing c < threshold in round t:
punishment = min(rate * (threshold - c), 30) * weight(t); weight = early_factor in rounds 0-3,
(1 - (t-4)/(t_end-4))^shape in rounds 4..t_end-1, 0 from t_end on.

- `threshold` (float, [6, 14]): the contribution below which a member is punished. Expected
  ~9-11, around the round-0 mean of 8.7 and v2's peak of 9.8; past 13 v2's surface fell steeply.
- `rate` (float, [1, 30], log): punishment points per point of shortfall, capped at 30. Expected
  ~5-15 (near-binary hard punishment below the bar); v2's peak sat at its range end 6.
- `early_factor` (float, [0, 1]): strength in rounds 0-3, before the first switch. The new
  question; I lean to 0-0.5 (finding 3-4) but cannot rule out 1, so the full range.
- `t_end` (int, [5, 20]): first round without punishment. Expected ~8-14: earlier with a full
  early phase (v1/v2 peaks at 8-11), later if the start is delayed.
- `shape` (float, [0, 3]): how the punishment fades over rounds 4..t_end (0 hard stop, 1
  linear, >1 front-loaded). Expected ~1-2, since v2's taper sat at its front-loading end.

### Justification

Findings 1 and 2 ground the core of the rule, hard punishment below a threshold near the group's
contribution and then stopping: `threshold`'s range is centred on v2's peak and `rate`'s extends
the direction v2's surface was still climbing; finding 2 (levels persist, no end-game decay) is
why `t_end` can be early and why spending should be concentrated, hence `shape`. Findings 3 and
4 (the first switch is where the pool is lost, and rounds 0-3 punishment drives it) ground
`early_factor`, and finding 5 is why it deserves a design dimension: the sweeps so far could
not separate early from mid-game punishment. What would contradict it: a peak with
early_factor near 1 (the contribution level bought in rounds 0-3 is worth more than the members
it costs, i.e. v2's profile was already right), or a peak at shape 0 with a late t_end (fading
does not matter). Not modelled: a separate discount for later decision rounds (v1's causal test
gave a pool effect of -1.3 with se 1.1, not worth a dimension), the base points of v2 (effect
1.3), a threshold relative to the group's mean (the peak at ~9.8 already sits at the mean, and
an absolute bar lets spending fall as contributions rise), gating punishment on the group's size
(larger groups lose more members in absolute terms but less in proportion; no clear direction),
and the other group's state.
