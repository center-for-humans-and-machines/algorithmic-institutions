## Key findings

1. On this stack a few heavy shocks at block starts cost no members and buy a lasting habit: v3's
   peak (24 points to members giving <=5 in rounds 0, 4, 8) kept 4.58 members like `zero` (4.57),
   and its members gave 9.92 vs 8.27 per round to the end, with nothing dealt after round 8; `ah`'s
   members gained 0.65 (the leak) and the shocks cost 0.80 per member-round; lead 19.17 vs 13.97
   (exploration 2).
2. A member's answer to a block-start shock grows with the dose without saturating up to 24
   (round 0: +1.5, +2.1, +2.7, +3.1 contribution in the next rounds at D 2-5 ... 16-24 vs +1.1
   unpunished); the punished leave more (stay 0.69 -> 0.56) but the group's size at the end does not
   move (exploration 3).
3. Of the block starts, round 4 is the one that pays: +2.34 +- 1.05 lead per round over the rest of
   the game (+3.27 +- 1.46 at hi<=7), +1.39 +- 0.71 in a second comparison, with slightly more
   members at the end; round 0 -0.10 +- 0.63, round 8 +0.79 +- 1.41 / +0.02 +- 1.13; rounds 12 and
   16 -2..+1.7 with large errors; a second shock round within a block mostly loses (exploration 4).
4. v3's surface preferred first=0, but its parametrization tied round 0 to round 4, so that read
   cannot tell the two apart; v3's hi profile (best 4-6, -5 from hi 5 to 12) is the clearest
   targeting result of all versions (explorations 1, 4).

## Hypothesis

Against `ah` on this stack, membership is set mostly by the switch model's reading of punishment
near decisions, while contribution habits form early and last. A good manager therefore punishes
rarely and hard: one heavy shock to the low contributors in the first round after a switch
decision, where the switch model weighs it least and the trained contribution has the most rounds
left, above all at round 4, right after the first and biggest reshuffle; nothing in the rounds
leading up to a decision, and nothing late in the game, where too few rounds remain to repay the
points. The rule: in round 4b (b = 0..3) each member of my group who timed in, is not alone and
gave at most `hi` gets the dose of that block start; no punishment in any other round.

- `D0` (float, [0, 30] uniform): the shock at round 0. Expect anywhere; small effect.
- `D4` (float, [0, 30] uniform): the shock at round 4. Expect 15-30.
- `D8` (float, [0, 30] uniform): the shock at round 8. Expect anywhere; small effect, maybe 10-25.
- `D12` (float, [0, 30] uniform): the shock at round 12. Expect 0-10.
- `hi` (int, 2..9): the highest contribution targeted. Expect 4-6.

### Justification

Finding 1 sets the shape: a sparse schedule of heavy block-start shocks, with no window and no
second round (finding 3's w=2 losses). Finding 3 is why each block start gets its own dose rather
than v3's tied window (finding 4): round 4 is the one robust gain, and the sweep can now set it high
while setting round 0, 8 and 12 to whatever they are worth, down to 0. Finding 2 is why every dose
range reaches the cap of 30: the answer has not saturated at 24 and the extra exits are refilled.
Uniform ranges from 0 keep `zero` and every subset of shocks inside the design, and the v3 peak too
(D0=D4=D8=24, D12=0, hi=5). Finding 4's hi profile sets hi to 2..9 around its 4-6 optimum, cutting
the clearly losing 10-12. Round 16 and after are left out (finding 3: no gain there, and the last
blocks give too few rounds for a habit to pay). What would contradict it: a peak with D4 low (round
4's gate gain was noise), or with D12 high (late shocks pay after all), or `own` not rising with D4.
Not modelled: per-player history (v3: no sticky types), the group's size (v3: no size pattern), a
dose graded by the shortfall (v2's lo and v1's proportional rule found nothing in it), and the
other group's state. The main risk is noise: the gate effects are 1.5-2 se, and four doses plus hi
spread 256 points over five dimensions, so the sweep's surface will be rough; the final check on
fresh seeds is what decides.
