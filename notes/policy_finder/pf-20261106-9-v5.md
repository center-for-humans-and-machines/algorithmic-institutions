## Hypothesis

A good manager runs an initiation, as before: it punishes shortfalls below its group's mean hard
in the first two rounds of each member's first block with it (the originals at the start, each
newcomer at arrival), and is quiet before every switch decision and late in the game. What v5
adds is that the price of that punishment is paid in members, and players price it relative to
the other group: they leave (and fail to join) when our pool per head falls below the other
group's. So the right intensity depends on the other manager: when the other manager punishes
too, its group's pool per head falls as well and our punishment costs few members; against a
lenient manager it costs many. Round 0 must be set blind (nothing is known yet), but from round 1
on the rule reads the other manager's punishments in `p` and scales its intensity with them.

The rule: shortfall = max(0, mean_c - c_i - s0) over valid members of our group; a member is
punished only while its tenure with us is under 4 rounds, in the first 4 - quiet rounds of a
block, before round t_end; punishment = slope x shortfall (the manager clamps it to 30), with
slope = k0 in round 0 and k1 x (1 + w x q) later, q the other manager's mean punishment per
member and round so far.
- `k0` (float, [2, 30], log): round-0 slope; I expect 6-15 (v4's common k 7, v4 per opponent
  5-15, and round 0 is the cheapest round in members).
- `k1` (float, [1, 20], log): later initiation slope against a manager who has punished nobody;
  I expect 3-7 (opponent 4's peak k 5.0 in v4, 3.4 in v3).
- `w` (float, [0, 0.3]): licence per point of the other manager's mean per-member punishment; I
  expect 0.05-0.2 (k 5 -> 15 across q 0 -> 10 means w ~0.2 if k1 carries it all; q at round 1 is
  0-11 for the opponents seen, so w = 0.3 already quadruples the slope).
- `s0` (float, [-0.5, 2.5]): tolerance below the mean; I expect 0.3-1.5 (v4 0.8-1.0; per
  opponent 0.1-1.8).
- `quiet` (int, fixed 2): silent rounds before each decision (all earlier sweeps).
- `t_end` (int, fixed 16): first round without punishment (flat in v3 and v4).

### Justification

Finding 1 says the opening is the only lever left in v4's family and frees the dimensions spent
on later-block punishment and on cap (cap's best was the game's own maximum), so v5 punishes only
in the first block of a member's stay and lets the clamp at 30 act as cap. Finding 2 gives the
mechanism (members compare per-head pools across groups, round 1 weighs more than round 0) and
finding 3 its consequence for timing, which is why round 0 gets its own slope `k0`. Finding 4 is
the direct evidence for the licence `w`: across four opponents in two sweeps the best intensity
is ordered by the other manager's harshness, and finding 2 explains the order. `k0 = k1`, `w =
0` reproduces v4's best region (stage 0 only), so the sweep can fall back on it; `s0` keeps its
narrowed range. It would be contradicted if the sweep put `w` at 0 (no gain from conditioning) or
`k1` at its lower edge with large `w` (then only harsh opponents justify round-1 punishment),
or if the score against opponent 4 fell below v4's 60.5 while opponent 3's did not rise. I did not
model the other group's contributions or pool directly (q is a cleaner, manager-made signal and
the pool gap partly reflects our own punishment), a separate licence for round 0 (impossible: the
other manager is not seen yet), reactions to the other manager's timing within a block (the four
opponents differ mostly in level), or later-block punishment of incumbents (flat in v4).
