## Key findings

1. v2 reached 71.9 +- 1.1 against `ah` (v1 67.1), and its fitted pool
   falls almost linearly with `last_round`, from 73.3 at 8 (the lower
   edge) to 63.7 at 23. `pre_switch` peaked at 0 and `fresh` at its top 3
   (exploration 1).
2. v2's best points set the norm early and then leave the group alone:
   ~5 punishment per member in round 0, ~3.5 at rounds 4 and 8, little
   after. Contribution per member climbs to 10.5 by round 9 and stays
   there to round 23 unpunished, members rise from 4.0 to 4.5, and the
   pool reaches 80-83 per round against ah's 55-63 (exploration 2).
3. Newcomers arrive below the norm (9.5 vs 10.8 per member at round 12)
   and respond to punishment like incumbents: at 0.5-0.9x the mean,
   +2.0-2.5 after 3 rounds at 6-14 points against +0.7 unpunished, at
   every switch round. At or above 0.9x there is no lift (exploration 3).
4. Punishing members at or above the others' mean costs ~0.64 pool per
   point per member-round across v2's points; late general punishment
   (rounds 9-15) costs a little (-0.2 to -0.3) (exploration 4).
5. `rel` has a sharp optimum near 0.97 (fitted 69 at 0.72, 66.7 at 1.12,
   58.5 at 1.2), `slope` near 2.8-3.0, `cap` flat at 18-25
   (exploration 1).

## Hypothesis

On this stack a good manager sets the group's norm early and then steps
back. In the first rounds it punishes every member below the group's
current mean, firmly and in proportion to the shortfall, hardest right
after a switch decision and never just before one. The artificial
humans' lift lasts for the rest of the game. Once the norm is set, more
punishment mostly costs points and exits, and a group that stops
punishing while `ah` keeps going draws `ah`'s members in. The one late
exception is newcomers. They arrive below the norm and are as trainable
as the early members, and a switch round is the cheapest moment to
punish them. So after the stop the manager gives only them a dose, in
their arrival round.

The rule: shortfall = max(0, `rel` x mean of the other valid members'
contributions this round - own contribution); base = min(`cap`, `slope`
x shortfall). Rounds 0..`last_round`: base x `fresh` in rounds 0, 4, 8,
12, 16, 20; x 0 in rounds 3, 7, 11, 15, 19; x 1 otherwise. After
`last_round`, only members who joined this round, in switch rounds 4, 8,
12 and 16, get base x `fresh` x `newcomer`. Everyone else gets 0.

- `rel` (float, [0.75, 1.1]): the laggard threshold as a fraction of the
  others' mean. I expect 0.92-1.0, near v2's sharp peak (finding 5).
- `slope` (float, [1, 8] log): punishment per point of shortfall. I
  expect 2-4 (v2 peak 2.8).
- `cap` (float, [10, 30]): the per-round maximum before the fresh
  multiplier. I expect 18-26, flat.
- `fresh` (float, [1, 6] log): the multiplier right after a decision. I
  expect 2.5-4. v2 peaked at its top 3 with a gentle slope, and the
  clamp at 30 limits how much a higher value changes.
- `last_round` (int, 2..14): the last round of general punishment. I
  expect 5-9. v2 peaked at its lowest value 8, but the norm takes ~2
  phases to set (contributions plateau by round 9, finding 2), so I
  doubt 2-3 will win. The range now lets the sweep check.
- `newcomer` (float, [0, 1.5]): the late multiplier for new arrivals. I
  expect 0.5-1. At 0 it is v2's rule, so the sweep can reject it.

### Justification

Findings 1 and 2 justify front-loading and the early stop, and they are
why `last_round` now extends well below v2's edge of 8. Finding 1's edge
peak at `pre_switch` 0 (and v2's finding that a punishment just before a
decision raises leaving most) is why the pre-decision round is fixed at
0 and no longer a parameter. Finding 3 motivates `newcomer`. The late
arrivals are laggards whom v2's stop never touched, and they respond as
the early members did. Finding 4 limits the dose to newcomers and keeps
`rel` below ~1.1, because general late punishment and hitting at-norm
members both cost pool. Finding 5 sets the narrower `rel`, `slope` and
`cap` ranges around v2's peak, and the widened `fresh` follows finding
1's edge.

What would contradict it: `newcomer` peaking at 0 would mean the exit
and payoff cost of punishing new arrivals outweighs their lift, or that
the norm carries newcomers along by itself (the herding in the
contribution model). `last_round` at 2-3 would mean the first dose is
all that matters. `fresh` at 6 would mean the round-0 hit should be
near-maximal for every laggard.

Not modelled: punishment history (repeat offenders, what `ah` dealt
before a player came over), the other group's state, and relapses by
incumbents after the stop. Relapses would need a second threshold, and
v2's data shows incumbents' contributions flat after the stop. Round 20
newcomers are also left out, since with 3 rounds left their lift about
equals its cost (exploration 5). The newcomer response estimate is
correlational (the rule decides whom it punishes), so `newcomer`'s range
starts at 0.
