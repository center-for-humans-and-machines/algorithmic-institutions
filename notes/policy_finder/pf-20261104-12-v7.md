## Key findings

1. Six versions' finals lie at 70.3-73.3 (best v5, 73.29, se 1.12) and v6's
   new `herd` was dead (effect 0.0003): the bar-and-taper family has a ~73
   plateau that re-drawing its dials will not leave (exploration 1).
2. The round-0 dose-response has no threshold and no saturation: round-1
   contribution of a 0-8 giver rises ~0.065 per point (+1.95 at 30) and
   leaving ~0.0024 per point, with 1-5 points doing nothing, so the
   spread of points over members is not a lever (exploration 2).
3. Pooling the 1024 design points of v3-v6 on each point's realised
   intensity per cell, punishing 0-9 givers in blocks 1-2 pays in every
   version (+0.037 to +0.062 pool per point of summed dose) and in blocks
   3-4 costs in every version (-0.065 to -0.113); block 1 alone is
   +0.11 to +0.44 per point (explorations 3, 4).
4. Under the geometric taper the late blocks' doses are tied to the middle
   ones (correlation 0.71-0.97), and among points with a hard start the
   pool is 71.7 whenever blocks 3-4 deal < 30 summed points, 70.1 (se
   0.23) or 65.4 (0.65) when they deal more (exploration 4).
5. Punishing givers of 10+ in block 0 (the peer bar does it in generous
   groups) costs -0.44 pool per point of summed dose (se 0.05), negative in
   three of four versions (exploration 3).

## Hypothesis

A good manager makes its group clearly more generous than the other group
in the first half of the game, when member flows are set and punishment
still has many rounds to repay, and then stops: it caps clearly low givers
at the first two rounds of each block, stays quiet before every switch,
and leaves the late blocks unpunished, because there a point no longer
earns back its cost while it still lowers the common good every player
weighs at the remaining switches. It never punishes members who already
give a lot, whatever their mates give, since that buys nothing and costs
members.

The rule: `punishment = slope * decay^block * [pos < 2] * [block <= stop]
* [t < 20] * max(0, min(bar, ceil) - c_t)`, `bar = (1-peer)*thr +
peer*m`, m the mean contribution this round of the member's current
group-mates (a lone member: bar thr).

- `thr` (float, [6, 11]): fixed part of the bar; expect 7.5-9.5.
- `peer` (float, [0.1, 0.9]): weight of the mates' mean in the bar;
  expect 0.4-0.7.
- `ceil` (float, [8, 15]): the bar's ceiling, a contribution at which no
  one is punished; expect 9.5-12 (15 nests v6's bar).
- `slope` (float, [5, 50], log): punishment per shortfall point in block
  0; expect 10-30.
- `decay` (float, [0.25, 1.0], log): per-block factor among the punished
  blocks; expect 0.45-0.8.
- `stop` (int, [1, 4]): last punished block; expect 2 (4 is v5/v6's
  schedule).

### Justification

Finding 1 is why v7 changes the rule's structure again rather than its
ranges. Finding 3 is the case for `stop`: the same sign in four
independently randomised sweeps, positive for the middle blocks and
negative for the late ones, and finding 4 shows why the taper could not
act on it (it moves both together) and that the cost appears exactly where
the late blocks get heavy. `decay` stays so the sweep can still shape the
punished blocks, now with a range reaching 1, since block 1 is the best
paying block per point (finding 3). Finding 5 is the case for `ceil`: the
peer bar's one bad side effect is that it reaches 10+ givers among
generous mates, and a ceiling removes that without giving up the peer
norm for the 3-9 givers. Finding 2 is why the rule stays a near-step in
the shortfall (high slope) with no per-member shaping. `follow` and `herd`
were removed because they were flat or dead in v5/v6; both rounds of a
punished block are punished alike, the middle of the 0.7-1.3 their peaks
spanned. What would contradict the hypothesis: stop's best at 4, ceil's
at 15, or no gain over v5's 73.3 - then the cell estimates were the
taper's and the bar's collinearity rather than costs, and the ~73
plateau is the ceiling of punishment in this stack. Not modelled: group
size, newcomers, forgiveness, expulsion and the rival group's state (all
dead ends of v4-v6), and punishment at block positions 2-3 or after round
19.
