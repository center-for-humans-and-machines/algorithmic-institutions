2. **Does the gain from a punishment depend on the player's own trajectory?** Built
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-7/v5_panel.py (sweep parquet -> [episode, player, round]
   arrays, my group = agent_group 0) and v5_trajectory.py on v4's sweep (128k episodes), stayers
   in my group, gain c[t+3] - c[t] by (c_t bin, punishment bin) and either the player's trend
   (c_t vs c_{t-1}) or whether they were punished at t-1. Trend does not change the effect of
   punishment, only the baseline (mean reversion): at c 3-5, 6-15 points add +2.0 for risers and
   +2.2 for fallers over their unpunished peers. Being punished the round before halves it: at
   c 0-2, 16-30 points buy +8.5 for a first-time (10.29 vs 1.80) but +4.2 for a repeat (6.38 vs
   2.16); at 3-5 with 6-15 points +3.2 vs +1.6; at 9-11 with 6-15 points +2.5 vs +1.6. Still,
   even half a gain of ~4 contribution points, persisting for ~15 rounds, is worth ~100 pool
   points against a 25-point punishment, so on contributions alone repeat punishment pays; the
   question is what it costs in members. Next: leaving by own punishment history.
3. **Is it the cumulative punishment or its recency that weakens the response?**
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-7/v5_repeat.py (v4 sweep, stayers, gain c[t+3]-c[t], effect =
   punished minus unpunished in the same cell). Recency, mostly: for a 0-2 contributor, 16-30
   points buy +6.3 if never punished before, +6.7 if punished earlier but not last round, +4.2 if
   punished last round; at 6-15 points +3.1 / +2.4 / +0.8. At 3-5: 16-30 points +5.9 / +5.9 /
   +4.3. Cumulative wear is milder (0-2 at 16-30 points: +6.3 with no history, +5.2 after 41-80
   points, +4.2 after 81+). So a punishment dealt right after another buys about 2/3 (heavy) to
   1/4-2/3 (moderate) of what it would after a pause, the next round's punishment of a still-low
   player largely duplicates the response already under way. Next: how much of the winning
   rules' spending is such back-to-back punishment.
4. **How much of the spending is back-to-back?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-7/
   v5_spend_share.py on v4's 15 best sweep points: in rounds 1-3 and 5-7, 64-74% of my group's
   punishment points go to players I punished the round before (spending 5.8, 4.6, 3.8 per member
   in rounds 1-3); 47% over the game. So nearly half the budget buys the weak back-to-back
   response. Hypothesis taking shape: spare (or punish less) a player punished last round, letting

   after the pause. That cuts the money spent, which is pool, and with it the common-good dip
   that costs members at the first switch (v4 notes, finding 2), at a small cost in contributions.
   Next: does the gap since the last punishment matter beyond one round (a cooldown length)?
5. **How long a pause restores the response?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-7/v5_gap.py (v4

   rounds all of it. So the pause length is a real choice between 1 and 3 rounds.
6. **Drafted v5's rule.** v4's core without the cap: for valid members of my group in rounds

## Key findings

1. Four rule shapes reach the same plateau against `ah` (finals v1 70.5, v2 69.6, v3 69.5, v4 69.2,
   se ~1.1); v4's common-good cap only hurt (margin profile 68.9 at -20, cap off, to 65.3 at +5)
   (exploration 1).
2. The winners' cost is front-loaded: pool 17-23 in round 0 vs 55 under `zero`, my group falls to
   3.7-3.8 members at the first switch while `zero`'s rises to 4.9, and the return comes late (v1:
   pool ~88 in rounds 16-23 vs 59) (exploration 1).
3. A punishment dealt the round after the previous one buys much less than one after a pause: for
   0-2 contributors 16-30 points add +4.2 to c[t+3] one round after the last punishment against
   +6.7 two rounds after and +7.9 three after (+5.8 never punished); at 6-15 points +0.8 vs +2.1 /
   +2.6. Cumulative wear is milder (explorations 2, 3, 5).
4. Under v4's 15 best points 64-74% of the punishment points in rounds 1-3 and 5-7 (47% overall)
   go to players punished the round before (exploration 4).

## Hypothesis

On this stack punishment buys a lasting contribution level and its cost is the pool spent plus
the members lost when the spending sinks the group's common good before the first switch. The
winning rules waste about half their budget on back-to-back punishment of players whose response
to the previous punishment is still under way, where a point buys a quarter to two thirds of what
it buys after a pause. A good manager punishes low contributors hard, then gives each punished
player a pause before punishing them again, buying nearly the same contribution gain for much
less spending, and so a smaller common-good dip and fewer members lost.

The rule, for valid members of my group in rounds t < t_end: raw = min(rate * max(threshold - c,
0), 30), times repeat_factor if the player was punished (by either manager) in any of the last
`cooldown` rounds.

- `threshold` (float, [6, 13]): contribution below which a member is punished; expected ~8-10
  (v3 9.3, v4 8.2, v2 ~9.8), maybe higher if cheaper repeats make a broader bar pay.
- `rate` (float, [2, 30], log): points per point of shortfall, capped at 30; expected ~6-15.
- `t_end` (int, [4, 20]): first round without punishment; expected ~8-14.
- `cooldown` (int, [1, 3]): rounds after a punishment during which the player gets the reduced
  punishment; expected 1-2.
- `repeat_factor` (float, [0, 1]): the share of the normal punishment dealt during the cooldown;
  expected ~0-0.5; 1 recovers v4's final rule.

### Justification

Finding 3 is the core: if a back-to-back point buys a fraction of a paused point, the money is
better spent after a pause, and finding 4 shows the winners spend about half their budget there,
so the lever is big enough to matter; finding 2 (and v4's finding that members follow the
common-good gap) says spending less early costs fewer members, the plateau's binding cost.
`cooldown` follows from the recovery profile (one round restores most, two all; finding 3),
`repeat_factor`'s range spans a hard pause (0) to the old rule (1) so the sweep can reject the
idea. threshold, rate and t_end keep v3/v4's grounded core (finding 1), with t_end allowed a bit
later because pauses spread the punishment. What would contradict it: a peak at repeat_factor ~1
(the lost contribution outweighs the saving, e.g. because a still-low player left unpunished
drags the others down via conformity) or no gain over ~70. Not modelled: the common-good cap
(finding 1), scaling by cumulative punishment (milder than the recency effect, a second dimension
for little), separate treatment of newcomers (they count as recently punished if `ah` just
punished them, which follows the same logic), and the timing of switch rounds (v1's
switch_factor was flat).
