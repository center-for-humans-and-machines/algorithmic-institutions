2. **Does group size change what punishment costs in members?** scripts/policy_finder/
   pf-20261104-7/v6_size.py on v5's sweep (128k episodes, 256 points). Group sizes are bimodal by

   +2.31 with no punishment in rounds 0-3 and -2.49 with more than 10 per member-round, P(empty)
   0.6% vs 38%; by the averaged cg gap of rounds 0-3 it is -2.78 (gap < -10) to +2.89 (gap > 10),
   with the switch at gap ~0. At round 7, punishment costs members at every size, most at 3-5 and
   8 (8 members: -1.23 unpunished vs -3.77 heavy), little at 1-2 (+1.78 vs +1.07). The gap, not
   the size, sets the direction; size only scales it. So size gating is not the lever I hoped.
   What stands out is that the whole game turns on which group wins round 3. Next: which of rounds
   0-3's gaps weighs most on the round-3 decision, and how the best sweep points' gap looks there.

4. **What does a punishment point cost in members, by group size?** scripts/policy_finder/
   pf-20261104-7/v6_size_cost.py, v5 sweep: within groups whose size was fixed through the
   window, OLS of the size change at decisions 7/11/15 on mean contribution, mean punishment per
   member and the other group's cg. Per point spent (slope / size) the member cost is ~0 for groups
   of 1 (+0.005/+0.004/-0.004) and -0.02 for 2, peaks at 3-5 (-0.039 to -0.052), and falls again

   where the earlier rules never looked: tiny groups (no-one to lose; the exodus model refills

## Key findings

1. Five rule shapes reach the same plateau against `ah` (finals v1 70.5, v2 69.6, v3 69.5, v4
   69.2, v5 69.7, se ~1.1), all "punish members below ~8.5-12 hard until round ~10, then stop";
   v5's pause after a punishment was rejected (repeat_factor peak 0.98, cooldown effect 0.01)
   (exploration 1).
2. The first switch sorts the game: by round 7 my group has 0-1 members in 40k and 7-8 in 35k of
   128k v5-sweep episodes; at round 3 the size change runs from +2.31 (no punishment) to -2.49
   (>10 per member-round), with P(empty) 0.6% vs 38% (exploration 2).
3. At the first switch members follow the per-capita cg gap of rounds 0-3 with weights 0.028,
   0.038, 0.053, 0.063 per point, and punishment costs nothing beyond the cg it removes; the best
   v5 points already front-load (9.8 per member in round 0, 3.7 in round 3) (exploration 3).
4. The member cost per punishment point spent depends on group size: ~0 at 1, -0.02 at 2, -0.04 to
   -0.05 at 3-5, -0.017 to -0.026 at 7-8, the same at decisions 7, 11 and 15 (exploration 4).
5. On the randomised stop round (v1/v4/v5 sweeps), punishing through one more window changes my
   pool per remaining round by: rounds 4-7 +0.6 to +4.6 (largest, +4.6, in full groups); rounds
   8-11 -0.8 to -5.2 in groups of 2-4 but +1.6 to +2.1 in groups of 5-7; rounds 12-15 -1.5 to -5.7
   at every size; arm-composition noise ~2 (explorations 5, 6).
6. Small groups do not gain from longer punishment despite their low member cost per point: they
   refill more slowly (2.62 vs 2.76 members after the next decision) (exploration 5).

## Hypothesis

On this stack punishment buys a contribution level that persists, and its cost is members, lost
through the per-capita common-good gap at switch decisions. After the first switch the game has a
winner and a loser group, and the two face different trade-offs: a big group loses few members per
point spent and multiplies a bought contribution by many members, so it should keep punishing low
contributors a few rounds longer; a small or mid-size group sits in the contested zone where every
point of gap moves members and it needs a high cg to refill, so it should stop sooner. The earlier
rules' single stop round (~10) is a compromise between the two. A good manager punishes everyone
below the bar hard in the first rounds and then lets its group's size decide when to stop.

The rule, for valid members of my group: raw = min(rate * max(threshold - c, 0), 30), dealt while
t < t_end_big if my group has at least n_big members this round, else while t < t_end.

- `threshold` (float, [6, 13]): contribution below which a member is punished; expected ~8.5-10.
- `rate` (float, [2, 30], log): points per point of shortfall, capped at 30; expected ~6-12.
- `t_end` (int, [4, 16]): first round without punishment in a group below n_big; expected ~8-9.
- `t_end_big` (int, [6, 20]): first round without punishment in a group of n_big or more;
  expected ~11-13.
- `n_big` (int, [3, 8]): the group size from which a group counts as big; expected ~5.

### Justification

Finding 1 grounds keeping v5's core and its ranges for threshold and rate, and dropping the pause.
Findings 2 and 4 say a group's size after the first switch changes what a punishment point costs in
members (half as much at 7-8 as at 3-5), and finding 5 shows the payoff to continuing in rounds
8-11 changes sign with size around 5, which is exactly the decision `t_end`, `t_end_big` and
`n_big` describe; finding 6 is why small groups get the earlier stop rather than, as the per-point
member cost alone suggested, a later one. Finding 3 says rounds 0-3 are best handled by the front-
loaded core, so both stop rounds are at least 4 and the size gate only acts after the first switch.
The plateau rule is the diagonal t_end = t_end_big, so the sweep can fall back to it. What would
contradict it: a peak on that diagonal or with t_end_big < t_end, a flat n_big profile, or no gain
over ~70; finding 5's effects are only ~2 pool per remaining round in about half the episodes, so
the expected gain is a point or two, near the noise. Not modelled: a size-dependent threshold or
rate (one dimension of conditioning at a time, and the stop round is where the data showed the
sign change), the cg gap itself (v4's cap failed), the pause (v5), the other group's size (it is
8 minus mine), and timing within a switch window (v1's switch_factor was flat; finding 3's weights
favour round 0, where the core already spends most).
