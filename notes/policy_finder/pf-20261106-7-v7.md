8. **Drafted the v7 rule.** It is v6 plus one parameter, with re-centred ranges: punishment = k * decay^block * pos * (shortfall / 5)^gamma * (fresh if the member is a newcomer, else 1), for valid members of my group. A newcomer is a member who was not in my group in the round before this block's start (block 1 onwards), so the factor covers their whole first block with me; someone who left and came back counts as new again. The changes and why:
   - New `fresh` [0, 2] (explorations 5-7). Arrivals are a quarter to two fifths of my group at each block start. They leave about twice as readily per sqrt point of punishment, with about the same lift, so their best punishment should be lower than a stayer's. Below 1 spares them, 1 is v6 exactly, and above 1 is v4's "treat the untreated" idea, kept reachable because the lift levels are uncertain.
   - `gamma` moves to [0.5, 3]: v6's profile rose monotonically to its 1.6 edge (exploration 1), and 0.1-0.5 was clearly bad (63.9-65.8). At gamma 3 the rule is close to a threshold: about nothing below a shortfall of 3, then the cap from about 6.
   - `k` [10, 100] log: v6 peaked at 27 with a profile clean on both sides. The lower end is lifted a little because 8-10 was the worst part.
   - `c0` [7, 15]: v6 peaked at 11. A steeper gamma spares near-misses, which lets a higher target pay, so the range moves up one.
   - `w`, `decay` and `follow` are kept: they peaked mid-range (0.48, 0.3-0.36, 0.56-0.64).

   I did not act on exploration 4's hint (less in rounds 1-2, more at the second and third block starts). The GP surface put follow at 0.6 and decay at 0.35 with the phase regression disagreeing, and the phases are tied by k. With `fresh` the later block starts get re-sized for newcomers anyway. Replayed cold on the human histories (v7_replay.py) at v6's peak: fresh 1 reproduces v6 (11.2 / 8.7 / 5.9 / 0, 5.8 at round 4). fresh 0 gives newcomers 0 at rounds 4 / 8 / 12 (stayers 5.9 / 1.3 / 0.4), and fresh 2 gives them 9.0 / 4.4 / 1.2. validate-rule passes on all 256 points.

## Key findings

1. v6's steepness exponent did not move the final result: 68.84 (71.6 / 69.5 / 74.3 / 59.9 against opponents 1-4) vs v5's 68.86. Its surface still rose monotonically in gamma up to the 1.6 edge (63.9 at 0.1 to 69.2), and the other parameters peaked mid-range (c0 11, w 0.48, k 27, decay 0.35, follow 0.6). The family sits on a plateau near 69, and opponent 4, the non-punisher, is always the weak spot (exploration 1).
2. Per-opponent surfaces are too noisy to use (pool se ~4.3 per point and opponent, R² ~0.3). A phase-level fit puts the best punishment at about the same place against every opponent (round 0 11-12.6 per member, rounds 1-3 1.6-3.2, rounds 4-11 ~2, nothing after 12). Neither gives a reason to condition on the other manager (explorations 2, 4).
3. The contribution drop at each block start is composition. Stayers keep their level (12.9 to 12.5), but 1.6-1.9 of my 4 members leave at decision 1 and 1.2-1.8 arrive at ~8-10. Next to the non-punisher, arrivals stay at 8.2-9.2 and my contributions slide from 9.8 to 9.0 over the late game (explorations 3, 5).
4. Per sqrt point of block-start punishment, arrivals leave about 2-2.5x as often as stayers (+0.016-0.023 vs +0.007-0.010, se ≤ 0.002), with about the same lift (0.95-1.27 vs 0.86-1.14 at c(b0+7)). Never-punished arrivals respond no more than punished ones. The v6 rule nonetheless punishes arrivals hardest (5.8-6.6 vs 1.4-4.1 per head at round 4) (exploration 6).
5. In the human data arrivals also leave more: +0.116 (se 0.030) above stayers at the same punishment and contribution, with the same linear slope (+0.038 per sqrt point) (exploration 7).

## Hypothesis

A good manager punishes the members furthest below a partly relative target hard in the first round, again at block starts with a shrinking size, and never right before a switch decision. That much is settled by six versions. The plateau now sits in *who* gets the later block-start punishment. After each decision about a third of the group is new, and those newcomers are the members who move. They respond to punishment as much as stayers but leave again about twice as readily per point, and each exit costs a contributor for at least a block. So a newcomer's first block with me should be priced on its own: probably gentler than a stayer's, letting them settle in before they are corrected by the regular schedule. I also give the steepness exponent room beyond v6's 1.6 edge.

The rule: punishment = k * decay^block * pos * (shortfall / 5)^gamma * (fresh for a newcomer, else 1) for valid members of my group, capped at 30. shortfall = max(0, (1 - w) * c0 + w * others' mean - c); pos = 1 / follow / follow^2 / 0 over a block's four rounds (follow^2 in round 23). A newcomer is a member who was not in my group in the round before the current block began (blocks 1-5).
- `c0` (int, [7, 15]): absolute part of the target; expected 10-12 (v6 11).
- `w` (float, [0, 1]): weight of the group's own norm in the target; expected 0.3-0.7 (v6 0.47).
- `k` (float, [10, 100], log): round-0 punishment at a shortfall of 5; expected 20-45 (v6 26, higher if gamma rises).
- `gamma` (float, [0.5, 3]): steepness in the shortfall; expected 1.3-2.2 (v6 at its 1.6 edge).
- `decay` (float, [0.1, 1], log): per-block shrink; expected 0.25-0.5 (v6 0.35).
- `follow` (float, [0, 1]): second-round share of a block's punishment (third: follow^2); expected 0.4-0.75 (v6 0.63).
- `fresh` (float, [0, 2]): factor on a newcomer's punishment in their first block with me; expected 0.3-0.9 (finding 4), with 1 = v6.

### Justification

Finding 1 keeps v6's structure and centres c0, w, k, decay and follow on v6's mid-range peaks. It moves gamma's range past the edge where its profile was still rising, and drops the flat-fine end that was clearly worse. Findings 3-5 give `fresh`. Newcomers are a large, identifiable share of every later block (finding 3). They carry about twice the exit price per point for the same lift in the simulated players (finding 4), and the human data agrees that they are the restless ones (finding 5). Under a concave lift and a linear exit cost, a higher exit price means a lower best punishment, so I expect fresh below 1. The range runs to 2 because the lift levels are confounded by the target, and because against the non-punisher the newcomers are untreated free riders who drag my late contributions (finding 3), which argues the other way. The sweep picks the side. fresh = 1 is exactly v6, so v7 contains v6's peak.

What would contradict it: a flat fresh profile (newcomer exits matter too little at the game level, ~1.2 arrivals per block), or a best fresh above 1 (lift outweighs exits). A best value at 0 would say newcomers should simply be left alone in their first block. A final pool not above ~68.9 within about two standard errors (~1.1) means the plateau holds.

Not modelled:
- Conditioning on the other manager. The best allocation looks the same against every opponent (finding 2), and v1's relative cap failed. The newcomer factor reacts to the other manager only indirectly, through who arrives.
- A separate amplitude for later block starts vs rounds 1-2. Exploration 4 hints at it, but the evidence is weak and fresh already re-sizes the later block starts for a third of the group.
- Saturation on punishment received (v4: flat; finding 4: no extra response from the never-punished).
