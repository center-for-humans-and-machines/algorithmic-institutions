# pf-20261105-11-v7

## Explorations

1. **Where v6 landed, paired.** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-11/g1_paired_v6.py
   pairs all six GP finals on their shared 2000 games (seed 1000). v6_gp
   (T 12.68, A 2.94, B 1.88, S 8) scored 649.34, -0.54 against v4_gp (paired se
   0.38). v2 -0.19, v5 -0.98, v1 -1.11, v3 -4.22. Per round, v4 bills +0.1 a player
   more than v6 in rounds 1-6, because its T 13.3 also fines c=13, and its mean c is
   +0.15 to +0.22 higher in every round from 7 to 23. So the fines on near-target
   players buy a lasting shift, and the GP's lower T lost it. Under v4_gp the mean c
   climbs 11.5 -> 17.0 (round 8), then slides 0.23 a round to 13.7 at round 23. Had
   it stayed at 17, rounds 9-23 would be worth ~+15 more. That slide is the one
   prize left that is big. Six versions say that holding the plateau with the
   window rule costs more than it saves. But that rule fines everyone below ~13 at
   ~1.2 a player-round. Next: what is the slide made of? Is it a broad drift of
   high contributors, or a few players collapsing? And what does a late fine do to
   a low contributor? The sweeps' S is drawn independently of the game, so I can
   compare S = r-1 with S >= r.
2. **What the slide is made of.** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-11/g2_slide.py,
   on the v4_gp final and the v6 sweep (all S=8). From round 8 to rounds 19-23,
   mean c drops 2.7-2.8. Players who fall by 10 or more points are 12% of players,
   and they make up -1.86 of that drop. The share of valid players at c <= 5 rises
   from 2.5% (round 8) to 15% (round 23). Most of them start the stretch at 16-20.
   Under v6, a player at c=0 in round 12 stays there: 2.1 next round and 3.2 six
   rounds on. So the slide is mostly **collapses**, not a gentle drift, and a
   collapsed clone does not come back by itself.
3. **What a late fine does to a low player, from the S natural experiment.**
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-11/g3_late_fine.py and g4_late_dose.py: low
   players at round r in 9..15, with S = r (fined once at r) against S = r-1 (not
   fined). The raw comparison mixes up fine size with the params' history, so
   g5_late_dose_matched.py gives the controls the fine their own params would have
   charged at r and compares within bins of that fine. Effect on own c over r+1..r+6:
   - c_r = 0: +0.6 (fine ~8), +1.7 (12), +4.1 (18), +7.5 (23), +9.8 (cap 30). Rounds
     12-15 give the same.
   - c_r 1-5: +1.1, +1.1, +2.3, +3.3, +5.0.
   - c_r 6-9: +0.2 to +2.4.
   - c_r 10-12 at the fines of ~4 the window rule gives them: +0.2 to +0.4.

   The others' c rises +0.8 to +1.4 after a zero is fined. The response is
   **convex** in fine size for zeros: the return per fine point rises from 0.14 to
   0.33. That is the opposite of the window players. It also explains why every S
   profile falls past 8. Extending the window spends most of its bill on 10-13
   contributors (+0.3 per ~4 points), while the few zeros it fines at the cap would
   pay.
4. **Is it persistent, and is it worth its cost?** g6_late_persist.py: c_r = 0 at
   r 9..13, counterfactual fine >= 24, S = r against S = r-1 (2769 fined, 3316
   controls).
   - Own effect by lag 1..10: +10.3, 9.8, 9.4, 9.2, 8.7, 8.5, 8.1, 7.9, 7.7, 7.4.
   - The others' effect **grows**: +0.61 at lag 1 to +2.88 at lag 10.
   - 18% of the fined are back at c <= 2 (66% of controls).
   - Chronic zeros (c <= 5 three rounds earlier) respond about half as much (+6.5 ->
     +4.2). Recent collapsers respond +11 -> +8.
   - c_r 1-3: +6 -> +4.5 own, +0.5 -> +1.8 others.

   Accounting for one cap fine on a zero with 10 rounds left: own ~87 points plus
   others ~53, so 140 c points x 0.15 = +21 score, against 15 for the fine. Net ~+6
   an event. So a **late floor** pays: after the window, fine only players at (or
   next to) zero, at the cap, while enough rounds remain. No earlier version tested
   this on real data; v2 left it out for "unknown persistence", and v3 screened only
   window-shaped maintenance on surrogates. Next: per-round value V(r) to set the
   last late round, and how often the event happens (the size of the prize).
5. **When does a late cap fine stop paying, and how often is it needed?**
   g7_late_value.py: net value = 0.15 x (own effect + 3 x others' effect, summed
   over every round to 23) - fine/2, with the fine ~28.5.
   - c_r = 0: +16.9 (r 9-10), +10.0 (11-12), +6.5 (13-14), +0.8 (15-16).
   - c_r 1-2: +11.0, +5.0, +0.5, +2.4 (n 44-212, noisy).

   The own part alone is 17.0, 14.6, 13.0, 11.1 against a cost of ~14.3, so the
   spillover to the others is what makes it pay. Others rise gradually, +0.6 ->
   +2.9 over ten lags, which looks like conditional cooperators answering the
   zero's return rather than the side fines of the window rule at r (~4 per group,
   worth ~+0.3). Frequency under v6 (no late fines): zero player-rounds per game
   go 0.05, 0.07, 0.08, 0.10, 0.12, 0.13 over rounds 9-14, and 24% of games have a
   zero somewhere in 9-15. So the prize is ~0.3-0.5 events a game at ~+10, roughly
   +3 to +5 on the score. That is several times anything left in the window family.
   I expect the last useful late round to be ~14-15 for zeros, and earlier if the
   floor also takes 1-2 contributors.
6. **Fining a non-responder again.** g8_repeat.py: zeros at r who were at 0 at r-1
   after a fine >= 24, S = r (fined again) against S = r-1. The second fine still
   gives +7.0 next round. The net is +11.3 (r 6-9) and +2.8 (r 10-14). So there is
   no case for a give-up count: a repeat fine on a zero still pays while rounds are
   left, which fits the earlier versions' finding that give-up does not pay.
7. **Drafted v7: v4's window plus a late floor.**
   - Rounds 0..S: v4's best-known point, `A + B*(T - c)` for c < T, with T 13.29,
     A 2.43, B 1.76, S 8, all fixed. It is the best paired final of six versions,
     and the sweeps have pinned that family to within ~1 (v6 entries 6-9). Spending
     the 256 points on it again would only add noise to the GP's peak.
   - Rounds S+1..E: only players at c <= F, a near-zero, are fined, with L.
   - L is fixed at 30, the cap. The zeros' response is convex (entry 3: +9.8 at 30
     against +7.5 at 23), so a smaller late fine loses value per point.
   - Swept: F int [0, 4] (I expect 0-2) and E int [11, 18] (I expect 14-15).
8. **Validation, and a change to F and E.** With F and E as int ranges, validate-rule
   merged the design into 40 distinct points (5 x 8), so 216 of the 256 points
   would have been lost. I made both floats with the same meaning on whole
   contributions and rounds: a late fine for c < F, in rounds S+1 up to but not
   including E. F [0.5, 4.5] fines c <= 0..4 and E [11.5, 18.5] ends the floor
   after rounds 11..18, each integer cell with an equal share. Now 256 distinct
   points; in effect each cell is replicated ~6 times on different seeds, which
   suits a GP on a stepwise surface. validate-rule passes. In a fresh process,
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-11/t7_rule_check_v7.py (F 2.5, E 15.5) gives:
   - rounds 0 and 8: v4's schedule (c=0 -> 25.8, c=2 -> 22.3, c=3 -> 20.5,
     c=13 -> 2.9, c=16/20 -> 0);
   - rounds 9 and 15: 30 for c=0 and c=2, 0 for c=3, c=13 and a timed-out zero;
   - rounds 16 and 23: all 0.

## Key findings

1. v6 lost 0.54 (paired se 0.38) to v4_gp on the shared final games. v4 still holds
   the best paired final (649.88) of six versions of the opening-window family,
   and that family is pinned to within ~1 (1).
2. The post-window slide (mean c 17.0 at round 8 -> 13.7 at round 23) is mostly
   collapses. 12% of players fall by 10+ points and make up -1.86 of the -2.8
   drop, and the share at c <= 5 rises from 2.5% to 15%. Collapsed players stay
   near zero unaided (2).
3. On the S natural experiment with matched counterfactual fines, a late fine on a
   zero contributor works, and its effect grows faster than the fine: own c over
   the next 6 rounds +1.7 at a fine of 12, +4.1 at 18, +7.5 at 23 and +9.8 at 30.
   The window rule's late fines of ~4 on 10-12 contributors buy only +0.2-0.4,
   which is why extending the window never paid (3).
4. The cap fine's effect persists: own +10.3 at lag 1 and still +7.4 at lag 10,
   while the other three players' c rises +0.6 -> +2.9 over the same lags. Only
   18% of the fined zeros fall back to c <= 2, against 66% of controls (4).
5. The net score value of one cap fine on a zero is +16.9 at rounds 9-10, +10.0
   at 11-12, +6.5 at 13-14 and +0.8 at 15-16. For c 1-2 it is +11.0 and +5.0
   early, ~0 from round 13. Zeros occur in ~24% of games in rounds 9-15, so the
   prize is ~+3-5 (5).
6. Fining again a zero who ignored a cap fine still pays: +7.0 next round, net
   +11.3 at rounds 6-9 and +2.8 at 10-14. A give-up count is not needed (6).

## Hypothesis

A good manager does two different jobs at two times. In the opening (rounds 0-8)
it builds the cooperative regime with a predictable price on any shortfall below
a moderate standard. That part is settled: v4's schedule. After the opening it
stops policing the many players who drift a little below the standard, because
small fines on them buy almost nothing. But it does not look away from a
collapse. A clone who drops to (near) zero stays there, and the others slowly
follow. The cheapest repair is one fine at the cap on that player alone. It lifts
the player by ~8-10 points for the rest of the game and pulls the others back up
with them. This pays only while enough rounds are left, so the floor ends a few
rounds before the end. The rule: in rounds t <= S, `A + B*(T - c)` for c < T; in
rounds S < t < E, `L` for valid players with c < F; else 0.
- `T` 13.29, `A` 2.43, `B` 1.76, `S` 8: the opening, fixed at v4's best-known
  point.
- `F`, the late floor: fine late only contributions below F. Sweep [0.5, 4.5],
  uniform, so each of c <= 0..4 gets an equal share.
- `E`, the floor's end: late fines in rounds 9 up to E (not included). Sweep
  [11.5, 18.5], uniform, so the last fined round runs 11..18.
- `L`, the late fine: fixed at 30, the cap.

### Justification

- Fixing the opening at v4's values rests on finding 1. Six versions of sweeps put
  that family's ceiling within ~1 of v4's point. The sweep's points are better
  spent on the new lever than on re-finding it.
- The late floor exists because of findings 2-4: the slide is collapses, collapses
  do not heal unaided, and a cap fine heals them for good and spreads to the others.
- `L` = 30 comes from finding 3: on zeros, the return per point rises with the
  fine up to the cap.
- `F`: finding 5 says zeros pay clearly and 1-2 contributors pay early. In g5, at
  the cap, c 1-5 gives +5.0 own against +9.8 for zeros, so 3-5 are likely near
  break-even. I expect F's best at 1-3 (c <= 0..2). The range takes c <= 4 to
  keep the best value off the top edge, and c = 0 only at the bottom.
- `E`: by finding 5, a zero's fine is worth +6.5 at rounds 13-14 and ~+1 at 15-16.
  So I expect the last fined round at ~14-15 (E ~15-16), earlier if F is high.
  [11.5, 18.5] brackets it by 3-4 rounds on each side.
- Finding 6 is why there is no give-up count.

What would contradict it:
- the sweep's best points at F < 1 together with E at 11-12, which would mean a
  late fine never pays on the clones (my natural-experiment accounting
  overstated the spillover);
- a final below v4's 649.88 on the paired games, given that the opening is
  identical to v4's.

The paired comparison is clean. On the final's seed the opening is v4's exactly,
so any difference comes from the floor.

Not modelled:
- The size of the late fine (cap, finding 3).
- A strike limit (finding 6).
- A distinction between recent and chronic zeros: chronic zeros respond about
  half as much, and the repeat-fine value is still positive (4, 6).
- Raising the opening's zero fine from 25.8 to the cap. v5 found it neutral, and
  it would change the known-good opening.
- Timeouts. A timed-out player is never fined; the code checks `valid`.

My estimates come from fines given under the window rule, where others in the
group were also fined lightly in the same round (~4 points a group). That may
overstate the spillover a little. The own-effect alone breaks even at rounds 9-12,
so the floor's worst case is about neutral.
