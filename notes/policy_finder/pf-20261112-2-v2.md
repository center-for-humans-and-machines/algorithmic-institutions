# pf-20261112-2-v2

## Explorations

1. **What did version 1 find, and what does the score really measure?** Read v1's notes, rule and sweep (`sweep.json`, `landscapes.json`, `final/sweep.json`). v1 assumed the score is a per-capita pool lead; the sweep's own `score` text says otherwise: per round each group's pool is 1.6*sum(c) - sum(p) (a total, empty group 0). So group size counts fully. v1's fitted peak sat at base = slope = 0, i.e. "never punish", and its final scored +16.76 +- 1.42 (own 62.1 vs ah 45.4; members 4.63 vs 3.37). The best raw sweep point (s245, a tiny fine) re-ran at 14.8, so nothing in v1 beat plain non-punishment; the top raw points (18-21) were noise (point SE ~2.9). So the bar for v2 is +16.8, and most of it comes from members (4.63 vs 3.37), not per-capita pool. Next: how spending trades contributions against members across v1's sweep points.

2. **How does spending trade off against members in v1's sweep?** Looked at the v1 per-round sweep data (both parquet parts, my group vs ah's, per point and seat) with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-2/v2_sweep_summary.py (approximate: empty group-rounds dropped, so levels are lower than sweep.json, differences are fine). Found per-capita contribution in my group rises steadily with spend (8.2 at spend <0.05 per member-round, 9.1 at 0.4-0.7, 9.9 at 1-1.5, 11.6 above 2.5) but members fall (4.90 -> 4.72 -> 4.55 -> 4.18) and the score falls with them (12.8 -> 11.2 -> 8.6 -> -1.6). At spend ~0.5 the per-capita net gain (+0.9*1.6 - 0.5 = +0.9) is outweighed by losing ~0.2 members of ~13 pool each. So membership is the lever. Also read the switch model's config (`configs/training/artificial_humans/switch_predictor/joint_exodus.yml`) and `environment.py`: it sees each player's own punishment, the *per-capita* common good of the player's group (1.6*sum c - sum p)/valid, the group label, the round, through an edge model (so the other group's numbers too) and an RNN fed every round. Next: which round's punishment and pool gap drive leaving and joining at a decision.
3. **What drives leaving and joining at a decision?** Pulled every valid player's decision-round record out of v1's sweep (4.2M records) with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-2/v2_switch_drivers.py and regressed (linear probability) the switch on own contribution, the per-capita common-good gap (mine - ah's) at the decision round and averaged over the block, own punishment at lags 0-3 and my group's size, in scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-2/v2_switch_analyse.py. Found the block-average per-capita gap is the main driver, symmetric: -1.1 pp leaving my group and +1.1 pp leaving ah's group per point of gap (plus ~0.3 pp for the decision round's own gap). Raw rates: members of my group leave 55% when my per-capita pool trails by >10, 13% when it leads by >10; ah's members leave 13% vs 60% in the same bins. Own punishment adds +1.5 pp per point at the decision round, +1.1 at lag 1, +0.55 at lag 2, ~0 (-0.3) at lag 3 (the first round of the block). Own contribution barely matters once the gap is in (-0.04 pp/point). So members follow the per-capita pool gap, and every punishment point lowers that gap one-for-one per capita while also pushing its target out if it falls late in the block. Next: a clean estimate of what punishing one player is worth in total pool, using v1's threshold as a regression discontinuity.
4. **A regression discontinuity at v1's threshold (dead end).** Compared members just below v1's threshold (fined) with those just above, with a slope correction, on their future value to my pool, in scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-2/v2_rd.py and v2_rd_analyse.py. The jumps were all over the place (-34 for fine 1, -7 for fine 2, -70 for fine 3; +18 to +29 for thresholds 2-11, -71 for 11-14): the comparison mixes points whose contribution levels mean different things, and the others' future value dominates the noise. Only one pattern looked consistent: fines in rounds 20-22, after the last switch, were the only ones with a positive total (+3.3), earliest fines the worst (-43). I do not trust the magnitudes. Next: a cleaner response estimate.

5. **How much contribution does a fine buy, and what does it do to the others?** In v1's sweep, conditional on a member's contribution, whether and how much it is fined varies with the random design point, so I regressed its contribution 1-3 rounds later (same group) on its fine, with contribution dummies, previous contribution, the others' mean, group size, its previous fine and round dummies, plus the fines on the others, in scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-2/v2_resp.py. Found per point of fine: +0.07/+0.12/+0.15 (lags 1/2/3) for zero-givers, +0.16/+0.21/+0.25 for givers of 1-4, +0.18/+0.21/+0.25 for 5-8, +0.16/+0.19/+0.25 for 9-12, the effect building over the lags. But each point of fine on someone else *lowers* each other member's next contributions by 0.01-0.02 (0.045 for givers of 17-20). Summing up for a group of five: a fine of F buys ~+0.2F a round from the target and costs ~-0.08F a round from the rest, so ~+0.2F of pool per round; it repays F over ~5 rounds before any membership effect, and the membership effect (entry 3) is negative. Also from v1's points: at equal spend, a later t_stop is better (residual correlation +0.22). So punishment as a contribution lever is about break-even in this stack, which is why v1's sweep peaked at no punishment. Next: is there a use of punishment that works through membership instead.

6. **Can a fine push a free-rider out cheaply, and is that worth it?** From the decision-round records of entry 3, switch-out rate of my members by their block-average contribution and their fine at the decision round itself (same script data, ad hoc pivot). Found that for block free-riders (block mean 0-1) the rate is 27% unfined, 33% at a fine of 1, 36% at 2, 41% at 2-4, 48% at 4-8, 57% above 8; for givers of 12-20 18% -> 44%. Concave, first point buys ~7 pp. Reasoning through the totals: a free-rider adds ~0 to my total pool but drags my per-capita pool down by ~1/n of it (about 3 points in a group of five), and entry 3 says every point of per-capita gap moves ~1.1 pp of leaving and joining for every player; once he is in `ah`'s group he lowers its per-capita pool and `ah` spends fines on him. The cost of a decision-round fine is the fine itself, and its effect on the gap that the switch model weighs is small (one round of the block, divided by n). So the hypothesis for v2: punish nothing except chronic free-riders, and only in the last round(s) before a switch, to show them the door; a fine in round 4k (first of the block) does nothing for leaving (lag-3 coefficient ~0) and is wasted. Next: draft that rule.
7. **Drafted the rule: "show free-riders the door".** A member of my group is fined a flat `fine` only if its mean contribution in my group since the block began (rounds 4k..t, valid rounds only) and its contribution this round are both below `thr`, only in the last `w` rounds of a block (ending at the decision round 3, 7, 11, 15, 19), and only up to the `n_dec`-th decision; never after round 19. Changes from v1 and why: v1 fined every round, which spends where neither lever works (first rounds of a block: no effect on leaving, entry 3; contribution response about break-even, entry 5); v2 concentrates spending where the switch model reacts to own fines (decision round +1.5 pp/point, the round before +1.1) and only on players whose presence drags my per-capita pool down (entry 6). The current-round condition stops it from fining someone who has just reformed (fining cooperators backfires, v1 entry 2). `fine` < 1 floors to no punishment at all, so the design contains v1's winner (never punish) as a region of ~10% of the points, which keeps the bar visible on the fitted surface.

8. **Does the rule do what I meant, and what would it spend?** Ran it on a hand-made history and on the `zero`-managed groups of the reference sims (static, no response) with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-2/v2_rule_test.py. It fines the chronic zero-giver in the last w rounds of each block, spares the reformed player and the timed-out round, and stops after round 4*n_dec-1. Found an engine trap on the way: `th.remainder` inside the rule's no-builtins scope raised `KeyError: '__import__'` on its first call in a fresh process (validate-rule had passed, probably because the module was already loaded there), so I replaced it with `t - 4*floor(t/4)`. Share of members flagged at a decision round: 6.5% (thr 1), 18% (thr 3), 24% (thr 4), 37% (thr 6), 57% (thr 10), with ~4.8 members per group. So thr 3, fine 4, w 1, n_dec 5 spends ~17 points a game (~0.15 per member-round, a tenth of v1's mid points), while the top corner of a thr range up to 10 would spend ~550 points a game; I cut thr to [0.5, 8] (above 8 it fines ordinary givers, whom v1 found backfire) and kept fine [0, 10] so the surface still has a bad region to bend down into. Revalidated: valid, 256 distinct points.

## Key findings

1. The score is a lead in *total* pool per round (1.6*sum c - sum p, empty group 0), not per capita, so members count fully; v1's sweep peaked at "never punish" and its final scored +16.76 +- 1.42, driven by members (4.63 vs 3.37) (exploration 1).
2. Across v1's points, per-capita contributions rise with spend (8.2 -> 11.6) but members fall (4.90 -> 4.18) and the score falls with them (12.8 -> -1.6 in my approximate measure); at equal spend, later stopping does better (exploration 2, 5).
3. Switching follows the block-average per-capita common-good gap: about -1.1 pp leaving my group and +1.1 pp leaving `ah`'s per point of gap; own fines push out at +1.5 pp/point at the decision round, +1.1 one round before, ~0 at the block's first round (exploration 3).
4. As a contribution lever a fine is about break-even: +0.16-0.25 per point from the target over the next 1-3 rounds, but -0.01 to -0.045 per point from every other member (exploration 5).
5. For block free-riders a decision-round fine raises leaving from 27% to 33% (1 point), 41% (2-4), 48% (4-8), 57% (8+): concave, the first point buys most (exploration 6).
6. In `zero`-managed groups 6.5-24% of members are block free-riders at a decision round for thresholds 1-4, so a door-showing rule spends ~0.1-0.3 per member-round, far below v1's 0.5-2 (exploration 8).

## Hypothesis

In this stack punishment does not pay as a way to raise contributions (it roughly breaks even and its costs show up in the per-capita gap that steers switching), so the default is not to punish. The one use that can beat plain non-punishment is membership: a chronic free-rider adds nothing to my total pool but lowers my per-capita pool, which steers players away from my group and toward `ah`'s. A small fine right at a switch decision pushes him out where the switch model is most sensitive to own fines, and is cheap because it lands only on few players in one or two rounds of the block. Once he is in `ah`'s group he lowers its per-capita pool and draws `ah`'s fines. So: fine only chronic free-riders, only just before a switch, and stop when switching is over.

The rule (`configs/managers/rule_based/pf-20261112-2-v2.yml`): a valid member of my group is fined `fine` if its mean contribution in my group since the block began and its contribution this round are both below `thr`, the round is in the last `w` rounds of its block, and t <= 4*n_dec - 1; otherwise 0.
- `thr` (float, [0.5, 8]): free-rider line. Expect 1.5-4.
- `fine` (float, [0, 10]): flat fine; below 1 means no punishment, so the design contains "never punish". Expect 1-5.
- `w` (int, [1, 4]): fining rounds per block, ending at the decision round. Expect 1-2.
- `n_dec` (int, [1, 5]): the number of switch decisions with fining before them. Expect 3-5.

### Justification

Finding 1 sets the bar and the target (members, total pool); finding 2 shows broad punishment loses members, hence frugal, targeted fining. `w`: finding 3 shows own fines push people out only late in the block (1.5 / 1.1 / 0.55 / ~0 pp per point at lags 0-3), so fines early in a block buy no exit and the contribution return is about nil (finding 4); w = 1-2 should win, w = 4 is in the range as v1's every-round style for contrast. `thr`: the gap mechanism of finding 3 values pushing out players far below the group's mean; finding 6 says thr 1-4 flags 6-24% of members, a cheap target, while above 8 it hits ordinary givers. `fine`: finding 5's concave exit response says the first points buy most, but a fine at the decision round barely moves the block-average gap (one round, divided by n), so a few points may still pay; [0, 10] covers none to heavy, and the floor gives a no-punishment region. `n_dec`: an exit is worth more the more rounds remain (and finding 2's later-stop residual argues against stopping too early), so I expect fining before most decisions; [1, 5] covers both. It would be contradicted if the sweep's peak sat at fine < 1 (no punishment at all, as in v1), or if w = 4 beat w = 1-2. I did not model a group-relative threshold, conditioning on the current gap or group size, or endgame fines in rounds 20-23 (entry 4's hint of a positive late effect was not credible next to entry 5's break-even response); each would thin the 256-point design for an effect I could not measure cleanly.
