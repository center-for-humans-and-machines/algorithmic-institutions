Next I want to see, in the v2 games, who exactly gets punished at good points, and what the shape of the response looks like against distance below the norm.

2. **How do contributions respond to punishment by distance to the norm, in the v2 games?** Built arrays of the v2 sweep (scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-11/d1_arrays.py, any sweep dir) and tabulated next-round change of members who stay, by c minus the others' mean and by punishment, on 40000 games, with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-11/d2_response.py. Most of the movement is regression to the norm: unpunished, players 8+ below rise 3.7, players 2-3 below 0.5, players 5+ above fall 2.3. Punishment adds on top roughly in proportion to the gap: 8+ below gains +2.5 more at p 20-30 (about 0.1 per point), 3-5 below +1.8, 1-2 below only +0.9 and it saturates at p ~10. Within 1 of the norm and above it punishment does nothing for contributions (-0.12 vs +0.04). So the punishment that pays is concentrated on players well below the norm, with diminishing returns near it, which matches v2's delta -2 tolerance with a high rate. Next: where the members go, i.e. who leaves my group and who joins, at the v2 final point and per opponent.

Hypothesis forming: front-load, with severity decaying from block to block, and spend less in the second round of each block, where it is close to the switch and the first round already did most of the work.

6. **Should newcomers get a grace round?** Tested with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-11/d5_newcomers.py on 40000 v2 games (blocks 2-5; 31% of members at a block's start are new). Newcomers leave more overall (logit +0.21) but are, if anything, less sensitive to punishment (pm 0.072, pm x new -0.022). Their next-round response to punishment when 2+ below the norm is the same (0.023 per point, interaction -0.009). So no case for sparing newcomers; dead end, not modelled.

7. **Drafted the v3 rule.** Kept v2's core and settled its edges, then added time structure.
   - Kept: shortfall below the others' mean plus `delta`, times `rate`; nothing in the decision round (pos 3) or the round before it (pos 2) in blocks 1-5, v2's best `late` = 0 now hard-coded; the end fade min(1, (23 - t)/3) in rounds 20-23.
   - Dropped: `kappa` (entries 1, 4: worse against every opponent), `floor` (flat, effect 0.4). A member alone in the group has no norm and is not punished.
   - New `decay`: severity is rate * decay^b in block b = floor(t/4) (0-5). Entry 5: the return on a point falls with the rounds left, and the front-loading opponents do as well as v2 in the same games. decay = 1 is v2.
   - New `mid`: multiplier for the second round of each block (rounds 1, 5, ..., 17). The pool loses 20-40 points in each punishing round (entry 5), and exits weigh recent punishment more (v2 notes). mid = 1 is v2.
   So v2's peak (rate 6.9, delta -2, decay 1, mid 1) lies inside the new space, and the sweep can only find it or better.

## Key findings

1. Version 2 is the best so far at 66.9 (v1 65.1). Its surface is dominated by `delta`: 67.6 at -2, 63.2 at 0, 28.9 at +6. Rate has an interior peak near 6.4, `late` and `kappa` were best at 0 (their edges) and `floor` was flat. Opponent 4, which never punishes, is still the weak pairing at 59.0 (entry 1).
2. In the simulator, punishment lifts contributions only of players clearly below the others' mean, roughly 0.1 per point for those 5+ below. It saturates at ~+0.9 for players 1-2 below and does nothing within 1 of the norm or above it. Most of the movement is regression to the norm anyway (entry 2).
3. The deduction is a large cost: v2 deals ~2.5 per head per round, ~10 pool per round. The pool is 26-37 in rounds 0-1 against 66-69 in rounds 2-3 (entries 3, 5).
4. Leaving rises with block-mean punishment (logit +0.053 per point) and falls with group size (-0.24 per member), with no size x punishment interaction. Newcomers are no more sensitive to punishment than incumbents (entries 4, 6).
5. No v2 lever moved the pool against opponent 4, and following the opponent's severity (`kappa`) lowered the pool against every opponent (entry 4).
6. Opponents 1 and 3 front-load punishment and fade to 0 by round 16, and their groups score as much as v2's in the same games (68.8 vs 68.3; 71.2 vs 71.8). A back-of-envelope return on a point of punishment falls from ~5.5 against ~5.4 of cost at round 0 to ~1.7 against ~2.3 at round 16 (entry 5).

## Hypothesis

A good manager in this simulator holds members to the group's own norm, with a small tolerance band below it. Beyond the band it punishes in proportion to the shortfall and hard, because that is where punishment moves contributions. It spends that discipline where it pays: early in the game, when the rounds left are many enough to repay the deduction and the exits, and early in each block, away from the switch decision. So severity should fall from block to block. The second round of a block may be worth less than the first, and the two rounds before each switch stay silent, as v1 and v2 found. Paying 10 pool points a round for discipline that only returns over many rounds is too much late in the game, and the front-loading opponents show that a manager that fades does as well.

The rule: punishment = rate * decay^b * max(0, others' mean + delta - c) * w_pos * min(1, (23 - t)/3), with b = floor(t/4). w_pos is 1 in the first round of a block, `mid` in its second and 0 in its third and fourth (blocks 0-4), and 1 throughout the last block.
- `rate` (float, [2, 20] log): points per point of shortfall in block 0. I expect 6-12. With decay below 1 the sweep may push the early rate above v2's 6.9, and the cap of 30 makes values near 20 nearly equivalent.
- `delta` (float, [-5, 0.5]): the tolerance band relative to the norm. I expect -3 to -1.5, around v2's -2. The surface falls sharply above 0, so the range stops at 0.5.
- `decay` (float, [0.4, 1.2]): the multiplier per block. I expect 0.65-0.9. At 1 the rule is v2, and above 1 it would escalate late, which the range allows to test.
- `mid` (float, [0, 1.5]): the multiplier for the second round of a block. I expect 0.3-1, uncertain; above 1 it would shift discipline toward the second round.

### Justification

Finding 2 keeps the relative target with a tolerance band (`delta`) and a steep proportional `rate`, and finding 1 centres both on v2's peak. Findings 3 and 6 motivate `decay`: the deduction is costly, its return falls with the rounds left, and front-loaded managers match v2 head to head. Findings 3 and 4 motivate `mid`: each punishing round costs 20-40 pool, and exits weigh recent punishment, so the second round of a block may be the costliest per point of gain. The hard zeros in a block's last two rounds come from v1's `lam` and v2's `late`, both best at 0 (finding 1). At decay = mid = 1 the rule is v2's peak exactly (entry 8), so the sweep can find v2 again if the new structure does not help.

These sweep results would contradict the hypothesis:
- `decay` at or above 1 would mean late discipline pays in the simulator after all.
- `decay` at its 0.4 edge would mean punishment after the first block is barely worth anything.
- `mid` at 0 would favour a single sharp round per block, and `mid` at 1.5 would mean the second round is the better moment.

What I chose not to model:
- Reacting to the other manager (`kappa` failed, finding 5).
- Group-size scaling (finding 4 shows no interaction).
- Grace for newcomers (finding 4).
- An absolute floor (flat in v2).
- Escalation for repeat free riders. The relative target already re-punishes them each block.

Opponent 4 remains the weak pairing, and none of these parameters targets it directly. The lower overall deduction should help there most, since against a lenient manager every point I deal is part of the payoff gap that drives switches. I could not simulate here: there are still no model weights in artifacts/.
