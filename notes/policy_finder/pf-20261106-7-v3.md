## Key findings

1. v2's final pool was 66.4 (69.0 / 66.8 / 71.0 / 58.7 against opponents 1-4), +0.9 on v1. Its surface was flat in horizon, budget and dec; only c0 and k mattered. It stopped punishing after round 6. Opponents 1 and 3 overtook its group late (76.8 vs 73.9 and 83.0 vs 77.2 in rounds 20-23), and their contributions kept rising to 12.1-12.6 while mine stalled near 11.5 (exploration 1).
2. Both of those opponents punish free riders hard again at the start of each later block, less each block (opponent 3: 30/16 to 0-4/5-9 contributors in rounds 0-1, 28/11 in rounds 4-5, 20/5.7 in 8-9, 10/2.8 in 12-13) (exploration 2).
3. Across v2's design points, punishment in rounds 4-7 paid +1.1 to +1.6 pool per unit against three of four opponents and about +3 on the late pool. Rounds 1-3 did not pay (-0.1 to -0.75) (exploration 3).
4. The lift from a punishment does not fade over the game. With the design's random parameters as the source of variation, it was +0.07-0.15 per point plus a sqrt term at c(t+4) in every phase, and as large at c(t+8). The exit cost per point was about half as large in the first round of a block (0.008-0.012) as later in the block (0.013-0.019; in v1's sweep 0.013-0.020 vs 0.023-0.030) (explorations 4, 5).
5. A point of punishment gains about 0.19 pool per remaining round and costs about 1 point plus a small exit cost, so it pays with more than about 10-12 rounds left. Block-start punishment should run into the middle of the game and stop before the end (exploration 4).

## Hypothesis

A good manager punishes free riders in proportion to their shortfall, and does it mainly in the first round of each four-round block. Then the lift (which does not fade) has the most rounds to pay back, and the punishment lies furthest from the next switch decision, where it would drive members out. It follows up more mildly in the block's next rounds, never punishes right before a decision, and shrinks the punishment block by block, stopping once too few rounds remain. Newcomers arriving at a block start are met by the same treatment. It does not condition on the other manager, because the timing makes punishment cheap in members whatever the other group does.

The rule: punishment = k * max(0, c0 - c) * decay^block * pos for valid members of my group, and 0 from block `nblocks` on. pos is 1 in a block's first round, follow in its second, follow^2 in its third and 0 in its last (follow^2 in round 23).
- `c0` (int, [6, 16]): the expected contribution. I expect 10-13; v2 peaked at 11, and punishment backfires above ~15.
- `k` (float, [1, 15], log): points per point of shortfall in round 0. I expect 3-6 (round 0 ~10-14 per member, the v1/v2 optimum). The range runs from mild to the 30 cap for most free riders.
- `decay` (float, [0.1, 1]): the per-block shrink factor. I expect 0.4-0.8, near opponent 3's 0.5-0.7. At 0.1 it is v2's round-0-only shock; at 1 it does not shrink.
- `follow` (float, [0, 1]): the second round's share of the block-start punishment. I expect 0.1-0.5, since rounds 1-2 paid little in v2. At 0 the rule punishes only block starts; at 1 it punishes the first three rounds of each block equally.
- `nblocks` (int, [1, 6]): how many blocks punish at all. I expect 3-4 (punishment until round 11 or 15, finding 5). At 1 it is v2-like; at 6 it never stops.

### Justification

Findings 2 and 3 are why later blocks are punished at all. Two of the four opponents do it and overtake v2 late, and the design points that punished in rounds 4-7 scored higher. Finding 4 gives the block structure: the lift is the same in any round, while the exit cost is lowest in the block's first round. That makes `follow` a fraction and the decision round zero (v2's dec optimum was 0). Finding 5 sets `decay` and `nblocks`: payback shrinks with the rounds left, and the shortfalls to correct shrink as contributions rise. `c0` and `k` keep v2's shortfall form, whose optimum (finding 1) sits inside their ranges. The ranges nest v2's shape (decay 0.1 or nblocks 1), so the sweep can fall back to it.

What would contradict the hypothesis: a best nblocks of 1 or a decay near 0.1, meaning later-block punishment does not pay after all (finding 3 was observational across v2's points). A best follow near 1 would mean the block position does not matter. A pool next to opponent 4 still far below the rest would mean the punishment gap drives players to the non-punisher whatever the timing.

What I chose not to model:
- v2's saturation by punishment already received. Its effect was the smallest (1.2), and the per-block decay already lowers repeat punishment.
- A threshold relative to the group's mean. Opponents 1 and 3 do well with absolute bands.
- Separate treatment of newcomers, or any response to the other manager's punishment. v1's relative cap was weak and hurt against the non-punisher.
- Rewards, which are impossible.
The response numbers come from simulated games of earlier versions, within-cell but still observational, so the ranges are kept wide.
