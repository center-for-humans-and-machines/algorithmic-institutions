# pf-20261105-11-v2

## Explorations

1. **What did version 1 get, and where did its sweep stop short?** Read v1's notes,
   rule and plots/simulation/policy_finder/pf-20261105-11_sweep/ (sweep.json,
   landscapes.json, final/sweep.json). v1 punishes `P*((T-c)/T)^g` below a target T
   and stops punishing in the last E rounds. Its final peak was T=16.1, P=25.3,
   g=1.15, E=10, with a score of 648.8 (se 1.4) on 2000 episodes. For comparison,
   `zero` gets ~586 and `humanlike` ~554. The landscape is interior in T (peak 16:
   624 at 6, 639 at 20), in P (peak ~26: 570 at P~5, 583 at P=1) and in g (peak ~1.1:
   601 at 0.25, 633 at 2.5). E was **at the edge**: the fitted score climbs
   linearly, ~2.3 per extra silent round, from 628 at E=1 to 648 at E=10. So harsh,
   nearly linear deficit punishment works, and stopping early is better than late.
   Nobody tested stopping before round 13. Next, I want to see what happens to
   contributions around the stop.
2. **Dynamics of v1's best point, round by round.**
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-11/b1_v1_final_rounds.py on final/per_round.parquet.
   - Round 0: c=11.5. Punishment is 7.75 per player (73% punished) and the payoff is 11.4.
   - Contributions climb fast: 15.0 at round 2, 16.8 at round 5, a plateau of ~17.5 from
     round 8.
   - Punishment falls to ~1.2-1.3 per player-round on the plateau.
   - After the stop (round 14) c only decays slowly: 17.6 to 15.4 by round 23, about
     -0.22/round, against `zero`'s 11.4 to 5.3. Payoff is ~30 in the silent rounds,
     ~28 on the punished plateau.
   So the clones carry the cooperative regime on for many rounds after the stick is
   gone. The plateau's bill, ~2.5 payoff per round, buys only a halt to a 0.22/round
   decay. Next, I want to check this across the sweep's stop rounds, not just this
   one point.
3. **Is the post-stop decay the same whatever the stop round?**
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-11/b2_sweep_stop.py, over the 13 strong v1 sweep points
   (P>=15, T>=12, 0.7<=g<=2), by last punished round 13..22.
   - On the plateau c drifts ~+0.01..0.08/round, at a bill of 1.1-1.5 per player.
   - After the stop c decays at 0.19-0.26/round for every stop round, after a lag of
     1-2 rounds.
   - Contributions plateau by round ~8-10.

   That gives a back-of-envelope rule for the last punished round r. One more
   plateau round costs ~2.6 payoff now. It delays the decay by a round, worth about
   0.6*0.23*(23-r) = 0.14*(23-r) later. Break-even is at ~19 rounds left, around
   round 4-5. Before the plateau (rounds 0-7) punishment is still building
   contributions, so it is worth more there. My guess is that the best stop lies
   around rounds 5-10, well below v1's earliest possible stop (13). This fits the
   landscape's linear E gain, ~2.3/round near round 13, which my calculation puts
   at 2.6-1.4=1.2, the same order. The caveat: a regime stopped before it is
   established might decay faster. I cannot simulate here (no model weights in the
   container), so the stop round must be a wide sweep parameter. Next, I want to see
   who pays the bill on the plateau.
4. **Who pays the bill, and who responds?**
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-11/b3_bill_by_c.py, v1 best point, by phase and
   contribution bin, next-round change punished vs unpunished.
   - On the plateau (rounds 9-13), the 1.2% of players at c=0 take 26% of the bill
     (25 points each). They jump +12.5 next round, against +2.6 for unpunished zeros
     after the stop.
   - The 11% at c=15 get 1 point each, from flooring a tiny shortfall, and 9% of
     the bill. They respond +0.33 against -0.12 unpunished. That is the best return
     per point (~0.45/pt), so the bare signal works.
   - The middle bins (c 4-9: +4.5 vs +3.9) return the least per point (~0.05-0.17/pt).
   - Unpunished 20s drift down -0.5/round.
   - After the stop, the share at c<=9 rises from 4.5% to 11.5% and the 20s fall
     from 49% to 41%. That accounts for about half of the decay.

   So v1's power curve with g~1.15 behaves like a linear price per shortfall point
   (~1.5/pt) plus a 1-point "tap" for any shortfall, which the floor creates by
   accident. I want that tap as an explicit parameter, so the sweep can size the
   signal and the price separately.
5. **Drafted v2.** Rule: for c < T, `p = A + B*(T - c)`, only in rounds t <= S, 0 otherwise.
   - `T`, target, [10, 20].
   - `A`, the tap any shortfall gets, [0, 6].
   - `B`, the price per point of shortfall, [0.5, 3.5] log.
   - `S`, the last punished round, int [2, 16].

   I replaced v1's curvature g with the tap A. v1's optimum was close to linear
   (g=1.15), and its g<0.5 points (a flat signal of size P) did badly, 601-623. The
   tap separates the cheap signal from the steep price, and A=1, B=1.5, T=16, S=13
   approximately reproduces v1's best. The main change is S: v1 could not stop
   before round 13 and its best was at that edge. S spans 2..16 so the sweep can
   find the stop on both sides of my 5-10 guess, with 13 still inside.
   I considered a late phase that hits only free riders, since half the post-stop
   decay is players falling to <=9. But a hit on a zero costs ~50 payoff points for
   a +10 next-round jump of unknown persistence, so it would not repay in the last
   ~8 rounds. I left it out to keep the design dense.
6. **Wrote and validated the rule.** configs/managers/rule_based/pf-20261105-11-v2.yml.
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-11/t2_rule_check_v2.py runs it in a fresh process
   (to catch v1's import trap). With T=16, A=1, B=1.5, S=8 the punishments are:
   c=0 -> 25, 5 -> 17.5, 10 -> 10, 15.5 -> 1.75, 16 and 20 -> 0. Round 8 is
   punished and rounds 9 and 23 are not. validate-rule passes on all 256 design points.

## Key findings

1. Against the clones, harsh, nearly linear punishment of shortfalls below a target
   of ~16 wins clearly. v1's peak (T=16.1, P=25.3, g=1.15) scored 648.8 against
   `zero`'s ~586. Weak punishment is the worst option: v1's landscape dips to 570
   at P~5 (1).
2. The best v1 points stop punishing as early as v1 allowed. The landscape gains
   ~2.3 per extra silent round, linearly, up to the edge E=10 (last punished round
   13) (1).
3. The regime outlives the punishment. Once punishment stops, contributions decay
   only 0.19-0.26/round, whatever the stop round (13-22). Under `zero` they fall from
   11.4 to 5.3 (2, 3).
4. Contributions plateau at ~16.5-17.5 by rounds 8-10. Holding the plateau costs
   ~1.2-1.4 punishment per player-round, ~2.6 payoff, only to stop that slow decay.
   So by the arithmetic, plateau punishment repays only with ~19+ rounds left (3).
5. Most of the lift is bought in rounds 0-5: c rises from 11.5 to 16.8, at a bill
   of 7.75, 5.1, 3.6, 2.7, 2.2 and 1.8 per player (2).
6. A 1-point tap for a small shortfall returns the most per point: c=15 players
   +0.33 vs -0.12 unpunished. Full free riders respond strongly to 25 points (+12.5
   vs +2.6). Mid-range shortfalls return the least per point (4).

## Hypothesis

A good manager sets up a cooperative regime early and harshly, then leaves it to
run by itself. In the first rounds every shortfall below a target gets a visible
tap plus a steep price per missing point. That makes withholding a loss (any price
above 0.6 per point does) and lifts the group from ~11 to ~17 within a few rounds.
The clones then keep contributing for many rounds with no punishment, decaying only
~0.2 per round. Holding the plateau with a stick costs more than that decay. So the
manager should stop well before v1's earliest stop (round 13), once the lift is in
and the rounds left can no longer repay the bill. The rule:
`punishment = A + B*(T - c)` for c < T while t <= S, else 0.
- `T`, target contribution: no punishment at or above it. Sweep [10, 20], uniform.
- `A`, the tap any shortfall gets. Sweep [0, 6], uniform.
- `B`, the price per point of shortfall. Sweep [0.5, 3.5], log-uniform.
- `S`, the last punished round. Sweep int [2, 16].

### Justification

Finding 1 sets the form and the ranges of T and B. v1's optimum, T=16 with
`P*(s/T)^1.15` at P=25, is close to linear at ~1.5 per point of shortfall. I expect
T at 14-18 and B at 1.2-2.5. The ranges [10, 20] and [0.5, 3.5] reach out to where
v1's landscape had fallen by 10-25 points. At the top end the manager's cap of 30
bounds the harshest settings.

Finding 6 motivates the tap `A`. v1 got a 1-point tap by accident of flooring, and
the signal is the cheapest lift per point. I expect A at 1-3. A=0 is the pure
linear rule. Up to 6 allows a strong flat signal, though v1's flat shapes (g<0.5)
did poorly, which argues against large A.

Findings 2-5 motivate `S`. The arithmetic in 3 puts the stop at rounds ~5-10:
after the build-up (finding 5), and where the rounds left no longer repay the
plateau's bill (finding 4). The range [2, 16] gives the sweep both sides, and still
holds v1's best stop (13).

What would contradict this: a best S at 13-16 would mean the plateau bill pays
after all. A best S at 2-3 would mean the clones' carry-over works even from an
unfinished lift. A best A near 6 with low B would mean the signal, not the price,
does the work.

Not modelled:
- A late phase aimed only at free riders, to stop the post-stop slide. A hit on a
  zero costs ~50 payoff points for a +10 next-round response of unknown
  persistence (4).
- Targets relative to the group: a relative target follows a decaying group down.
- A per-player history. The clones' response does not build on past punishment
  (v1's notes).
- Timeouts: they are rare (~2%) and cannot be punished.
- Curvature: v1 found it near 1, so it went in favour of the tap, which keeps the
  design at four parameters.
