2. **What makes contributions decay once punishment stops?** scripts/policy_finder/

## Key findings

1. The v3 family sits at ~652-654: v3's final is 651.8, and its other re-checked points
   scored 652.5-654.1. v3's landscape peaked at four edges: standard 24 (top), horizon 5
   (bottom), floor 1 (only zero contributors) and tail 12 (main phase plus tail ending
   at round ~17) (exploration 1).
2. Without punishment, contributions decay about 0.26 per round. Most of that is a
   diffuse drift of the high contributors toward the group (players at 15-21 lose
   0.65/round; players above the others' mean by 5+ lose 1.45). Collapses to zero
   explain only about a quarter of it. So a late phase can only lift the group's lowest
   player, and must not touch the high ones (exploration 2).
3. Round 0 keeps coming out differently from the other rounds. Spending points there on
   c < 10 is the best use of points in the regression over all 768 rules (+25..+33 per
   unit, se ~8), but spending them on 10-13 contributors loses (-41.8, se 13.2). The finer
   cells are too collinear to read (exploration 3).
4. In the late phase, a free rider's first punishment is worth more than a repeat: next
   contribution 12.2 after a first hit, against 3.8-8.6 after a repeat. Repeats take only
   ~30% of the tail's points, so capping them is worth about 1 point at most
   (exploration 4).
5. The best standard and curvature of v2's and v3's sweeps lie on one ridge: among the
   top 20% of points they correlate 0.8, and the ratio curvature/standard (the curve's
   steepness near c = 0) varies half as much as the curvature itself. Mean score peaks at
   a ratio of 0.09-0.13 (explorations 5, 7).

## Hypothesis

A good manager makes an expensive first impression and then stays cheap. In the opening
rounds it punishes every player below a high standard. The punishment falls off steeply
with the contribution: about 30 for a free rider, a light touch just below the
standard, nothing for full contributors. The clones' raise is persistent and spreads to
the group, so these early points repay several times over. Round 0 is a separate case.
Contributions there are not yet a response to anything, and the round is the most
expensive one, so it may want a lower cut-off that spares mid contributors. After a few
rounds, punishing mid contributors no longer repays, but a player who has dropped to
(almost) nothing still lifts himself and the group enough to repay a full punishment.
So until a late round the manager punishes only such free riders, and after that nobody.
The rule: s = opening in round 0, standard later; punishment = dose * (max(s - c, 0) /
s)^(rate * s) for valid players in rounds t < horizon; the same, only for c < floor, in
rounds horizon <= t < end; 0 afterwards.
- `standard` (float, [17, 32]): the cut-off from round 1 on. I expect 22-28, since v3
  was still rising at 24.
- `rate` (float, [0.07, 0.18], log): steepness, roughly the fraction of the dose lost
  per contribution point near zero (curve exponent rate * standard). I expect 0.10-0.13.
- `dose` (float, [22, 42]): the punishment of a zero contributor, clamped at 30. I expect
  28-35.
- `opening` (float, [6, 30]): the round-0 cut-off. I expect 10-24; opening = standard
  gives v3's round 0.
- `horizon` (int, 2..10): end of the main phase. I expect 4-6, since v3 was at its
  bottom edge of 5.
- `floor` (float, [0.5, 8], log): the free-rider line after the main phase. I expect
  0.5-2, where anything up to 1 means only zero contributors.
- `end` (int, 9..24): first round with no punishment at all. I expect 15-19; 24 means
  punishing until the last round.

### Justification

Finding 1 sets the starting point: v3's structure, with its ranges moved past the edges
its landscape ran into. The standard reaches 32, the horizon goes down to 2, and the
floor goes below 1 on a log scale, so the "zeros only" region gets about a quarter of
the draws. The tail is now set by its end round, which runs from 9 to 24. Its
break-even depends on the rounds left, not on how long it lasts, and v3's peak ended it
at ~17. Finding 2 explains why the late phase targets only the lowest contributors and
why no rule can stop the late drift cheaply. Finding 5 is the reason for `rate`. The
sweep then searches the steepness and the cut-off separately instead of along a
diagonal ridge, and its range covers the ridge from standard 17 (0.099) to 32 (0.131).
Finding 3 is the reason for `opening`. The evidence on round 0 conflicts (v3's binned
dose-response said its mid-contributor touches pay), so I leave the choice to the sweep
and make the parameter nest v3 (opening = standard). The gain I expect is modest, a few
points from a cheaper round 0 and better-placed edges.

What would contradict this:
- a best `opening` near `standard`, or a flat opening profile (round 0 is not special);
- a best `end` at 24, or a best `floor` near 8 (the late phase is not about free riders);
- a best `rate` at an end of its range.

Not modelled:
- a cap on repeated late punishments (finding 4: about 1 point at most);
- a gradually falling standard. It would hand low contributors light punishments, which
  v3 found worse than none;
- group-relative standards and credit for rising (no gain in v2's tests);
- timeouts, beyond excluding timed-out players.
