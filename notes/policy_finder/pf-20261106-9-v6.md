## Hypothesis

A good manager runs an initiation: it punishes shortfalls below its group's mean hard in each
member's first rounds with the group, then stays quiet, because in the artificial humans a first
punishment lifts a member's contribution for the rest of the game while repeats and late
punishment add little and quiet rounds before switch decisions keep members. The price differs
by instrument: round 0 is cheap in members, round 1 weighs about twice as much in the first
switch decision, and newcomers, who arrive below the incumbents (most of all from a lenient
manager's group), are worth initiating hard as long as enough rounds remain. So the three
should have their own intensities, and the newcomer initiation its own horizon. This rule reads
only our members' histories, so it carries over to any other manager; against a lenient one,
where newcomers are weakest, the newcomer slope matters most.

The rule: shortfall = max(0, mean_c - c_i - s0) over valid members; punishment = slope x
shortfall (clamped to 30 by the manager) for members whose tenure with us is under 4 rounds, in
the first two rounds of a block only; slope k0 in round 0, k1 in round 1, kn in later rounds,
and newcomers only up to the arrivals at round 4*nb.
- `k0` (float, [3, 30], log): round-0 slope; I expect 8-20 (v5 flat 8-23).
- `k1` (float, [0.5, 15], log): round-1 slope; I expect 2-5 (v5's shared k1 peaked at 3.3-4.5,
  and that peak was pulled up by newcomers).
- `kn` (float, [1, 40], log): newcomers' slope in their first two rounds; I expect 8-25 (the
  gain still rose in v5's top tercile >10; 40 saturates the clamp for any shortfall above ~1).
- `s0` (float, [-0.5, 2.5]): tolerance below the mean; I expect 0.5-1.5 (v5 0.7-1.3).
- `nb` (int, 1-5): number of switch rounds whose newcomers are initiated; I expect 3-4 (3 is
  v5's t_end 16; at nb 5 a round-20 newcomer has only 4 rounds to repay).

### Justification

Finding 1 says v5's family is tuned out and frees w's dimension; finding 2 says an initiation's
gain lasts, so where to spend punishment is a question of first-time doses. Finding 3 supports
full doses for clear shortfalls (k0 high, the clamp as cap) and a tolerance s0 near +1 rather
than punishing at-mean members. Finding 4 is the reason for splitting k1 and kn: the exogenous
variation in v5's design shows newcomers' later contributions and the late pool rising with
their slope beyond 10, while the shared slope's optimum sat at ~4, which fits a round-1 cost (v5
finding 3) dragging it down; with the instruments separate the sweep can set each. nb gives the
newcomer horizon back to the sweep now that the newcomer dose is free (it was flat when tied to
a moderate k). It would be contradicted if kn's best sat at or below k1's, or if the score
against opponent 4 did not rise above v5's 61.0. I did not model the other manager's
punishments (w was flat), re-punishment of incumbents in later blocks (flat in v4), a separate
tolerance or reference mean for newcomers (the group mean already includes them and kn carries
the intensity), or the quiet rounds, fixed at 2 as in every sweep.
