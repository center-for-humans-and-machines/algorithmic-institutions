1. **Where do v1 and v2 stand, and do they differ in how they win?** Read
   both versions' notes, `landscapes.json` and `final/`, and re-ran
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-12/v1_final.py on both finals. They
   tie: v1 70.3 (4.23 members), v2 70.8 (se 1.1, 4.34 members) at thr 9.8,
   slope 5.8, decay 0.60, quiet 2. They tie by different routes. v1
   punishes flat and gets contributions up to 13.3 by the end, with fewer
   members and a punishment bill in every block (pool 50-71 in the punished
   rounds, against 85-91 in the quiet ones). v2 tapers, so its late blocks
   are almost free (pool 77-83 in rounds 12-23), but its contributions stop
   at ~11.8, no higher than `ah`'s group (12.0); its whole edge over `ah`
   is members (4.37 vs 3.63) and a smaller late bill. In both, round 0
   is the most expensive round of the game: pool 12.5-17.6 (v2 deals 10.8
   per member, half the group punished) against ~68 in the quiet rounds
   2-3. v2's surface is flat around its peak (thr profile peaks at 9.8-10.2,
   slope 4.7-7.8 within 0.4, decay 0.5-0.7 within 0.2): another
   reshuffle of the same four parameters will not move the score. A
   structural change is needed. Candidates: (a) keep v1's contribution
   level while paying less for it, by punishing in proportion to how far a
   member *fell* rather than how far below a fixed bar it sits; (b) treat
   the first block differently from the rest (round 0 is where the bill
   is largest and the herding seed is set); (c) spare newcomers. Next: on
   the cached sweeps, which punishments bought lasting contributions per

2. **Which punishments buy lasting contributions per point, by kind of

   cache_sweep.py and ran scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-12/v3_cells.py:
   for members of the rule's group in the punished rounds (block position 0

   the block and the next (gain over c_t) on the punishment received, and of

   0.10-0.18 contribution points per punishment point per later round

   like stayers (0.10-0.17). It falls by block (c<=9: 0.18-0.19 in block 0,
   0.11-0.13 in block 4), confirming v2's decay. Two contrasts stand out.
   (i) Position: in block 0 a point at position 1 costs 1.07 pp of leaving
   against 0.68 at position 0 (later blocks 0.79-0.86 vs 0.48-0.55), for
   the same gain. (ii) Peers: a low contributor's gain per point rises and
   his leave cost falls with the others' mean contribution (3-6

   with others at 15-20). Persistent free riders are not a lost cause:
   0-contributors still gain 0.11-0.12 per point. Next: the group-level

3. **What moves members at the decisions?** Ran v3_flows.py (tables) and

4. **Drafted v3.** Kept v2's core (`slope * decay^block * shortfall` below a
   bar, punishment only in the first two rounds of each block, none in
   rounds 20-23) and changed two things, each with a parameter that nests
   v2 at one value so the sweep can at worst find v2 again. (a) `follow`,
   a continuous multiplier on the second round of each block (0 = v2's
   quiet 3, 1 = v2's quiet 2), because exploration 3 says the position-1
   punishment costs 2-3x more members per point than position 0 for the
   same contribution gain, while the sweeps could only switch it on or off.
   I dropped `quiet` itself: quiet 2 was best in both versions, quiet 1
   (punishing position 2) was worse in both, and `follow` covers 2 vs 3.
   (b) `peer`, mixing a fixed bar with the mean contribution of the
   member's current group-mates: bar = (1-peer)*thr + peer*mates' mean.
   Exploration 2 found punishment buys more and costs fewer members when
   the others give a lot, and exploration 3 that the contribution gap to
   `ah`'s group is the strongest driver of members; a peer-relative bar
   presses free riders harder exactly in groups that are pulling ahead,
   and eases off in groups that have collapsed, where a fixed bar spends
   the most for the least. A lone member is judged against thr alone.
   Not changed: newcomers (exploration 2: they answer like stayers),
   persistent free riders (still 0.11-0.12 per point).

## Key findings

1. v1 (70.3) and v2 (70.8) tie by different routes: v1 by contributions (13.3
   by the end) paid for in every block, v2 by members (4.37 vs `ah`'s 3.63)
   with contributions no higher than `ah`'s group (11.8 vs 12.0); v2's
   surface is flat near its peak, so a reshuffle of its four parameters
   will not move the score (exploration 1).
2. A punishment point buys a fairly uniform 0.10-0.18 contribution points per
   later round across members' own contribution, recent change and newcomer
   status, falling from 0.18-0.19 in block 0 to 0.11-0.13 in block 4
   (exploration 2).
3. The same point costs more members in a block's second round than in its
   first: individually 1.07 vs 0.68 pp of leaving in block 0; at the group
   level -0.084 vs -0.031 net members per point of mean punishment at
   decision 3, -0.030 vs -0.016 later (explorations 2, 3).
4. Punishment works better among generous peers: a 3-6 contributor gains
   0.144 per point and leaves at +0.42 pp per point with others at 15-20,
   against 0.096 and +0.95 pp with others below 6 (exploration 2).
5. Member flows follow the contribution gap between the groups: net -1.2 to
   +2.7 members at decision 3 from 3+ points below to 3+ above, +0.27 per
   point of block mean contribution at decision 3 and +0.12 later
   (exploration 3).
6. In the sweeps, punishing only a block's first round (quiet 3) and its
   first two (quiet 2) come out close (v2's best single points 71.5 with
   4.7 members vs 74.1 with 4.4), the cap of 30 limiting what the first
   round alone can deal (exploration 3).

## Hypothesis

A good manager makes its group visibly more generous than the other one
early, because members flow towards the more generous group, and pays as
little in punishment and departures as that takes. It punishes members who
give less than the group's bar at the start of each 4-round block, hardest
in the first block and tapering after, follows up more lightly (if at all)
in the block's second round, where the same punishment costs two to three
times as many members, and never in the two rounds before a switch decision
or after the last one. Its bar follows what the rest of the group gives, so
free riding is pressed where peers are generous (and punishment works best)
and not chased in a collapsed group.

The rule: `punishment = slope * decay^block * w_pos * max(0, bar - c_t)`,
with `bar = (1-peer)*thr + peer*mates' mean`, `w_pos` 1 at a block's first
round, `follow` at its second, 0 at its last two and in rounds 20-23.

- `thr` (float, [6, 15]): the fixed bar. v2 peaked at 9.8 (within 1.3 of
  the top from 8.9 to 11.5); with peer weight it blends with mates' means
  of ~10-12, so I expect 8-12; the range keeps room on both sides.
- `peer` (float, [0, 1]): weight of mates' mean in the bar. 0 is v2; I
  expect 0.3-0.7 (finding 4 favours pressing in generous groups, but
  bars of 13+ hit 9-12 contributors, whose gain per point is lowest).
- `slope` (float, [1.5, 12], log): punishment per shortfall point at round
  0. v2 peaked at 5.8, flat from 4.7 to 7.8; I expect 4-8, and with
  `follow` below 1 perhaps higher, to deal more in the cheap first round.
- `decay` (float, [0.35, 1.2], log): per-block factor. v2 peaked at 0.6,
  flat 0.5-0.7; I expect 0.5-0.75 (finding 2); values above 1 were clearly
  worse in v2, so the range stops at 1.2.
- `follow` (float, [0, 1.5]): second-round punishment relative to the
  first. I expect 0.3-0.8 (finding 3 says cheaper than 1, finding 6 that
  dropping it entirely does not clearly help); 1.5 allows the opposite.

### Justification

Finding 1 says the score now rests on members and on the bill, and finding
5 that members follow the contribution gap, so punishment is worth what it
adds to the group's generosity early on (findings 2, 5) minus the members it
drives out (finding 3); hence `slope` and `decay` as in v2, the first block
hardest. Finding 3 grounds `follow` and the fixed quiet last two rounds of
each block; finding 6 says the right size of the follow-up is an interior,
continuous question, which the old discrete `quiet` could not ask. Finding
4 grounds `peer`. Both new parameters contain v2's best at one value (peer
0, follow 1), so the sweep can return to it. What would contradict the
hypothesis: best `follow` at 1 or above (the follow-up pays after all; my
member-cost slopes were confounded by the design points), best `peer` at 0
(a fixed bar is as good), or at 1 with thr irrelevant (the bar should be
purely relative). Not modelled: newcomers (they respond like stayers,
finding 2), giving up on persistent free riders (they still respond),
the other group's state (`ah`'s punishment and contributions drive
members to us but we cannot act on them, except by being more generous),
and timed-out players, who are charged nothing anyway. The readings come
from earlier sweeps' design points, observational at the level of a
single member; the sweep is the test.
