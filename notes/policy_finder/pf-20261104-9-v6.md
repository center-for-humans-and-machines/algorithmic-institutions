## Key findings

1. Five versions have landed at 67.1 / 71.9 / 72.9 / 71.3 / 71.8, with
   zero at about 60. v5's per-capita-gap budget was rejected: its margin
   peaked at -15, where the budget does not bind. The bar for v6 is v3's
   72.9 (exploration 1).
2. A laggard's history does not change what a point buys. The lift-to-exit
   ratio is 8.8-14.4 across dips, steady and rising members, chronic and
   occasional laggards, c = 0 and c > 0, and group levels. Only the depth
   of the shortfall moves it: 8.6 / 10.8 / 15.7 for gaps < 3 / 3-7 / >= 7
   (exploration 2).
3. The game is an investment. v3 loses pool in rounds 0-3 (45.4 against
   zero's 53.3) and earns in rounds 12-23 (86.9 against 59.3), with c per
   member 12.2-12.5 and no punishment late. Late headcount is close to
   zero's (4.2-4.5 against 4.6-4.7) (exploration 3).
4. A point of punishment is worth more early and in fresh rounds. Its
   late value (rounds 13-23) is +0.88 / +0.86 / +0.37 in the fresh round
   of phases 0 / 1 / 2, and +0.28 / +0.41 / -0.05 in the within-phase
   rounds. Every earlier version punished all phases alike up to a hard
   stop (exploration 5).
5. The spend-versus-pool regression over the 1280 design points is too
   collinear to resolve phase or depth effects (exploration 4).

## Hypothesis

A good manager on this stack invests early and then lets the norm carry
itself. It punishes laggards relative to their own group, in proportion
to their shortfall, hardest in the round right after a switch decision,
and never just before one. Because the value of a point falls from phase
to phase as the group's norm settles and fewer rounds remain to collect
the lift, it eases off gradually rather than stopping all at once.

The rule: shortfall = max(0, `rel` x mean c of the other valid members -
c); dose = min(30, `slope` x shortfall x `decay`^phase), x `mid` in rounds
1-2 of a phase, 0 in the pre-decision round and after `last_round`.

- `rel` (float, [0.68, 1.0]): the laggard threshold as a fraction of the
  others' mean. I expect 0.78-0.88.
- `slope` (float, [3, 30] log): points per point of shortfall in round 0. I
  expect 7-14.
- `mid` (float, [0, 1.1]): the within-phase dose as a share of the
  fresh-round dose. I expect 0.3-0.7.
- `decay` (float, [0.25, 1.6] log): each later phase's intensity relative
  to the one before. I expect 0.5-0.9.
- `last_round` (int, 6..18): the last round with punishment. I expect
  8-12, or later if decay is low.

### Justification

Finding 1 says that adding a comparison with the other group did not
help, and finding 2 that keying on a member's history would not either,
since the per-member economics are flat across histories. So v6 keeps the
proven linear relative dose (`rel`, `slope`), whose value grows with the
shortfall's depth (finding 2). Finding 3 says that the early punishment
is an investment whose return comes late without enforcement, so how much
to spend in each phase is the decision that matters. Finding 4 supports
`mid` < 1, with fresh rounds worth 2-3x within-phase rounds. It also
supports `decay`: phase 2 is worth about half of phase 1, a gradient
that a constant intensity with a hard stop can only approximate. The
ranges include v3's corner (decay 1, mid about 0.37, last_round 10), so
the sweep can return to it.

What would contradict it: a decay peak at about 1 with a sharp
`last_round` would mean the hard stop was already the right shape. A
decay peak above 1 would mean escalating against persistent laggards
pays, against finding 4's values. Finding 4's numbers are correlational
(rules chose their targets) and leave out the lift before round 13, so
the sweep has the final say.

Not modelled: the per-capita-gap budget (rejected by v5's sweep), history
and newcomer terms (finding 2, and v3's dead newcomer parameter), a
deadband below the threshold (rel already sets where punishment starts),
and late or endgame punishment beyond what `decay` and `last_round` allow
(the late norm holds without it, finding 3).
