## Explorations

1. **How do human managers punish, and how do contributions respond?** Looked at contribution, punishment (one copy per game, valid rows) with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-6/human_basic.py; before that at the reference sims with ref_sims.py. Sims: total group pool (1.6*sum c - sum p) is zero 59.1 vs ah 44.9 when they face each other, because zero ends up with 4.6 members vs 3.4; ah alone (ah_vs_ah) grows the pool from 44 to 61 over the game while zero_vs_zero decays 54 -> 48. So punishment does lift contributions but ah loses members. Human data: managers punish low contributors (c=0: mean 4.4; c=20: 0.26), mostly by deviation below the group mean (dev < -8: 10.6). Next-round change in contribution: a c 5-9 player goes -0.33 unpunished, +1.09 at p 2-5, +2.56 at p 10-30; c=0 goes +1.0 / +1.7 / +4.0. Punishing high contributors backfires hard (c=20 with p 2-5: -9.4). So the gain per punishment point is ~0.3-0.4 contribution points next round, diminishing; small punishments of low contributors look efficient, any punishment of high contributors is harmful. Next: what drives leaving, since the sims say membership is where ah loses.
2. **What drives humans to leave a group?** Looked at switch decisions (block = 4 rounds; left = group differs next block) with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-6/human_switch.py. Checked first that `common_good` in the human CSV is the group total 1.6*sum c - sum p (it is; the sim's `common_good` is per valid member). Overall leave rate 0.29 (0.44 after block 0, ~0.24 later). Leaving rises with punishment received in the block: 0.19 at 0, 0.34 at 3-8, 0.49 at 15-30, 0.60 above 30. The punishment in the decision round itself counts most: 0.22 at 0, 0.49 at 3-8, 0.67 above 8. Logit: per point, last-round punishment +0.066 vs earlier-in-block +0.016 (4x), own-minus-other group mean payoff -0.12 per point, group size -0.14 per member (bigger groups hold on); own contribution has no effect once these are in. High contributors (15-20) are barely moved by the little punishment they get. So the cost of punishing is members, and it is concentrated in the rounds right before a decision (rounds 3, 7, 11, 15, 19) and in a lower group payoff than the other group's. Next: does the simulated stack show the same, since that is what my rule faces.
3. **Does the AH stack respond like the humans?** Looked at the three reference runs with an `ah` side (per_round.parquet) with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-6/sim_response.py. Yes, closely. Next-round change for c 5-9: +0.06 unpunished, +0.37 at p 1-2, +0.56 at p 3-5, +1.34 at p 10-30; c=20 punished 10-30: -4.6. For low contributors (c<=9) the lift over the unpunished baseline persists and even grows over 4 rounds in the same group (p 1-2: +0.44 after 1 round, +0.69 after 4; p 10-30: +1.83 -> +2.36), so it is a lasting shift, strongly concave in the punishment. Leaving in the sim: 0.22 unpunished in the block -> 0.32 at 3-8 -> 0.64 above 30; per point in logit, decision-round punishment 0.061 vs earlier rounds 0.026. Rough accounting for a c<=9 player: 1-2 points buys ~0.5 contribution for 4+ rounds, ~3 pool points for ~1.5 spent; at 10+ points the lift per point falls to ~0.1, worth ~0.7 pool over 4 rounds, below the 1 point it costs before counting members lost. So: small punishments of low contributors pay, large ones do not, and punishment in a decision round is ~2.5x as costly in leaving as earlier in the block. Next: does punishing some members drive out the others, and absolute vs relative shortfall.
4. **Do others' punishments drive members out, and is the shortfall absolute or relative?** Looked at ah_vs_ah blocks with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-6/sim_group.py. Logit of leaving: own decision-round punishment +0.056/pt, own earlier +0.020/pt, mean per-round punishment of the other members +0.085, group mean contribution -0.075/pt, group size -0.21/member. Unpunished members barely react to others being punished until it exceeds ~4 points per member per round (leave 0.19-0.22 vs 0.35 above 4). A good group (high mean contribution, many members) holds its members. Two-round change in contribution (c<=12): -0.36 per point of deviation from the group mean (strong herding to the mean), +0.19 per sqrt(punishment point), and the punishment effect grows with how far below the mean the player is (-0.034 x dev x sqrt p), while absolute contribution adds nothing (-0.005). So the shortfall should be measured against the group's current mean, and the response is concave, so a cap matters. Next: draft the rule.
5. **Drafted the rule.** Punish each own-group member in proportion to how far they fall below the group's current mean minus a margin, capped; scale it down in the decision rounds (3, 7, 11, 15, 19) where leaving reacts most; stop after a last round, since a lift bought late has few rounds left to pay back (entry 3: one round of +0.4 is worth ~0.6 pool, less than a point spent). Five parameters: margin, rate, cap, decision-round multiplier, last round. I expect it to beat `ah` by punishing less and more narrowly than it (ah: ~1.8 per member per round, also high contributors sometimes), keeping and attracting members like `zero` does (4.6 members) while still lifting free riders.
6. **Replayed the draft on ah's sim states (open loop), and where do low contributors sit?** With scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-6/rule_on_ah_sims.py and low_groups.py. Two surprises. First, `x.sum(dim=-1, keepdim=True)` and `th.remainder` raise KeyError '__import__' in a fresh process without builtins (validate-rule had passed, presumably because torch was already warm there), so I rewrote the code with `th.sum(...).unsqueeze(-1)` and `t - 4*floor(t/4)`. Second, the relative rule barely punishes the worst players: at margin 2, rate 0.5, cap 4 it deals 0.68 on average to c=0 players vs ah's 4.7, because low contributors cluster in low groups (ah_vs_ah: the median group mean seen by a c<=4 player is 4.8, a quarter see below 2.6; zero's group: median 3.5) and their own contribution drags the mean down. A purely relative target lets a whole group slide to zero unpunished, which is exactly where zero's group drifts (8.6 -> 8.1). So I changed the target to max(mean of the *other* members - margin, an absolute floor): relative in good groups (herding, entry 4), absolute in bad ones. To stay at 5 parameters I dropped `last_round` and fixed only the obvious part of it: no punishment in round 23, where it cannot change any later contribution or switch. The rounds 20-22 tail is left to rate and cap.
7. **Replayed the revised rule and validated it.** Same replay script. Across the sweep box the rule runs from almost no punishment to about ah's level: margin 4 / floor 3 / rate 0.3 / cap 2 deals 0.08 per member-round; margin 2 / floor 6 / rate 0.5 / cap 4 deals 0.53 (2.35 to c=0 players, about 0 above c=10); margin 0 / floor 8 / rate 1 / cap 8 deals 2.08 (ah: 1.93, with 4.7 to c=0 and still 0.56 to c>14). So the sweep covers both regimes, keeping members like zero and lifting contributions like ah, plus everything between. The code ran in a fresh process on 300 sim episodes, and validate-rule passes on all 256 design points. Where I expect the best point: margin 1-3, floor 4-8, rate 0.3-1, cap 2-6, decision_mult near 0.

## Key findings

1. `ah` loses on group size. Against `zero` its group's total pool is 44.9 vs 59.1, with 3.4 vs 4.6 members, even though its members contribute more (9.0 vs 8.1). (Exploration 1)
2. Small punishments of low contributors produce a lift that lasts, and the effect flattens out at high punishment. In the sim, c<=9 players punished 1-2 points are +0.44 above baseline after one round and +0.69 after four. At 10-30 points the lift is only +1.8 to +2.4. Punishing high contributors backfires (c=20 punished 10-30: -4.6 next round). (Explorations 1, 3)
3. Leaving rises with punishment (0.22 unpunished vs 0.64 above 30 points in a block). A point of punishment in the decision round weighs about 2.5-4x as much as one earlier in the block (logit 0.056-0.066 vs 0.016-0.026), in both humans and sims. (Explorations 2, 3, 4)
4. Contributions herd toward the mean of the other members (-0.36 per point of deviation over two rounds). Punishment works better the further below the mean a player is, and absolute contribution adds nothing beyond that. (Exploration 4)
5. Low contributors cluster in low groups: under `ah` the median group mean a c<=4 player sees is 4.8. A target set only relative to the group therefore misses them. (Exploration 6)

## Hypothesis

A good manager punishes less than `ah`, and only where it pays. It punishes lightly and with a cap, and only members clearly below the others' mean or below an absolute floor. It eases off in the rounds right before a switch decision. This keeps members like `zero` does while still getting the lasting lift from low contributors.

The rule sets target = max(others' mean - margin, floor) and punishment = min(rate x shortfall, cap). This is scaled by `decision_mult` in rounds 3/7/11/15/19, is zero in round 23, and applies to own valid members only.

- `margin`, float, [-2, 6]: how far below the others' mean a member may fall without punishment.
- `floor`, float, [0, 14]: the absolute contribution expected from everyone.
- `rate`, float, [0.1, 3] log: punishment points per point of shortfall.
- `cap`, float, [1, 15] log: the most any member gets in one round.
- `decision_mult`, float, [0, 1]: how much of the punishment is kept in rounds just before a decision.

### Justification

Finding 1 means total punishment should be lower than `ah`'s. Finding 2 supports a moderate `rate` and a small `cap` (expected rate 0.3-1, cap 2-6). The ranges still reach `ah`-like levels in case the sim rewards punishment more than I estimate. Finding 4 supports the relative target and `margin` (expected 1-3). Finding 5 supports `floor` (expected 4-8), and floor 0 switches it off. Finding 3 supports `decision_mult` (expected near 0).

Two sweep results would contradict this. A peak at high rate/cap with decision_mult near 1 would mean the contribution lift outweighs the loss of members. A peak at the lowest rate and cap would mean any punishment loses.

Not modelled: a player's own contribution trend, the state of the other group, round-dependent strength, and the rounds 20-22 tail. I left these out to stay within five well-supported parameters.
