# pf-20261105-2

## Explorations

1. **What do the reference sims say about the size of the prize?** Read
   `plots/simulation/29_EXP1_reference/aggregates.csv`. Under `zero` the
   artificial humans' contributions decay from 11.6 (round 0) to 5.5 (round 23),
   mean payoff per round from 26.6 to 23.0, about 590 summed. Under `humanlike`
   contributions rise from 11.6 to ~13.2 and stay there, but the manager deals
   ~2-3 points per player per round, and the payoff sum is only ~553. So the
   human manager *loses* ~35 points per game to the zero manager: per round the
   cost of punishment to the mean payoff is 2 * mean punishment (the punished
   player's points plus the pool's share), ~5, while the gain from contributions
   is 0.6 * (13.2 - c_zero), which only reaches ~4.6 in the last rounds. Next:
   how contributions respond to punishment in the human data, by contribution level.
2. **The contribution models' inputs.** Read
   `configs/training/artificial_humans/contribution/script_21_no_grid.yml` (the
   training config of the clones in `29_EXP1_reference.yml`). Inputs are only
   previous contribution, previous punishment (numeric) and previous validity,
   with an edge model (the other players) and an RNN. No round number: the
   artificial humans cannot see the end coming, so they never anticipate that
   punishment stops. Punishment in the last round has no future to buy and is
   pure loss; punishment in the last few rounds buys only a few rounds of extra
   contributions. Next: response sizes in the human data.
3. **How do humans respond to punishment?** `scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-2/human_response.py`
   on the pilot csv (valid rows followed by a valid row). Mean change of
   contribution next round, by own contribution x punishment received:
   c 0-4: +0.9 unpunished, +1.5 (p 1-3), +4.2 (p 7-10), +7.1 (p 16-30);
   c 5-9: +0.1, +0.8, +2.4, +5.1 (p 16-30); c 10-14: -1.0 unpunished, +0.3 to +1.4
   punished; c 15-19: -1.3 unpunished, ~+0.4 for p 1-10, but -5 for p > 10;
   c = 20: -0.9 unpunished, -4.6 (p 1-3), -10 (p 4-6), -13.6 (p 7-10).
   OLS: next_c = 0.23 + 0.72 c + 0.26 others' mean + 0.18 p (+ smaller round term).
   So punishment works on low contributors, and roughly scales with the amount;
   punishing a full contributor is a disaster. Also contributions are sticky
   (0.72 own + 0.26 others ~ 0.98), so an increase bought in round t persists
   for many rounds -- that is what makes early punishment pay. Next: the same
   table on the artificial humans under `humanlike`.
4. **Do the artificial humans respond like the humans?** `sim_response.py` on
   the reference parquet. Payoff sums: zero 589.3 (se 2.1), humanlike 550.6
   (se 3.4). Under humanlike, next-round change by own c x punishment:
   c 0-4: +0.8 unpunished, +1.1 (p 1-3), +1.8 (p 4-6), +2.7 (p 7-10),
   +4.2 (p 11-15), +4.9 (p 16-30); c 5-9: -0.0, +1.2, +1.6, +2.2, +3.0, +2.9;
   c 10-14: -0.75, +0.1, +0.4, +0.35, +0.6, -0.1; c 15-19: -0.8 unpunished and
   worse when punished; c = 20: -0.75, falling to -2.9 with p. So the clones
   reproduce the human pattern, a bit damped (OLS slope on p 0.07 vs 0.18 for
   humans, but that pools the backfire on high contributors). The response to
   punishment is concave: per point it is largest for small punishments of mid
   contributors (c 5-9, p 1-3: +1.2 for ~2 points). Surprise: under zero, an
   unpunished full contributor drops -1.7 the next round, under humanlike only
   -0.75: a group in which defectors get punished keeps its cooperators up
   (edge model / RNN spillover). The humanlike manager punishes c 0-4 with 6.0
   on average, c 5-9 4.6, c 10-14 2.9, c 15-19 1.05, c = 20 0.3 -- the last two
   are probably wasted or harmful. Next: regressions with lagged change and the
   others' punishment as controls, to see spillover and the per-point value.
5. **Per-point value of punishment, with controls.** `sim_regress.py`: under
   humanlike, per contribution bin, next_c on c, previous c, others' mean c,
   own punishment piecewise (0-3, 3-10, >10) and others' mean punishment.
   Own-punishment slopes: c 0-4: 0.04 / 0.21 / 0.12 per point; c 5-9:
   0.07 / 0.11 / -0.02; c 10-14: 0.16 / 0.00 / -0.05; c 15-19: 0.04 / -0.08;
   c = 20: -0.09 throughout. Others' mean punishment: +0.01 (c 0-4), +0.07,
   +0.08, +0.10 (c 15-19), +0.03 (c 20) -- a real spillover: punishing one
   player by x lifts each other player by ~0.08 x/3. Persistence (c + prev_c +
   oth_c coefficients) ~0.95-1.0. Back of the envelope: a point of punishment on
   a low contributor buys ~0.2 direct + ~0.08 spillover = ~0.28 contribution
   next round, persisting at ~0.95/round; with 20 rounds left that is ~3.6
   contributed points, worth 0.6 * 3.6 = 2.2 to the group's total payoff, against
   a cost of exactly 2 (the player's point plus the pool's). So punishment is
   only just worth it even when aimed well and early, and turns into a loss as
   the horizon shrinks; the humanlike manager loses because it also punishes
   high contributors (backfire) and keeps punishing to the last round. Under
   zero the share of players at c <= 9 grows from 35% (round 0) to 76% (round 23):
   there is a large decay for punishment to stop. Next: a crude surrogate
   simulator from these regressions, only to place the parameter ranges.
6. **Drafted the rule and a crude surrogate to place ranges.** Draft:
   `punishment = min(p_max, k * relu(c_star - c)) * clamp((23 - t) / H, 0, 1)`,
   i.e. punish the shortfall below a contribution norm, capped (the response is
   concave in the amount), tapered linearly to zero over the last H rounds (a
   point's worth shrinks with the rounds left to recoup it; the clones cannot
   see the end, so the taper costs nothing in anticipation), and always zero in
   the last round. `surrogate.py` fits the exploration-5 regressions per
   contribution bin with bootstrap residuals and plays this rule on a grid. It
   reproduces zero well (585.9 vs 589.3 real) but in it no punishing rule
   beats zero (best 583.9: c_star 8, k 0.25, H 24 -- low norm, small k, long
   taper), and a humanlike-ish manager (Poisson punishment at the reference's
   per-bin means) scores 503 vs the real 550. So the surrogate is ~45 points too
   pessimistic about punishment: the clones' RNN carries something the linear
   fit misses. I take from it only the ordering (small, early, aimed at low
   contributors beats large, late or aimed high), not the sign.
7. **Is there a climate effect the surrogate misses?** `norm_effect.py`:
   unpunished players' next change, matched on the group's mean contribution,
   zero vs humanlike. Under humanlike they do better at every level: c 0-4 in a
   group at 12-16: +2.7 vs +1.4; c 5-9 in a group at 16-20: +3.2 vs +1.3; c = 20
   in a group at 0-8: -5.4 vs -7.9. Not explained by this round's group
   punishment (flat) nor own past punishment (flat), so partly selection,
   partly the RNN's memory. The main driver of everyone's change is the group's
   mean contribution (conformity: a full contributor in a group at 0-8 drops
   ~-8, at 16-20 only ~-0.6). So the manager's real job is to keep the group mean
   from sliding; lifting the lowest contributor lifts the others through
   conformity. Accounting: humanlike spent ~115 points of payoff on punishment
   (2 x 2.4 x 24) and got ~76 back in contributions; ~17% of its punishment hit
   c >= 15 (backfire) and ~25% fell in the last 6 rounds. Dropping those alone
   could turn its -39 into a gain over zero. Ranges must also reach near-zero
   punishment so the sweep can fall back toward the zero manager if punishment
   does not pay.
8. **Wrote and validated the rule.** `configs/managers/rule_based/pf-20261105-2.yml`,
   the draft of 6 with four parameters (c_star, k, p_max, H). I considered a
   fifth (leniency for players whose contribution just rose, or a norm relative
   to the group mean) but found no clear evidence for either in 3-7, and fewer
   dimensions keep the 256-point design dense. `check_rule.py` on a toy group
   (c = 0, 8, 12, 20; c_star 12, k 0.6, p_max 6, H 10): 6 / 2.4 / 0 / 0 up to
   round 13, halved at round 18, all zero at round 23. `validate-rule` passes:
   256 distinct design points.

## Key findings

1. The zero manager beats the human-like one on the clones, 589.3 vs 550.6 per
   game: punishment costs the group's mean payoff 2 x the mean punishment per
   round and the human-like manager's ~2.4 per player is not repaid (exploration 1, 4).
2. The clones see only last round's contributions, punishments and timeouts
   through an RNN, never the round number, so they cannot anticipate the end;
   punishment in the last round is pure loss (exploration 2).
3. Punishment raises the next contribution of low contributors (clones, c 0-4:
   +0.8 unpunished to +4.9 at p 16-30; c 5-9: up to +3.0) and backfires on high
   ones (c = 20: -0.75 unpunished to -2.9); humans show the same, stronger
   (exploration 3, 4).
4. The response is concave in the amount: per point it is largest for small
   and middling punishments (controlled slope for c 0-4: 0.21/pt for p 3-10,
   0.12 beyond 10; c 10-14 respond only to p <= 3) (exploration 5).
5. Punishing one player lifts the others a little (+0.07-0.10 per point of
   others' mean punishment for c 5-19), and contributions are sticky (persistence
   ~0.95-1.0), so a bought increase lasts; a well-aimed early point returns
   ~2.2 against its cost of 2, a late one much less (exploration 5).
6. Conformity drives the dynamics: a full contributor's next change is ~-8 in a
   group at 0-8 and ~-0.6 in one at 16-20; under zero the share at c <= 9 climbs
   from 35% to 76%, so the manager's job is to stop the group mean's slide
   (exploration 5, 7).
7. Of the human-like manager's punishment ~17% hit c >= 15 and ~25% fell in the
   last six rounds; a linear surrogate is ~45 points too pessimistic about
   punishment (503 vs 550 for a humanlike-ish manager), so the sign of the net
   effect cannot be settled here, only the ordering: low norm, moderate amounts,
   early beats high, large, late (exploration 6, 7).

## Hypothesis

A good manager punishes only players who fall clearly below a contribution
norm, in proportion to the shortfall but capped at a moderate amount, early in
the game, and fades punishment out toward the end, never punishing in the last
round. Punishment pays only through contributions in later rounds, so its worth
falls with the rounds left; it pays best on low contributors, at moderate
amounts, and it lifts the whole group through conformity. Punishing full or
near-full contributors, or punishing heavily, burns points.

Rule: `punishment = min(p_max, k * max(c_star - c, 0)) * clamp((23 - t) / H, 0, 1)`
- `c_star` (float, [4, 20]): the contribution norm; at or above it, no
  punishment. Expected best 10-15.
- `k` (float, [0.1, 4], log): punishment points per point of shortfall.
  Expected 0.3-1.5; the low end (at most 2 points, mostly floored to 0-1) lets
  the sweep approach the zero manager.
- `p_max` (float, [1, 30], log): the per-player, per-round cap. Expected 4-10;
  30 means no cap.
- `H` (int, [1, 24]): the taper horizon; punishment shrinks linearly over the
  last H rounds and is zero at round 23. Expected 6-16; 1 is a cut in the last
  round only, 24 a taper from the start.

### Justification

Finding 1 shows the cost side dominates unless punishment is aimed and
economical, so every parameter limits spending: `c_star` keeps punishment off
the high contributors where it backfires (finding 3, 7: c 10-14 respond only
weakly, c >= 15 negatively, hence the expected 10-15, with the range [4, 20]
wide enough to let the sweep find a low or a strict norm). `k` sets the dose by
shortfall, because the gain per point is largest for the lowest contributors
(finding 3); `p_max` exists because the response saturates (finding 4), so
spending beyond ~10 per player buys little; its log range covers both a tight
cap and none. `H` comes from findings 2 and 5: a point's return is roughly
proportional to the rounds left to recoup it and turns negative well before the
end, and the clones cannot anticipate the fade; the range spans a last-round cut
to a full-game taper. The k and p_max ranges reach near-zero punishment, so if
punishment does not pay on the clones (finding 7 leaves that open) the sweep
can fall back near the zero manager's 589. What would contradict the hypothesis:
the sweep's best points all at the lowest k (punishment does not pay at all),
or at c_star near 20 (punishing high contributors pays), or at H = 1 (no fading).
Not modelled: punishment based on a player's history (repeat defectors,
players who just rose) or on the others' contributions, because exploration 7
found no clear effect of past own punishment and I wanted few dimensions for
the 256 points; timeouts, because they are rare (1.9%) and a timed-out player
cannot be punished anyway.
