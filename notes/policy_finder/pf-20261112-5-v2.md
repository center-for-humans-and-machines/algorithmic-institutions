# pf-20261112-5-v2

## Explorations

1. **What did version 1's sweep say?** Read plots/simulation/policy_finder/pf-20261112-5_sweep/
   (sweep.json, landscapes.json, final/sweep.json). Found the fitted peak at the near-silent corner
   (k=0.2 the range's floor, thr=1.3, w=1, end=0), final 17.08 +- 1.43 on fresh seeds, against
   `zero`'s 13.97 +- 1.44 in the reference sims: within noise of never punishing. Sweep points have
   se ~2.9 each, so the landscape is mostly noise on a plateau ~14 that falls off for heavy
   punishment (k 1.6-4: mean lead 6.1; w=4: 6.1, w=0/1: 13.9). The second-best raw points had low
   thr (~0.5, only clear free riders punished) with k from 0.3 to 3. So v1 found no punishment that
   beats `zero`; v2 has to find a mechanism, not tune v1. Next: v1's 128,000 simulated games are
   an exogenous-variation dataset on the real stack; mine them for who leaves and who answers.
2. **Across v1's points, what does punishment buy and cost?** Compacted the sweep per-round data
   with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-5/v1_compact.py (to /tmp) and binned the 256 points by
   punishment per member-round with v1_check.py (recomputed scores match sweep.json exactly).
   Found: at 0-0.5 pts/member-round the lead stays ~14.3, members 4.58, own contribution 8.1-8.4;
   at 1-2 contributions rise to 9.4 but members fall to 4.44 and lead to 12.3; at 2-4 c=10.6,
   members 4.22, lead 4.7; above 4 the group collapses (3.5 members, lead -25). So punishment does
   raise contributions on this stack, and the members it costs eat the gain. A regression of the
   lead on where the punishment went (by the punished member's contribution, v1_alloc.py) is too
   collinear to read, except that rounds 20+ punishment does not hurt. Next: player level, which
   members leave over punishment and which members answer it.
3. **Player level in v1's games: exits and answers by the punished member's contribution.** With
   v1_response.py, in the rule's group. Exit at a decision by own block contribution (rows) and own
   block punishment total: a block total up to 4 points costs no exits at all (e.g. c 5-10: 23.4%
   unpunished, 22.7% at 1-4), 4-12 costs +2-3 points of percentage, 12-30 +7-10, >30 +20. Free
   riders (block mean c<=2) leave 29% unpunished vs 19% for c>10, and a free rider leaving costs the
   pool nothing. Unpunished members do not leave more when others are punished (23.2% -> 21.7%).
   Next-round contribution change: c=0 members +1.32 unpunished, +2.46 at 5-10 points, +5.75 at
   20-30; c 5-10: -0.12 unpunished, +1.21 at 2-5, +1.71 at 5-10; c>15: no answer at all.
   Unpunished members drift down when others are punished (c 10-15: -0.57 -> -1.1), possibly
   confounded (v1 punished in spread-out groups, where high contributors herd down anyway).
   So the asymmetry I want: hit free riders (cheap exits, big answers) and spare contributors;
   punish small amounts per block to stay under the exit threshold. Next: does the answer last?
4. **Does the answer last?** v1_persist.py: members of the rule's group followed for 4 rounds after
   a punishment (stayers only, others' mean 6-12). The extra contribution over unpunished members of
   the same contribution does not fade, it grows: c 5-9 punished 3-8: +0.95 at +1, +1.53 at +4;
   c 1-4 punished 8-15: +1.5 -> +2.0; c=0 punished 15-30: +1.3 -> +2.0. Still confounded by who v1
   chose to punish (relative rule), so next I want a clean contrast.
5. **A clean causal read: round 0.** Round 0 is played before any manager acts, so its state has the
   same law at every point; v1 points with w=1 punish only round 0 in block 0, points with w=0 never
   do. With v1_round0.py I computed v1's prescribed round-0 dose for every member at every point and
   compared w=1 (dealt) with w=0 (not dealt) at equal dose and own contribution. Contribution
   answer, rounds 1 and 3: c 5-9, dose 2-5: +0.58/+0.47; 5-10: +1.35/+1.13; 10-20: +2.27/+1.85;
   20-30: +4.0/+3.65. c 1-4, 20-30: +3.6/+3.0. c=0 answers less (20-30: +2.1/+1.3), c 15+ not at
   all. Exits at the round-3 decision: none extra up to a dose of 10 for c>=1, +5-10 percentage
   points at 10-30. Unpunished members whose group-mates got a large total dose (40+) contribute
   +2.2/+2.8 more and leave 6 points less: the earlier negative spillover (3) was confounding.
   Group level (v1_round0_group.py): prescribed round-0 dose 10-20 adds 7.2 contribution over rounds
   1-3 but costs 0.10 members at the decision; 20-40 adds 11.7 and costs 0.23; 40+ adds 33 and the
   group is 0.29 *larger*. Overall lead differences are within ~+-1-3 (noise ~0.7). So each
   punishment point roughly pays for itself in contributions and loses it again in members; the
   heavy-dose free-rider case is the exception that looks positive.
6. **What moves players between groups in the sims?** v1_flows.py, per decision: the share of my
   members leaving and of the other group's members joining me, against the block's mean member
   payoff gap (mine - theirs). Leave rate falls from 59% (gap < -6) to 30% (gap ~0) to 13% (gap > 6);
   join rate rises from 14% to 30% to 68%. With no punishment, my group's mean contribution alone
   moves the leave rate 41% (c<4) -> 23% (c>13) and the join rate 26% -> 45%. A member's average
   payoff is 20 + 0.6*cbar - pbar, so a punishment point costs as much payoff as 1.7 points of group
   contribution buy back: punishment pays for membership only if it lifts the group mean by more
   than 1.7x its per-member cost, durably. So the target is: punish few (only those whose answer is
   big and whose exit is cheap), and leave everyone else's payoff alone.
7. **The same contrast at every round: v1's gate as a natural experiment.** v1_gate.py: at round
   t<20 with block position pos, points with w=pos+1 punish this round and points with w=pos do
   not, with the same gating history before; compared at equal prescribed dose and own
   contribution. Contribution 4 rounds later (stayers), treated minus control: c 1-11 gains +0.1-0.2
   at dose 1-3, +0.35-0.45 at 4-8, +0.6-0.7 at 9-15, +1.0-1.2 at 16-30 - so about 0.07 contribution
   per point at small doses, falling to 0.05 at large ones. c=0 does not answer moderate doses at
   all (+0.03 at 1-3, +0.15 at 4-8; 0.00-0.03 at 3-10 whatever the others give); c 12+ barely
   (+0.05-0.13). At equal own contribution the answer does not depend on the others' mean (c 3-7:
   +0.31-0.45 across others' means 0-20), so an absolute target fits the AH better than v1's relative
   one. Exits rise with the dose but are the same at every contribution, and depend strongly on the
   block position: at pos 0 a dose of 4-8 adds 0.9 points of percentage, at pos 3 (the decision
   round) 7.1; 9-15: 3.9 vs 14.0. So: punish only right after a decision, in small doses, and only
   members who contribute something but little.
8. **Spillover on the unpunished, same natural experiment.** v1_gate_spill.py: members prescribed
   no punishment, by their group-mates' prescribed total dose. They contribute more when the gate
   was open, about 0.013 per point dealt to others and lasting (+0.034 at +4 for 1-5 points, +0.195
   for 10-20, +0.38 for 20-40), and do not leave more (+0.1 points of percentage at most). With
   ~3.6 group-mates the spillover per point roughly matches the direct answer, so a punishment point
   buys ~0.12 lasting contribution in total, 0.19 pool per remaining round. Rough account for one
   point at pos 0 with ~12 rounds left: +2.3 pool from contributions, -1 for the point itself, about
   -0.4 for the extra exit chance (0.0016 per point times a ~2*1.6*8 pool-per-round swing); so a
   small positive margin early in the game, gone by the last blocks, and eaten by any extra loss of
   joiners from the lower member payoff (exploration 6). Expect a modest gain, early.
9. **Drafted the v2 rule.** A flat nudge `D` to every member of my group who contributed at least
   `lo` and less than `a` this round, only in the first `w` rounds of each block and only in the
   first `blocks` blocks (rounds before 4*blocks), never in round 23, never a timed-out member or a
   member alone. Changes from v1: absolute target (7), a floor `lo` that spares the zero
   contributors who do not answer (7), a flat dose rather than one proportional to the shortfall
   (the answer per point is about the same across c 1-11, and small doses are the efficient ones),
   and a horizon `blocks` instead of v1's `end` multiplier (the gain scales with rounds left; v1's
   `end` had no measurable effect, length scale 24.7).
10. **Hand check and intensity; narrowed two ranges.** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-5/
   test_rule_v2.py: with D=5, lo=1, hi=8, w=1, blocks=4 a member at c=0 or c=9 gets nothing, members
   at 1 and 8 get 5 in rounds 0, 4, 8, 12 only (nothing in 1, 3, 15, 16, 20, 23), the other group
   nothing. On the zero group's contributions in the reference sims 11% of member-rounds are 0 and
   12% are 20; lo=1, hi=8 targets 46% of member-rounds, so that point deals ~0.4 points per
   member-round, the light regime where v1 stayed level with `zero` (2). First draft had D up to 20
   and w up to 3: the corner D=20, hi=12, w=3, blocks=6 deals ~10 per member-round, which v1 showed
   collapses the group, and pos 2 costs 5x the exits of pos 0 (7). Narrowed D to [1, 12] log and w to
   [1, 2] so the design is not spent where the answer is already known. validate-rule passes (256
   distinct points).

## Key findings

1. Version 1's sweep found nothing better than never punishing: its peak sits at the silent corner
   (k=0.2, the floor) with a final 17.08 +- 1.43 vs `zero`'s 13.97 +- 1.44; light punishment is a
   plateau ~14, heavy punishment collapses the group (above 4 points per member-round: 3.5 members,
   lead -25) (explorations 1, 2).
2. Punishment raises contributions on this stack, but in v1 the members it cost ate the gain
   (1-2 points per member-round: c 8.1 -> 9.4, members 4.58 -> 4.44, lead 14.3 -> 12.3)
   (exploration 2).
3. The answer is lasting and depends on the punished member's own contribution, not on the
   group's: in the gate natural experiment, c 1-11 members gain ~0.07 contribution per point at
   small doses, still there 4 rounds later; zero contributors do not answer moderate doses
   (+0.00-0.15) and c 12+ barely (+0.05-0.13); the others' mean does not change the answer
   (explorations 5, 7).
4. Exits from a punishment depend on its dose and on when in the block it falls, not on who gets it:
   dose 4-8 adds 0.9 points of percentage of exits at the block's first round and 7.1 at the
   decision round (exploration 7).
5. Punishing some members makes the unpunished ones contribute more (~0.013 per point dealt to
   group-mates, lasting) without more exits, so the total answer per point is ~0.12 contribution
   (explorations 5, 8).
6. Movement between groups follows the members' payoff gap: leave rate 59% -> 13% and join rate
   14% -> 68% from a gap below -6 to above +6; a punishment point costs a member as much payoff as
   1.7 points of group contribution give back (exploration 6).

## Hypothesis

A good manager keeps its members' payoff high, so that it keeps its members and draws `ah`'s, and
spends punishment only where a point buys the most lasting contribution for the fewest exits: on
members who give something but little (they answer; zero contributors and high contributors do
not), in small flat doses (the answer per point is highest at small doses and exits grow faster
than linearly), only in the round(s) right after a switch decision (exits there cost a fifth of
those in the decision round), and only early in the game (the answer lasts, so its value grows with
the rounds left). The rule: in an open round, each member of my group who timed in, is not alone and
contributed between `lo` and `hi` gets `D` points; open rounds are the first `w` rounds of each of
the first `blocks` 4-round blocks, never round 23.

- `D` (float, [1, 12] log): the flat dose. Expect 3-8.
- `lo` (int, 0..2): the lowest contribution punished. Expect 1.
- `hi` (int, 3..12): the highest contribution punished. Expect 6-10.
- `w` (int, 1..2): open rounds at the start of each block. Expect 1.
- `blocks` (int, 1..6): how many blocks from the start are open. Expect 3-5.

### Justification

Findings 1, 2 and 6 say membership dominates, and that a manager's main asset against `ah` is a
higher member payoff, so the rule stays light: at its center (D=5, hi=8, w=1, blocks=4) it deals
~0.4 points per member-round (exploration 10), and D=1 or blocks=1 is close to `zero`, so the sweep
can fall back to it. Finding 3 motivates the absolute band `lo..hi`: the answer is a property of the
member's own contribution, so `hi` around 6-10 catches the responsive range c 1-11 while sparing the
12+ contributors, and `lo` tests whether to spare the zero contributors (finding 3 says spare them,
lo=1; lo=0 or 2 are the alternatives). Findings 3 and 4 motivate the flat small dose `D` (range
[1, 12] log, expected 3-8: below ~3 the answer is tiny, above ~10 exits jump). Finding 4 motivates
`w` (1 expected; 2 is the only alternative worth testing, pos 2-3 cost 5-8x the exits). Finding 5
and the lasting answer motivate `blocks`: the account in exploration 8 is positive with ~12 rounds
left and turns negative late, so I expect 3-5; the full 1..6 range is cheap with integer sampling.
What would contradict it: a sweep peak at D near 1 or blocks=1 (any punishment loses the joiners it
costs), or at hi=12 with lo=0 (a broad punishment beats a targeted one, i.e. the spillover dominates).
Not modelled: per-player history (escalation for repeat low contributors, sparing recently punished
members), a dose that varies with the rounds left, and the other group's state; each is plausible
but the effects measured here are small against the score's noise (se ~2.9 per sweep point), and
five parameters already use 256 points thinly. The causal reads come from v1's own games and gate,
which are the stack this rule will meet, but they are short-run (4 rounds): how long the answer
lasts beyond that is the main uncertainty.
