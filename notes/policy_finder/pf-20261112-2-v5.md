## Key findings

1. v4's relative-shirker rule lost to never-punish on the same seeds: -0.29 +- 0.19 at the `gp` slot (-0.82 +- 0.54 per fired game), about zero in two other slots; the bar is still never-punish on the lucky `gp` seeds, 16.757 (exploration 1).
2. Over 147 fining points of four sweeps paired with never-fining points at the same index, the mean effect is -2.19 / -0.42 / -1.66 / -0.22 for v1-v4; cut by the round of the first fine, every slice is negative, including first fines after the last switch (-0.74 to -1.02 per point in rounds 20-23), so the endgame is no lever (exploration 2).
3. The race decides what a fine costs: per point of spend, -1.3 to -1.6 when my per-capita pool is within 5 of ah's over the block, -0.51 at a lead of 5-10, -0.11 at 10+, with member losses -0.18 to -0.22 vs -0.09 (exploration 3).
4. Combined with a large shortfall the cost disappears: first fine out of a lead >= 10 on a member >= 8 below the others +0.18 +- 0.23 per game (8597 games); in blocks 0-1 +0.33 and +0.42, in blocks 2-5 -0.36 to -0.50; small first fines (<= 1.5) +0.51 +- 0.32 (exploration 3).
5. ah's group being empty is the extreme of the same lead, not a separate lever: there too only shortfalls >= 8 break even or better (+0.08, +0.59 +- 0.60) and only in block 1 (exploration 4).
6. The rule's race measure tracks the true one closely from round 1 on (correlation >= 0.989), and in never-punish games it would fire in 26% (lead 10, gap 8, rounds 0-7) down to 2% of games, so most games stay exact replays of never-punish (exploration 5).

## Hypothesis

Against this stack a fine pays for itself only where it does not cost members. Members follow the per-capita pool gap through a sigmoid: in a close race every point of fine tips some members toward ah's group, and they are the score; when my group already leads by a wide margin they are far from the door and a fine on a clear shirker (far below the rest of my group) buys his contribution without buying an exit. That contribution is worth most early, while it can still shape the gap at the coming switches. So a good manager stays out of the way almost always, and fines only clear relative shirkers, only out of a large lead, only in the first blocks. I expect this to be break-even to slightly positive (+0.1 to +0.3 per game it fires in), and the design keeps never-punish as the fallback.

The rule (`configs/managers/rule_based/pf-20261112-2-v5.yml`): a valid member of my group is fined `fine` if the other valid members' mean contribution this round exceeds his by at least `gap`, my block-to-date per-capita pool leads ah's by at least `lead`, and t <= 4*last_block - 1.
- `lead` (float, [0, 25]): the race line, in per-capita pool points. Expect 10-15.
- `gap` (float, [5, 15]): the shortfall line, in contribution points below the other members' mean. Expect 8-10.
- `fine` (float, [0, 6]): flat fine; below 1 floors to no punishment (a sixth of the design). Expect 1-3.
- `last_block` (int, [1, 6]): no fines after round 4*last_block - 1 (1 = only the first block, 6 = all game). Expect 2.

### Justification

Findings 3 and 4 are the core and set `lead`: the per-point return climbs monotonically over seven lead bins from about -1.5 to about 0, and the cells with the race and the shortfall both large are the first non-negative ones in four versions of paired data; the range [0, 25] starts at "no race condition" (which is v4's rule, known to lose, finding 1) and ends where the rule fires in 2-7% of games (finding 6), which is nearly never-punish, so the best should be inside, around 10-15 where the cells peak. `gap` follows v4's evidence and finding 4 (the cells peak at a shortfall of 8 for every lead line); [5, 15] runs from the costly side to nearly off. `fine` [0, 6]: finding 4 says the smallest fines did best and larger ones no better, so the top is lower than v4's 10; the bottom sixth is never-punish, the honest fallback of finding 2. `last_block` [1, 6]: finding 4's blocks 0-1 vs 2+ split puts the expected best at 2, with "every round" (6) and "first block only" (1) as the contrasts; finding 2 says the endgame is no lever, which 6 lets the sweep confirm. It would be contradicted by a peak at fine < 1 or lead near 25 (no state in which fines pay; never-punish is simply best here), by a peak at lead near 0 (the race gradient was an artefact of which rules happened to fine when), or at last_block 6. What I chose not to model: the group sizes (v4's `n_min` had no effect on its landscape, and the race measure already reacts to a collapsing group), ah's group being empty as its own case (finding 5: it is the extreme of the lead), a fine scaling with the shortfall or the lead (thin evidence, and it would thin the design), and a separate round-0 correction for ah's unknown first fines (the rule is merely more cautious in round 0). A caution I repeat from v4: the cells behind finding 4 come from rules that also fined elsewhere later in the same games, and the same "a narrower rule does at least as well" argument did not hold up in v4's final, so I expect a small effect either way; the final's pairing with never-punish on the same seeds is the fair test.
