## Key findings

1. Six rule shapes sit on one plateau against `ah` (finals v1 70.5, v2 69.6, v3 69.5, v4 69.2,
   v5 69.7, v6 68.8, se ~1.1); v6's size gate was flat (n_big and t_end effects 0.6), and all six
   rules ignore everyone after the stop round ~10 (exploration 1).
2. After the stop, ~1 newcomer per episode arrives at each later switch (1.27 / 1.12 / 1.02 / 0.97
   at rounds 8-20), giving 10.1-11.4 against the incumbents' ~13.1 and rising only ~0.5 over the
   block (exploration 2).
3. Conformity runs both ways: in unpunished windows the incumbents move -1.88 over 3 rounds when
   the newcomers give 8+ less, -1.09 at 4-8 less, -0.22 within 1, +1.79 when they give 4+ more
   (exploration 2).
4. Punishing a low newcomer in its arrival round raises it (0-3 givers +5.50 at 21-30 points vs
   +2.51 unpunished; 4-7 givers +3.68 vs +0.41), halves the incumbents' drift (-0.3 to -0.5 vs
   -1.16) and lowers its chance of staying from ~0.75 to ~0.55 (exploration 3; confounded by the
   stop round of the design point).
5. Newcomers are not more responsive than incumbents (incumbent 0-3 givers +6.90 at 21-30 points),
   so what sets them apart is their position below a converged group, not freshness (exploration 4).
6. A norm clause costs 14-47 points per episode if it acts in the arrival round only and 45-164 if
   it acts over the whole block, depending on the slack (exploration 4).

## Hypothesis

On this stack punishment buys a contribution level that persists because the contribution model
conforms each player to its group's mean; the earlier rules buy that level in rounds 0-~10 and then
leave it unguarded. At each later switch about one player arrives from `ah`'s group 2-3 points below
my group's level and, through the same conformity, pulls the incumbents down by up to ~2 points for
the rest of the game. A good manager therefore keeps the plateau's early phase and then switches to
enforcing the group's own norm: it punishes, briefly and in proportion to the shortfall, whoever
falls well below the other members' current mean, above all newcomers in their first round(s),
so that the new arrival is pulled up to the group instead of the group down to it.

The rule, for valid members of my group: while t < t_end, core = min(rate * max(threshold - c, 0),
30); at every round, norm = min(late_rate * max(mean of the other valid members' c - slack - c, 0),
30), dealt in full to members who were outside my group in any of the last `window` rounds and
times `incumbent_factor` to the others (nothing in a group of one); the member gets the larger of
the two.

- `threshold` (float, [6, 13]): early bar; expected ~9.
- `rate` (float, [2, 30], log): early points per point of shortfall; expected ~7.
- `t_end` (int, [4, 16]): first round without the early bar; expected ~8-10.
- `late_rate` (float, [0.3, 10], log): norm points per point below the bar; expected ~2-4.
- `slack` (float, [0, 8]): how far below the others' mean a member may fall; expected ~2-4.
- `window` (int, [1, 4]): rounds after arrival a member counts as new; expected 1-2.
- `incumbent_factor` (float, [0, 1]): share of the norm punishment for non-newcomers; expected 0-0.5.

### Justification

Finding 1 is why the core and its ranges are v6's minus the flat size gate, and why the new idea
acts where none of the earlier rules did, after the stop. Findings 2 and 3 give the mechanism and
its size: about one low newcomer per later switch and a drag of 1-2 points on 3-4 incumbents for
~10 rounds, of order 50-100 pool per episode at stake against 14-47 points spent (finding 6).
Finding 4 grounds `late_rate` and `slack` (the response and the leaving cost both grow with the
punishment, so a proportional, moderate fine below a slack) and finding 6 grounds a short `window`.
Finding 5 is why the clause's bar is relative to the group's current mean and why
`incumbent_factor` exists: the case for punishing late is the gap to the norm, which newcomers
mostly have but incumbents who drop also have; v6's evidence that late punishment against an
absolute bar costs pool argues for a low factor. What would contradict it: late_rate peaking at its
low end (clause off), slack at 8, or no gain over ~70; since finding 4 is confounded by the stop
round, the gain may be smaller than the rough sum, a point or two. Not modelled: newcomers' origin
(always `ah`'s group here), rewarding newcomers who give above the mean (punishment cannot reward),
a separate early bar for the first switch (v3-v6 found the early schedule flat), and the group size
(v6's gate was flat).
