# pf-20261106-9

## Explorations

1. **What does the game look like on average, and what did human managers do?** Looked at
   contribution, punishment, common_good and group sizes (one copy per game, 50 games, 9600
   rows) with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-9/explore1.py. Mean contribution is flat at
   ~8-10 over rounds; human managers punished ~3.5 per player in rounds 0-3 and dropped to ~1
   by the end. They punished by deviation below the group mean: 7.6 for players >10 below the
   mean, 1.0 near the mean, ~0.7 above it; 3.3 for zero contributors vs 0.26 for full ones.
   Group sizes spread widely (1 to 8, and 0 for the empty side); the mean pool per group-round
   is 55. Since the pool is 1.6*sum(c) - sum(p), group size matters as much as per-head
   contribution; next I want to see what punishment buys in contributions and what it costs in
   members.

2. **Does punishment raise contributions, and does it drive players away?** Looked at the
   next-round change in contribution of players who stayed, by punishment and own level, and at
   switch decisions per 4-round block, with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-9/explore2.py.
   Low contributors (0-5) rise by +1.0 when unpunished, +1.3 after 2-5, +2.3 after 5-10, +3.6
   after >10: a gain of roughly 0.25 points of contribution per point of punishment, i.e.
   ~0.4 pool points next round for each pool point spent. High contributors (15-20) who are
   punished collapse (-8 to -10 after >5). Switching: 19% of players switch after a block
   with no own punishment, 24% (1-4), 35% (4-10), 44% (10-20), 55% (>20). Group-level: 13% leave
   when their group punished >2 less per head than the other, 59% when >2 more. Payoff gap
   counts too: 11% leave when own group pays >4 more, 54% when it pays >4 less. Players also
   leave groups of 2-4 more than groups of 5-8 (~40% vs ~23%), and low contributors switch
   more (32% vs 19%). So punishment is a poor deal in pool terms and an expensive one in
   members; the good manager is likely lenient, especially compared with the other manager.
   Next: does a mild early punishment of free riders pay off over the longer run (persistence),
   and how do the AH model's inputs look.
3. **Which human manager styles went with a large pool?** Looked at per game-and-group manager
   style (mean punishment per member-round, how strongly punishment tracked shortfall from the
   group mean) against the group's mean pool and size with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-9/explore3.py (100 game-groups, correlational only).
   Pool by own punishment quintile: 57.8 (0-0.56 per head), 58.9, 63.9 (1.3-1.8), 50.8, 28.1
   (>3.1). Groups that punished >1.3 per head more than the other group averaged 2.65 members
   and a pool of 32, vs 5.35 members and 56 when they punished >1.3 less. Targeting matters most:
   groups whose punishment correlated most negatively with deviation from the group mean
   (corr < -0.57) had pool 73.7 and mean contribution 11.5, vs 28.3 and 6.5 for untargeted
   (corr > -0.13). OLS: pool = 39 - 4.1*pun - 56*targ. So my working hypothesis: small,
   strictly targeted punishment of below-mean contributors, kept below the other group's
   level. Next: when in the 4-round block punishment hurts most, and how persistent the
   contribution effect is.

4. **Timing: which punishments drive switching, and how long does the contribution gain last?**
   Logit of the switch decision on the four punishments of the block, own/other pool in the
   decision round, own size and contribution; OLS of next-round contribution, with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-9/explore4.py. Per punishment point, log-odds of leaving
   rise 0.011 (1st round of block), 0.017, 0.033, 0.050 (decision round): punishment right
   before a decision costs ~5x more members than right after one. Own pool lowers leaving
   (-0.15 per 20 pool points), the other group's raises it (+0.20 per 20): players chase the
   bigger pool, so a pool lead snowballs. For low contributors (<=8) each punishment point
   raises next-round contribution by +0.14 (0.23-0.10) and still +0.15 two rounds later; for
   higher contributors it lowers it by -0.10. With a 0.77 carry-over of own contribution, one
   punishment point on a low contributor buys roughly 0.22 pool points a round for several
   rounds: around break-even over the rest of the game early on, a loss late. The AH switch
   model (configs/training/.../switch_predictor/opt_50ep.yml) takes prev_common_good,
   prev_punishment and agent_group, with an RNN and edges, matching these drivers.
   So: punish early in a block, stay quiet before decisions, stop near the end, and the
   affordable amount depends on how harsh the other manager is.
5. **Drafted the rule.** Five parameters, from entries 2-4: punish only members below the
   group's mean contribution this round (the targeting of entry 3), by `k` points per point of
   shortfall beyond a tolerance `s0`; nobody in the `quiet` rounds before each switch decision
   (entry 4: punishment in the decision round costs ~5x the members of one right after a
   switch; no quiet in rounds 20-23, where there is no decision left); nobody from round `t_end`
   on (the contribution gain needs rounds left to repay its cost); and `k` scaled by
   1 + `w` * (the other manager's mean punishment per member-round so far, read from `p` and
   `in_group`), since what drives players away is punishment relative to the other group
   (entry 3: 2.65 vs 5.35 members). Timed-out members are left out of the mean and not punished.
   Tested it on a hand-made history with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-9/test_rule.py:
   mean 7.5, s0=2, k=0.5 gives 2.75 to a zero contributor and 0.25 to a 5; 0 in round 3 with
   quiet=1 and in round 23 with t_end=22; x3 when the other manager has punished 4 per head and
   w=0.5. A side finding: in a fresh Python process the first torch call with keyword or scalar
   overloads (`sum(-1, keepdim=True)`, `th.remainder`) does a one-time import, which the
   rule's builtin-free scope blocks; the simulation has warmed torch up long before the rule
   runs and validate-rule passes, but I dropped `keepdim` anyway and the test warms up first.
   validate-rule passes on all 256 design points.

## Key findings

1. Punishment buys little contribution: a low contributor (<=8) gives about +0.14 more per
   punishment point next round and +0.15 two rounds later, while punished high contributors
   drop (-0.10 per point; -8 to -10 after >5 points) (explorations 2, 4).
2. Punishment drives players out: 19% switch after a block with no own punishment, 55% after
   more than 20 points; groups punishing >1.3 per head more than the other group averaged 2.65
   members vs 5.35 when punishing >1.3 less (explorations 2, 3).
3. Timing matters: per punishment point, log-odds of leaving rise 0.011 in the first round of a
   block and 0.050 in the decision round (exploration 4).
4. Players chase the bigger pool: own pool lowers leaving (-0.15 log-odds per 20 points), the
   other group's raises it (+0.20), so member gains snowball (exploration 4).
5. Among human managers, targeting beat volume: groups whose punishment tracked shortfall from
   the group mean most closely had pool 73.7 vs 28.3 for untargeted ones; moderate punishment
   (1.3-1.8 per head) went with pool 63.9, heavy (>3.1) with 28.1 (exploration 3).

## Hypothesis

A good manager keeps its group the more attractive one, since the pool grows with members as
much as with per-head contribution and players move towards the bigger pool and away from
punishment. It therefore punishes little, only clear free riders relative to the group's own
mean, early in each 4-round block and never just before a switch decision, stops when too few
rounds are left to repay the cost, and allows itself more when the other manager is harsh
(players cannot escape to a gentler group), less when the other is lenient.

The rule: punishment = k * (1 + w * other_mean_p) * max(0, group_mean_c - c_i - s0), applied to
valid members, except in the `quiet` rounds before each decision (rounds < 20) and from round
`t_end` on.
- `k` (float, [0.05, 2], log): points per point of shortfall. I expect 0.2-0.7; at 0.05 the
  rule almost never punishes (floors to 0), at 2 it is harsh, so both corners are covered.
- `s0` (float, [0, 8]): unpunished shortfall below the mean; I expect 1-4, so near-average
  players are left alone (finding 1: punishing those near or above the mean backfires).
- `quiet` (int, 0-3): silent rounds before each decision; I expect 1-2 (finding 3).
- `t_end` (int, 6-24): first round with no punishment; I expect 16-22 (24 = never stop).
- `w` (float, [0, 1]): licence from the other manager's harshness; I expect 0.2-0.6. The other
  manager's mean punishment per head is ~1-3 for humans, so w=1 can triple k.

### Justification

Findings 1 and 2 together say a punishment point returns well under a pool point next round
while costing members, so `k` should be small and the range reaches down to effectively no
punishment; finding 5 says that what punishment there is should be targeted at shortfall from
the group mean, which is the rule's base term, with `s0` sparing the near-average contributors
whom finding 1 shows collapse when punished. Finding 3 motivates `quiet`, and the
time-limited carry-over in finding 1 motivates `t_end`. Finding 2's relative effect (members
leave towards the less punishing group) and finding 4's snowball motivate `w`: against a harsh
manager the switching penalty of our punishment is smaller, against a lenient one larger. It
would be contradicted if the sweep puts k at its lower edge with w irrelevant (then no
punishment is best, and the rule degenerates into that, which is still a valid answer), or if
quiet=0 and t_end=24 win clearly (timing would not matter in the AH models). I did not model
the other group's contributions or pool directly (a manager cannot change them, and reacting
to them adds parameters the sweep can't fill well), group size effects on the threshold,
history-based targeting of repeat free riders, or rewarding newcomers; the evidence from 50
games, correlational at the group level, is too thin to tune them.
