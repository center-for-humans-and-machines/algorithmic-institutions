## Hypothesis

A good manager raises the group with a short, hard, convex opening and
then only guards against defectors until about round 15. In the opening
what matters is how hard a middling contributor is hit: a player at 10
should pay about 7 points, a free rider the cap, a player at 16-19 next
to nothing, and players already above their group's mean are spared.
After the opening only players at or near zero are fined, with a fixed
large fine. Five versions of this family show its ceiling is ~656; v6
does not add structure but spends the sweep where the pooled evidence
puts the peak, in coordinates where the peak is sharp, so that the final
is less a lucky draw. The rule:
punishment = mid_fine x (max(0, 20-c)/10)^curvature x [c < mean(c valid)
+ gate_margin] x [t < window] + net_fine x [window <= t < net_end] x
[c <= net_floor], capped at 30 by the manager.
- `mid_fine` (float, 5-10, log): the opening fine of a player at 10.
- `curvature` (float, 1.7-3.2, log): convexity of the opening fine
  around c=10; higher spares near-full contributors more and hits free
  riders harder.
- `window` (int, 5-8): rounds of the opening.
- `gate_margin` (float, 1-8): in the opening only players below the
  group's mean plus this margin are fined; ~8 is off.
- `net_floor` (int, 1-4): after the opening only players at or below it
  are fined.
- `net_end` (int, 12-18): round at which the net stops.
- `net_fine` (float, 18-30): the fixed fine of a defector in the net.

### Justification

Findings 1 and 2 say the family is saturated and its finals are limited
by the sweep's precision, hence a consolidation rather than a new
structure (v1-v5 already tried tapers, escalation, drop nets, group
triggers, token fines, lowest-only fines on surrogates, all losing).
Finding 3 is the reason for mid_fine: it turns the F-curvature ridge into
one sharp axis; I expect its best at 6-8 and set 5-10 (log, centred on
7), where the pooled profile falls 3-4 at either end. Finding 4 sets the
rest: curvature flat over 2.0-2.9, so the wider 1.7-3.2; window 6-7 in
5-8; gate 3-4 in 1-8 (8 keeps the ungated rule in the design: a peak at
the top means the gate is worth nothing on the real clones, as v5's
finals, gated 652.7-654.4 vs ungated 650.4-653.5, could not settle);
floor 2-3 in 1-4; end 14-15 in 12-18; net fine 22-26 in 18-30 (30 is the
cap). Finding 5 is the expected payoff of the narrowing. The hypothesis
is contradicted if v6's final is not at least ~655 (the pooled surface
would then be overconfident), or if the landscape puts mid_fine at 5 or
10 (the peak was mislocated). Not modelled: timeouts beyond excluding
them from the group mean (~2%, no lever), new structure (finding 1 and
v1-v5's dead ends), and the free rider's fine, which the cap makes moot.
