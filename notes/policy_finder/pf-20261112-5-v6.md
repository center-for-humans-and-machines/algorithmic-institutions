## Key findings

1. The block-start shock family (v3-v5) is saturated: slot-adjusted over all five sweeps (slot
   sd 2.68, residual 1.13), v5's design averages 15.48, the best of all, but every parameter's
   binned effect within it is <= 0.8 (hi 5-6 best, D4 26-30 best, D0 and D8 flat, s mildly
   favouring 0.6-1) (exploration 1).
2. Points buy contribution at a near-constant rate (~0.1 contribution per point over the next 3
   rounds at round 0, about half that at round 4), while the extra exit flattens above 20 points
   (-0.071 / -0.080 / -0.083 at 15-20 / 20-25 / 25-29): doses near the cap are the cheapest in
   members. Unpunished members also rise, +0.14 per 10 points dealt to the others (+0.33 for
   members giving 11-20) (exploration 2).
3. At rounds 4 and 8, members giving 0-2 answer a shock about half as much as members giving 3-5
   (+0.76 vs +1.53 at round 4, +0.45 vs +1.35 at round 8, over the next 3 rounds) and leave as
   often; at round 0 they answer well (2.1-2.6 vs 3.0-3.2) (exploration 3).
4. In the lead, per 10 points, shocks on 0-2 contributors are worth +0.43 / -0.05 at round 4
   (stayer / newcomer) and -0.49 / -1.37 at round 8, against +0.49 / +0.63 and +0.62 / +2.18 on
   3-5 contributors (v3-v5 pairs; all-version pairs agree); at round 0 0-2 is still positive
   (+0.37 vs +1.05). About half of v5's round-4/8 points went to members giving 0-2
   (exploration 4).
5. Newcomers answer a round-4/8 shock better and leave less than stayers (c 3-5: +1.62 vs +1.32,
   exit -0.06 vs -0.12), but v5's sweep did not reward sparing stayers (finding 1)
   (exploration 3).

## Hypothesis

On this stack the score is mostly membership, which no shock schedule moves much, so the
manager's lever is a few heavy shocks at block starts that set a lasting contribution habit:
round 0 (the habit leaks at the first decision but has 23 rounds to work), round 4 (right after
the biggest reshuffle, the switch model weighs it least) and round 8. A good manager aims each
shock where it buys the most contribution per point: at members who give little but not almost
nothing. Right after a switch decision, someone giving 0-2 answers a shock weakly, so the points
spent on them are mostly lost to the pool; members giving about 3 to 5-6 answer best. The rule:
in round 0 every member of my group who timed in, is not alone and gave at most `hi` gets `D0`;
in rounds 4 and 8 such a member who gave between `lo` and `hi` gets `D4` (`D8`); nothing in any
other round.

- `D0` (float, [0, 30] uniform): the round-0 shock. Expect 10-30, small effect.
- `D4` (float, [10, 30] uniform): the round-4 shock. Expect 22-30.
- `D8` (float, [0, 30] uniform): the round-8 shock. Expect 10-30 once 0-2 contributors are
  spared.
- `lo` (int, 0..4): the lowest contribution shocked at rounds 4 and 8. Expect 2-3.
- `hi` (int, 4..7): the highest contribution shocked. Expect 5-6.

### Justification

Finding 1 is why I keep v4/v5's frame (block-start shocks at 0, 4, 8, nothing from round 9 on,
the stayer split dropped since v5's sweep found no gain from it) and why I look for a new lever
rather than tuning doses. Findings 3 and 4 are the lever: at rounds 4 and 8 half the points went
to members giving 0-2, who answer half as much as 3-5 contributors and whose shock is worth
nothing to negative in the lead (round 8 -0.5..-1.4 per 10 points), so `lo` lets the sweep
spare them; lo 0 is the v3-v5 rule, so the design keeps the earlier best region and the sweep
can reject the idea. lo stops at 4 because 3-5 contributors are the best responders of all and
must stay targetable with hi 4..7. lo is not applied at round 0, where 0-2 contributors still
answer well (finding 3) and their shock is still positive (finding 4). Finding 2 sets the dose
ranges: no saturation and the cheapest exits near the cap, so all three reach 30 and D4 starts
at 10 (v4/v5's best D4 was high). hi spans 4..7 around v3-v5's 5-6 optimum (3 and 8 lost in v5).
What would contradict it: a sweep peak at lo 0-1, or `own` falling as lo rises (the band
contrasts come from game pairs whose later policies differ, so their levels are confounded, and
v2's lo 0/1/2 showed no gain - though v2 mixed rounds 0-1 and 4-5). Not modelled: the newcomer
split (finding 5: positive in pairs, null in v5's sweep), the group's size and the member's gap
to the group (no pattern in exploration 3), rounds 12+ (negative in v3-v5), and a dose graded by
contribution (the band is a coarser, sweep-friendly version of it). Expected gain: ~0.3-0.8 pool
points over the family's level, near the resolution of the paired final.
