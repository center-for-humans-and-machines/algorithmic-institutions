## Key findings

1. Six versions sit at 67.1 / 71.9 / 72.9 / 71.3 / 71.8 / 72.7. v6's
   decay peaked at 1.0, and its best recheck (s202, which escalates in
   phase 1 by 1.42) scored 74.2 +- 1.15. With slope 12-18 and a cap of 30,
   the dose is nearly binary (exploration 1).
2. Herding is linear in the points the others get (+0.041 per point at
   round 3), and the number of punished members adds nothing, so
   concentrating punishment is no lever. In round 0 a full 30 to a
   laggard costs almost no extra exit (stay 0.578 vs 0.595 unpunished)
   and lifts it by +7.1 vs +1.9 by round 3 (exploration 2).
3. A quarter of the late group still free-rides unpunished (22-24% at
   c <= 5). But a late phase of punishment loses pool through headcount:
   -23 / -26 pool over the rest of the game at rounds 12 / 16 with one
   deep laggard, 0.15-0.3 fewer members at the end. Round 8 is
   break-even, and v2's late cut-offs were never positive (explorations
   3-6).
4. The whole headcount gap to zero is made at the first decision. 1.60
   of my members leave against zero's 1.10, the non-laggards at 0.351
   against 0.273. After round 7 the flows are zero's (exploration 7).
5. Exit per point depends on the position in the phase. Own punishment
   costs +0.0020 / +0.0029 / +0.0051 in leaving at positions 0 / 1 / 2
   (phase 0), and the per-capita pool gap weighs more the closer it is
   to the decision. Members lost per point are 0.0111 / 0.0141 / 0.0203 in
   phase 0 and about 0.0066 / 0.0094 / 0.0141 in phases 1-2. The first
   decision is about twice as sensitive to the gap (exploration 8).
6. Lift per point is +0.079 / +0.074 / +0.038 at positions 0 / 1 / 2 of
   phase 0, and +0.045 / +0.058 / +0.030 in phase 1. Position 2 is the
   worst punishing round in every phase, with half the lift and the most
   exit. Position 1 is nearly as good as position 0 (exploration 9).

## Hypothesis

A good manager on this stack invests in the norm early and then stops.
In each early phase it hits laggards, relative to their own group, in
proportion to their shortfall, hard in the round right after a decision.
It follows up the next round on those still lagging, and then goes
quiet for the last two rounds before the decision. Those are the rounds
whose punishment and lowered per-capita pool the switch model weighs
most when members decide to leave, and when ah's members decide to join.
Because the first decision is twice as sensitive to the pool gap as the
later ones, phase 0's intensity and the later phases' are separate
choices. The punishment stops before round 12, where a lost member
outweighs a lifted free rider.

The rule: shortfall = max(0, `rel` x mean c of the other valid members -
c); dose = min(30, `slope` x shortfall x position weight x phase
weight). The position weight is 1 / `w1` / `w2` / 0 at positions 0 / 1 / 2
/ 3 of each 4-round phase, the phase weight is 1 in rounds 0-3 and
`later` from round 4, and the dose is 0 after `last_round`.

- `rel` (float, [0.66, 0.98]): the laggard threshold as a fraction of the
  others' mean. I expect 0.78-0.86.
- `slope` (float, [4, 40] log): points per point of shortfall in round 0. I
  expect 9-20.
- `w1` (float, [0, 1.5]): the position-1 dose as a share of the fresh dose.
  I expect 0.6-1.0.
- `w2` (float, [0, 1]): the position-2 dose as a share of the fresh dose. I
  expect 0-0.3.
- `later` (float, [0.4, 2.5] log): the intensity from round 4 on relative
  to phase 0. I expect 1-1.6.
- `last_round` (int, 6..14): the last punishing round. I expect 8-11.

### Justification

Findings 1-3 say the family's core is right and that its levers outside
the early phases are exhausted. Concentration (2) and late or endgame
enforcement (3) are both dead ends, and v6's geometric decay was flat
at 1. So v7 keeps `rel`, `slope` and `last_round` (whose top I narrowed
to 14 because of finding 3), and spends its new parameters where finding
4 puts the whole remaining loss: the first exodus. Findings 5 and 6 give
two gradients that no earlier version could follow. Position 2 is worse
than position 1 on lift (half) and on exit (1.4-1.8x), so v6's single
`mid` at 0.4 had to average them; splitting it into `w1` and `w2` lets
the sweep give position 1 a full follow-up and drop position 2. The
first decision's gap sensitivity is about twice the later decisions',
while the phase-1 lift per point is lower. That makes the phase-1
intensity a genuine trade-off, so `later` gets a log range centred on
1, which holds both v6's peak and s202's 1.42.

What would contradict it: w2 peaking near w1, or at its top, would
mean the position-2 exit cost is overstated by my correlational fits.
w1 peaking near 0 would mean the follow-up is not worth its exit. A
`later` peak at an end of its range would mean the phase trade-off is
one-sided. All the per-point numbers come from rules choosing their
targets, so they are associations, not experiments. The sweep decides.

Not modelled: concentration on single members, late or endgame
enforcement and history terms (findings 2 and 3, and v6's finding 2).
Also v5's pool-gap budget, which its sweep rejected, and a separate
phase-2 intensity, which `last_round` and `later` already cover since
phases 1 and 2 have the same exit costs (finding 5).
