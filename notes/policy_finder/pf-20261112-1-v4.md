## Key findings

1. v3's final point gained +0.234 (se 0.161) over the zero manager on the shared final seed, +1.18 per touched game in 396 of 2000 games; the bar to beat is 16.99 (explorations 1, 2).
2. The three sweeps share seeds and batch layout, so unpunished games replay identically across versions (97.5% of both-unpunished slots) and every punished game has a zero-manager twin: paired change per punished game v1 -3.70, v2 -1.84, v3 +1.19 (exploration 4).
3. A full-group push pays with a real control: per touched game +1.62 at decision 7, +0.72 at 11, +0.25 at 15, -0.85 at 19; +1.39 at 8 members, +0.89 at 7; one or two targets +1.2 to +1.5, three -0.12; it raises my pool (+0.8 at decision 7) and lowers ah's (-0.8 to -1.0) (exploration 5).
4. The dose matters: 1-10 points +0.33 per touched game, 15-20 +1.45, 20-25 +2.10, 25-30 +1.14; targets with window means 1-5 gain more than zero givers (exploration 5).
5. Per design point, the gain on the score is +0.56 (se 0.09, 30% of games touched) for n_full 7 with bar >= 3, amount >= 14, last_dec <= 3, against +0.27 for n_full 8 in the same region; the bar's gain still rose at v3's top value 6 (exploration 6).
6. Pushes at sizes 2-6 lose in v2's paired rows (-0.2 to -6 per game, more with more targets), though those rows include v2's repeated later pushes (exploration 5).

## Hypothesis

The zero manager wins the members and stays the backbone; the only punishment that pays on this stack is a push into an (almost) empty opponent group. When my group holds 7 or 8 players, a free rider punished hard in a decision round either leaves into ah's group, where he arrives alone or nearly so with a large previous punishment that ah answers by punishing him round after round (ah's pool sinks), or stays and is pushed again; my remaining group gives a little more, and ah's group is too small to draw bystanders. It pays early (rounds 7 and 11 most, 15 a little), not at 19, with a dose around 20-25, on the lowest one to three members whose window mean is low but not necessarily zero. v4 keeps v3's rule and puts its whole design where the paired effects are positive: `n_full` (int, fixed 7: push when my group has at least 7 members), `bar` (int, [3, 8]: a member is pushable if his window mean is at most bar; expected best 5-7), `amount` (int, [14, 30]: points dealt to each pushed member in the decision round; expected 20-25), `n_push` (int, [1, 3]: most members pushed per decision, lowest window means first; expected 2-3), `last_dec` (int, [2, 3]: last decision with a push, 11 or 15; expected 3).

### Justification

Finding 2 gives a seed-matched control the earlier versions lacked, and finding 3 is the mechanism measured with it: positive at 7 and 8 members, early decisions positive, 19 negative, hence `last_dec` [2, 3]. Finding 5 fixes `n_full` at 7: the per-push gain at 7 is smaller but pushes fire three times as often, doubling the total, and finding 6 argues against going below 7. Findings 4 and 5 set `bar` [3, 8] (no gain below 3, still rising at 6, above 8 unmeasured) and `amount` [14, 30] (best 20-25, low doses buy little). `n_push` [1, 3]: per point three was best (+0.23 vs +0.15), per touched game three targets lose, so I leave it to the sweep. Expected gain over zero: about +0.4 to +0.6 on the score, against v3's +0.23 (finding 1). What would contradict it: a paired effect near zero or negative for v4's points in the next sweep (its games are paired with v1-v3 at the same slots), especially for high bars (pushing contributors of 6-8) or n_push 3. Caveats: I chose the region on the same sweep games that measured it (a winner's-curse risk, softened by its breadth); not modelled: pushes at 6 or fewer members, the lone free rider at size 1, sparing zero givers (who gained less), and anything at decision 3 (groups are never full there).
