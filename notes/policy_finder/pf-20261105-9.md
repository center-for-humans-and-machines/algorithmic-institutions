# pf-20261105-9

## Explorations

1. **What do the reference managers score, and where does the gap come from?** Looked at
   `plots/simulation/29_EXP1_reference/aggregates.csv` and per_round.parquet with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-9/explore_sim.py. Score (sum over 24 rounds of the mean
   valid payoff): zero 589.3, humanlike 550.6. Under zero, contributions decay from 11.6 to
   5.5; under humanlike they rise from 11.6 to ~13.2 and stay there, with 2-3 points of
   punishment per player per round. Late in the game humanlike's payoff is level with
   zero's (~23.8 vs 23.0 at round 23), but the first ~12 rounds cost it ~3-5 points per
   round. So punishment does sustain cooperation in the clones, but the humanlike manager
   pays too much for it, too early and on the wrong people. The bar to beat is zero's 589.
   Next: who the humanlike manager punishes and how the clones respond.

2. **How do the clones respond to punishment, by own contribution?** Same script, humanlike
   run, next-round change in contribution by contribution bin x punishment bin. Low
   contributors respond to punishment in a dose-dependent way: c in (0,5] goes +0.03 when
   unpunished, +0.76 (p 1-2), +1.35 (p 3-5), +2.59 (p 6-10), +3.99 (p>10); c=0 goes +1.4 ->
   +5.3. High contributors punished go *down* (c=20: -0.75 unpunished, -2.2 with p 6-10).
   Humanlike punishes c in 10-15 45% of the time and even c=20 8% of the time - money wasted
   or harmful. Under zero, high contributors fall by ~1.7 per round; under humanlike
   unpunished high contributors fall only 0.75, so a punishing regime also stabilises the
   cooperators (they see free riders being disciplined / others rising). Regression c_next ~
   c + others: c 0.79, others' mean 0.18 - conditional cooperation, so raising a free rider
   also lifts the others. Next: the pilot random_1 rules, which were linear in the shortfall
   with random slopes, to see which slope/threshold paid.

3. **Which random_1 rules did well with humans?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-9/explore_pilot.py
   fits punishment ~ contribution per random_1 group (49 groups). Rules were roughly
   p = slope*(thr - c) with thr ~19 (median 19.4) and slope 0.04-1.66. Steeper slope ->
   higher late contributions (corr 0.67; top third slope 1.0-1.7: late c 17.5, late payoff
   27.6; bottom third: 11.1 and 21.4) while mean punishment barely changes (2.9 vs 3.6),
   because a steep rule is obeyed. Mean punishment over the whole game correlates -0.89 with
   payoff: what costs is punishment actually dealt, not a harsh rule. So a steep threat on
   the shortfall from (near) full contribution, which is rarely triggered once obeyed, looks
   right. Next: the cost of early punishment and the end game, where punishment cannot pay.
4. **Does a punishment keep paying in later rounds, and does it spill over?** Checked what the
   clones see: configs/simulation/manager_testing/29_EXP1_reference.yml and
   configs/training/artificial_humans/contribution/script_21_no_grid.yml - the contribution
   clone reads only previous contributions, punishments and validity of the group, through an
   RNN; no round number. So it has no end-game of its own, and a punishment in the last round
   can only cost; one late in the game has few rounds left to pay back. With
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-9/persistence.py (humanlike sim, c at t+k on punishment at t,
   controls c, c_prev, others' c and p, round FE): for a low contributor 1 point raises own c by
   ~0.085 at t+1 and still ~0.08 at t+6 (punishment + punishment:low); the others' mean
   punishment raises one's own c by 0.06 (t+1) to 0.13 (t+6). So a point on one player buys
   ~0.15-0.2 contribution points per round for many rounds, i.e. ~0.1 group payoff per round
   for a cost of 2 (player + pool): it pays back only over ~10-20 rounds. Punishment is an
   early-game investment; I want a round after which the rule stops. Next: is the response
   linear in the dose, or do small punishments buy more per point?

5. **Dose-response with controls, sim vs humans.** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-9/dose.py,
   next and 3-ahead contribution on punishment bins, controlling c and others' c, by own
   contribution band. Sim, c 10-16: p 1-2 gives +0.62 (t+1) and +0.76 (t+3), p 3-5 +0.52/+0.78,
   p 8-12 only +0.55/+1.2: strongly concave, the first 1-2 points do most of the work. Sim,
   c 0-9: roughly 0.15-0.25 per point up to ~12 points, still concave (p 1-2: +0.46/+0.58; p >12:
   +2.05/+2.27). Sim, c 17-20: punishment lowers next contribution (p 5-8: -1.21), recovering by
   t+3. Humans show the same shape, noisier (c 17-20, p 5-8: -3.06). So: punish the shortfall
   from a target, never those at/near full contribution, with a cap well below 30 because the
   marginal point buys little; stop punishing some rounds before the end. That makes a 4-param
   rule: target, slope, cap, stop round. Next: draft and validate it.
6. **Drafted the rule and checked its first-round cost.** configs/managers/rule_based/pf-20261105-9.yml:
   p_i = min(slope * max(target - c_i, 0), cap) while t < stop_round, else 0. Validated
   (256 distinct points). scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-9/cost_check.py applies it to the
   zero sim's round-0 contributions (median 11, quartiles 7/16): target 15, slope 1, cap 5
   deals 2.8 points per player = 5.7 payoff per player in that round; target 18, slope 0.5,
   cap 3 deals 2.0 (3.9 lost). That is the same order as humanlike's early losses, so whether
   the rule beats zero hinges on compliance arriving fast and on a small cap; the cap and slope
   ranges reach down to ~1 point so the sweep can find a "token punishment" regime, and
   stop_round down to 2 so it can find near-zero punishing if that is best. I chose not to add
   a group-relative target: an absolute target lifts the whole group, a relative one lets it
   slide together (zero's decline from 11.6 to 5.5 is a group slide).

## Key findings

1. The bar is the zero manager, 589.3, not humanlike, 550.6: humanlike sustains contributions
   (13.2 vs 5.5 at round 23) but pays 2-3 punishment points per player per round, which costs
   double (exploration 1).
2. Humanlike wastes punishment: it punishes 45% of players at c 10-15 and 8% at c = 20, and
   punishing players at c 17-20 lowers their next contribution (sim -1.21 for 5-8 points,
   humans -3.06) (explorations 2, 5).
3. Among random_1 rules (p ~ slope * (thr - c), thr median 19.4), steeper slopes gave higher
   late contributions (corr 0.67) at hardly more punishment dealt (2.9 vs 3.6); punishment
   dealt correlates -0.89 with payoff (exploration 3).
4. A punishment point on a low contributor buys ~0.15-0.2 contribution points per round,
   own + spillover, persisting at least 6 rounds; it repays its cost of 2 only over ~10-20
   rounds, so it must come early (exploration 4). The clones have no round input, so the
   last rounds' punishment is pure loss.
5. The dose response is concave: for c 10-16 1-2 points already give +0.6-0.8, 8-12 points
   barely more; for c 0-9 about 0.15-0.25 per point (exploration 5).

## Hypothesis

A good manager makes punishment an early, cheap, targeted investment: it punishes only
shortfalls from a target contribution, mildly (a few points, capped), never those at or near
full contribution, and it stops punishing a number of rounds before the end, when the
remaining rounds can no longer pay the punishment back. The rule:
p_i = min(slope * max(target - c_i, 0), cap) for t < stop_round, else 0.
- `target` (float, [8, 20]): the contribution asked for; below it a player is punished.
- `slope` (float, [0.1, 4], log): punishment points per point of shortfall.
- `cap` (float, [1, 20], log): the most any player gets in a round.
- `stop_round` (int, 2..24): the first round with no punishment.

### Justification

Findings 1 and 4 say punishment pays only through later contributions and only if it comes
early, hence stop_round; I expect its best near 12-20 (benefits need ~10 rounds to repay), the
range 2-24 lets the sweep reach almost-never-punish (close to zero's 589) and always-punish.
Finding 2 says punishing near-full contributors backfires, hence a target below which only
shortfalls count; random_1's thresholds sat near 19 (finding 3), but the clones' harm at c 17-20
suggests 14-19, so the range 8-20 covers both. Finding 3 favours a steep slope (deterrence), and
finding 5 a small cap (the first points do the work); I expect slope ~0.5-2 and cap ~2-8, and
log ranges let the sweep explore token punishments as finely as harsh ones. What would
contradict this: the sweep's best at stop_round near 2 or cap near 1 (punishment never pays
against these clones), or best at target 20 with a large cap (a harsh full-cooperation norm
pays after all). Not modelled: group-relative targets (finding 1: the group slides together),
rewarding improvement or forgiving a player who raised their contribution (no clear
evidence beyond what the shortfall already captures), and timeouts (the env drops a
timed-out player's punishment, and timeouts are rare, ~2% of rows in the reference sims; I did
not study how they affect the others' contributions).
