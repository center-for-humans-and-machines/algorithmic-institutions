# pf-20261104-5-v4

## Explorations

1. **Where did v3 end up?** Read v3's `final/sweep.json` and
   `landscapes.json`, and v1's and v2's finals for comparison. v3's
   surface peak is k=7.0, thr=9.3, cap=30, budget=60, quiet=0.96,
   t_stop=2. On fresh seeds it scores 68.3 (se 1.05). The best final point
   is s132 (k=5.5, thr=11.6, cap=28, budget=140, quiet=0.57, t_stop=8) at
   70.2 (se 1.17). v1 scored 67.3 and v2 67.6, so the bar is ~68-70 and
   all three versions sit on the same plateau within ~2 se. The profiles:
   - thr is the strongest parameter, peaking at 9-9.5. It falls to 62.7
     at 4 and 61.4 at 14.
   - k rises up to ~7 and is flat beyond.
   - cap rises steadily to its top edge of 30 (66 at 5, 69 at 30).
   - budget is nearly flat, with a broad top at 40-90.
   - quiet is flat for the third time.
   - t_stop is best at its *lower edge* of 2 (69.0) and falls to 67.1 at
     20.
   So a hard push in rounds 0-2 on anyone giving below ~9, then nothing,
   is as good as anything else found. The range [2, 21] cut off t_stop 0
   and 1, so the early window is not resolved. The budget mechanism did
   not add measurable value, because with t_stop=2 hardly anyone gets past
   the first push. Next: in v3's sweep, which rounds' punishment pays, and
   what happens to members around the first decision.
2. **Which punishment pays in v3's sweep, by point?** Summarised all 256
   v3 points with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v4_points.py (our
   group = group 0) and regressed them with v4_points_reg.py.
   Score on punishment by class, for rounds 0-2 (P0) and rounds 3+ (P3):
   - P0 on 3-6 givers: +0.20 per point (se 0.04).
   - P0 on 0-2 givers: -0.005.
   - P0 on 7-10 givers: -0.05.
   - P0 on 11+ givers: -0.45.
   - From round 3 on, every class is within ±0.04, except 11+ (-0.09).
   This repeats v3's exploration 4 on new rules. The score barely
   correlates with the other group's score (0.02). Ours trades members
   for contribution: n1 (members in rounds 4-7) correlates -0.87 with
   cm1 (contribution per member). With no punishment n1 is ~4.85, and
   each early point on a 0-2 giver costs 0.019 members, on an 11+ giver
   0.067. The per-round regression is too collinear to read, because
   every rule punishes rounds 0-2 alike.
   Player level (v4_value.py, part 1): I took a member's future
   contribution to our pool (1.6 x its contributions in our group over
   t+1..23) minus the dose, against unpunished members of the same class.
   - In rounds 1-2 it is positive for 0-5 givers at doses of 5+ (+5 to
     +42), negative for 6-8 givers (-5 to -13), and strongly negative for
     9+.
   - Later rounds look positive for low givers too. But that comparison
     is confounded: the late punished come from harsh rules that lift the
     whole group, and the point-level numbers do not support it.
   So the shape of the dose in rounds 0-2 is tuned. Next: timing *within*
   the first block. v1 (exploration 4) found that leaving weighs a
   punishment by its distance to the decision: +0.36pp per point 4 rounds
   before, up to +1.24pp in the decision round. Round 0 is then the
   cheapest round to punish in terms of leaving, and v3 punished rounds
   0-2 equally.
3. **Does leaving weigh a punishment by its place in the block, in v3's
   sweep too?** With scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v4_switch_timing.py
   (v3 sweep part 1) I ran a linear probability model of leaving at the
   decisions after rounds 3, 7 and 11. It covers members who were in our
   group through the block, and controls for their mean contribution, both
   groups' per-member shares and our group size. Leaving per point received
   at block position 0/1/2/3:
   - Decision after round 3: 0.54/0.83/1.20/0.49pp.
   - After round 7: 0.34/0.66/0.97/0.72pp.
   - After round 11: 0.29/0.64/0.96/0.75pp.
   - The decision-round point also lowers our share, which costs a
     further ~0.4pp per point. So a point at position 3 costs about as
     much as one at position 2.
   A point at the block's first round costs a third to half of the leaving
   of a point two rounds later. The next-round contribution response per
   point (stayers giving <10) is 0.12/0.15/0.19 at positions 0/1/2 of
   block 1, and similar in blocks 2 and 3. So the earlier point buys
   somewhat less immediate lift but much less leaving. The ratio is
   0.22/0.18/0.16 at positions 0/1/2. Leaving is expensive: a 1pp higher
   chance of losing a ~10-giver with ~20 rounds left is worth ~3 pool
   points, three times the point itself. So the timing inside a block is a
   lever v1-v3 never used. quiet only touched position 3, and v3's peak
   punished positions 0, 1 and 2 alike. It also suggests punishing
   newcomers in their first round with us (rounds 4, 8, ...), at the
   cheapest point of their block. Leavers also give slightly *more* than
   stayers (+0.1 to +0.4pp per contribution point), so leaving costs
   contributors.
4. **Drafted v4.** I kept v3's base, k * relu(thr - c_i), for valid
   members, and v3's per-player taper exp(-R_i / budget), where R_i is
   all punishment received from either manager. Changes:
   - Replaced `quiet` with a within-block weight `w`. The punishment in a
     round is multiplied by w^pos, where pos = t mod 4 is the round's
     place in its switching block (0 in rounds 0, 4, 8, ...; 3 in the
     decision rounds). w=1 is v3 without quiet. w=0 punishes only in the
     first round of each block, where exploration 3 says a point costs
     the least leaving and where newcomers arrive. quiet was flat three
     times; w^3 covers the decision round anyway.
   - Dropped `cap`. It ended at its top edge of 30 in v2 and v3, and the
     manager already clamps at 30. The ramp's width is now 30/k.
   - Opened `t_stop` to [0, 23]. v3's best was at its lower edge of 2, so
     0 and 1 must be reachable. With w small, t_stop decides how many
     block starts get a push.
   Checked in a fresh process with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/test_rule_v4.py (k=3, thr=8, w=0.5,
   budget=20, t_stop=12; contributions 0/0/4/7/11; player 0 received 5
   every past round):
   - Round 0 gives 24/24/12/3/0.
   - Rounds 1/2/3 are scaled by 0.5/0.25/0.125, and round 4 is back to
     full.
   - Player 0 tapers: 8.8 in round 4 and 1.2 in round 12.
   - Nothing is charged from round 13, or to timed-out or other-group
     players.
   - With w=0 the rule punishes rounds 0 and 4 and nothing in between
     (torch gives 0^0 = 1).
   The validator passes on all 256 points.

## Key findings

1. v1, v2 and v3 sit on one plateau. Their final scores are 67.3, 67.6
   and 68.3 (v3's best final point 70.2), each with se ~1.1. v3's peak is
   a hard push in rounds 0-2 on anyone giving below ~9 (k=7,
   thr=9.3, cap at its 30 edge), then nothing (t_stop=2, the lower edge
   of its range). quiet is flat for the third time, and budget nearly so
   (exploration 1).
2. Across v3's points, rounds 0-2 punishment on 3-6 givers pays +0.20
   score per point. On 0-2 givers it pays nothing (-0.005), on 7-10
   givers -0.05 and on 11+ givers -0.45. Punishment from round 3 on is
   near zero per point. The score trades members against contribution
   per member (n1 vs cm1 correlation -0.87) (exploration 2).
3. Leaving weighs a punishment point by its place in the 4-round block.
   It costs 0.29-0.54pp at the block's first round, 0.64-0.83pp at the
   second and 0.96-1.20pp at the third. The decision round costs about as
   much as the third, once its effect on our share is counted. The next
   round's contribution lift per point is 0.12/0.15/0.19 at positions
   0/1/2. So the early point gives the most lift per point of leaving
   (exploration 3).
4. A 1pp higher chance of losing a ~10-giver with ~20 rounds left costs
   ~3 pool points, three times the punishment point itself. Leavers give
   slightly more than stayers (exploration 3).

## Hypothesis

A good manager here invests in an early, firm push on under-contributors.
In this simulation the lift from it lasts. It places that push where it
drives the fewest players away, which is the first round after each
switch opportunity, far from the next decision. Those are rounds 0, 4,
8, ..., where newcomers also arrive. The manager tapers each player's
punishment as the player's received total grows, and stops after a few
blocks. The rule punishes each valid member i by
k * max(0, thr - c_i) * exp(-R_i / budget) * w^(t mod 4), and not at all
after `t_stop`; the manager clamps it at 30.

- `k` (float, [1, 15], log): punishment points per contribution point
  below the threshold. 30/k is the width of the ramp up to the 30 clamp.
  I expect 5-12, close to a step.
- `thr` (float, [5, 14]): the contribution from which a member is safe. I
  expect 8-11.
- `w` (float, [0, 1]): the weight per round of distance from the block's
  first round. 0 punishes only at block starts, and 1 punishes every round
  alike. I expect 0.2-0.6.
- `budget` (float, [5, 300], log): the received-punishment scale of the
  per-player taper. I expect 30-150, with a weak effect.
- `t_stop` (int, [0, 23]): the last punishing round. I expect 0-8,
  reaching later block starts if w is small.

### Justification

Finding 1 says another retuning of v3's shape will not move the score,
so v4 adds a lever of timing rather than of dose. Finding 3 is the basis
of `w`. Leaving charges a punishment two to three times more when it
comes late in a block, while the lift differs much less. Finding 4 says
leaving is the dominant cost, so moving the push to block starts should
buy the same lift for fewer lost members. That is the trade-off finding 2
shows the score is bound by. w spans [0, 1] because w=1 recovers v3 and
w=0 is the pure block-start schedule. The power form follows the roughly
steady rise of the leaving cost over the positions.

`k` and `thr` keep the dose shape that finding 2 supports: hard on low
givers, nothing for 11+.
- `k` goes up to 15 because v3 was flat above 7. With fewer punishing
  rounds a strong single push may matter more.
- `thr` keeps v3's peak at ~9.3 well inside the range.
- `cap` is dropped because it sat at the clamp of 30.

`budget` is kept with v3's range. Its effect was weak at t_stop=2, but
with w small and t_stop later it is what lets block-start pushes go to
newcomers and fresh players rather than to non-responders. `t_stop` now
starts at 0 because v3's peak sat at its lower edge of 2.

The hypothesis would be contradicted in two ways:
- The sweep peaks at w near 1. Then timing within the block does not
  matter, and the leaving gradient is offset by the stronger lift later
  in the block.
- The score stays at ~68. Then the members-versus-contribution trade-off
  is not moved by timing.

I chose not to model these:
- Separate weights per block position. The power form has one parameter
  and matches the monotone cost.
- Eviction of non-responders at decision rounds. Exploration 3's numbers
  put the gain from removing a free rider at ~0.08 members retained.
- Thresholds relative to the group mean.
- Anything about the other group, which the rule cannot punish.
