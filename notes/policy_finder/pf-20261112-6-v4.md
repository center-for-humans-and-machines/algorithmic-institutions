## Key findings

1. All three earlier finals are `zero` or nearly so (16.76, 17.01, 17.05 in the same seed slot, where zero scores
   16.76); v3's peak, round-4-only punishment of persistent low contributors in big groups, is zero + 0.29
   (exploration 1).
2. On v3's sweep, where the round-4 punishment is as good as random given the history, a point dealt at round 4
   raises the rest-of-game lead by +0.027 (se 0.017; +0.067, se 0.038, for points punishing only then) and own
   contributions by +0.11 per round for the rest of the game, while costing only 0.004 members at round 8, gone by
   round 12 (explorations 2, 3).
3. The round-4 effect does not depend clearly on own size or on the groups' gap (n=6 +0.014, n>=7 +0.025; gap bins
   -0.015 to +0.037), so v3's size condition is not needed (explorations 2, 4).
4. Per point, smaller hits pay better (+0.084 at amt 2-4 down to +0.022 at 11-17), and persistence filters add
   nothing (streak 1/2/3: +0.033/+0.017/+0.032) (exploration 3).
5. Whom: middling contributors (5-10) respond best to small hits (+0.157 lead and +0.21 contributions per round per
   point at amt 2-5, no member loss), free riders only to big ones (+0.042 at 9-17, -0.036 at 2-5) (exploration 5).
6. Humans and the reference sims agree: 5-10 contributors rise after 1-3 points (humans +0.70 vs -0.40
   unpunished; sims +0.30 vs +0.02), 11-15 a little, 16-20 cut back, free riders rise on their own (exploration 6).

## Hypothesis

Membership is still the score, so a good manager stays close to `zero`, but there is one moment where punishment
pays: the first round of a block right after a switch decision (round 4, maybe 8), the farthest from the next
decision and with the most rounds left for the raised contributions to carry, by herding and the players' own
persistence. There the best use of a point is a small nudge to the members who give a middling amount, who move up
after a small hit and do not leave over it; free riders need a large hit or none, and high contributors are left
alone. The rule: at rounds 4, ..., 4*last_b, valid members with lo < c <= thr get amt, members with c <= lo get
amt_fr; nothing else, ever.
- `last_b` (int, [1, 3]): last block start that punishes (1 = round 4 only, 3 = rounds 4, 8, 12). I expect 1, maybe
  2; 3 lets the sweep see the decline that v2 found at 12/16.
- `thr` (int, [4, 16]): upper limit of the nudge. I expect 10-13 (the middling band, where humans and sims respond
  to small hits); 16 lets the sweep see high contributors turn the effect negative, 4 is v3-like.
- `amt` (float, log [1, 8]): the nudge. I expect 2-4 (exploration 5's best bin); 1 is a token, 8 is where the
  per-point return has fallen.
- `lo` (int, [0, 5]): free-rider cutoff. I expect 1-3.
- `amt_fr` (float, [0, 16]): free-rider hit. I expect either < 1 (none) or 10+ (exploration 5); uniform so both
  ends are sampled.

### Justification

Finding 1 sets the bar: v3's peak was already a round-4-only rule, so v4 keeps the timing and changes the target and
dose. Finding 2 supports punishing at round 4 at all and `last_b` starting at 1; finding 3 is why the size condition
is gone; finding 4 is why there is no streak and why `amt` is log-scaled toward small values; findings 5 and 6
support the two tiers (`lo`, `amt_fr` for free riders; `thr`, `amt` for middling contributors) and `thr`'s range
across the middling band. What would contradict it: a sweep surface falling in thr and amt (nudges to middling
contributors cost more members than finding 5 suggests), or a peak at thr <= lo with amt_fr ~ 6, i.e. back at
v3's rule. Not modelled: round 0 (its free-rider punishment lost -0.138 per point, and the first decision is the
most sensitive), conditioning on group size or the gap (finding 3), a threshold relative to the group mean (an
absolute band is what the response tables show), and mid-block or decision-round punishment, which every earlier
version found costly in members.
