Both versions saturate at ~650 with the same idea, so another tweak of the dose shape will not move much; I need a new lever.
2. **Where does the best rule still lose payoff?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-3/v3_final_rounds.py on both finals and `zero`. v2's best: round 0 payoff 11.0 (zero 26.9), round 1 18.2, round 2 22.1, then contributions climb 11.5 -> 17.4 by round 9 with punishment falling 7.9 -> 1.35 per player; after the horizon (9) contributions decay steadily, 17.4 -> 14.0 at round 23 (-0.15 to -0.3 per round), low contributors (c<=5) 3% -> 15%. Mean payoff is 20 + 0.6 mean(c) - 2 mean(p), so the bill is roughly: entry fee in rounds 0-4 ~45 payoff points vs a 30-ish steady state, the post-horizon decay ~16 (sum of the shortfall from 17.4 times 0.6), and the plateau at 17.4 rather than 20 ~37. Longer horizons (v1 s092: horizon 20) hold 17.6 to round 19 but pay ~1 per player-round for it and end lower (635). So the decay after the horizon and the maintenance cost before it are the same problem: maintenance is too expensive. Next: who receives the maintenance punishment.
3. **Who absorbs the maintenance punishment?** Loaded v2's sweep (256 rules x 500 games) with a generalised loader scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-3/v3_load.py (it reproduces the sweep's score of run s000 exactly, 638.15) and split the punishment of the 63 runs scoring > 640 by own c and by the player's past summed punishment, scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-3/v3_maint.py. Rounds 6-11: players at c<=2 get ~9 per round and hold 32% of all points although they are 3% of player-rounds; among them the ones already punished 60+ get 13% of all points and answer with +2.8 next round (vs +1.3 unpunished), while never-punished c<=2 answer +9.9. In rounds 3-5 the same gradient: c<=2 fresh +13.9, past 1-30 +10.7, 30-60 +6.7, 60+ +4.5. So the clones habituate: a player who has eaten 60+ points and is still at 0-2 is a poor investment, and that is where much of the maintenance money goes. The other striking line: unpunished players at c 13-20 fall every round (-0.4 to -2.1, more the more they were punished before), and punished ones (small doses) rise. Next: exact-c look at the small nudges on high contributors, since the decay after the horizon is that group falling.
4. **Do small nudges hold the high contributors?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-3/v3_nudge.py, rounds 3-14 of v2's sweep, change 1 and 4 rounds later by exact own c and own p (p varies across rules at fixed c). Unpunished, everyone at c 12-19 falls: -0.6 to -1.5 next round, -0.9 to -2.5 four rounds later. A nudge reverses it below 17: c=15 four rounds later -0.96 (p=0), 0.00 (p=1), +0.87 (p=2), +1.31 (p 3-4), +1.75 (p 5-8); c=13: -0.94, 0.01, +0.92, +1.90, +2.84; and the gain grows from k=1 to k=4. At 17-19 the nudge only slows the fall (c=18: -1.83 vs -0.53 at p=2, -0.30 at p 3-4), and these points are rare (they only occur under thr 18-20 rules). Economics: 2 points on a c=15 player cost 1.0 mean payoff (2 x 2/4) and buy ~1.8 contribution four rounds later, worth 0.27 mean payoff per round while it lasts, so it repays in ~4 rounds if it persists. This is confounded (strict rules also punish the others), but the sign and the dose-response are clean and monotone. So the maintenance should be cheap and broad (1-3 points on anyone a bit below a high standard), not big doses on the hard-core free riders who stopped responding. Local simulation is not possible (artifacts/artificial_humans holds only copula json, no weights), as v2 found.
5. **Does the nudge still work late, and does habituation make big doses on repeat offenders a waste?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-3/v3_late_hab.py. (a) The nudge works as well late as early: change three rounds later for c 15-16 is -0.67 / +0.01 / +0.73 / +1.19 (p = 0 / 1 / 2 / 3-5) in rounds 3-8, -1.09 / +0.04 / +0.72 / +0.98 in rounds 9-14 and -1.34 / -0.02 / +0.44 / +0.79 in rounds 15-20; the unpunished fall gets steeper late, so the nudge's relative gain is ~1.3-1.8 points throughout. c 17-18 again only slow their fall (-1.84 vs -0.53 at p=2 in rounds 15-20). (b) Habituation is real but not fatal: c<=4 in rounds 2-10, change two rounds later under a 21-30 dose is +15.5 for never-punished players, +11.3 at 21-45 past points, +8.0 at 81+ (unpunished: +1.7 to +1.25). A 25-point dose costs 12.5 mean payoff; +8 from one player is worth 1.2 per round, so even a habituated free rider repays in ~10 rounds, which the early rounds still have. So a punishment budget per player would save little in rounds 0-10; I will not spend a parameter on it.
6. **Does a repeated nudge wear off?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-3/v3_nudge_hab.py: c 13-17, rounds 6-18, change three rounds later by the number of earlier small (1-4 point) punishments. p=0 / 1 / 2 / 3-4: never nudged -0.98 / +0.44 / +0.83 / +1.04; nudged 6+ times before -1.52 / -0.18 / +0.66 / +1.12. One point loses some of its bite with repetition, two to four points do not, and players who were nudged before fall faster when the nudge stops. That is what a standing maintenance rule needs. Rough economics: 2 points on one player cost 1.0 mean payoff (mean payoff = 20 + 0.6 mean c - 2 mean p) and buy ~1.8 contribution three rounds on, worth 0.27 mean payoff per round, so it repays in ~4 rounds and is worth doing until ~4 rounds before the end; whether a standing nudge on everybody below a standard beats its steady cost (~fraction below standard x 2 x nudge per round) depends on how many sit below it, which I cannot simulate, so the sweep must decide, with nudge 0 (v2's rule) inside the design.
7. **Drafted the rule.** Two phases with separate clocks, combined by a max per player: (i) v2's shock, `big = min(30, pmax * (s/thr)^gamma)` with s = max(0, thr - c), for rounds t < horizon, flat (v2's taper sat at 0); (ii) a standing nudge, `nudge` points to anyone with c < mthr, for rounds t < stop. `punishment = max(big, nudge_term)`, timed-out players 0. ## Key findings

1. v1 (649.7) and v2 (651.9, se 1.3) reach the same level with two dose shapes; v2's top design points span thr 13-20, gamma 0.9-2.6, warn 0-3.6 within ~5 points, and its peak sits at the edges thr 20, pmax 30, taper 0 (exploration 1). The dose shape is a ridge; further gains need a new lever.
2. Under v2's best rule contributions climb 11.5 -> 17.4 by round 9 and then decay steadily to 14.0 at round 23 once punishment stops; the decay costs ~16 payoff points, and v1's long-horizon rules that held ~17.6 paid as much for it with big doses (exploration 2).
3. Maintenance money goes mostly to hard-core free riders: in rounds 6-11 players at c<=2 (3% of player-rounds) take 32% of all points, and those already punished 60+ answer much less (+2.8 next round vs +9.9 for never-punished) (exploration 3). Even so, a big dose still repays on a habituated free rider early in the game (+8 vs +1.25 two rounds later at 81+ past points) (exploration 5).
4. Unpunished cooperators below full contribution fall every round (c 12-19: -0.9 to -2.5 four rounds later), and a 1-4 point nudge stops or reverses it below 17: c=15 four rounds later -0.96 (p=0), 0.00 (1), +0.87 (2), +1.31 (3-4); at 17-19 a nudge only slows the fall (exploration 4).
5. The nudge works as well late as early (c 15-16, three rounds later, p=0 vs p=2: -1.34 vs +0.44 in rounds 15-20) and 2-4 point nudges do not wear off with repetition (nudged 6+ times before: -1.52 vs +0.66 at p=2) (explorations 5, 6).
6. A 2-point nudge on everyone below a standard costs 0.6 (standard 13) to 0.9 (15) to 2.3 (20) mean payoff per round under v2's rule, against ~1.3-1.8 contribution held per nudged player, worth 0.2-0.27 mean payoff per round each (exploration 8).

## Hypothesis

A good manager on these clones does two different jobs with two different tools. First a shock: in the opening rounds, big convex doses on low contributors (near 30 for free riders, ~10 for c around 8, little near the standard, nothing for full contributors), which lifts the group from ~11 to ~17 and is what v1 and v2 already found. Then maintenance: the slow decline after the shock is cooperators a little below full contribution drifting down, and it is cheaply stopped by a standing small punishment (1-2 points) on anyone below a maintenance standard, kept up until a few rounds before the end, instead of big doses on hard-core free riders who have stopped answering. The rule: `punishment = max(big, small)` per valid player, with `big = min(30, pmax * (max(0, thr - c)/thr)^gamma)` for c < 20 and t < horizon, and `small = nudge` for c < mthr and t < stop.
- `thr` (int, [15, 22]): the shock standard and the scale of the convex dose; I expect 18-21 (v2's peak at 20, still rising there).
- `pmax` (float, [18, 45]): the shock dose for a zero contributor before the clamp at 30; I expect 30-40 (v2's peak at the edge 30).
- `gamma` (float, [1.0, 3.2]): curvature of the shock dose; I expect 1.6-2.5 (v2's 2.09).
- `horizon` (int, [4, 16]): first round without shock doses; I expect 7-11, possibly earlier than v2's 9 if the nudge takes over the maintenance.
- `nudge` (int, [0, 4]): the standing small punishment in points; I expect 1-2; 0 recovers a v2-like rule.
- `mthr` (int, [13, 20]): the maintenance standard; I expect 14-17 (below that the nudge is cheap, above 17 it only slows the fall).
- `stop` (int, [8, 23]): first round without nudges; I expect 18-21 (a nudge needs ~3-4 rounds to repay).

### Justification

Findings 1 and 3 keep v2's shock (convex in the shortfall, large for free riders, flat in time) and widen its two edge parameters, thr and pmax, past v2's ranges. Finding 2 locates the remaining loss in the decay after the shock and in costly maintenance; findings 4 and 5 show a cheap lever against exactly that decay: small doses on near-cooperators hold them, late as early, and do not wear off, which grounds `nudge`, `mthr` (findings 4 and 6: the lever works below ~17 and gets expensive with a broad standard) and `stop` (finding 5 and the ~4-round payback in exploration 6). Combining by max keeps the two tools separate, so the nudge also serves as v2's warn for near-standard players during the shock. It would be contradicted if the sweep put nudge at 0 or stop at its low end (a standing nudge does not repay its steady cost; findings 4-5 then reflect the confound that strict rules also punish the others), or mthr at 20 (even full-ish cooperators need it). Not modelled: a per-player punishment budget against habituation (finding 3: it would save little early, when most of the big doses fall), taper and v2's warn (flat timing was best and the nudge covers warn), standards relative to the group, and reactions to timeouts. The nudge's value rests on cross-rule comparisons in v2's sweep, not on a simulation of this rule, since the clones' weights are not available here.
