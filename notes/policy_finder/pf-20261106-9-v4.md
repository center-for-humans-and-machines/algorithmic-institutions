8. **Drafted v4.** Keep v3's core (punish k x shortfall below the mean beyond s0, capped, outside
   the quiet rounds, until t_end), but replace the hard window by stages of a member's stay:
   since arrivals only happen at block starts, a member's tenure divided by 4 is the number of
   whole blocks it has spent with us (stage 0 = its first block, the originals' block 0 and a
   newcomer's arrival block). A member is punished only in its first `n_stages` stages, and in
   stage s gets `decay`^s times the stage-0 punishment (cap included). Why: entries 2-3 say the
   per-point return falls by about half after the first block (0.055 -> 0.033 -> 0.02), and v3's
   window surface says the second block still paid (65.8 at window 2 -> 68.8 at 8), so the
   right second-block intensity is likely somewhat below the first; v3 could only choose all or
   nothing. decay=1, n_stages=2, t_end=9 reproduces v3's best exactly, so the sweep can fall
   back on it. quiet is fixed at 2: v1, v2 and v3 all put it there (v3: 65.9/68.8/66.9 at
   1/2/3), and entry 5's per-opponent disagreement on it is noise-level; freeing it would cost a
   dimension. Ranges narrowed around v3's peak where the surface was clear (s0, k, cap), widened
   where it hit an edge (t_end low end 8 -> 5).

## Key findings

1. v3's best (68.7 in its final: 70.7 / 70.3 / 73.8 / 60.0 against opponents 1-4) punishes only in
   rounds 0, 1, 4, 5 and 8, costing ~115 pool points a game; its window parameter sat at its
   upper edge (65.2 at 1 to 68.8 at 8), s0 peaked at 0.4-0.8, k at 5-7, cap at 24-26, quiet at 2,
   t_end was flat and slightly best at its lower edge (exploration 1).
2. Against the non-punishing opponent 4 we lose members at the first switch (3.6 in block 2)
   through fewer arrivals rather than more exits: exit rates at decision 3 differ little across
   opponents (0.31-0.35 unpunished, 0.41-0.50 at 21-30 points) (explorations 1, 6).
3. The per-point effect of our punishment falls with the round, not with the punishment the
   member had received before from anyone: own effect ~0.055 at rounds 0-1 lasting 15 rounds,
   ~0.033 at 4, ~0.02 at 8; interactions with earlier punishment from us or the other manager are
   at most 0.01 per log unit (explorations 2, 3).
4. At round 0 the response is near-linear in the punishment and larger for members further below
   the mean (~0.1 per point at 0-1.5 below, ~0.14 at 3-5 below) (exploration 7).
5. Per-opponent surfaces differ (an oracle would gain ~1.9), but the slices through the common
   peak contradict the per-opponent peaks; the consistent parts are s0 0-2, cap >= 23, k 4.5-7
   (lower for opponent 4) (exploration 5).

## Hypothesis

In the artificial humans a punishment buys most in a member's first rounds with the group and
progressively less afterwards, while the members it drives out are replaced by arrivals whatever
the other manager does. A good manager therefore runs an initiation: it punishes shortfalls below
the group's mean hard in the first two rounds of a member's first block with us (the originals at
round 0, a newcomer at arrival), stays quiet before switch decisions, punishes again in later
blocks of the member's stay only at a reduced intensity and for a limited number of blocks, and
stops when too few rounds remain. The rule only reads our own members' histories, so it carries
over to any other manager.

The rule: tenure = consecutive rounds in our group before this one, stage = floor(tenure/4);
punishment = decay^stage * min(cap, k * max(0, mean_c - c_i - s0)) for valid members with
stage < n_stages, zero in the `quiet` rounds before each decision and from round `t_end` on.
- `k` (float, [2, 16], log): points per point of shortfall; I expect 4-8 (v3 5-7).
- `s0` (float, [-1, 2.5]): tolerance below the mean; I expect 0-1.5 (v3 0.4-0.8; per opponent
  0.2-2.7).
- `cap` (float, [16, 30]): stage-0 per-round maximum; I expect 23-30 (v3 24-28).
- `n_stages` (int, 1-3): blocks of a member's stay in which it can be punished; I expect 2.
- `decay` (float, [0.25, 1.5]): intensity factor per earlier block with us; I expect 0.5-1.
- `quiet` (int, fixed 2): silent rounds before each decision.
- `t_end` (int, 5-17): first round with no punishment; I expect 8-12.

### Justification

Finding 1 shows the second block of a stay still paid in v3 (window at its upper edge), while
finding 3 shows its per-point return is about half the first block's; with only all-or-nothing in
v3, `decay` lets the sweep choose an intermediate second-block intensity, and `n_stages` replaces
the window (since arrivals happen only at block starts, tenure/4 counts whole blocks, and window 8
equals 2 stages). decay=1, n_stages=2, t_end=9 is v3's best, so the sweep can recover it. Findings
1 and 5 set k, s0 and cap's ranges around the consistent peak region, and finding 4 keeps the
linear-in-shortfall shape with a high cap. t_end's range opens down to 5 because v3's sat at its
lower edge (finding 1). quiet is fixed at 2 because all three earlier sweeps put it there and
finding 5's per-opponent disagreements on it look like noise. It would be contradicted if the
sweep's best decay sat at 1.5 (escalation, not decay) or at 0.25 with n_stages 1 scoring like
v2 (then the second block does not matter). I did not model opponent-dependent behaviour (finding
2: exits barely depend on the opponent; finding 5: the per-opponent differences are not
consistent), experience-based targeting from `p` (finding 3: no interaction), or late
maintenance punishment of incumbents (v2's surface lost with it).
