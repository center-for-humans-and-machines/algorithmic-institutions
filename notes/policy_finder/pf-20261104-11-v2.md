# pf-20261104-11-v2

## Explorations

1. **What did version 1's sweep say?** Read
   plots/simulation/policy_finder/pf-20261104-11_sweep/landscapes.json and
   final/sweep.json. Found the final peak (margin -0.10, slope 1.66,
   horizon 6, pre_switch 0.46, cap 16.7) at 65.98 +- 1.01 pool with 4.42
   members against `ah`; the best re-checked design point s198 (margin
   -0.26, slope 2.46, horizon 8.7, pre_switch 0.07, cap 19.2) at 67.94 +-
   1.07. The fitted surface gives `horizon` and `pre_switch` no effect at
   all (length scale 100, effect < 0.001 points) while `margin`, `slope`
   and `cap` each move it ~4.5 points. That is a surprise against my v1
   hypothesis: the sweep went for harsh punishment (slope 1.7-2.5, about
   the top of what I expected and margin ~0, not 2-5) and the taper did not
   matter. Next: look at the per-round sweep data to see what the good
   points actually do round by round and why horizon is flat.
2. **What do v1's best points do round by round?** Looked at
   final/per_round.parquet (group 0 is the rule's) and the reference sims
   with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-11/v1_final_rounds.py. Found the
   winners punish hard at the start and then stop: the final peak deals
   15.5, 13.0, 9.6 points per group in rounds 0-2, 3.1 in round 3 and 0
   from round 6 on, losing pool early (40-51 vs zero's 54-56) but then
   holding 9.5-9.8 per member for the rest of the game against 8.2-8.6 in
   the `zero` group of zero_vs_ah, with 4.4-4.6 members (zero: 4.9 after the
   first switch, decaying to 4.5). Pool from round 8 on: ~70 vs zero's
   ~62. So the early punishment buys a contribution lift that lasts the
   whole game without being renewed; that is why `horizon` was flat: once
   the norm is set, later punishment adds about what it costs. The
   contribution model is a GNN with an RNN (configs/.../25_LEVIN_run1...),
   so a lasting memory of early punishment is plausible. Next: across all
   256 points, separate the effect of each phase's dose.
3. **Which phase's punishment buys the lift, and which costs members?**
   Summarised every sweep point (own punishment per group-round in rounds
   0, 1-2, 3, 4-7, 8-11, 12-19, 20-23; members; contribution per member
   from round 8 on, `c_late`) with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-11/v1_sweep_features.py and
   v1_sweep_regress.py. Found `c_late` (R2 0.85) rises by about 0.07 per
   point per group-round of punishment, roughly equal for every phase, plus
   a concave extra for the early dose; members at round 4 fall 0.03-0.05
   per point dealt in rounds 0-3, and late punishment (rounds 12-23) costs
   pool directly (-0.78 per point) and members (-0.03). Binned by early dose
   (rounds 0-2): pool 60.6 at <1, 62.8 at 3-6, 64.3 at 6-10, 64.6 at 10-15,
   65.2 at 15-30; c_late 8.2 -> 11.4; members 4.59 -> 4.16. And 116 of the
   256 points dealt under 1 point per group-round early: the v1 design
   spent nearly half its budget on near-`zero` rules that the data now say
   lose 5+ points. Among points with a real early dose, those with no late
   punishment did best (65.0 vs 62.9-63.4). So: punish hard early, then
   (almost) stop; v2's ranges must put most of the design there. Next: who
   to punish early - does the lift come from the punished low givers or
   from everyone, and do high givers punished early leave or sulk?
4. **Who should the early punishment hit?** Followed the players who start
   in the rule's group across all sweep points, their punishment in rounds
   0-2 against their contribution in rounds 8-23 (`c_late`, wherever they
   are) and leaving at round 4, by round-0 contribution, with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-11/v1_early_targets.py. Found raw
   gradients for every band: e.g. givers at 1-5 go from 5.4 unpunished to
   7.8 at 3-6 points per round and 9.8 above 10; givers at 6-10 from 8.4 to
   11.2-12.4; leaving rises from ~0.3 to ~0.45-0.55. With round 0-2
   contributions controlled, the lift per point is 0.13-0.17 for givers at
   0-5 but 0.29-0.40 for givers above 5. Unpunished players also give more
   when the others are punished (c=0 givers 5.6 -> 9-11). This is heavily
   selected (the rule punishes by contribution), so I trust it only for
   the sign: the lasting lift is not confined to free riders, which goes
   against v1's "never punish high givers" (that was a next-round effect in
   `ah` sims; the lasting effect is different). So the sweep should be free
   to reach above the mean: `margin` down to clearly negative values.
5. **Does the round of the punishment matter?** Regressed `c_late` and
   leaving at round 4 on the punishment in round k, controlling only for
   what precedes it (contributions in rounds 0..k, punishments 0..k-1,
   round-0 group mean), with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-11/v1_round_timing.py. Found per point:
   round 0 +0.15 c_late and +0.014 leaving; round 1 +0.33 and +0.005;
   round 2 +0.31 and +0.003; round 3 +0.31 and +0.004 (se ~0.005 and
   0.0005). A round-0 point buys half the lift for three times the
   departures. This contradicts v1's recency picture (built on `ah` sims,
   by-block means), and is still selection-prone: the round-0 punishment
   falls on players before any of their behaviour is known, and later
   points land on players who stayed low. But it says the first-round
   punishment should be a free parameter, not tied to the rest. The
   pre-switch round 3 shows no extra departures per point here, consistent
   with the flat `pre_switch` in the v1 landscape; I drop that parameter.
6. **Drafted v2.** The structure changes from a taper to a short, flat
   campaign: punish members by their shortfall below the others' mean
   (`margin`, `slope`, `cap` as in v1) in rounds 1..`stop`-1, with round 0
   scaled by its own factor `first`, and nothing from round `stop` on.
   `horizon` and `pre_switch` go (flat in the v1 landscape; entry 3 says
   punishing past the campaign loses pool). Ranges are moved to where
   entries 1-3 put the good values, so the design no longer spends half
   its points near `zero`: `slope` log [0.5, 6] (v1 profile peak 1.3-2.5),
   `cap` log [4, 30] (v1 peak 12-25), `margin` [-4, 3] (v1 peak -1..1, and
   entry 4 argues for trying below), `first` [0, 1.5], `stop` int [2, 12]
   (v1's best ended punishment by round 6-9).
7. **Replayed the draft offline and tightened the ranges.** Ran it on the
   `zero` group's recorded histories of zero_vs_ah (300 episodes, no
   feedback, so only the dose means anything) with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-11/replay_dose_v2.py; it runs in a
   fresh process (no lazy-import trap from v1 entry 8) and validate-rule
   passes. A v1-peak-like point (margin -0.1, slope 1.66, cap 16.7,
   first 1, stop 6) deals 14-17 per group-round in rounds 0-5, almost
   all to givers at 0-10 and none to givers at 15-20, which is what the v1
   peak did. But the harsh corner of my first ranges (margin -4, slope 6,
   cap 30, stop 12) dealt 72-96 per group-round for 12 rounds, about six
   times `ah`, far past anything that did well in v1 (the heaviest v1
   point, 25 per round, already lost members: 3.95). So I narrowed to
   `margin` [-3, 3], `slope` log [0.5, 5], `cap` log [4, 25], `stop` int
   [2, 10]; the corner now deals 54-70, still beyond the v1 frontier so a
   peak there would not be cut off by much, and the mild corner (margin 3,
   slope 0.5, cap 4, first 0, stop 2) deals ~2 points in round 1 only,
   near `zero`, as a fallback.

## Key findings

1. v1's sweep peaked with harsh, early-only punishment: final 65.98 +-
   1.01 (best re-checked point 67.94 +- 1.07) against `ah`, with `horizon`
   and `pre_switch` flat in the fitted surface and slope 1.3-2.5, cap
   12-25, margin -1..1 on the plateau. (Explorations 1, 2)
2. Early punishment buys a contribution lift that lasts the whole game
   without renewal: the v1 peak holds 9.5-9.8 per member from round 4 to
   23 after punishing only in rounds 0-5, against 8.2-8.6 in the `zero`
   group; pool ~70 vs ~62 from round 8 on. (Exploration 2)
3. Across the 256 v1 points, pool rises with the dose in rounds 0-2 (60.6
   below 1 point per group-round to 65.2 at 15-30) while members fall only
   4.59 -> 4.16; with a real early dose, no late punishment is best (65.0 vs
   ~63); 116 of v1's points sat in the losing near-`zero` region.
   (Exploration 3)
4. The lasting lift is not confined to free riders: with early
   contributions controlled, givers above 5 gain 0.29-0.40 later
   contribution per early point, low givers 0.13-0.17 (selection-prone).
   (Exploration 4)
5. A round-0 point buys about half the lasting lift of a round-1 to 3
   point (+0.15 vs +0.31-0.33 c_late) for about three times the departures
   (+0.014 vs +0.003-0.005); round 3 shows no extra departure cost.
   (Exploration 5)

## Hypothesis

Against these artificial humans a good manager is a short, hard
norm-setter: it punishes members who give less than the rest of the group
heavily during the first few rounds, which leaves a contribution level
that persists for the rest of the game, and then stops punishing for good,
so that it pays no further pool and gives members no reason to leave while
`ah` keeps charging its group. The first round may deserve less weight than
the next ones, as a punishment there lands before anything is known about
the players and costs more members per point of lift.

The rule: shortfall = max(0, mean of the other valid members'
contributions this round - `margin` - own contribution); punishment =
round(min(`cap`, `slope` x shortfall x w(t))), with w(0) = `first`, w(t) =
1 for 1 <= t < `stop` and 0 from `stop` on; timed-out members and members
with no valid peer get none.

- `margin` (float, uniform [-3, 3]): free-riding tolerance below the
  others' mean; negative reaches members a little above it. Expected best
  -1.5 to 1.
- `slope` (float, log [0.5, 5]): points per point of shortfall. Expected
  best 1.3-3.
- `cap` (float, log [4, 25]): most points per member per round. Expected
  best 10-20.
- `first` (float, uniform [0, 1.5]): multiplier on round 0. Expected best
  0.3-1.
- `stop` (int, [2, 10]): first round without punishment. Expected best
  4-8.

### Justification

Findings 1-3 set the frame: the pool is won by a lasting contribution lift
bought early, and lost by punishing late, so v2 replaces v1's taper and
pre-switch damping (both flat in finding 1) with a campaign of `stop`
rounds and nothing after. `stop`'s range 2-10 brackets v1's effective end
(round 6-9 at the best points) and lets the sweep test both a campaign
over before the first switch decision (2-4) and one that also trains the
first newcomers (5-10); late punishment beyond 10 lost pool in finding 3.
`slope`, `cap` and `margin` keep v1's meaning; their ranges are centred on
v1's plateau (finding 1) and, for `margin`, pushed below it because
finding 4 says the lasting lift reaches above-mean givers too; the upper
corners are kept below ~6x `ah`'s dose after the replay (exploration 7).
`first` comes from finding 5: if round-0 points really buy less and cost
more, the peak will sit below 1; [0, 1.5] lets the sweep say round 0
matters more instead. What would contradict this: a peak at `stop` 10
with high `slope` (a longer campaign pays), at `first` near 1.5, or in the
mild corner (punishment does not pay, against finding 3). Not modelled:
the other group's state, repeat offending, a separate rule for newcomers
(they arrive from `ah`'s group, already punished), and the pre-switch
round (no extra departure cost in finding 5, flat in v1's landscape).
