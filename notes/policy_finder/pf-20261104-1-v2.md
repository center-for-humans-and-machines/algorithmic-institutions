# pf-20261104-1-v2

## Explorations

1. **What did version 1's sweep find?** Read
   plots/simulation/policy_finder/pf-20261104-1_sweep/{sweep.json,
   landscapes.json, final/sweep.json}. My v1 hypothesis (light punishment,
   close to `zero`) was wrong: the fitted surface rises steadily with
   punishment and peaks at three range edges, slope 3.0 (top), tol 0
   (bottom), cap 15 (top), with t_stop 12 inside its range, and
   pre_switch has no effect (effect 0.001, length scale 100). Profiles:
   slope 0.1 -> 61.0, 3.0 -> 66.1; tol 0 -> 66.1, 6 -> 62.1; cap 1 -> 64.6,
   15 -> 66.1; t_stop 0 -> 63.7, 12 -> 66.1, 23 -> 64.6. Final on fresh
   seeds (2000 episodes): the peak scored 66.0 (se 1.1, 4.0 members) and
   the best design point s183 (slope 2.16, tol 0.35, cap 15, t_stop 11)
   67.6 (se 1.1, 4.34 members). The v1 reference: `zero` against `ah`
   scores 59.1 / 61.8. Per-point se in the sweep is ~2, so the sweep can
   resolve only effects of a few points. So harsh, untolerant punishment
   early in the game, stopping around round 11-12, is what pays; the
   ranges stopped short at the harsh end. Next: look into the per-round
   sims of the final points to see why harsh punishment pays (members?
   contributions?) and what happens after the stop.
2. **Why does harsh early punishment pay?** Looked at the per-round sims of
   v1's final points (our group vs ah, plus the zero_vs_ah reference) with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-1/v2_final_traj.py. At the peak (slope 3, tol
   0, cap 15, t_stop 12) our members' mean contribution climbs 8.7 -> 12.5
   over rounds 0-12 at ~3.5-5 points of punishment per member-round, and
   after the stop it holds (12.8 at round 13, 11.9 at round 23) while the
   pool jumps from ~64 to ~80-84 because nothing is deducted any more; the
   group then grows 3.9 -> 4.4 members over the last three switches. So
   the cost of punishment is paid early, the norm it builds outlasts it,
   and once it ends the group attracts members. The lighter final points
   (s063, s043: 1-1.6 per member-round, c ~9.2) keep more members early
   (4.7) but never lift contributions and end at 63-65. Next: measure on
   the sweep's own sims (256 policies, so punishment varies for reasons
   other than the player's state) how much a point of punishment moves the
   next contribution and the chance of leaving.
3. **How much does a point of punishment move contributions and leaving
   under the rule itself?** Packed v1's sweep sims into arrays
   (scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-1/v2_panel.py) and ran
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-1/v2_response.py on our group's members
   (within-block round pairs, both rounds valid). Regression c_next ~ c +
   mates' mean + p + min(p,5): +0.102 per point of punishment, no
   saturation (min(p,5) term 0.002), with c 0.73 and mates 0.24 (so a
   lift is ~97% persistent per round). Holding the shortfall to the
   mates' mean fixed, more punishment means a bigger rise in every bin
   (shortfall 6-10: +1.9 unpunished, +2.8 at 5-10, +3.3 above 10).
   That is three times what I estimated from `ah`'s own punishments in v1
   (0.03), which were confounded with who gets punished. Leaving at the
   next decision (players who were in our group the whole block): 22.4%
   unpunished, 26% at 4-10 points over the block, 30.5% at 10-20, 36% at
   20-40, 43% above 40; low contributors leave more at every level (0-4
   contributors 27% -> 45%, 15-20 contributors 18% -> 22%). So punishment
   pays: ~0.1 contribution per point, persisting, plus herding onto
   mates; the cost in leavers is real but falls mostly on low
   contributors, whose departure costs the pool little. The response is
   linear, so the v1 cap of 15 and slope of 3 likely stopped short. Next:
   what draws members in from `ah`'s group, and how the joiners behave.
4. **What draws `ah`'s members over to us?** Ran
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-1/v2_join.py on the sweep arrays: per
   episode and decision, the share of `ah`'s members who join us, by our
   group's mean contribution minus `ah`'s over the block and by our mean
   punishment per member-round. Joining rises steeply with the
   contribution lead (rounds 7-19, unpunished blocks: 16% when we trail by
   4+, 35% at -2..0, 45% at 0..2, 55% when we lead by 4+) and falls with
   our punishment (lead 0..2: 45% at 0, 48% at 0-1, 40% at 1-2, 32% at
   2-4, 24% at 4-8 per member-round). Light punishment (up to ~1 per
   member-round) costs no joiners at all. So after the harsh phase the
   group need not go fully silent: a light maintenance dose that keeps
   free riders from eroding the lead should keep the joiners coming. v1
   could only stop dead at t_stop; next: is there a gain from punishing
   members at the mates' mean (raising the bar, since v1's tolerance
   wanted to go below 0)?
5. **Does punishing a member at or above the mates' mean raise its
   contribution?** Ran scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-1/v2_atmean.py on
   ah_vs_ah and the human data, mid contributors (5-16), change of next
   contribution by gap to mates' mean x punishment. Sim: at the mean
   (gap -1..1) 0 -> -0.12, 3-8 points -> +0.33 (about +0.05 per point,
   half the slope below the mean); 1-4 above the mean -0.65 -> -0.12 at
   3-8 points; far above the mean (4+) heavy punishment hurts (-1.9 ->
   -2.4). Humans: at the mean, heavy punishment makes them drop (-0.02 ->
   -0.68). So raising the bar a little above the mates' mean may pay in
   the sim, but it is weaker than punishing shortfalls and must never hit
   the top contributors. I will let the reference move part of the way
   from the mates' mean toward full contribution (20), which never
   punishes a full contributor, and let the sweep choose that weight,
   including slightly negative (a tolerance that scales with headroom).
6. **Did v1's pre-switch easing do anything?** Ran
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-1/v2_preswitch.py: per point, our members
   after each decision regressed on the params. pre_switch's coefficient
   is -0.015 members at round 4 and below 0.02 thereafter (log slope
   -0.10), pool coefficient 0.35 with se ~2 per point. Nothing; the
   timing finding of v1 (exploration 6 there) was correlational and does
   not survive the policy variation. Dropping pre_switch.
7. **Drafted v2.** punishment_i = min(cap, slope * max(0, ref - c_i)),
   ref = mates' mean + aspiration * (20 - mates' mean), at full dose in
   rounds 0..t_stop and times `late` afterwards; 0 for timed-out members
   and a member alone. Changes from v1: tol replaced by aspiration
   (exploration 5; aspiration < 0 still gives a tolerance, now scaled to
   the headroom, and aspiration > 0 raises the bar without ever touching a
   full contributor); pre_switch dropped (exploration 6); a maintenance
   factor `late` instead of a dead stop (exploration 4); slope and cap
   ranges extended past v1's edges (explorations 1, 3).
8. **Does it run, and what doses do the ranges span?** validate-rule
   passed (256 distinct points). Replayed open-loop with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-1/v2_replay.py on the human games and
   ah_vs_ah: v1's peak as a v2 point (slope 3, aspiration 0, cap 15,
   t_stop 12, late 0) 2.3-2.5 per member-round; soft corner (0.5, -0.3,
   3, 2, 0) 0.05 (practically `zero`); a mid point (2, 0.1, 12, 10, 0.2)
   2.1 with 41% of member-rounds punished; harsh corner (10, 0.4, 30, 23,
   0.6) ~20 per member-round, which surely empties the group, so the
   sweep should find the top where I expect it, inside. I first had
   aspiration up to 0.5; narrowed it to 0.4 since the at-mean gain is
   half the shortfall gain (exploration 5). Hand check (slope 2,
   aspiration 0.25, cap 12, t_stop 10, late 0.2), a group giving 20, 10,
   0, 16: the 10 faces ref 12 + 0.25*8 = 14 -> 8; the 0 faces 16.5 -> 33
   capped at 12; the 16 and 20 get 0; at round 11 the doses become 1.6
   and 2.4 (floored to 1 and 2).

## Key findings

1. v1's sweep rewarded harsh, untolerant early punishment: its surface
   peaked at three range edges (slope 3 top, tol 0 bottom, cap 15 top)
   with t_stop ~12; its best points scored 66.0-67.6 against `ah`, vs
   59-62 for `zero` (exploration 1).
2. The norm built by early punishment outlasts it: contributions rose
   8.7 -> 12.5 during the punishing rounds and stayed ~12 to the end, and
   the group grew 3.9 -> 4.4 members after the stop (exploration 2).
3. Under the rule, a point of punishment lifts the punished member's next
   contribution by ~0.10, linearly (no saturation), with ~97% of a lift
   carried over per round; leaving rises from 22% unpunished to 36% at
   20-40 points per block, mostly among low contributors (exploration 3).
4. `ah`'s members join us mostly for a contribution lead (16% when we
   trail by 4+, 55% when we lead by 4+) and are put off by punishment above
   ~1-2 per member-round (45% -> 24% at 4-8), but not by lighter doses
   (exploration 4).
5. Punishing a member at the mates' mean raises it only about half as much
   per point as punishing a shortfall; punishing members far above the
   mean lowers them (exploration 5).
6. v1's easing before switch decisions had no effect on members (-0.015
   members per unit, exploration 6).

## Hypothesis

Against this stack a manager wins by investing early: in the first part of
the game it punishes every member who gives less than its group-mates (and
possibly a little above that, toward full contribution), in proportion to
the shortfall and hard, so that contributions climb and the group's lead
over `ah` builds; then it switches to a light maintenance dose that keeps
the norm from eroding without scaring off the `ah` members who are drawn
in by the lead. The punishment cost is paid while the payoff horizon is
long; the joiners and the persistent norm pay it back in the second half.

Rule: punishment_i = min(cap, slope * max(0, ref - c_i)) with ref = mates'
mean + aspiration * (20 - mates' mean); multiplied by `late` after t_stop.
- `slope` [0.5, 10, log]: points per point of shortfall. Expect 2-6, above
  v1's edge of 3 but not far; log so both the moderate and the brutal end
  are sampled.
- `aspiration` [-0.3, 0.4]: where the reference sits between the mates'
  mean and 20. Expect 0-0.2; negative is a headroom-scaled tolerance, the
  continuation of v1's tol below its edge at 0.
- `cap` (int) [3, 30]: largest punishment per member-round. Expect 12-25;
  v1's 15 was at its edge and the response is linear.
- `t_stop` (int) [2, 23]: last round of the disciplining phase. Expect
  8-14 (v1's peak 11-12); may move earlier if the dose is harsher.
- `late` [0, 0.6]: maintenance factor after t_stop. Expect 0.1-0.3, i.e.
  well under ~1 point per member-round once contributions are high.

### Justification

Findings 1 and 3 make the dose harsh and proportional to the shortfall with
the slope and cap ranges extended past v1's edges: the response per point is
linear and lasting, and the extra leavers are mostly free riders. Finding 2
is why the disciplining is confined to an early phase (t_stop), and finding
4 why the phase after it is light rather than silent (late) and why the
phase must end at all (punishment above ~2 per member-round repels
joiners). Finding 5 gives aspiration with a range centred just above 0;
finding 6 removes pre_switch. The hypothesis is contradicted if late's
best is at 0 with a clear slope (maintenance costs more than it keeps), if
aspiration's best sits at -0.3 (tolerance again wanted), or if slope/cap
peak at their low ends (v1's harsh edge was noise). Not modelled: memory of
a member's past contributions or punishments, treating newcomers
differently, and the other group's state (e.g. matching `ah`'s level);
the sweep's ~2-point noise per design point would not resolve them at
this budget alongside five dimensions. All analysis here is on v1's sweep
sims and reference sims; the causal check is the sweep.
