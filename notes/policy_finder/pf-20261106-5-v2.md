# pf-20261106-5-v2

## Explorations

1. **Where did version 1 land?** Looked at v1's sweep.json and landscapes.json with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-5/v2_s1_sweep.py. The final result (v1's surface peak on fresh seeds) is pool 64.0 (se 0.5): 67.0 / 65.3 / 68.4 / 55.2 against opponents 1-4. The peak sits at the *harsh* corner, the opposite of what I expected: slope 2.75 (top of the log range is 4), tol 0.9, cap0 11, follow 1.7 (barely matters, effect 0.13), t_stop 8 (at the lower edge). Over the 256 points, log(slope) is the only clear correlate of the pool (r 0.19; 0.19/0.04/0.22/-0.06 by opponent). Single points are noisy (median se 1.9 against a spread of 2.1 sd), so I should read groups of points rather than single winners. So "punish little" was wrong for the artificial humans, and an early stop was preferred; next I want to see what punishment the points actually dealt, and what the opponents do.
2. **What did v1's points actually deal, and what do the opponents do?** Aggregated v1's per-round sweep data per point, opponent and phase (rounds 0-3, 4-7, 8-15, 16-23) with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-5/v2_s2_perround.py and v2_s3_points.py. v1's design was far too mild: on average my group got 0.25 punishment points per member-round early and 0.03 late; 141-151 of 256 points dealt under 0.05 early, only ~20 over 1. The opponents: opponent_1 punishes 4.5 per member in rounds 0-3, 2.0 in 4-7, 0.8 in 8-15, 0.0 at the end; opponent_3 is similar (5.0, 3.7, 1.6, 0.0); opponent_2 is steadier (3.3, 2.0, 1.5, 1.0); opponent_4 never punishes. The harsh-early opponents end with the biggest pools (opponent_3: contribution 11.3-11.7 per member, pool 73 in rounds 16-23, opponent_1 65), and their groups shrink only a little early (4 -> 3.5-3.6 in rounds 4-7) and recover. In my group, binning points by early punishment per member: against opponent_1 the pool goes 60.6 (none) -> 62.5 (0.3-1) -> 64.0 (1-2) -> 65.2 (2-4); opponent_2 60.7 -> 62.0 -> 63.4 -> 65.0; opponent_3 62.1 -> 63.9 -> 65.6 -> 68.0; opponent_4 51.4 -> 51.8 -> 52.7 -> 51.0. Members at the end barely move (4.0 -> 3.96, 3.9, 3.8 at the top bin) while late contributions rise by 2-3 points per member. So for these artificial humans punishment raises contributions much more than it drives members off, and the effect outlasts the punishment; the best observed level (2-4 per member early) is at the top of what v1 could deal and below the human-like opponents' 4.5-5. Opponent_4 (a non-punisher) is the exception: there the harsh points lose members (3.68 at the end) and gain nothing. Next: the artificial humans' response per player in the simulated data - contribution change and switching after punishment, by contribution level.
3. **How do the artificial humans respond, per player?** Looked at v1's final per-round data (all six candidates, both groups, so every manager's punishment) with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-5/v2_s4_ah_response.py. Next-round change in contribution of stayers: a zero contributor goes +1.5 unpunished, +2.3 at 2-5 points, +3.2 at 5-10, +4.5 at 10-30; four rounds later the gaps are wider (+2.4 vs +3.4 / +4.7 / +6.0), so the effect persists and even grows. Contributors at 1-15 respond the same way (5-10: -0.1 unpunished vs +1.7 at 5-10 points). Contributors at 16-20 lose when punished (-1.4 unpunished vs -2.3 to -3.4 for full contributors). Switching at a decision rises with the points received in the block: 23% unpunished, 31% at 4-8, 37% at 8-20, 45% at 20-40, 56% above 40; but it is the low contributors who leave (29% for block-mean c<=3 vs 19% for c>15 unpunished), so a punished free-rider who leaves costs the pool little. In a logit, the own group's mean punishment (+0.117) and the other group's (-0.124) cancel almost exactly, own block punishment adds only +0.008 per point, own contribution -0.047, group size -0.24. So, unlike the human-data reading of v1, punishment of low contributors is an investment with a return of roughly 0.2 contribution points per punishment point per later round, and members are lost mainly in proportion to how much harsher I am than the other manager.
4. **What did the best v1 rule do over the game?** Phase summary of the final candidates separately (same aggregation script on final/, first pass mixed the six candidates together and gave a wrong nonzero late punishment, so I split by candidate). The peak (slope 2.75, tol 0.9, cap 11, t_stop 8) deals 2.4-2.9 per member in rounds 0-7 and nothing after. It pays for it in rounds 0-3 (pool 47-48 vs 50-51 for the milder s003) but its contributions climb to 10-12 per member and stay there after the punishing stops: late pools 73-78 against opponents 1-3, 60 against opponent_4, vs 69-72 and 61 for s003, which keeps punishing (1 per member) until round 14. Against the non-punishing opponent_4 it loses members in rounds 4-7 (3.24) but they come back (4.05 at the end). Opponent_3, the best opponent group (late pool 79), punishes ~11 per member in rounds 0-1 and then only in the first two rounds after each switch (4-5, 8-9, 12-13), a "front-loaded and at each regrouping" pattern. So the recipe looks like: a hard, short opening, then little or nothing; the open questions are how hard, how short, and whether a brief re-application to newcomers after a switch helps. v1 could not reach this cleanly: its punishment was tied to the group mean (so it depended on everyone else), and t_stop's best was at its lower edge (8).
5. **Do newcomers after a switch need their own treatment?** Looked at the first round of each block (4, 8, ..., 20) in v1's final data with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-5/v2_s5_newcomers.py. Newcomers contribute less than incumbents (8.5 vs 10.7 at round 4, 10.3 vs 10.9 at round 20) and respond to punishment at least as well: three rounds later, a newcomer at c 10-15 punished 5-10 points is +3.6 vs -0.6 unpunished (incumbents +1.8 vs -0.6); at c<=5 both groups go about +4.7 after 10-30 points vs +0.8-1.3 unpunished. The response to punishment does not wear off in late blocks. So I do not need a newcomer-specific term: a rule that punishes by absolute contribution level catches low-contributing newcomers anyway. But it means late punishment of clear free-riders is still productive, as long as enough rounds are left to recoup it (with ~0.2 contribution points per punishment point per round and the 1.6 multiplier, break-even is about 3 remaining rounds). v1's late phase punished relative to the group mean, which also hit 10-15 contributors, where the return is weaker; that may be why it preferred to stop entirely.
6. **Drafted v2.** Punish a valid member of my group by how far its contribution falls short of an *absolute* target, `slope` points per point of shortfall, capped at `cap` per round. Two phases: an opening (rounds before `t_open`) with a high target `target_open`, then a maintenance phase with a lower `target_late` (0 = no punishment after the opening, which reproduces v1's peak). Rounds 21-23 are never punished (break-even ~3 rounds, entry 5); I fixed that rather than sweeping it, since v1's t_stop already showed late punishment matters only by ~1 pool point. Changes from v1: the target is absolute rather than relative to the group mean (the artificial humans' response depends on their own level, entry 3, and an absolute target makes punishment fade by itself as contributions rise); I dropped the opponent-following cap (v1's `follow` moved the fitted pool by 0.13 points; the harsh v1 peak did better than mild points even against the non-punisher, 55.2 vs ~51); the opening length can now go below 8 (v1's t_stop sat at its lower edge 8).
7. **Calibrated the ranges on human histories.** Replayed the draft as group 0's manager on the 50 human games with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-5/v2_t2_replay.py (fresh process, so no torch-import quirk; `t < 21 * th.ones_like(t)` runs fine). target_open 10, slope 2, cap 11, t_open 8 deals 4.4-5.3 per member in rounds 0-7 on 52-62% of members, then nothing (human managers 3.5-4.6 in rounds 0-3, 1.6-1.9 in 4-7); target 12/5, slope 1, cap 10, t_open 6 gives ~4.3 in the opening and 0.6-1.0 after (on ~20% of members); the low corner (4, 0.4, 3) gives practically nothing; the high corner (18/12, slope 8, cap 30) 23-27 per member, absurd, so I narrowed slope to [0.3, 6] and cap to [2, 25] and target_open to [4, 17] (17+ would hit the 16-19 contributors, who lose 2-3 points when punished, entry 3). The design still spans "never punish" to well above the harshest opponent's opening (opponent_3, ~11 per member in rounds 0-1). validate-rule passes: 256 distinct points.

## Key findings

1. v1's peak was at the harsh end of its ranges (slope 2.75 of max 4, tol 0.9, cap 11, t_stop 8 at its lower edge), final pool 64.0 (67.0 / 65.3 / 68.4 / 55.2 against opponents 1-4); `follow` had no effect (0.13 pool points) (exploration 1).
2. v1's design was mostly too mild to matter: most points dealt under 0.05 punishment per member early; across points the pool rises with early punishment, e.g. 62.1 -> 68.0 against opponent_3 from none to 2-4 per member, with end-of-game members almost unchanged (exploration 2).
3. The artificial humans raise contributions after punishment when they are below ~15, persistently (zero contributors: +2.4 unpunished vs +6.0 four rounds after 10-30 points), and lose 1-3 points when they are at 16-20 (exploration 3).
4. Switching follows relative harshness (own group's mean punishment +0.117, the other's -0.124 on the logit) and own contribution (-0.047), barely own punishment (+0.008 per point); the leavers are mainly low contributors (exploration 3).
5. The best v1 rule pays ~3 pool points in rounds 0-3 for a hard opening and then holds contributions of 10-12 per member without punishing: late pools 73-78 against opponents 1-3; the best opponent (opponent_3) punishes ~11 per member in rounds 0-1 and briefly after each switch (exploration 4).
6. Newcomers contribute less (8.5 vs 10.7 at round 4) and respond to punishment as well as incumbents; the response does not fade in later blocks (exploration 5).

## Hypothesis

Against these artificial humans, punishment of low contributors is an investment: it costs the pool its points now and returns about 0.2 contribution points per point in every later round, while member losses depend mostly on being harsher than the other manager and fall on free-riders. So a good manager front-loads: in the opening rounds it punishes everyone clearly below a fairly high contribution level, hard enough to move them, which sets a high contribution norm that persists after it stops; afterwards it at most keeps a light check on clear free-riders (including newcomers), and never punishes in the last rounds, when there is no time left to recoup the cost. High contributors are never punished. The rule: a valid member of my group contributing `c` gets `min(slope * max(target - c, 0), cap)`, with `target = target_open` in rounds before `t_open` and `target_late` afterwards, and nothing in rounds 21-23.

- `target_open` (float, [4, 17]): contribution level that is punished below in the opening. I expect 8-13: about the group mean, as v1's peak (mean - 0.9) effectively used, possibly higher.
- `t_open` (int, [1, 14]): length of the opening. I expect 4-8: v1's peak sat at its lower edge 8, and the best opponents fade after round 4-8.
- `target_late` (float, [0, 10]): contribution level punished below after the opening; 0 switches it off. I expect 0-6: only clear free-riders, if at all.
- `slope` (float, [0.3, 6], log): punishment per point of shortfall. I expect 1-4 (v1's peak 2.75): high enough that a free-rider gets close to the cap.
- `cap` (float, [2, 25]): largest punishment per member per round. I expect 8-15 (v1's peak 11; opponent_3 deals ~11 per member in its opening).

### Justification

Findings 2, 3 and 5 are why the rule punishes at all and front-loads it (`target_open`, `slope`, `cap`, `t_open`): the punishment's return outlasts it, and v1's best point earned its lead in rounds 8-23 after it had stopped punishing. Finding 3 also says the reference should be the member's own level and that 16-20 contributors must be spared, hence an absolute target capped at 17. Finding 1's `t_stop` at the edge is why `t_open` now reaches down to 1. Findings 5 and 6 (a non-fading response, low-contributing newcomers) are why a maintenance target exists after the opening (`target_late`), with 0 in range so the sweep can drop it; the fixed silence in rounds 21-23 comes from the ~3-round break-even (exploration 5). Finding 4 says the member cost of punishment is relative to the other manager, but v1's `follow` did nothing (finding 1) and harsh openings beat mild ones even against the non-punisher (55.2 vs ~51), so I did not model the opponent: the rule ignores `p`. What would contradict this: a peak at low `slope`/`cap` or `target_open` (punishment not worth it), or at large `t_open` with high `target_late` (steady punishment beats a front-loaded one). Not modelled: newcomer-specific punishment (finding 6 says the absolute target catches them), punishment timed to the switch rounds (opponent_3's pattern), reactions to the opponent's harshness, and group size.
