# pf-20261112-5

## Explorations

1. **How do `zero` and `ah` fare against each other in the reference sims, on the score?** Looked at
   plots/simulation/25_LEVIN_run1_ah_zero_pairings_batched/per_round.parquet with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-5/ref_sims.py and ref_scores.py (the latter uses
   aimanager.simulation.pool_scores, the score's own code). Note: `common_good` in the parquet is the
   per-member share, but the score is the group's *total* pool, 1.6*sum(c) - sum(p). Found `zero`
   leads `ah` by 13.97 +- 1.44 pool points per round (60.46 vs 46.49). Almost all of it is size:
   zero's group averages 4.57 members vs 3.43 for ah's; per member the pool is about equal (12.87 vs
   13.27 per player-round), because ah's punishment (~1.76/player-round) buys ~1.35 more contribution
   (= 2.2 pool points) and costs 1.76 back. Size jumps right at the first switch (4.0 -> 4.9 after
   round 4). So I now think membership is the lever, and punishing mostly costs members; next I want
   to see what the switching model reads, to know what makes players leave or join.
2. **What makes human players switch, and how do contributions answer punishment?** Looked at
   experiments/2group_8agent_50ep.csv (one copy per game, 50 games) with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-5/human_switch.py: each decision row (rounds 3,7,11,15,19)
   against the player's own mean punishment and payoff over the 4-round block, and the block's
   punishment/payoff gap to the other group. Found switch rate rises with own punishment from 20%
   (never punished in the block) to 59% (mean >6/round); with the payoff gap to the other group from
   11% (>4 ahead) to 54% (>4 behind). In a logit with both, own punishment (+0.10/pt) and payoff gap
   (-0.14/pt) carry it, group-level punishment gap and own contribution do not. Switching is most
   frequent at the first decision (43%), then ~25%. Contributions do answer punishment: next-round
   change is -0.48 when unpunished, +0.81 after 2-5 points, +2.25 after >10; among contributors <10,
   +0.52 vs +1.33 (2-5 pts) vs +3.38 (>10). So per round a punishment point buys at most ~0.25-0.3
   contribution points (~0.4-0.5 pool points) next round: punishment pays only if its effect lasts
   several rounds and nobody leaves over it. Human managers punished mostly low contributors (3.3 at
   c=0, 0.4 at c>15) and most in rounds 0-3. So I think: never punish while a switch is still ahead,
   and the only place punishment might pay is after the last decision (round 19), when nobody can
   leave. Next: check in the reference sims how much punishment lifts contributions when size is
   held fixed, to size that endgame gain.
3. **How do the artificial humans answer punishment, and is size really the whole story?** Looked at
   the reference sims with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-5/sim_response.py and sim_rounds.py
   (per player-round next-round contribution change by own punishment; per-round c, p, per-player
   pool and size of each manager's group). Found the AH contribution model answers punishment
   cumulatively at group level: under ah vs ah contributions climb 8.70 -> 10.54 over the game, under
   zero vs zero they sag 8.74 -> 7.67, so per-player pool ends 15.9 vs 12.3. Per individual the
   short-run answer is small: for contributors at 5-10, a 2-5 point punishment adds ~+0.4-0.5
   contribution over the unpunished at +1..+3 rounds, 1-2 points add ~+0.3; for contributors at
   10+ any punishment leaves them about as they were. Switching in the sims follows own block
   punishment like the humans (23.6% unpunished, 34% at 1-3/round, 61% at >6). In ah vs zero, the
   first decision decides most of it: zero's group goes 4.00 -> 4.93, then slides back to 4.54 as
   ah's (smaller, more cooperative) group pays its members more per head (pool/head 15.0 vs 13.7).
   So a manager wants zero's size and some of ah's rising contributions; punishment has to be light,
   aimed at clear under-contributors, and placed where it costs the fewest exits. Next: does it
   matter *when* in a 4-round block the punishment falls?
4. **Is the switch decision driven by recent punishment more than by early-block punishment?**
   Looked at decisions after rounds 7-19 with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-5/recency.py: logit
   of switching on own punishment at each of the 4 rounds of the block plus the previous block's mean.
   Found strong recency in the sims: coefficient 0.019 (+-0.002) at the block's first round, 0.029,
   0.044, 0.059 at the decision round itself; humans 0.026, 0.023, 0.016, 0.099 (+-0.02). The
   previous block's punishment does not raise switching (-0.023 sims, -0.063 humans). So a point
   dealt right after a switch costs ~1/3 of the exits of one dealt in the decision round, while its
   contribution effect still lasts the next ~3 rounds. That is a timing lever ah does not use.
   Also: rounds 20-23 are after the last decision, so punishment there costs no exits at all, and
   punishment in round 23 can only lose (no later contribution to raise).
5. **Drafted the rule.** Punish a member in proportion to how far they fall short of a fraction of
   the others' mean contribution in my group, `punishment = k * relu(thr * mean_others - c_i)`, but
   only in the first `w` rounds of each 4-round block (right after a switch, where exits are
   cheapest), and in the endgame rounds 20-22 (no exit possible) at `end * k`; never in round 23.
   `w = 0, end = 0` or a tiny `k` is the `zero` manager, so the sweep can fall back to it if
   punishing never pays. A timed-out member, or a member alone in the group, gets nothing.
6. **How hard does the draft punish for given k and thr, and is the k range right?** Looked at the
   zero group's contributions in the zero-vs-ah reference sims with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-5/shortfall.py, applying the rule's shortfall and the
   manager's floor. Found shortfalls are small, because contributions within a group are close: at
   thr=1 half the members are short, by 3.6 on average; at thr=0.8, 38% by 2.9. With k=0.1 almost
   nobody is punished at all (the floor to integers eats it), k=1 at thr=1 gives 1.6 points per
   member-round (in an active round), k=2 gives 3.4 - ah deals ~1.8 on average. So my first range for
   k, [0.05, 2] log, spent a third of its log span on values that do nothing. Changed it to
   [0.2, 4] log: the low end is a near-silent manager (a deadband from the floor), the high end
   about twice ah's intensity. Re-validated; hand-checked the timing and targeting with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-5/test_rule.py (a c=0 member among 10/10/20 gets 13.3 at
   k=1, thr=1 in rounds 0-1 and 4 with w=2, 6.7 in rounds 20 and 22 with end=0.5, nothing in 2-3,
   19 or 23; the other group's players nothing). Side note: under an exec with no builtins, a cold
   torch process raises on the first th.clamp/sum(keepdim)/comparison (lazy imports); validate-rule
   and the sim warm torch first, so the test script does the same.

## Key findings

1. The score is the group's *total* pool, so size is the main lever: `zero` leads `ah` by 13.97 +-
   1.44 per round, almost all from members (4.57 vs 3.43), with per-player pool about equal (12.87
   vs 13.27) (exploration 1).
2. Players leave over their own punishment and over a payoff gap to the other group: humans switch
   20% when unpunished in the block vs 59% at >6/round; the sims reproduce it (23.6% vs 61%)
   (explorations 2, 3).
3. The first decision moves the most players (43% humans, ~39% sims switch; zero's group jumps
   4.00 -> 4.93), and size then drifts back toward the group that pays more per head (exploration 3).
4. Punishment does raise AH contributions, mostly cumulatively: ah vs ah climbs 8.70 -> 10.54 while
   zero vs zero sags 8.74 -> 7.67 (per-player pool 15.9 vs 12.3 by the end); the individual
   short-run answer is small, ~+0.3-0.5 contribution for 1-5 points on 5-10 contributors, ~0 on
   contributors at 10+ (explorations 2, 3).
5. The switch reads recent punishment most: per point, the decision round weighs 0.059 vs 0.019 at
   the block's first round in the sims (humans 0.099 vs 0.026); the previous block's punishment does
   not raise switching (exploration 4).
6. Rounds 20-23 follow the last decision, so punishment there costs no members; round 23
   punishment raises nothing afterwards (exploration 4, from the rules).
7. Group contributions are tight, so a shortfall rule needs k around 1-2 to approach ah's intensity;
   below ~0.2 the integer floor silences it (exploration 6).

## Hypothesis

A good manager here is first a recruiter: it keeps its punishment low enough that its own members
stay and `ah`'s punished members cross over, since every member adds 1.6 times their contribution
to the pool. Within that, it buys some of the contribution growth that punishment produces by
aiming light, proportional punishment only at members clearly below their group's norm, and by
timing it where it costs the fewest exits: in the rounds right after a switch decision (the switch
model weighs recent punishment most) and in rounds 20-22, after the last decision; never in the
rounds just before a decision and never in round 23. The rule:
`punishment = k * relu(thr * mean_others - c_i) * gate(t)`, where `gate` is 1 in the first `w`
rounds of each 4-round block of rounds 0-19, `end` in rounds 20-22 and 0 in round 23; timed-out
members and a member alone get nothing.

- `k` (float, [0.2, 4] log): punishment points per point of shortfall.
- `thr` (float, [0.3, 1.3]): the share of the other members' mean contribution a member must reach.
- `w` (int, 0..4): rounds at the start of each block in which punishing is allowed before round 20;
  0 = never while a switch is ahead, 4 = every round.
- `end` (float, [0, 3]): multiplier on k in rounds 20-22.

### Justification

Findings 1-3 say membership dominates the score and that punishment costs members, so the rule
contains `zero` (w=0 with end=0, or k near 0.2 with low thr) and the sweep can return to it; I
expect the best region to be light punishment: k around 0.5-1.5, thr around 0.7-1.0 (target only
clear under-contributors, since 10+ contributors do not answer punishment, finding 4). The range
of k is sized by finding 7: its low end is silent, its high end ~2x ah. thr spans "only free riders
at under a third of the group" to "everyone below 1.3x the group", wide on purpose, since the
switch model's tolerance of being singled out is unknown. Finding 5 motivates `w`: I expect w = 1-2
(punish right after a decision, when the next one is 3-4 rounds away), with w=0 if any punishment
costs more members than it earns; the whole 0..4 range is cheap to cover with 256 points. Finding
6 motivates `end`: in 20-22 the exit cost is gone, so the only question is whether 1-3 rounds of
contribution response pay for the points; finding 4's short-run answer (~0.4 contribution, 0.6 pool
points, per 1-5 points) suggests not by much, so I expect end in 0-1.5 and the range to 3 lets a
stronger AH response show. What would contradict this: a sweep peak at w=0, end=0 (punishment
never pays: the AH's contribution answer is too weak), or a peak at w=4 and high k (the AH switch
model is less sensitive than the data suggest). Not modelled: rewarding newcomers or reading the
other group's state (the manager can only punish, and its own members' payoff is all it controls;
the payoff gap is served by punishing little), escalation on repeat offenders and per-player history
(kept to 4 parameters for a 256-point sweep), and the first block's special weight (rounds 0-3 use
the same `w` gate as every block; the rule's recency timing already applies there).
