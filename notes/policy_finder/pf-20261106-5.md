# pf-20261106-5

## Explorations

1. **What does the game look like in the human data?** Looked at contribution, punishment, group size and switching per round with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-5/a1_overview.py (50 games, one copy each). Mean contribution is flat at about 9 of 20 across all 24 rounds (8.8 in round 0, 9.2 in round 23), while human managers punish a lot early (mean 3.7, half of players punished in round 0) and then less (about 1.0-1.3, 20% punished, by the end). Pool scales almost linearly with group size: 14 for a group of 1, 47 for 4, 83 for 6, 97 for 7. Switching is heavy: 43% of players switch at the first decision, then 24-29%. So since the score is the pool, not a per-head number, holding and attracting members is probably worth as much as raising contributions; next I want to see what makes people leave.
2. **What drives switching?** Logit of switching at each decision on block (4-round) features with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-5/a2_switch.py (1760 player-decisions, 31% switch). Own punishment in the block raises leaving: 20% if never punished, 37% at 4-8 points summed, 56% above 20. The group's mean punishment matters beyond one's own (coef +0.18 per point) and the other group's mean punishment pulls the other way (-0.26): it is relative harshness. Group mean contribution keeps people (-0.20 per point), the other group's attracts them (+0.09), and high contributors are the more mobile (+0.07 per point of own mean c). Payoff gap to the other group is the strongest single predictor (switch rate 11% when ours pays 5+ more, 57% when theirs pays 5+ more). So punishment costs members, especially if the other manager is milder; next: does punishment raise contributions enough to pay for that?
3. **Does punishment raise contributions?** Next-round change in contribution by own contribution and punishment band, with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-5/a3_response.py (stayers only, valid rounds). For low contributors it does, roughly: c=0 goes up +1.0 unpunished vs +4.0 after 10-30 points; c in 1-5 goes +0.1 unpunished vs +1.3 (3-5 pts), +1.9 (6-10), +3.3 (>10). That is about +0.25-0.3 contribution per punishment point next round, i.e. 0.4-0.5 pool points back per point spent, before any persistence. For high contributors it is poison: a full contributor punished 3-5 drops 9.4, punished >10 drops 16.5. In a pooled OLS controlling for own and group contribution, own punishment has no linear effect (+0.015, ns) - the gains for low contributors and losses for high ones cancel - while group mean punishment has a small positive one (+0.09/pt, a deterrence signal). Human managers punished by relative standing: 52-58% of players 4+ below the group mean were punished (mean 4-7 points), 12-14% of those above. So punishment, if any, should target only clear free-riders, be mild, and never touch high contributors; next: does it persist, and do punishing groups end up with bigger pools at the group level?
4. **Does the effect persist, and what happens at the group level?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-5/a4_persist.py. Among low contributors (c<=6) who stay, the punishment coefficient on the contribution change grows with the horizon: +0.12/pt after 1 round, +0.14 after 2, +0.16 after 3, +0.20 after 4 (stayers only, so selection flatters it). So a point spent on a free-rider may return ~0.2-0.3 pool points per remaining round - possibly a net gain over a long horizon, a loss near the end. At the group level the picture is the opposite: comparing the two groups within each game, each extra point of mean punishment goes with -0.33 members and -4.1 pool points per round; early punishment (rounds 0-3) predicts later size strongly (corr -0.52, -0.40 members per point). Members carry the pool (pool-size corr 0.63 across groups). So I now think the main lever is not to punish much, especially not early and not in relation to the other manager; punishment should be cheap, targeted, and fade towards the end. I also read the artificial humans' configs: the switch model sees prev_common_good, prev_punishment and group (with edges across players, so it sees both groups), the contribution model prev_contribution, prev_punishment and group. Next: is the pull of a group its total pool or per-head payoff?
5. **Total pool or per head - what pulls players?** Logit of switching at the decision round on the two groups' pools, with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-5/a5_pool_pull.py. Per-head pool fits better than total pool (log-lik -942 vs -967): -0.06 per point of own per-head pool, +0.05 per point of the other's. Punishment in the decision round itself adds +0.08-0.10 per point to the switching logit (10 points nearly triples the odds), and with both groups' mean punishments in, the other manager's mean punishment keeps people (-0.09) more than ours pushes them (+0.04). Low contributors are the most mobile (37% switch vs 20% of 16-20 contributors). So members compare payoffs and harshness across groups, and the competitor's harshness sets the bar: a manager that is milder than the competitor gains members. That suggests the one useful thing to read from `p` is how hard the other manager punishes, and to stay under it.
6. **Drafted the rule.** Punish a member only for falling short of this round's group mean (valid members) by more than a tolerance, linearly in the shortfall (slope), capped per player by a base cap plus a multiple of the other manager's recent mean punishment per member (last 4 rounds, valid members of the other group; 0 when there is none). No punishment from a stop round on, since a point spent late has no future rounds to pay back (finding 4) and round 23 is pure cost. Full contributors and anyone at or above the mean are never punished (finding 3). Ranges are set so the sweep can reach "almost no punishment" (small slope, big tolerance, cap near 0, follow 0) as well as human-like levels.
7. **A torch quirk while replaying the rule.** Replaying the draft on the human histories (scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-5/t1_replay.py) first failed with `KeyError: '__import__'`: in a fresh Python process, the first call of some torch paths (a keyword argument such as `keepdim=`/`min=`, or a comparison with a Python float) does a one-time `import torch` that the builtin-free rule scope cannot do. validate-rule passed because its own random-history setup had already warmed torch, and the simulation surely has too, but I rewrote the code without keyword arguments or Python-scalar comparisons (`th.maximum(x, th.ones_like(x))`, `th.relu`, `th.ones_like(...) - in_group`) so it cannot depend on that. Same rule otherwise.
8. **What do the punishments look like on human histories?** Same script, the rule run as group 0's manager on the 50 human games. At slope 1, tol 0, cap 15, no stop, it punishes 1.1-1.8 points per member per round, about the human managers' level from round 4 on (1.0-2.0) and well under their opening (3.5-4.6); at slope 0.5, tol 3 it punishes 0.15-0.3 per member; at slope 0.2, tol 6 practically nothing (the manager floors to integers). So the ranges span "never punish" to roughly human strength and above (slope up to 4), which is what I want the sweep to decide, since I cannot simulate the artificial humans here. Validation passes on all 256 design points.

## Key findings

1. The pool is carried by group size: 47 for a group of 4, 83 for 6, 97 for 7, and 24-43% of players switch at each decision (exploration 1).
2. Punishment drives players out: switching rises from 20% (never punished in the block) to 56% (more than 20 points), and relative harshness matters - our mean punishment +0.18 per point on the switching logit, the other manager's -0.26 (exploration 2; again +0.04 vs -0.09 at the decision round, exploration 5).
3. Within games, each point of extra mean punishment goes with -0.33 members and -4.1 pool points per round; early punishment (rounds 0-3) predicts later size (corr -0.52) (exploration 4).
4. Punishment does raise free-riders' contributions, persistently: about +0.12/pt after one round rising to +0.20 after four for contributors at 0-6 (exploration 4; +1.0 vs +4.0 next round for zero contributors unpunished vs 10+ points, exploration 3).
5. Punishing high contributors is very costly: a full contributor punished 3-5 points drops 9.4 next round (exploration 3). Human managers punished by relative standing: 52-58% of those 4+ below the group mean vs 12-14% of those above (exploration 3).
6. Players chase per-head payoff, not total pool (log-lik -942 vs -967), and low contributors are the most mobile (37% vs 20%) (exploration 5).

## Hypothesis

A good manager in this game is mainly a magnet: since the score is the total pool and members compare harshness and per-head payoff across the two groups, it should punish less than the competitor, never punish anyone at or above the group's level, and spend punishment only on clear free-riders, where it pays back over several rounds - so not near the end. How much it can afford depends on the other manager: next to a harsh one it still looks mild while punishing free-riders, next to a mild one any punishment costs members. The rule: a valid member of my group whose contribution falls more than `tol` below this round's group mean (valid members) gets `slope` points per point of the excess shortfall, capped per player at `cap0 + follow * (the other manager's mean punishment per valid member over the last 4 rounds)`, and no one is punished from round `t_stop` on.

- `slope` (float, [0.05, 4], log): punishment per point of shortfall beyond the tolerance. I expect 0.3-1.5: below ~0.2 it rounds to nearly nothing, 1 is about human strength.
- `tol` (float, [0, 10]): unpunished shortfall below the group mean. I expect 2-6: only clear free-riders, not people near the mean.
- `cap0` (float, [0, 15]): per-player cap when the other manager punishes no one. I expect low, 0-6, given findings 2-3.
- `follow` (float, [0, 3]): cap increase per point of the competitor's mean punishment. I expect 0.5-2: room to punish grows with the competitor's harshness while staying under it on average; 0 switches the adaptation off.
- `t_stop` (int, [8, 23]): first round with no punishment. I expect 14-20; 23 means only the last round is spared.

### Justification

Findings 1-3 say members are the pool and punishment, especially relative to the other group's and early, loses them; that is why punishment is shortfall-based with a tolerance (only a minority is ever punished), why its strength is capped, and why the cap is tied to the competitor's own harshness (`cap0`, `follow`): finding 2's relative coefficients (ours vs theirs) say a harsh competitor makes our punishment cheap in members, a mild one makes it expensive. Finding 4 is why punishment is there at all (`slope`) and why it stops (`t_stop`): its return accrues over later rounds, so late points cannot pay back. Finding 5 is why the reference is the group mean and contributions at or above it are never touched (`tol` >= 0). Finding 6 (per-head payoff, mobile free-riders) adds that losing a free-rider costs the pool nothing directly, so I did not add anything to retain them. What would contradict it: a sweep peak at large `slope` with `cap0` high and `follow` near 0 (punishment pays regardless of the competitor), or a peak at the bottom of `slope`/`cap0` (punishing is never worth it, the adaptation useless). Not modelled: the decision rounds specifically (punishment right before a switch weighs more, finding in exploration 5, but `t_stop` and the caps were the simpler levers within five parameters), a player's history (repeat offenders), and group size (a bigger group could afford more), because the artificial humans' switch model sees only pool, punishment and group, and I preferred few readable parameters to a richer rule I cannot test here.
