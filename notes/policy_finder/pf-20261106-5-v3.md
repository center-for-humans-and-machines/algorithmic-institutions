# pf-20261106-5-v3

## Explorations

1. **Where did version 2 land, against version 1?** Looked at both versions' final results, v2's sweep.json and landscapes.json with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-5/v3_s1_sweep.py. v2's final is the best so far: pool 65.17 (se 0.53), 67.5 / 66.8 / 69.4 / 56.9 against opponents 1-4, vs v1's 63.98 (67.0 / 65.3 / 68.4 / 55.2). Its peak is a short, very hard opening: target_open 9.7, t_open 9, slope 4.35, cap 22.4, target_late 0 (at its edge: no maintenance at all). That means anyone below ~5 gets the full ~20 points in rounds 0-8, and nothing afterwards. The surface is dominated by target_open (effect 14 pool points, length scale 0.36, i.e. a sharp optimum near 9-10: bins 58.7 / 60.4 / 60.6 / 59.7 / 57.3 from 4 to 17); slope effect 4, cap 2.4, target_late 2.0, t_open only 1.0 (flat 62.7-63.75 over 1-14). target_late hurts monotonically (60.1-60.4 for 0-4, 58.6-58.8 above 4), most against opponent_4 (52.0 -> 49.9). Single points are still noisy (median se 2.0). The peak pays in members: 3.59-4.08 at the end vs ~4.0-4.4 for mild v1 points; against opponent_4 (the non-punisher) it scores 56.9 with 3.59 members, the weakest pairing by 10 points. So the remaining room is probably (a) opponent_4, where the harsh opening drives members to the mild neighbour, and (b) the members lost in general; next I want per-round trajectories of v2's peak against each opponent: when members leave, who leaves, and what the pool does after the opening.
2. **What does v2's peak do round by round, next to each opponent?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-5/v3_s2_traj.py on v2's final data (peak candidate, my group vs the other group per round). v2's peak deals 7.7 per member in round 0 falling to 2.5 in round 8, then nothing; contributions climb from 8.8 to 12.4-13.3 per member by round 9 and stay flat for the rest of the game (10.8 against opponent_4), so the norm it sets persists for 15 rounds without any punishment. It loses members at every decision of the opening: 4 -> 3.52 / 3.68 / 3.45 / 2.94 after round 3 against opponents 1-4, recovering to ~3.9-4.4 by the end. The striking comparison is opponent_3: it punishes even harder (10.8 and 9.3 per member in rounds 0-1) but *nothing* in rounds 2-3, then again only in rounds 4-5, 8-9, 12-13 (the first two rounds of each block) - and it *gains* members at the first decision (4.55) while we lose them, and its late pool is 86-88 vs our 80-83 at the same contribution per member (13.2-13.3). Its pool in rounds 2-3 jumps to 70 because it stops paying. So I suspect the timing within a 4-round block matters: punishment just before a switch decision costs members, punishment just after one is cheap. Next: test that directly on the switching decisions.
3. **Does the timing of punishment within a block drive switching?** Logit of switching at each decision on own punishment and both groups' mean punishment per member in each of the block's four rounds, plus contributions and group size, over all players of v2's final data (1.92M player-decisions, 27% switch), with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-5/v3_s3_switch_timing.py. Strong recency: own punishment +0.008 / +0.017 / +0.030 / +0.037 per point in block rounds 0/1/2/3, own group's mean punishment +0.017 / +0.038 / +0.059 / +0.043, the other group's mean -0.011 / -0.031 / -0.055 / -0.029. So a point dealt in the first round of a block costs about a quarter of the members a point in its last two rounds costs, and the switch model compares the two managers mainly on the last two rounds before the decision. Group mean contribution keeps players (-0.091), the other's attracts (+0.074), group size keeps (-0.17 per member). This explains entry 2: v2's continuous rounds 0-8 punishment hit rounds 2-3 and 6-7, right before decisions. So v3 should punish only in the first round(s) of each block and stay silent in the rounds before a decision; next: does the contribution response depend on where in the block the punishment falls, and how long it lasts?
4. **Does the contribution response depend on where in the block the punishment falls?** Change in contribution 1 and 4 rounds after punishment, by contribution level and punishment band, and an OLS slope (contributors at <= 11, controlling for c) per block position and per phase of the game, on a 15% sample of v2's final data, with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-5/v3_s4_contrib_resp.py. The 4-round response is the same in block positions 0, 1 and 2 (+0.126, +0.127, +0.128 contribution per punishment point) and a little lower in position 3 (+0.103); so moving punishment to the start of a block loses nothing on contributions while (entry 3) costing a quarter to half the members. By phase: +0.085 (rounds 0-3), +0.094 (4-7), +0.079 (8-11), +0.070 (12-15), and -0.007 for punishment in rounds 16-19 - late punishment buys nothing at all. By level the picture of v2 holds: below 11 punishment raises contributions (c 7-11: -0.06 unpunished vs +2.9 four rounds after 15-30 points; c 0-3: +3.6 vs +6.1), at 11-15 it does nothing, at 16-20 it hurts (-1.9 vs -5.2). So a target near 10-11 (v2's peak 9.7) is right, and punishing after round ~16 is wasted. (I also checked whether I could run the artificial humans here: the artifacts folder has only copula calibration files, no model weights, so no local simulation.)
5. **Drafted v3: punish at the start of each block only.** Same absolute-target shape as v2 (a valid member of my group gets `min(slope * max(target - c, 0), cap)`), but punishment happens only in the first `window` rounds of each 4-round block (rounds with `t mod 4 < window`), i.e. right after a switch decision and as far as possible from the next one, where entry 3 says it costs the fewest members and entry 4 says it moves contributions as much. The opening target `target_open` applies in the first `n_open` blocks, the maintenance target `target_late` after that, and nothing from round `t_end` on (entry 4: no return after ~16). Changes from v2: the block window (new, the main idea), the opening counted in blocks rather than rounds (t_open was flat in v2 and the block structure now matters), and the end round swept instead of fixed at 21. `window` = 4 reproduces v2's continuous punishment, so the sweep can still pick v2's shape if I am wrong. Because punishment now falls in fewer rounds, a stronger hit per round may be needed (opponent_3, the best pool among the opponents, deals ~10-11 per member in rounds 0-1 of the game), so I keep the cap range up to 30 and the slope up to 8.
6. **Calibrated the ranges on human histories and validated.** Replayed the draft as group 0's manager on the 50 human games with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-5/v3_t3_replay.py. A v2-like setting (target 9.7, slope 4.35, cap 22.4, window 4, until round 9) deals 8.4-10.4 per member in rounds 0-8 on 52-62% of members; the same strength with window 2 and one opening block deals ~9.5 in rounds 0-1 only, close to opponent_3's opening (10.8, 9.3); window 2 with target_late 6 until round 16 adds 2.6-3.5 per member in rounds 8-9 and 12-13 on a quarter of members; window 1 punishes only the first round after each decision. The low corner deals practically nothing (0.3 in round 0), the high corner (14/12, slope 12, cap 30, window 4) ~20 per member every round, absurd but it bounds the design. I narrowed target_late back to [0, 10] (v2's profile fell monotonically from 0, so its upper end only needs to show that). validate-rule passes: 256 distinct design points.

## Key findings

1. v2's peak (a hard continuous opening in rounds 0-8: target 9.7, slope 4.35, cap 22.4, no maintenance) is the best result so far, pool 65.17 (67.5 / 66.8 / 69.4 / 56.9 against opponents 1-4); target_open dominates its surface (sharp optimum near 9-10.5) and t_open is flat (exploration 1).
2. That opening costs members at every decision it spans: 4 -> 2.94-3.68 after round 3; opponent_3 punishes even harder but only in the first two rounds of each block and gains members (4.55) and a higher late pool (86-88 vs 80-83) at the same contribution per member (exploration 2).
3. The artificial humans' switching weighs punishment by recency within the block: own punishment +0.008 / +0.017 / +0.030 / +0.037 per point in block rounds 0-3, own group's mean +0.017 / +0.038 / +0.059 / +0.043, the other group's mean -0.011 / -0.031 / -0.055 / -0.029 (exploration 3).
4. The contribution response does not depend on block position (+0.126 / +0.127 / +0.128 / +0.103 per point four rounds later for contributors at <= 11), works below ~11 and hurts at 16-20, and vanishes for punishment after round 16 (-0.007) (exploration 4).

## Hypothesis

Against these artificial humans punishment of members below about 10 is an investment that raises their contributions for the rest of the game, and its member cost depends on when it is dealt: the switch decision mostly remembers the last two rounds before it, while the contribution response does not care where in the block the punishment fell. So a good manager punishes hard, but only in the first round or two after each switch decision, never in the rounds leading up to the next one; it front-loads (a high target in the first block or two, which sets a norm that persists), at most keeps a lower target for free-riders and newcomers in later blocks, and stops by round ~16, after which punishment buys nothing. A valid member of my group contributing `c` gets `min(slope * max(target - c, 0), cap)` in rounds with `t mod 4 < window` and `t < t_end`, where `target = target_open` in the first `n_open` blocks and `target_late` after.

- `target_open` (float, [5, 14]): contribution below which a member is punished in the opening. I expect 9-11 (v2's optimum 9.2-10.5).
- `n_open` (int, [1, 4]): number of 4-round blocks with the opening target. I expect 1-2 (v2's t_open 9 was ~2 blocks, but flat).
- `target_late` (float, [0, 10]): target after the opening; 0 = none. I expect 0-6: now that it falls only in cheap rounds, maintenance may pay where v2's continuous one did not.
- `window` (int, [1, 4]): punishing rounds at the start of each block. I expect 1-2; 4 reproduces v2.
- `slope` (float, [0.5, 12], log): punishment per point of shortfall. I expect 3-8 (v2's peak 4.35, near its top).
- `cap` (float, [5, 30]): most points per member per round. I expect 18-30: with fewer punishing rounds each hit may need to be harder (v2's peak 22.4, near its top).
- `t_end` (int, [8, 22]): first round with no punishment. I expect 13-17.

### Justification

Findings 2 and 3 are the reason for `window`: a point in block round 0 costs about a quarter of the switching a point in round 3 costs, and the opponent that punished only at block starts kept and gained members; finding 4 says nothing is lost on contributions by moving punishment there, so the trade looks one-sided. Finding 1 is why the absolute-target shape, the opening target near 10 (`target_open`) and hard hits (`slope`, `cap`, both widened upwards since v2's peak sat near their tops and the window concentrates punishment into fewer rounds) are kept. Finding 4's dead response after round 16 is why `t_end` is swept over [8, 22] rather than fixed at 21, and its level dependence is why the target never goes above 14. `n_open` and `target_late` let the sweep decide whether maintenance in later blocks pays now that it is cheap in members (newcomers arrive exactly at block starts). What would contradict this: a peak at `window` 4 (block timing does not matter in the full simulation) or at `window` 1 with tiny effects of everything else; `target_late` again best at 0 would mean only the opening matters. Not modelled: reacting to the other manager (finding 3 shows the other group's late-block punishment pushes players to us, but my rule cannot change that, and v1's opponent-following did nothing), per-player histories and group size, to keep the parameters readable and because 256 points over 7 dimensions are already thin.
