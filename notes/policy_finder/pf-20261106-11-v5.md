## Explorations

1. **Where did v4 land, and is the v3 family still improving?** Read the v4 notes, rule, `final/sweep.json` and `landscapes.json`. The v4 final is 68.3 (se 0.55), against v3's 68.7: no gain. Per opponent it is 70.9, 68.5, 73.6 and 60.2, within 1 of v3's 71.0, 69.4, 74.4 and 60.0. The v4 peak (rate 6.5, delta +0.08, decay 0.58, mid 0.2) sits far from v3's (6.6, -1.29, 0.4, 1.22), yet both score the same, and the fitted surface's effects are small (rate 3.1, delta 4.4, decay 2.7, mid 4.1) with a peak sd of 0.86. 2. **How noisy are the sweep points, and where does the v4 final lose pool?** Read all four `sweep.json` files, and ran scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-11/h1_dose.py (dose bins at rounds 0-1) and h2_v4_final.py (v4 final per round and opponent). A sweep point's pool has se ~2.1, and its per-opponent spread across the v4 design (sd 4.6-4.9) is mostly noise (per-opponent se ~4.3). Within a shortfall bin, the dose response at round 0 is roughly linear: +2.0 at p 1-5 up to +5.1 at p 30 for members 4-8 below. Leaving at the first switch rises from 0.33 to 0.45 along the same bins, so a cap on punishment has no clear case. In the v4 final, the pool dips at every block start: 14, 43, 61, 70 and 76 in rounds 0, 4, 8, 12 and 16, against ~78 in the rounds after. Against opponent 4 my group's contribution per head peaks at 10.9 and sags to 10.3, and its members drop 4 -> 3.69 at the first switch. Against opponents 1 and 3, by contrast, contributions climb to 12-12.8 in both groups. The other manager sets the population's cooperation level, and that level carries into my group through switchers. 3. **Do the best parameters differ by opponent?** Fitted a GP per opponent over the 512 v3+v4 design points (same code), with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-11/h3_per_opp_gp.py. The common optimum (rate 7.7, delta -1.41, decay 0.49, mid 1.03) gives a mean of 68.8 over opponents, and per-opponent optima would give 69.5, so +0.7 is the most full tailoring could buy inside this family. The per-opponent peaks differ mainly in `mid`: 1.06 and 1.20 against opponents 1 and 3 (who punish 10.8 per head in round 0), but 0.22 (the range edge) against opponents 2 and 4 (3.8 and 0). Opponent 2 gains most from tailoring (73.2 own best vs 71.3 at the common point). That is a hint that how hard I may punish after round 0 depends on how hard the other manager punishes, which I can see in `p` from round 1 on. Next: the mechanism, i.e. what drives exits and whether others' punishment affects my members.

4. **Spillover: does punishing one member move the others?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-11/h4_spill.py and h5_spill_shape.py, on unpunished members of my group in the v3/v4 sweeps, regressing the next-round change on own c, the others' mean c, the others' mean punishment and group size. An unpunished member's contribution rises by 0.046-0.059 per point of the others' mean punishment at rounds 0, 4 and 8, and by 0.026-0.039 at second rounds. This does not fade over blocks, unlike my earlier reading of the own response. It is the mean that matters: the others' max adds nothing (-0.005 to +0.009), and the share punished at a given mean is negative (-0.23 to -0.37), so concentrated punishment spills over slightly better than spread. The others' punishment also raises the unpunished member's chance of leaving: +0.0034-0.006 per point at rounds 0-1, but only +0.001-0.0015 at round 4. Block 0's punishment thus costs exits from the bystanders too, and later blocks' much less. Next: the dose shape of the own response and of leaving, cleanly.

5. **Dose shape, within narrow shortfall bins.** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-11/h6_leave_shape.py (regression with dose splines) and h7_dose_narrow.py (bins: shortfall 5-7 and 2.5-4, with dose varying through rate/delta across design points). At round 4, same bin: +0.9 to +3.3 to block end, leaving 0.214 -> 0.348. So the own response does NOT really fade by round 4 in this cleaner cut (0.08 per point to the block end). Entry 4 of v4 (0.014 at t=4) came from a regression whose linear gap controls absorb the effect. I correct that belief. What does fade is the bystanders' exit cost (entry 4) and the rounds left to collect the gain. 6. **Does the opponent change the exit cost of my punishment?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-11/h8_exit_by_opp.py: leaving at the first switch on own and bystander punishment in rounds 0 and 1, per opponent. 7. **What pulls players in at a switch?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-11/h9_inflow.py: per game and switch, the share of each group that changes, on both groups' block punishment per head, their mean contribution in the block's last round and my size. One point of group mean contribution at the decision round is worth as much membership as 4-6 points per head of block punishment. Membership follows the cooperation gap between the groups. Punishment is the price of that gap, not only its enemy. The rule's structure already uses this: it pays early so that contributions are high by round 3. Next: check whether entry 3's `mid` split is real or noise, by looking at the GP slices.

8. **Newcomers at block starts.** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-11/h10_newcomers.py (v4 sweep, t=4 and 8). 10. **Drafted and checked the v5 rule.** It keeps v3/v4's core: rate * decay^b * max(0, others' mean + delta - c) * w_pos * end-fade. w_pos is 1, mid, 0, 0 by position in blocks 0-4, and the block-5 end-fade is min(1, (23 - t)/3). ## Key findings

1. The v3/v4 family has plateaued. The v4 final is 68.3 against v3's 68.7, from very different peaks, and a GP over both sweeps' 512 points peaks at ~69 (rate 7.7, delta -1.41, decay 0.49, mid 1.03), with per-point se ~2.1 (entries 1-3).
2. The own response to punishment does not fade by round 4 when measured within narrow shortfall bins: ~0.08 contribution to the block's end per point at both rounds 0 and 4. Leaving rises ~0.004 per point, near-linearly, with no fixed cost of being punished at all. This corrects v4's finding 3 (entry 5).
3. Punishment spills over. Unpunished members rise ~0.05 per point of the others' mean punishment at every block start. Bystander exits rise +0.003-0.006 per point in block 0, but only +0.001 later (entry 4).
4. Membership follows the cooperation gap between the groups. Inflow share rises +0.016-0.030 per point of my group's mean contribution at the decision round and falls -0.004-0.005 per point of my block punishment per head. It rises +0.004-0.010 per point of the other manager's (entry 7).
5. The exit cost of my own punishment is the same against every opponent (entry 6). But the payoff of severity is not: log(rate) x harsh opponent is +0.87 (se 0.29) on the pooled surface, while mid, decay and delta show no significant interaction (entry 9).
6. The opponent's stance is legible in `p` after round 0. The four opponents deal 10.8, 3.8, 10.8 and 0 per head in round 0 (entry 2).
7. Newcomers arrive ~2 below the norm and respond to punishment like stayers. Neither grace nor extra severity is indicated (entry 8).

## Hypothesis

In this simulator a good manager disciplines early and in proportion to how far a member falls below the others' mean (with a small tolerance). It front-loads that discipline, stays silent in the two rounds before each switch, and lets the end fade. Punishment buys lasting contributions from the punished and from bystanders. Membership follows the cooperation gap between the groups and the punishment gap. So how much severity pays depends on the other manager. Against a harsh manager my punishment is matched, and the population is primed to cooperate, so firmer discipline pays. Against a lenient one, every point I deal widens the punishment gap that pushes my members away, so lighter discipline pays. The rule reads the other manager's stance from its round-0 punishments and scales its own severity from round 1 on.

The rule: punishment = rate * decay^b * stance * max(0, others' mean + delta - c) * w_pos * min(1, (23 - t)/3), b = floor(t/4), w_pos = 1, mid, 0, 0 by position in blocks 0-4 (1 in block 5), stance = 1 in round 0 and exp(kappa * (q0 - 6)/6) after, q0 = the other manager's mean punishment per member in round 0.
- `rate` (float, [3, 20] log): points per point of shortfall in block 0. I expect 6-10.
- `delta` (float, [-3, 0.8]): tolerance relative to the others' mean. I expect -2 to 0.
- `decay` (float, [0.15, 0.85]): severity multiplier per block. I expect 0.35-0.65.
- `mid` (float, [0.2, 1.8]): weight of a block's second round. I expect 0.6-1.3.
- `kappa` (float, [-0.5, 1.0]): how far severity follows the other manager's stance. I expect 0.1-0.5; 0 is v4's rule.

### Justification

Finding 1 sets the ranges of the four core parameters. They are centred on the pooled GP optimum and trimmed only where v4's profiles fell clearly (decay at 0 and 0.8, delta at -3.5). Every v3 and v4 peak stays inside, so the sweep can find them again. Finding 2 removes my earlier reason for pushing decay towards 0. The geometric decay is kept because it tracks the shrinking number of rounds left to repay a point and the falling shortfalls, and the pooled surface's decay peak is ~0.5, not an edge. Findings 3 and 4 explain why the front-loaded proportional form works. Punishment raises the whole group's contribution, and a high group contribution at the decision round is what draws members, while each point of punishment per head pushes them out. That trade-off is relative to the other group, so findings 4, 5 and 6 motivate `kappa`. The value of severity differs by opponent (log rate x harsh +0.87, ~3 se), and the opponent shows its type in round 0. The size I expect for kappa comes from the per-opponent GP optima (rate ~5.5 against lenient opponents and ~7.7+ against harsh ones, a log-ratio of ~0.3-0.5 over q0 differences of ~1.6 in (q0-6)/6 units). The range reaches below 0 and up to 1, so the sweep can also reject or overshoot it.

What would contradict the hypothesis:
- A best kappa near 0 (or negative) would mean the rate x opponent interaction lives in round 0, which cannot react, or that it was noise. v2's whole-game kappa failed, and this is the risk.
- A gain of kappa only against opponent 2 or only opponent 4 would mean the stance proxy picks up something other than harshness.

What I chose not to model:
- A separate round-1 opponent response (mid x harsh +0.56 ± 0.39, not significant).
- Grace for newcomers (finding 7).
- A dose cap or minimum (finding 2: linear dose, no fixed cost).
- Targets taken from the other group's contributions. Plausible from finding 4, but untested and a second new dimension.
The expected gain is modest (+0.3-0.7 over the ~69 plateau): the family is near its ceiling, and this lever only acts on the roughly half of the punishment dealt after round 0.
