## Key findings

1. Six versions have reached 64.0 / 65.2 / 68.5 / 67.4 / 67.7 / 67.7. v6's size-dependent maintenance did nothing (every parameter's correlation with the pool within +-0.08, quintile means within 1.2 points, against a point se of 2.15) (exploration 1).
2. My rule's group already out-pools every opponent's group in the same games (e.g. 74.6 vs 62.8 against opponent_2), mainly through members. The remaining headroom is in contributions: 10.2 -> 12.9 per member over the game against the best opponent, 10.1 -> 10.4 against the non-punisher (exploration 2).
3. Late contributions are polarised and individual: 19-22% of my group give 0-5 from block 1 on. A player's mean contribution in rounds 12-23 follows their rounds 0-3 level almost one to one, whether they were hit ~47 or ~24 points in rounds 0-3, and the hard-hit low starters leave. So the opening works through the group norm, not by reforming the one punished (exploration 3).
4. Across 1280 sweep points, depth per hit drives the pool (+3 for 15-30 points per hit vs -3 for under 5). Hitting more than 65% of the group is bad at every depth, and at a given total dose a larger share hit costs 3.7 per unit (exploration 4).
5. Within a given round-0 composition (an almost experimental comparison, because the design point decides who is hit), hitting only one of two or three low members beats hitting all of them by 3-6 pool points per round, consistently in v4, v5 and v6. Hitting someone who is not low costs 3-14 (exploration 5).
6. The same holds in round 1: hitting one beats hitting two low members by 3.6 and three by 2.9-3.2, se ~0.5. It roughly holds at rounds 4 and 8 for groups of 3-6, where the best count is 0-2 and over-hitting costs 3-14 (exploration 6).

## Hypothesis

Against these artificial humans, punishment works as a public example that sets the group's norm. It mostly does not reform the player it lands on (that player's long-run level is their own, and if hit hard they tend to leave), and each extra player hit costs members and goodwill. So a good manager makes a hard example of the worst one or two contributors at the moments that set the norm: rounds 0 and 1, before the first switch, and the first round after each of the next three switch decisions, when newcomers arrive. It never hits most of the group, even when most of the group is low, never hits anyone not clearly below the norm, and does not punish in rounds 2-3 of any block or from round 13 on. The rule: in rounds 0-1 and 4, 8, 12, a valid member of my group below the round's target is punished `slope * (target - c)` (clamped to 30 by the manager), but only if they are among the k lowest contributors of the group that round (ties broken by player index).

- `target_open` (float, [7, 13]): the opening's eligibility bar. I expect 9-11, where every earlier version's opening landed (8.8-11.2). With a rank cap it may sit higher, because the cap, not the bar, now protects the near-norm players.
- `slope` (float, [2, 20], log): points per point of shortfall. I expect 6-15, because depth (15-30 per hit) is what pays (finding 4) and the cap keeps the breadth down.
- `k_open` (int, [1, 3]): the most hit in each opening round. I expect 1-2 (findings 5, 6).
- `target_late` (float, [2, 12]): the eligibility bar at rounds 4, 8, 12. I expect 5-9 (v6's 6.1, v3's 8.8). The low end nearly switches maintenance off.
- `k_late` (int, [1, 2]): the most hit in each maintenance round, whatever the group's size. I expect 1 (finding 6).

### Justification

Findings 3-6 are the core. The punished player's own long-run contribution barely depends on how hard they were hit (3), while depth per hit carries the pool (4). Within a fixed composition, fewer hits do better than more, in round 0 (5), in round 1 and at later block starts (6). So `k_open` and `k_late` cap the breadth, and `slope` with its high range lets each hit be deep. `target_open` and `target_late` stay as eligibility bars, because hitting anyone not clearly low is the costliest mistake in 5 and 6. The timing (rounds 0, 1, 4, 8, 12) is what every version since v3 has kept, and finding 1 is why v6's size_gain and window_late are gone. I still ignore the other manager: v4-v6 found the best doses to be the same against every opponent, and finding 2 says I already win the member race against three of four.

What would contradict this: `k_open` best at 3 with no gain over v6's 67.7, which would mean the composition result of finding 5 was a target confound (points with low targets also differed in later rounds). Or the best points sitting at slope 2, which would mean depth does not carry over to a single hit.

Not modelled: a forgiveness rule for players who improved between rounds 0 and 1 (round 1's rank cap already spares most of them); punishing based on the other group's state; group-size-dependent caps (finding 6 found no size dependence worth a parameter); and any punishment after round 12 (findings 4 and earlier versions: rounds 16-23 punishment costs 2.1 per point).
