2. **Is the flat end two effects cancelling?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-8/
   v7_cache.py caches v5's and v6's sweeps (both hit c < 2 players in rounds horizon <= t <
   end with the full dose). scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261105-8/v7_late.py takes every valid

   end = t (no hit from t on). The rules are the same before t, so the two groups differ
   only by that one hit (mean dose 26). Outcome: the group's payoff summed over t..23, with
   controls for the others' contribution, own previous c and t. Over all of them the effect
   is -1.3 (se 0.2) per hit. Split, the picture changes:
   - a first late hit on a fresh drop (previous c >= 5): +2.7 (0.3);
   - a fresh drop from 15-20: +4.6 (0.3);
   - a fresh drop from 5-13: -3.6 to -4.2;
   - a repeat late hit: -5.0 (0.3);
   - a player who was already low the round before: -7.3 (0.6).
   By round, fresh drops are worth +12.1 at t = 9-11, +7.1 at 12-13, +1.4 at 14-15, -2.2
   at 16-17 and -6.2 at 18-19. All other lows are worth +0.1, -2.1, -6.0, -7.4, -9.9.
   Fresh drops by players with no opening hit are worth +7.9, by players with 3+ opening
   hits +0.4. So the flat end is indeed two things cancelling. A sudden collapse of a high
   contributor is worth punishing until about round 15. A chronic low is not worth
   punishing after round ~11. v7_events.py on v6's final counts them: fresh drops from 15+

   Next: the opening, where the money is (50 points of punishment). Is a repeated hit on a

3. **In the opening, is a repeated hit worth less than a fresh one?** scripts/policy_finder/

   in v5 and v6, with end > t+1. For every valid player with c >= 2 I compute the dose the
   rule would give, and sum it per group by type:
   - stuck: hit at t-1 and not risen since;
   - rising: hit at t-1 and risen;
   - fresh: not hit at t-1, in practice a player who gave ~18-20 (or timed out) and has
     now dropped.
   I regress the group's payoff summed over t..23 on treated x points of each type, with
   controls. Net value per point:

   | rounds | stuck | rising | fresh |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 3-4 | +0.16 | +0.32 | +0.82 |
   | 5-6 | +0.01 | +0.04 | +0.73 |
   | 7-9 | -0.21 | -0.21 | +0.17 |

   punishment buys most when it answers a fresh drop from a high contribution. Hitting the

## Key findings

1. Six versions of one family stand at finals of 648.2-654.3; the best re-checked points
   reach 655.9-656.6. v6's end had no effect at all, and v6's final still spends ~50 of
   its ~114-point loss on punishment, while contributions slide from 17.2 to 14.1 after
   round 7 (exploration 1).
2. After the opening, a late hit's value depends on who it hits. On a fresh drop
   (previous c >= 5, first late hit) it is worth +2.7 per hit (se 0.3), +4.6 when the
   drop is from 15-20. On a repeat it is worth -5.0, on a player already low last round
   -7.3. Fresh collapses still pay at rounds 12-13 (+7.1) and break even at ~15; chronic
   lows are neutral at 9-11 and negative from 12. v6's flat end was these two cancelling
   (exploration 2).
3. Collapses from 15+ happen ~0.03 times per game and round, steadily over the game
   (exploration 2).
4. In the opening's later rounds the same split holds per point:

   | rounds | fresh drop from ~18-20 | repeat |
   |---|---|---|
   | 3-4 | +0.82 | +0.16 to +0.32 |
   | 5-6 | +0.73 | ~0 |
   | 7-9 | +0.17 | -0.21 |

   At 5-9 both mild (+0.24) and deep (+0.45) fresh drops pay (exploration 3).

## Hypothesis

A good manager makes one expensive first impression and then reacts only to news. For
the first ~5-7 rounds it punishes every player by their shortfall from full contribution
on a steep curve (about 30 for a free rider, ~7 at half, nothing near 18+). This lifts
the group to ~17, and the clones' raise persists and spreads. After the opening, hitting
the same low player again no longer repays: he has already taken the lesson or will not.
A cooperator who suddenly drops is different. His drop is news, and an answer to it
still lifts him and the group for long enough to repay, until about round 14-16. So
after the opening the manager punishes, on the same curve, only a player who gave at
least `high` last round and gives less now, and from round `end` nobody. The rule:
base = free * (max(20 - c, 0) / 20)^log2(free / mid). Every valid player gets it in
rounds t < horizon. In rounds horizon <= t < end, only a player with valid previous
contribution >= high and c < previous gets it.
- `free` (float, [24, 34]): the curve's value at c = 0, clamped to 30. I expect 28-32.
- `mid` (float, [4.5, 9.5]): punishment at c = 10, which sets the steepness. I expect
  6-8.
- `horizon` (int, 3..9): the opening phase's end. I expect 5-7.
- `high` (float, [10, 20]): the drop line on the previous contribution. I expect 14-18.
- `end` (int, 10..20): the first round with no punishment. I expect 13-16.

### Justification

Finding 1 says the curve and the opening are settled, and that v6's late phase did
nothing on net. Findings 2 and 4 show why. Fresh drops by cooperators repay well past the
opening (+0.7/point at 5-6, +12 per collapse at 9-11, +7 at 12-13), while repeats and
chronic lows lose from round ~7-12. This grounds:
- the drop condition;
- `high`: collapses from 15-20 pay, from 5-13 lose, and the opening's fresh drops come
  from 18-20;
- `end`: fresh collapses break even at ~15;
- a possibly shorter `horizon`: repeats are ~0 from round 5.

`free` and `mid` keep v6's curve, which the pooled fit of 1280 rules placed at P(0) 28-30
and P(10) ~7. `free` may now go past 30 because v6's peak sat at that edge. Finding 3
sizes the gain: ~1.5-2.5 points over v6, the same order as the noise between finals.

What would contradict this:
- a landscape that wants `high` at 20 and `end` at its low edge. That would mean late
  drops are not worth answering after all.
- a final below v6's 654.3.

Not modelled:
- a separate (smaller) dose for drops: the data only covers doses of ~26-30;
- a group-relative standard: no gain in v1-v2;
- chronic free riders after the opening: neutral to negative (finding 2);
- timeouts, beyond not treating a return from a timeout as a drop.
