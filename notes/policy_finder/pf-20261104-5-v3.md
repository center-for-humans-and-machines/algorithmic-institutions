# pf-20261104-5-v3

## Explorations

1. **Where did v2 end up?** Read v2's `sweep.json`, `landscapes.json` and
   `final/sweep.json`, and ran
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v2_mechanism.py (v1_mechanism.py on
   v2's final points). Found that v2's peak is k=5, thr=7, cap=30,
   decay=0.92, t_stop=13. It scores 67.6 (se 1.0), which is no real gain on
   v1's 67.3. Three parameters sit at an edge (k and cap at the top, thr at
   the bottom). So the surface wants a step: punish anyone below ~6 with
   close to the maximum and spare everyone else. The fitted profiles fall
   steeply with thr (68.8 at 7, 52.5 at 16), rise with k (62.1 at 0.5, 68.8
   at 5), are nearly flat in cap (67.0 at 4, 68.8 at 30), and peak at decay
   0.91-0.95 and t_stop 12-14. Several quite different final points tie at
   ~67 (s050: k=1.5, thr=12.4; s176: k=2.9, thr=11.6). The gp's mechanism
   is the same as v1's: it punishes 4.2 per member-round in rounds 0-3, 2.3
   in 4-7 and 1.6 in 8-11, then nothing from round 16. Late members give
   11.1 (zero: 8.3) and the group holds 4.6-4.8 members (zero: ~5.0). A
   sweep point has 500 episodes (pool_se ~2.1), so the top of the sweep is
   noisy. Two versions of the same shape have plateaued at ~67, so a third
   tuning of it will not move far. Next: learn what the artificial humans'
   switch and contribution models actually read, to find a lever the shape
   does not use.
2. **What do the artificial humans read?** Read the training configs
   (configs/training/artificial_humans/switch_predictor/joint_exodus.yml and
   contribution/group_switching_contribution_50ep_vnode_stimulus_skip.yml).
   The model weights are not in the container. The switch model (mlp+rnn+edge)
   reads per player and round its group's `common_good`, the `punishment` it
   received, its `agent_group` and the `round_number`. Its edges connect all
   8 players, so it also sees the other group's pool and punishments. It
   never sees contributions or payoffs directly. The contribution model
   (node+edge+rnn, group vnode, stimulus skip) reads the previous
   contribution and punishment and the group, with edges to the others. So
   leaving is driven by the *total* pool of each group (not per member) and
   the punishment received. A large pool may hold and attract members by
   itself, which would be a feedback loop. Next: check this in v2's sweep
   data: switch rate against the own and the other group's pool and the
   punishment received in the decision round.
3. **What drives a switch in v2's sweep?** Loaded part 1 of v2's sweep
   (128 points x 500 games) into dense arrays with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v3_load.py, and tabulated every
   player's decision at rounds 3/7/11/15/19 with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v3_switch_drivers.py. `common_good`
   in the data is the per-member share: cg x valid members equals the pool
   exactly. Found an overall switch rate of 28.8%. By share gap (own minus
   other) it falls from 57.9% (gap below -10) to 14.2% (gap above +10). By
   the punishment received in the decision round it rises from 23.7% (none)
   to 53% (6-10) and 71% (21-30). A linear probability model gives per
   point: own share -1.0pp, other share +0.8pp, decision-round punishment
   +0.72pp, punishment in the 3 rounds before +0.47pp, each member of the
   own group -2.3pp. The first decision is the leakiest (43% against
   24-28% later). Large groups hold (n=8: 17.5%) and are common in the
   sweep. So a point of punishment costs ~1.5x more leaving in the decision
   round than in the rounds before it. Leaving only hurts the score when
   the leaver contributes: the score is the total pool, so a free rider who
   leaves costs nothing. Next: across v2's points, how the score depends on
   whom the punishment hits (by contribution class) and when (decision
   rounds or not).
4. **Whom should the punishment hit?** Summarised every v2 sweep point
   (both parts) with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v3_points.py. For
   rounds 0-11 I summed the punishment per game that went to members giving
   0-2, 3-6, 7-10 and 11+, the punishment in decision rounds, and the
   punishment in rounds 12-23. Then I regressed the score, the late (12-23)
   member contribution and the late members on these sums with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v3_points_reg.py. Found, per point
   of punishment:
   - On members giving 3-6: +0.13 score (se 0.03), +0.049 late contribution.
   - On members giving 0-2: -0.03 (se 0.02), no effect on late contribution
     (-0.002). These members do not move, so the punishment only costs pool.
   - On members giving 7-10: -0.03, a small lift (+0.015) bought with
     members (-0.003 each).
   - On members giving 11+: -0.20.
   - In rounds 12+: -0.04.
   - In decision rounds: -0.07 on top of the rest (se 0.03).
   R2 is 0.60 for the score and 0.93 for the late contribution. Among the
   top points, s227 punishes only in rounds 0-2 and still scores 68.6. So
   the productive part of the dose is the moderate under-contributors. v2's
   step at thr=7 with k=5 worked by charging 3-6 givers 5-20 points, and
   the 30s it charged near-zero givers were waste. This contradicts my
   earlier "free riders are the target". The classes are endogenous (a
   punished 0-giver who moves to 4 is then counted in 3-6), so next I check
   it at the player level: in v2's sim, how a player's later contribution
   responds to a punishment, by its contribution class and the dose.
5. **Player-level response in v2's sim.** With
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v3_response.py (v2 sweep part 1,
   group 0, rounds 0-10, players who stay 4 more rounds) I looked at the
   change from c(t) to the mean of c(t+1..t+4), by class at t and the
   punishment at t. Found a strong, lasting personal response, much
   stronger than v1 measured on the reference sims. The lift over the
   unpunished in each class is:
   - 0-2 givers: +0.3 for 1-3 points, +1.4 for 4-7, +2.5 for 8-12, +5.2
     for 21-30, or ~0.2-0.25 per point.
   - 3-6 givers: +0.6, +1.6, +2.8, +5.6, or 0.22-0.3 per point.
   - 7-10 givers: 0.25 per point at small doses, 0.14 at 21-30.
   Regression slopes are 0.14-0.18 per point, with the group mean at t
   adding 0.4 per point (herding). Per point the response is mildly
   concave, and the best value per point is in the 4-12 range.
   So at the player level free riders do respond. Then why does the
   point-level regression credit nothing to the 0-2 class? Candidates:
   points that charge 0-2 givers 30 are in the concave region; punished
   free riders leave; or some free riders are persistent and absorb
   punishment round after round without moving. Next: does a player's
   response fade with the punishment it has already received?
6. **Does the response fade with past punishment?** With
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v3_repeat.py (v2 sweep part 1, group
   0, rounds 1-10) I split the change over the next 2 rounds by the
   player's cumulative punishment from us before t and the punishment at t.
   Found that it fades strongly.
   - 0-2 givers with no past punishment: 1-7 points lift them by 1.8 over
     the unpunished in the same row (4.51 vs 2.67). With 31-60 already
     received the lift is -0.1, and with 61+ it is -0.5. Only doses of
     16-30 still move them (+3.3 to +2.1).
   - 3-6 givers: 1-7 points lift them by 1.4 when fresh and by 0.2 at
     31-60.
   - The share of 0-2 givers still at 0-2 next round after 1-7 points
     rises from 50% (fresh) to 76% (31-60) and 84% (61+).
   This is partly selection: those who were punished a lot and are still
   low are the non-responders. The policy reading is the same either way.
   Punishment is well spent on players not yet punished much, and wasted
   on those who have absorbed a lot and still give little. This explains
   exploration 4's zero credit for the 0-2 class: rules that charge every
   0-giver every round pour points into the non-responders. So the new
   lever for v3 is a per-player budget: scale each player's punishment
   down with what it has already received, so the rule gives everyone a
   firm first push and stops feeding those who do not move. Next: decide
   whether "already received" should count only our punishments or also
   ah's.
7. **Ours or everyone's punishment?** With
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v3_repeat_all.py (v2 sweep part 1,
   0-6 givers in group 0, rounds 4-15) I split the same response by the
   cumulative punishment from both managers and by ah's alone. Found that
   the total separates cleanly. The lift from 1-7 points over the
   unpunished in the same row is +2.5 for players never punished, +1.1 at
   1-10 received, +0.5 at 11-30, +0.34 at 31-60 and +0.3 at 61+. By ah's
   punishment alone the gradient is weak (+0.65 down to +0.5). So the
   budget should count all the punishment a player has received in the
   game, and the response halves within the first ~10-20 points. Larger
   doses fade more slowly (16-30 points: +6.0 fresh, +2.8 at 61+), so the
   taper should be smooth, not a hard cut.
8. **Drafted v3.** Kept v2's base, min(cap, k * relu(thr - c_i)), for
   valid members in rounds t <= t_stop. Added the per-player budget, a
   factor exp(-R_i / B), where R_i is the total punishment player i has
   received so far from either manager. Added `quiet` again, a factor on
   the decision rounds 3/7/11/15/19, because exploration 4 put an extra
   -0.07 per decision-round point at the point level and exploration 3
   found the decision round's punishment weighs ~1.5x on leaving. I
   dropped v2's `decay`, because the budget does its job (strength falls
   as punishment accumulates) and does it per player: newcomers and
   players never punished still get a firm first push.
9. **Tested and validated the draft.** Ran the rule in a fresh process
   with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/test_rule_v3.py (k=3, thr=8,
   cap=20, budget=20, quiet=0.5, t_stop=12; contributions 0/0/4/7/11; player
   0 has received 5 points in every past round).
   - Round 0: 20/20/12/3/0.
   - Player 0 then tapers as its total grows: 15.6 in round 1, 5.7 in round
     5 and 1.0 in round 12. Player 1, never punished, stays at 20.
   - Round 3 is halved.
   - Nothing is charged from round 13, and nothing to timed-out or
     other-group players.
   The validator passes on all 256 design points. The ranges:
   - k [1, 10] log. v2's peak sat at its top edge of 5, and the budget
     makes a strong first push cheaper.
   - thr [4, 14]. v2's peak was at its bottom edge of 7, but other top
     points had 11-12 with lower k.
   - cap [5, 30]. Flat in v2.
   - budget [5, 300] log. At 5 a player gets about one firm push; at 300
     the taper hardly bites over a game's typical 30-60 received, which
     recovers v2's shape.
   - quiet [0, 1].
   - t_stop [2, 21]. v2 peaked at 12-14. A budget may let a later stop pay,
     because it no longer feeds the non-responders.

## Key findings

1. Versions 1 and 2 plateaued at the same score with the same shape (67.3
   and 67.6, se ~1). v2's peak sits at the edges k=5, thr=7, cap=30: a
   step that charges anyone below ~6 hard (exploration 1).
2. In the simulation, leaving reads the per-member pool shares of both
   groups and the punishment received. It does not read contributions. A
   decision-round point adds ~0.72pp to the leaving probability, against
   ~0.47pp for a point in the three rounds before it, and the first
   decision is the leakiest (43% against 24-28%) (exploration 3).
3. Across v2's 256 points, punishment on members giving 3-6 is what pays:
   +0.13 score per point, +0.049 late contribution. Punishment on 0-2
   givers earns nothing (-0.03 score, no late lift), and punishment on 11+
   givers costs 0.20 per point. Decision-round points cost an extra 0.07
   (exploration 4).
4. At the player level the response is strong and lasting, at 0.14-0.3
   contribution points per punishment point over the next 4 rounds, with
   +0.4 per point of the group mean (herding) (exploration 5).
5. The response fades with the punishment a player has already received.
   For 0-6 givers, 1-7 points lift a never-punished player by +2.5, one
   with 11-30 received by +0.5 and one with 61+ by +0.3. Punished 0-2
   givers with 61+ received stay at 0-2 84% of the time, against 50% when
   fresh. The total from both managers separates this better than ours
   alone (explorations 6, 7).

## Hypothesis

A good manager in this simulation invests early punishment in players who
will respond and stops spending on those who do not. A first, firm
punishment of an under-contributor lifts its giving for several rounds, and
the lift spreads through herding. Each further point on the same player
buys less, and players who have absorbed a lot and still give little are a
pure drain on the pool. So the manager should give every under-contributor,
newcomers included, a strong push. It should taper each player's punishment
as that player's received total grows, hold back in the round before a
switch decision, and stop punishing late in the game so that the group is
the cheap one to be in. The rule punishes each valid member i by
min(cap, k * max(0, thr - c_i)) * exp(-R_i / budget), times `quiet` in
rounds 3/7/11/15/19, and not at all after `t_stop`. R_i is all the
punishment i has received so far from either manager.

- `k` (float, [1, 10], log): punishment points per contribution point below
  the threshold, for a player not yet punished. I expect 3-8.
- `thr` (float, [4, 14]): the contribution from which a member is safe. I
  expect 7-11.
- `cap` (float, [5, 30]): the most a member gets in one round. I expect
  15-30, with a weak effect.
- `budget` (float, [5, 300], log): the received-punishment scale on which a
  player's punishment fades. I expect 15-60.
- `quiet` (float, [0, 1]): the factor on decision-round punishments. I
  expect below 0.5.
- `t_stop` (int, [2, 21]): the last punishing round. I expect 10-16.

### Justification

Finding 1 says the v1/v2 shape is exhausted, so v3 adds a mechanism rather
than retuning. Finding 5 is the basis of `budget`. The response to a
punishment falls by 80-90% once a player has received a few tens of
points, so the half-life of a player's punishment should be of that order
(budget 15-60). The log range from 5 to 300 runs from "one push each" to
"no taper", and the high end recovers v2. Finding 3 fits this reading: the
points that charged 0-2 givers every round got nothing for it, because
many of those players are past their responsive phase. The budget cuts
that spending without dropping the first, effective push. Findings 1 and 4
keep `k` and `thr`. v2's best sat at its k edge, and the budget lowers the
cost of a strong first push, so `k` reaches 10. `thr` runs from 4 to 14 so
that both v2's step at ~7 and its 11-12 points are inside. Findings 2 and 3
bring back `quiet`, which was inert in v1's light regime but should matter
at v2/v3 doses. `t_stop` stays because v2's late-phase punishment cost
score (finding 3, -0.04 per point). Its range is extended to 21 in case
the taper makes later pushes on fresh newcomers pay. `cap` stays, because
the response is mildly concave per point (finding 4).

The hypothesis would be contradicted if the sweep's best points sit at
`budget` near 300 (no taper, so v2 was right) or near 5 with poor scores,
if `quiet` is again flat, or if the score does not beat ~67.6.

I chose not to model several things:
- A lower contribution bound sparing the 0-2 givers. The budget handles
  the non-responders among them without giving up the fresh ones, who
  respond (finding 5).
- A threshold relative to the group mean. Herding acts through the mean
  anyway.
- The other group's pool, which drives leaving (finding 2). The rule
  cannot change it except by keeping its own pool high.
- v2's `decay`. The budget makes the strength fall with time on a
  per-player basis.
