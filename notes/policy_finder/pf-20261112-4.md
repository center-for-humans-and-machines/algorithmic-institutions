# pf-20261112-4

## Explorations

1. **How do `ah` and `zero` fare against each other in the reference sims?** Looked at
   per_round.parquet of 25_LEVIN_run1_ah_zero_pairings_batched with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-4/ref_sims.py (pool recomputed as 1.6*sum(c)-sum(p) per group,
   empty group = 0, averaged over 24 rounds). Found `zero` leads `ah` by +14.2 (se 2.0) as group 0 and
   +13.7 as group 1; ah-vs-ah and zero-vs-zero are ~0. Under `zero` the mean contribution drifts from 8.6
   to ~8.1, under `ah` it stays ~9.0-9.4, so punishment does buy ~1 point per player of contribution,
   but `ah` punishes ~2-3.5 per player early (falling to ~1), which costs about as much as it buys, and
   the decisive thing is size: after the first switch the zero group holds ~5.2 members vs ~3.8.
   So the pool score is mostly a headcount game; never punishing is a strong baseline. Next: what
   the switching model reacts to (own punishment? payoff difference? pool difference?).
2. **What makes artificial humans switch, and how do contributions answer punishment, in the sims?**
   Looked at the ah_vs_ah run of the reference sims with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-4/ref_switch.py.
   The switch model (configs/training/artificial_humans/switch_predictor/joint_exodus.yml) takes
   own group's common_good, own punishment, group and round, with an RNN and an edge model, so it
   remembers history and sees the other group. Found: switch rate at a decision round rises with
   own punishment that round, 0.24 (p=0) / 0.30 (1-2) / 0.38 (3-5) / 0.49 (6-10) / 0.65 (>10); and with
   the pool gap, 0.59 when own pool trails by >30 down to 0.17 when it leads by >30. Punishment one
   round before the decision shows nearly the same gradient (0.25 -> 0.64), probably because
   punishment is persistent per player, so I cannot separate timing here. Contribution response
   (next c - c, stayers): for c in 5-9, +0.12 unpunished vs +0.49 (p 3-5) and +0.84 (p 6-10); for
   c<=4 it is +2.2 regardless; for c>=15 punishment does not help (-1.6 vs -2.3). So punishment buys a
   small, mid-contributor-only rise, while each point of it costs a pool point and raises leaving.
   The trained models are not in the container (no .pt), so I cannot simulate. Next: the human data,
   to see whether punishment early in a 4-round block hurts switching less than at the decision round.
3. **Do the human data separate punishment timing, and how did human managers punish?** Looked at the
   50 human games (one copy each) with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-4/human.py. Logit of switching
   at decision rounds: own punishment that round +0.067/point, one round before +0.041, two +0.025,
   three +0.020, pool gap -0.091 per 10 pool points, own contribution ~0. So punishment right at the
   decision round costs most; punishment early in a block costs about a third of that.
   Contribution response (OLS, stayers): next c = 0.64 c + 0.32 group mean c - 0.13 p + 0.20 p*(c<10);
   i.e. +0.07 per punishment point for low contributors, negative for high ones; humans' next c for
   c>=15 with p>10 drops by 6.7. With 0.64 persistence and 1.6 multiplier, one punishment point buys at
   most ~0.3-0.5 pool points over the following rounds: a net loss even before switching.
   Human managers punished c=0 by 3.3 on average, c=20 by 0.3, and front-loaded it (3.5/round in rounds
   0-3, ~1.1 by the end). Game-group mean pool correlates -0.36 with mean punishment and +0.60 with size.
   But in the sims ah_vs_ah groups average ~56 vs ~51 for zero_vs_zero, so in the simulated stack
   punishment pays in pool for a fixed membership; ah loses to zero only through members.
   Next: the sims' contribution response, including group-mates (herding), to see whether removing a
   free rider helps the rest.
4. **How does the simulated contribution model answer punishment, own and group-mates'?** Looked at
   the reference sims with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-4/ref_response.py (OLS of next c on c, own
   p, own p x (c<10), mean of group-mates' c and p, round dummies; ah_vs_ah stayers). Found next c =
   0.66 c + 0.31 mates' c - 0.016 p + 0.050 p*(c<10) + 0.036 mates' p; the same two rounds ahead
   (0.049, 0.046). So a low contributor answers own punishment with +0.034/point, everybody answers the
   group's punishment with +0.036, high contributors slightly resent own punishment, and with
   0.66 + 0.31 ~ 0.97 the group mean is near a unit root: shocks persist for the rest of the game.
   Pool per member per round (net of punishment): ah groups 13.5-13.9, zero groups 12.4-12.9, so at a
   fixed membership punishment pays ~+1 pool/member/round; zero wins only by holding ~1 more member.
   So the target is ah's contribution lift without its member loss: punish low contributors, early
   in the game (long horizon, persistence), early in each 4-round block (switching responds most to
   punishment at the decision round and decays with lag, entry 3), never at decision rounds (own
   punishment and a lower own pool both push members out), and not near the end (no horizon left).
   Next: draft the rule.
5. **Drafted the rule.** configs/managers/rule_based/pf-20261112-4.yml: punishment = k * max(0, target -
   c) for members who contributed, only in the first `window` rounds of each 4-round block (rounds
   20-23 are not restricted by the window, as no decision follows them) and only in rounds before
   `stop`. Four params: k [0.05, 2, log], target [4, 20], window int 1..4, stop int 2..24. Human
   managers are close to k ~0.2, target ~16 (c=0 -> 3.3, c 5-9 -> 2.0, c 15-19 -> 0.7, entry 3), so
   ah's level lies inside; k=0.05 floors to 0-1 points, so the range reaches the zero baseline from
   above. I left out a separate "expel free riders at the decision round" idea (punishing them
   then lowers the own pool that every member sees, entry 2) and a size-aware term, to keep the
   design at 4 dims for 256 points. validate-rule passes (256 distinct points).
6. **Does the simulated switch model weigh early-block punishment less?** Looked at decision rounds
   7-19 of ah_vs_ah and ah's group in ah_vs_zero with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-4/ref_lags.py
   (logit of switching on own punishment at lags 0-4, own c, pool gap, round). Found per-point
   coefficients 0.033 (decision round), 0.039 (lag 1), 0.027 (lag 2), 0.011 (lag 3), -0.005 (lag 4),
   se 0.003, and -0.076 per 10 pool points of gap. So punishment in the first round of a block
   (lag 3) costs about a third of punishment in the last two rounds, and punishment four rounds
   back (the previous block) is forgotten; punishing at the decision round also costs through the pool
   gap every member sees. This backs `window` and suggests window 1-2 will win; I keep 3-4 in the
   range so the sweep can show it.

## Key findings

1. Never punishing beats `ah` by +14 pool points (se 2) in the reference sims, almost entirely
   through membership: the zero group holds ~5.2 members vs ~3.8 after the first switch (entry 1).
2. At a fixed membership punishment pays in the sims: pool per member per round, net of punishment,
   is 13.5-13.9 under `ah` vs 12.4-12.9 under `zero` (entry 4); ah_vs_ah groups average ~56 vs ~51
   for zero_vs_zero (entry 3).
3. The simulated contributions answer punishment modestly but persistently: +0.034 per point for
   low contributors (c<10), +0.036 per point of group-mates' mean punishment, -0.016 for high
   contributors, with c persistence 0.66 plus 0.31 on mates' c, so lifts carry over (entry 4).
   Humans show the same sign pattern (+0.07 low, -0.13 high; entry 3).
4. Switching rises with own punishment, 0.24 at p=0 to 0.65 at p>10 (entry 2), and falls with the
   own pool lead (-0.076 logit per 10 points in sims, -0.091 in humans; entries 3, 6).
5. The switch model's memory of punishment decays within a block: 0.033/0.039/0.027/0.011/-0.005 per
   point at lags 0-4 before the decision (entry 6); humans 0.067/0.041/0.025/0.020 (entry 3).
6. No decision follows round 19, and late punishment has few rounds to pay back; human managers
   front-loaded punishment (3.5/round in rounds 0-3, ~1.1 at the end; entry 3).

## Hypothesis

A good manager here keeps the contribution lift that punishment buys without the member loss that
makes `ah` lose to `zero`: members are what fills the pool. It punishes only low contributors, in
proportion to their shortfall, lightly, and only at the start of each 4-round block, when the switch
model will have largely forgotten it by the decision round, never in the decision round itself (which
would also lower the own pool every member compares), and only early enough in the game for the
persistent lift to repay the points it costs.

The rule: punishment = k * max(0, target - c) for members who contributed, in rounds t with
(t mod 4 < window or t >= 20) and t < stop.
- `k`, punishment points per point of shortfall: [0.05, 2, log]; I expect 0.1-0.4 (ah-like 0.2 and
  below); 0.05 is effectively zero after flooring.
- `target`, the contribution below which a member is punished: [4, 20]; I expect 10-16, as only
  c<10 answers punishment and mid-range targets keep the high contributors untouched.
- `window`, rounds allowed at the start of each block: int 1..4; I expect 1 or 2.
- `stop`, first round never punished: int 2..24; I expect 8-16, where the horizon still repays.

### Justification

Findings 1 and 2 together say the stake is member count, but that punishment is not worthless per
member, so a rule should neither copy ah nor stay at zero: hence a small `k` with the range reaching
down to effectively zero (k=0.05) and up past ah's severity, letting the sweep locate the trade-off.
Finding 3 says only low contributors answer their own punishment while everyone answers the group's,
which is why the punishment is a shortfall below `target` rather than a flat amount, and why `target`
can sit below 20. Findings 4 and 5 ground `window`: own punishment and a lower own pool drive leaving,
but the memory of punishment fades over three rounds, so punishment at the block's first round costs
about a third as much in switching as at the end of the block. Finding 6 grounds `stop`. What would
contradict it: the sweep's best points at window 4 with large k (switching does not matter as much as
the lift), or at the bottom of k with stop low (any punishment loses, and zero is the best one can do).
Not modelled: deliberately pushing free riders out at decision rounds (it lowers the own pool every
member sees, finding 4), conditioning on group sizes or the other group's pool, and treating
newcomers differently; each would add a dimension to a 256-point design without evidence it pays.
