## Key findings

1. The v2/v3 family has converged. v3 scored 72.9 +- 1.1 against v2's
   71.9, its surface has no parameter at an edge, and its best sweep
   points (73-77) recheck at 70-73 on fresh seeds. `cap` and
   `newcomer` had no effect (exploration 1).
2. On this stack the contribution model sees only the last contribution,
   the last punishment and the group (no round number), and the switch
   model sees own punishment, the per-capita pool of each group and
   the round (exploration 1). v3's group reaches 12.2-12.6 per member
   from round 11 on (zero: ~8), and its lifted leavers carry the lift
   into ah's group, which climbs from 9.2 to 12.4 (exploration 2).
3. v3 pays for its lift with members: 0.39 of them leave after round 3
   (0.44 of the punished) against 0.275 under zero. After that the
   flows are close to zero's (exploration 4).
4. A round-0 punishment of a 0.6-0.8x laggard lifts its contribution by
   +0.46 / +1.16 / +1.70 / +2.12 for <=5 / 5-12 / 12-22 / >22 points,
   flat to round 23, at a cost of 1 / 4 / 7 / 12 points of staying.
   At-mean members gain ~+0.3 at most. Each point the others get
   lifts a member by +0.05-0.07 (herding) (exploration 3).
5. Deep laggards gain about twice as much per point as mild ones
   (+0.06-0.09 vs +0.02-0.04 per round-1 point), and a round-1 point
   buys about a quarter of a round-0 point at twice the exit cost
   (explorations 3, 5).
6. Across v3's points, round-0 points to deep (<0.5x) laggards go with
   +4.7 pool per point, those to 0.5-0.8x laggards with -3.9. The
   within-phase rounds are about neutral for laggards, and points to
   >=0.8x members are a loss in every round class (exploration 6).

## Hypothesis

A good manager on this stack sets the norm early and then leaves the
group alone, as v2 and v3 found. Within that, the size of the dose
matters more than the timing. A deep laggard punished hard right after
a decision lifts for the rest of the game, and its herding pulls the
others up. A mild laggard gains little per point, and every point
raises its chance of leaving, so a large dose for it costs more in
exits and points than it returns. The within-phase rounds buy far less
than the fresh rounds. So the dose should rise steeply (convexly) with
how far short a member falls. It should be concentrated in rounds 0,
4 and 8, and the round before a decision and everything after the
norm is set should be left alone.

The rule: s = clamp((`rel` x mean of the other valid members' c - c) /
(`rel` x that mean), 0, 1); dose = `top` x s^`power`, x 1 in rounds 0,
4, 8, ..., x `mid` in rounds 1-2, 5-6, 9-10, ..., x 0 in rounds 3, 7,
11, ...; nothing after `last_round`; clamped to 0-30 by the manager.

- `rel` (float, [0.6, 1.05]): the laggard threshold as a fraction of
  the others' mean. I expect 0.8-0.95. v3 was flat at 0.75-0.9, and a
  convex dose makes the margin of the threshold cheap, which may push
  it up a little.
- `top` (float, [10, 100] log): the fresh-round dose for a member who
  gives nothing. Above 30 the dose saturates for deep laggards. I
  expect 40-90. v3's best corresponds to ~80 at power 1.
- `power` (float, [0.4, 3] log): convexity of the dose in the relative
  shortfall. I expect 1.3-2.2. 1 is v3's linear shape, and below 1 is
  kept so the sweep can reject the idea.
- `mid` (float, [0, 1]): the within-phase dose relative to the fresh
  round. I expect 0.1-0.4. v3 sat at 0.37, findings 5-6 say lower,
  and 0 (fresh rounds only) is now reachable.
- `last_round` (int, 4..14): the last round with punishment. I expect
  8-11 (v3 peak 10).

### Justification

Findings 4, 5 and 6 are the case for `power`. The lift per point is
largest for deep laggards, and for mild ones the exit cost (finding 3:
punished members are what drives v3's extra leavers) overtakes the
lift at a moderate dose. That calls for a dose that rises faster than
the shortfall, with the clamp at 30 (reached through `top`) bounding
the deep end. Finding 5's round contrast is the case for letting `mid`
go to 0. Finding 1 is why v4 changes the shape rather than retuning
v3, and why the dead `cap` and `newcomer` are gone. Findings 2 and 3
keep the early stop and the empty pre-decision round: the lift lasts
without a round input to undo it, and late punishment only adds exits.
Finding 4's at-mean result keeps `rel`'s top near 1.

What would contradict it: `power` peaking at or below 1 would mean the
mild laggards' herding contribution (finding 4's +0.05-0.07 per point
for everyone else) outweighs their exits, or that my per-depth
contrasts are an artefact of regression to the mean among the lowest
contributors. `mid` peaking near 1 would mean that repeated pressure in
the first phase matters more than the per-point numbers suggest.

Not modelled: separate doses for round 0 and rounds 4 and 8 (the
regression's classes are too collinear to support it), a late
maintenance dose (v3's `newcomer` and the late-response numbers in
exploration 3 found nothing worth a parameter), punishment history and
the other group's state. The player-level numbers come from the rule's
own choices across the design, so they are close to design-driven, but
the depth classes still differ in who the players are. That is why
`power`'s range spans below 1.
