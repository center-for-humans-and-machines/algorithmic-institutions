## Explorations

1. **What does the score reward, and how do `ah` and `zero` fare against `ah`?** Read
   `src/aimanager/simulation/pool_scores.py` and ran
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-8/ref_sims.py on the reference sims. The score is the
   group *sum* of 1.6c - p per round (not per member; the `common_good` column is per member),
   so group size enters linearly. `zero` (never punishes) leads `ah` by 13.97 (se 1.44): it
   holds 4.57 members against 3.43, at ~8.3 contribution per member against ah's ~9.0 with
   ~1.5 punishment per member. Per member, ah's punishment buys +0.7 contribution (+1.1 pool)
   at 1.5 points of punishment, so it is net negative even before the exodus it causes. So the
   baseline to beat is ~14, and the main lever is membership; next I want to see in the human
   data how contributions respond to punishment by contribution level, and what makes players
   switch.
2. **How do human contributions and switches respond to punishment?** Ran
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-8/human.py on the deduplicated human data (9600 rows).
   Human managers punished mostly low contributors (mean 3.3 at c=0, 0.26 at c=20). Next-round
   change in c: a (0,5] contributor rises +0.08 unpunished but +1.5 (p 3-8) and +3.1 (p>8)
   when punished; a 20-contributor drops -1.8 unpunished but -6 to -15 when punished at all.
   So punishment works only on low contributors, and punishing high contributors is
   disastrous. Switching (28.6% per decision) rises with own mean punishment over the last 4
   rounds (19% at 0 to 58% above 6) and with the group's mean punishment (74% above 6); a
   linear probability model gives ~+2.4pp per point of own and +2.7pp per point of group
   punishment, -2.2pp per point of group mean contribution, -3pp per extra member. No model
   weights are in the container (artifacts hold only copula json), so I cannot simulate; next
   I check whether the artificial humans in the reference sims show the same responses.
3. **Do the artificial humans respond the same way?** Ran
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-8/ah_response.py on the reference sims (768k rows).
   `ah` punishes like the humans (3.75 at c=0, 0.36 at c=20). AH next-round dc for a (0,5]
   contributor: +0.50 unpunished, +1.06 at p 1-3, +1.58 at p 4-8, +2.29 at p>8; at c=0 +1.56
   vs +2.95 at p>8. So the response exists but is about half the humans' (and confounded with
   ah's targeting). High contributors drop more when punished (-1.8 -> -3.8 at c=20). Switching:
   31% per decision under ah vs 24% under zero; own 4-round mean punishment 0 -> 22%, >6 ->
   61%; LPM: +2.0pp per point of own and +2.1pp per point of group-mean punishment, -1.3pp per
   point of group mean contribution, -4.3pp per extra member. Under zero, 13% of
   contributions are 0 and 31% in (0,5]. So a punished low contributor gives at most ~+1.5-2
   contribution (+2.4-3.2 pool) next round for a cost of ~8+ points now plus a higher exodus
   risk for the whole group. That only pays if the rise persists for several rounds; next I
   check persistence and the spillover of others' punishment onto a player's contribution.
4. **Does the rise persist, and do others' punishments spill over?** Ran
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-8/persistence.py on the ah-managed groups, rows at rounds
   0,4,8,12,16 (so the next 3 rounds are in the same group). A (0,3] contributor punished 5-10
   is up +2.36/+3.03/+2.98 after 1/2/3 rounds vs +1.12/+1.31/+1.55 unpunished, so the gap
   (~1.3-1.7) persists at least 3 rounds. The response is concave: a light punishment (0,2]
   already buys +0.4-0.6, i.e. per point the first points are the most efficient. An OLS of
   next-round dc gives only +0.07 per point of own punishment for c<=6 (~0 for c>6), +0.016
   per point of others' mean punishment (no deterrence spillover worth paying for), and +0.24
   per point of others' mean contribution (strong herding). Rough book: a 1-2 point
   punishment of a free rider buys ~0.5 contribution = 0.8 pool for ~3 rounds (2.4) for 1.5
   points; a 10 point punishment buys ~2 contribution x 3 rounds = ~10 pool for 10 points, i.e.
   only light punishment of the lowest contributors could beat never punishing, and only if
   the exodus it causes is small. Next: switching of low contributors and inflow from the
   other group.
5. **Who leaves, and does our punishment deter newcomers?** Ran
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-8/switching.py. Under ah, a low contributor (4-round mean
   c <= 2) leaves 32.8% unpunished, 34.1% at a 4-round mean punishment of (0,1], 41.7% at (1,2],
   46.9% at (2,4], 58.4% above 4. So a mean punishment under ~1 per round barely moves exit,
   while more does. Zero-group players leave *less* when ah's group punishes more (27.3% at 0
   down to 15.8% when ah's last-round mean punishment is above 4): players compare groups, so
   our own punishment would also cut the inflow from ah's group. The first decision (after
   round 3) is by far the biggest flow: 46% leave ah and 31% leave zero, which is where zero
   wins most of its member lead (4.9 vs 3.1 at round 4). Every member, even a low
   contributor, is worth keeping: losing a c~3 member to ah costs us ~4.8 pool a round and
   ah gains about that minus the ~3 it then punishes him. So the core of a good rule is
   "punish (almost) never", with any punishment light, aimed at the lowest contributors, and
   kept away from the first decision. Next: does the timing of a punishment within a 4-round
   block matter for exit?
6. **Does the timing of a punishment within a 4-round block matter for exit?** Ran
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-8/recency.py: an LPM of the switch at decisions 7-19
   (ah groups, players who stayed the whole block) on own punishment at each lag. Own
   punishment in the decision round itself adds +0.97pp per point, one round earlier +0.77,
   two +0.44, three (the block's first round) only +0.15; others' punishment +0.45/+0.39/
   +0.39/+0.21. At the first decision (round 3) others' punishment weighs more (+1.5pp per
   point in round 3). So a punishment dealt in the first round of a block costs ~6x less
   exit than one dealt just before the decision, while its contribution gain (entry 4) lasts
   the whole block; and after round 19 nobody can leave. Drafted the rule: punish members
   whose contribution is at or below a threshold, a flat light amount (the response is
   concave, entry 4), only in the early rounds of each block, never before a start round
   (to keep the first, biggest decision clean), never in the last two rounds (no rounds
   left to recoup it), and only valid players (a timed-out one is charged 0 anyway).

   shortfall-scaled amount and a separate end-game amount because the expected gains from

## Key findings

1. The score sums 1.6c - p over the group's members, so membership enters linearly; never
   punishing (`zero`) already leads `ah` by 13.97 (se 1.44), on 4.57 vs 3.43 members, while
   ah's punishment buys +0.7 contribution per member for 1.5 points (exploration 1).
2. Punishment raises next-round contributions only of low contributors and hurts high ones:
   in the artificial humans a (0,5] contributor goes +0.50 unpunished vs +1.06 at p 1-3 and
   +2.29 above 8; a 20-contributor -1.8 vs -3.8 (explorations 2, 3; humans the same, stronger).
3. The rise persists at least 3 rounds (gap ~1.3-1.7 for a 5-10 point punishment) and is
   concave: a 1-2 point punishment already buys +0.4-0.6. There is no deterrence spillover
   to others (+0.016 per point) but strong herding (+0.24 per point of others' mean c)
   (exploration 4).
4. Exit rises with punishment: a low contributor leaves 32.8% unpunished, 34.1% at a mean of
   (0,1] per round, 46.9% at (2,4]; players also flee *toward* the less punishing group (27%
   -> 16% leaving zero's group as ah punishes more); the first decision is the largest flow
   (exploration 5).
5. Timing matters for exit: own punishment in the decision round adds ~1pp per point to the
   switch, in the block's first round only 0.15pp; after round 19 nobody can leave
   (exploration 6).

## Hypothesis

A good manager here is mainly a magnet: membership is worth far more than any contribution
punishment can buy, so it punishes almost never. What it can add over never punishing is a
light, flat nudge to the lowest contributors, dealt where it costs the least exit: right
after a switch decision (so it fades before the next one), not in the first rounds that feed
the big first decision, and in rounds 20-21 when nobody can leave anymore; never in the last
two rounds, where it cannot pay back. The rule:

- `thr` (int, [0, 8]): a valid member contributing `thr` or less this round is a free rider.
- `amount` (float, [0.8, 12] log): the flat punishment of a free rider; below 1 it floors to
  0, so the low end reproduces `zero`.
- `last_pos` (int, [0, 3]): the latest position in a 4-round block (0 = first round after a
  decision, 3 = the decision round) in which it punishes; 3 removes the timing restriction.
- `start` (int, [0, 12]): the first round it may punish in.

I expect the best values at `amount` 1-3, `thr` 0-3, `last_pos` 0-1 and `start` 4-8.

### Justification

Finding 1 makes membership the main lever and sets the bar (~14 lead) a punishing rule must
beat; it is why `amount` reaches down to an effective 0, so the sweep can fall back on
never punishing. Findings 2 and 3 justify punishing only low contributors (`thr`) and with a
flat, light amount: the first points buy the most and high contributors only fall when
punished; `thr` up to 8 covers the 44% of zero-group contributions at 5 or less plus some
margin. Findings 4 and 5 justify the timing: `last_pos` lets the sweep confine punishment to
the start of each block, where it costs ~6x less exit than just before a decision, and
`start` up to 12 lets it keep the first one to three decisions clean; the hard-coded
free rounds 20-21 and silent rounds 22-23 follow from no exit after round 19 and no time to
recoup at the end. `amount` up to 12 is wide on purpose: the persistence numbers in 3 say a
heavy punishment of a zero-contributor roughly breaks even on contributions, and I cannot
rule out that the artificial humans reward it more than my confounded estimates show. What
would contradict the hypothesis: a sweep peak at high `amount` with `last_pos` 3 and `start`
0 (punishing everywhere pays), or a flat landscape whose best points all have `amount` < 1
(any punishment loses, `zero` is optimal). Not modelled: punishment relative to the group's
mean (herding pulls toward it, but the absolute threshold already picks the lowest
contributors in most groups), a shortfall-scaled amount and a separate end-game amount
(gains below the noise, exploration 7), and pushing free riders into ah's group (a leaving
member costs us his contribution, more than ah loses by punishing him, exploration 5).
