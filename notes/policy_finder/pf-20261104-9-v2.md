## Explorations

1. **Where did version 1 land?** Read v1's sweep.json, landscapes.json and

2. **What separates good and bad design points in v1's sweep?** Loaded
   both per_round parquets into dense arrays [point, episode, player,
   round] (scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-9/v1_sweep_load.py; the rule

   (v1_sweep_points.py). The rebuilt pool matches sweep.json exactly.
   Across the 256 points the pool correlates with contribution per member

3. **How do the artificial humans respond to my punishment?** Members of

   Leaving at the next decision (rounds 3, 7, ... only): 0.28 unpunished

4. **How long does the lift last, and does it spill over?**
   v1_sweep_persist.py: laggards (< 0.75x the others' mean) unpunished the
   round before, punished or not at round t0, change in c after k rounds
   for stayers. At t0 = 1: unpunished +1.1 (k=1) -> +1.8 (k=12);
   punished 4-8: +3.6 -> +5.7; 9-14: +4.6 -> +7.9; 15-30: +5.5 -> +10.8.
   Same at t0 = 5 and 9. The lift does not decay over 12 rounds; it grows
   (partly because the same rule keeps punishing them, so this is an
   upper bound on a single punishment's effect). Spillover on unpunished
   members is small: at 0.75-1.0x the mean, 0.0 whatever is dealt to
   others; above 1.5x, -2.0 with nothing dealt to others vs -2.9 with > 6
   per other member (some of this is regression to the mean that heavy
   punishers cause by lifting the laggards). So a punishment point dealt
   early buys ~0.3-0.5 contribution points for the rest of the game, i.e.
   ~0.5-0.8 pool points per round for ~20 rounds: easily 10x its cost in
   round 1, far less in round 20. Next: what punishment costs in
   switching, and when in a phase it costs least.

5. **What does punishment cost in switching, and when in a phase?**
   v1_sweep_switch.py, decisions after rounds 3, 7, 11, 15, 19. Base
   leave rate from my group 0.237 (from ah's 0.331). Leaving rises with
   the punishment a member got in each round of the phase, but much more
   for recent rounds: punished in only one round of the phase (mean ~3
   points), leave 0.232 if it was the phase's first round (0, 4, 8, ...)
   vs 0.253 / 0.268 / 0.284 for the 2nd / 3rd / 4th round, against 0.219
   never punished. With 15-30 points: 0.41 in the first round vs 0.52 in
   the last. The switch model forgets: a punishment right after a
   decision barely moves the next decision. Unpunished members leave more
   when the group's mean punishment is high (0.22 up to 2 per
   member-round, 0.26 at 2-4, 0.36 above 4), and ah's unpunished members
   join less (0.27 at 0-0.5 down to 0.16 above 4). Heavy-punishing points
   lose their low contributors (leave 0.37 vs 0.27 for c <= 3), not their
   high ones (0.17 either way). So: timing within the phase is a nearly
   free lever. Punishing right after a switch buys the same lasting lift
   at a fraction of the exit cost, and gives the laggard three rounds of
   contributing at the higher level before the next decision. v1's
   pre_switch only scaled round 3; the sweep put it at 0.4, which agrees.
   Next: draft v2.

6. **Is the human-data warning about punishing average contributors true

## Key findings

1. On this stack punishment pays: v1's final result is 67.1 +- 1.0
   against ~58-61 for its near-zero corner, and across v1's 256 points
   the pool tracks contribution per member (r = 0.63), not headcount
   (r = -0.04) (explorations 1, 2).
2. The artificial humans' lift from a punishment rises with its size up
   to 30, with diminishing returns: +2.5 to +5.4 points after 3 rounds
   for near-zero contributors at 1-30 points, against +1.4 unpunished
   (exploration 3).
3. The lift does not fade. Laggards punished 9-14 in round 1 are +4.6
   after 1 round and +7.9 after 12 (unpunished +1.1 -> +1.8), and the top
   points' contributions keep rising after they stop punishing. This is
   why v1's last_round had no effect (explorations 2, 4).
4. The switch model weighs recent punishment most. A punishment in the
   round just after a decision barely raises leaving (0.232 vs 0.219
   never punished), one in the round just before raises it most (0.284).
   With 15-30 points, 0.63 of laggards are still in the group 4 rounds
   later after a first-round punishment, against 0.51 after a last-round
   one (explorations 5, 8).
5. Expected kept contribution (share staying x (c + lift)) is about
   equal for punishments in positions 0-2 of a phase and clearly worst at
   position 3. So the timing is two points, not a gradient (exploration
   8).
6. In the sim, punishing at- or above-average members does not backfire
   (-0.3 vs -0.65 unpunished at 1.0-1.25x), but it buys little. The
   laggard lift is about the same at any group level, so there is no case
   for an absolute floor (exploration 6).
7. Heavy group-wide punishment (> 4 per member-round) drives unpunished
   members out (leave 0.36 vs 0.22) and keeps ah's members from joining
   (0.16 vs 0.27) (exploration 5).

## Hypothesis

A good manager on this stack sanctions laggards, judged against their own
group's current contributions, firmly and early. The artificial humans'
contributions rise with the punishment and stay up for the rest of the
game, so an early punishment point buys contributions for many rounds.
What punishment costs, beyond its points, is exits, and the switch model
weighs recent punishment most. So the manager should hit laggards right
after a switch decision (rounds 0, 4, 8, ...), when newcomers have just
arrived and the next decision is furthest off, and go easy in the round
just before a decision. It stops when too few rounds remain for the lift
to repay the points. It never punishes the at- or above-average members,
because that buys little and the group's mean punishment drives
unpunished members away.

The rule: shortfall = max(0, `rel` x mean contribution of the other valid
members this round - own contribution); punishment = min(`cap`, `slope` x
shortfall) x `fresh` in rounds 0, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, x `pre_switch` in
rounds 3, 7, 11, 15, 19, x 1 otherwise, and 0 after `last_round`.

- `rel` (float, [0.4, 1.2]): the laggard threshold as a fraction of the
  others' mean. I expect 0.8-0.95 (v1 peaked at 0.83 and lost ~2 points
  at 0.5). The range goes past 1 because the sim does not punish that
  (finding 6).
- `slope` (float, [0.7, 8] log): punishment per point of shortfall. I
  expect 2-4 (v1 peaked at 2.5 and fell past 3.6). It is widened upward
  in case a lower `pre_switch` needs a higher base.
- `cap` (float, [6, 30]): the most a member gets in one round before the
  timing multiplier. I expect 14-25: v1's profile was still flat at its
  top of 16, and the sim's response keeps rising to 30 (finding 2).
- `fresh` (float, [0.5, 3] log): the multiplier right after a decision.
  I expect 1.3-2.5 (findings 4, 5). Below 1 is kept so the sweep can
  reject the idea.
- `pre_switch` (float, [0, 1.5]): the multiplier just before a decision.
  I expect 0.2-0.6 (v1 peaked at 0.44; finding 4).
- `last_round` (int, 8..23): the last round with punishment. I expect
  20-22 by the end-game arithmetic (exploration 8), but the surface may
  again be flat (finding 3), and 8-11 lets it test an early stop.

### Justification

Findings 1-3 justify punishing at all, and firmly: the response rises up
to 30 points and lasts, which sets `slope` and the widened `cap`.
Finding 6 keeps the relative threshold `rel` (no floor) with its top
near 1. Findings 4 and 5 give the two timing multipliers: punishment is
cheapest in exits right after a decision and dearest just before one,
and positions 1-2 are worth about the same as each other, which is why
v2 has two multipliers and not a geometric ease (my first draft,
exploration 7). Finding 7 is why the threshold stays relative and the
cap is not set at 30 outright: punishing too many members too hard
makes the group's mean payoff, which players compare, fall.

What would contradict it: `fresh` peaking below 1, which would mean the
lift from a fresh-round punishment (slightly smaller, exploration 8)
matters more than retention. `pre_switch` at the top of its range would
contradict finding 4. A peak at `last_round` 8-11 would say the lift
from early punishment is all the group needs. Caveat: the persistence
numbers are upper bounds, since the same rule keeps punishing the same
players.

Not modelled: punishment history (repeat offenders, the other manager's
punishments), the other group's state, and an absolute contribution
floor (finding 6). Concave or convex shapes of punishment in the
shortfall are also left out, because `slope` and `cap` together already
give a linear-then-flat shape.
