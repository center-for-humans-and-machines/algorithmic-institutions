## Key findings

1. All versions' finals play the same games in the GP peak's slot (seeded per slot), so they are
   paired: per game the peaks' leads correlate 0.87-0.95, v3 - v4 = +0.22 +- 0.48. The slot-1
   seeds are a favourable draw (all peaks 17-19, all other final points 13-17), and v5's peak will
   play them too: beating v3's 19.17 needs a real gain, not luck (exploration 1).
2. The sweeps share seeds per slot across versions too: 4 versions x 256 slots, slot effects sd
   2.66 against a residual 0.92; slot-adjusting cuts v4's point-to-point sd from 2.90 to 1.07
   (explorations 2-4).
3. Ex ante (slot-adjusted v4 params): D4 is the one clear lever, +0.68 - 0.11 x^2 per 10 points,
   still rising at the cap of 30 (~+1.0 there); D0 ~+0.4 at ~20; D8 null; D12 -0.26 per 10; hi
   best at 5-6 (+0.8..+0.9 over hi 2, 8-9 at -0.7..-0.8). The best v4-family point is only ~+0.5
   above v4's peak (exploration 4).
4. In game pairs identical until round 4, a punished stayer leaves my group by round 8 2-3x more
   often than a punished newcomer (+4.8..+6.3 vs +1.8..+2.6 percentage points), while both raise
   their contribution alike (+0.4..+0.6 for the rest of the game); per 10 points the lead gains
   +0.46 / +0.12 on newcomers giving <=5 vs +0.20 / -0.32 on stayers (explorations 5, 6).
5. Targets giving 3-5 answer best (+0.6 vs +0.45 contribution) and pay best within a round;
   targets above 5 lose; sparing the 0-2 contributors gains nothing ex ante (v2's lo: 14.36,
   14.24, 14.19) (explorations 5, 6).
6. Punishment from round 12 on loses (round 12 -0.24..-0.27 per 10 points, 16 ~-1.0, 20 the
   points' cost), as in v3's and v4's notes (explorations 4, 5).

## Hypothesis

On this stack the score is mostly membership, and a shock buys a lasting contribution habit at
the price of some exits; every exit both shrinks my group and carries the trained habit into
`ah`'s pool. A good manager therefore shocks the low contributors hard at the starts of the first
blocks, above all right after the first switch decision, and aims that shock at the members whom
it costs the fewest exits: the newcomers who just arrived, who answer as well as stayers but leave
far less often over it. Stayers get a reduced share of the dose (possibly none), the round-0
shock and round 8 are left to the sweep, and nothing is dealt from round 9 on. The rule: in round
0 every member of my group who timed in, is not alone and gave at most `hi` gets D0; in rounds 4
and 8 such a member gets D4 (D8) if they were not in my group the round before, s x D4 (s x D8)
if they were; nothing in any other round.

- `D0` (float, [0, 30] uniform): round-0 shock. Expect 10-25, small effect.
- `D4` (float, [10, 30] uniform): round-4 shock to a newcomer. Expect 22-30.
- `D8` (float, [0, 30] uniform): round-8 shock to a newcomer. Expect anywhere, maybe 10-25.
- `s` (float, [0, 1] uniform): stayers' share of the round-4/8 dose. Expect 0-0.5.
- `hi` (int, 3..8): highest contribution targeted. Expect 5-6.

### Justification

Finding 3 sets the frame (v4's family, round 4 the lever, D4 to the cap) and finding 6 drops round
12, which v4 still spent ~0.4 on. Finding 4 is the new parameter: the shock's contribution gain is
the same on newcomers and stayers but its exit cost is 2-3x higher on stayers, so s lets the sweep
shift the dose toward newcomers; s=1 is v4's rule, so the design contains v4's best region and
the sweep can show if the split is noise. D4's range starts at 10 because v4's 0-10 bin was its
worst and the ex-ante slope is positive to 30; its top is the manager's cap. D0 and D8 keep the
full [0, 30] because the evidence on them conflicts (ex ante ~+0.4 and null; in the pairs
negative), and at round 8 sparing stayers may change the sign. hi spans 3..8 around the 5-6
optimum (finding 5; 2 and 9+ lose clearly in v3/v4). What would contradict it: a sweep with s
near 1 at its peak, or `members` not rising as s falls (the exit difference in finding 4 is
from game pairs that keep differing after round 4, so it may be partly the later policy). Not
modelled: sparing zero contributors (finding 5: nothing ex ante), the group's size (its splits
in the pairs are confounded by membership), newcomer weighting in round 0 (everyone is new
there), and rounds 12+ (finding 6). Main risk: the gain is likely ~0.5-1 pool points, near the
paired final's resolution (+-0.5); finding 1 means that comparison will at least be paired.
