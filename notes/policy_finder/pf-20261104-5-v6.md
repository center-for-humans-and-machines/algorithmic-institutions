# pf-20261104-5-v6

## Explorations

1. **Where did v5 end up?** Read v5's `final/sweep.json` and
   `landscapes.json` and compared with v4's. v5's surface peak (k=6.0,
   thr=10.3, w=0.48, d=0.73, t_stop=14) scored 70.2 (se 1.09) on fresh
   seeds; its other final points 66.9-69.3. v4's peak scored 71.3 (se
   1.07) and its s168 72.7 (se 1.15). So v5 did not beat v4: the bar is
   still v4's ~71.3. v5's profiles are all interior and gentle: k 67.3 at
   2, 70.9 at 5.5, 65.9 at 30; thr 66.5 at 6, 70.9 at 10, 61.4 at 14; w
   68.5 at 0, 70.9 at 0.5, 68.2 at 1; d 69.2 at 0.15, 71.0 at 0.73, 69.1
   at 1.3; t_stop 68.1 at 3, 70.9 at 13-15, 68.7 at 23. d's effect is 1.9
   pool points, i.e. the block decay bought nothing measurable. The
   sweep's top points (500 episodes, se ~2.2) all sit at 72-73.5 and fall
   back to 67-69 on fresh seeds: winner's curse of ~3-4 points.
   So five versions of "graded push on the shortfall below ~10, timed by
   block" sit on one plateau at 67-72. Retuning the dose, its timing
   over blocks or within a block, or a per-player taper does not move it.
   What none of them read is the *state* of the game: group size, the
   other group, how the group as a whole is doing. Next: in v4+v5's
   sweeps, what separates good episodes from bad under the same rule.
2. **What separates good from bad episodes?** Built a per-game table of
   v5's sweep (128k games, both parts) with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v6_episodes.py. The score's sd
   across games is 48.7 against ~2 between nearby design points. Our size
   in block 1 (rounds 4-7) is spread almost uniformly over 0-8: 13% of
   games have 0 members, 17% all 8. The score is 27 when n1=0, 58 at 3, 77
   at 5, 96 at 8; the mean size over blocks 1-5 alone explains 51% of the
   score's variance across games. So the first switch is a mass
   migration, not the trickle of 0.3-0.5 members I modelled in v3-v5,
   and who wins it decides most of the score. That surprised me; v1's
   "members 4.3 vs 4.6" hid it in the mean.
3. **What drives the migration?** Per game and decision (v5 sweep part 1)
   with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v6_migr.py: our size change
   dn against each group's per-member pool share (1.6*sum c - sum p)/n.
   At the round-3 decision the block-average share gap (ours - theirs)
   alone explains R2 = 0.54 of dn (slope 0.19 members per share point);
   the decision round's gap alone 0.45; our or ah's punishment add nothing
   once the gap is in (pblk -0.01, pdec +0.004 per 10 points). The curve
   is a steep sigmoid: dn = -2.9 at gap < -20, -2.3 at -8..-6, -1.0 at
   -4..-2, +0.3 at -1..0, +1.0 at 0..1, +2.0 at 2..4, +2.7 at 6..8, +2.9
   above 12. Half the swing happens within +-2 share points. Later
   decisions follow the same gap with a flatter slope (0.08 per point at
   rounds 7, 11, 15, R2 ~0.2, plus regression to the middle, -0.3 per
   member above). The gap's sd is 10.9 at round 3, 7.8 of it from
   contributions and 5.2 from punishment; v5's rules punish 83 points in
   block 0 against ah's 52, so our share is 1.9 points lower on average
   from punishment.
   So the first block is a race for members on per-member pool share,
   and every punishment point in it is a share point lost
   (1/(4n) of a point of block-average share each), partly bought back by
   the lift in contributions. The cost is state dependent: where the race
   is close a few share points move ~0.5 members each; where it is won or
   lost by a wide margin they move almost nothing. No version so far reads
   that state.
4. **Is the cost of block-0 punishment state dependent in the sweep?**
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v6_race.py. State: the round-0
   contribution gap between the groups (set before any punishment, sd
   4.2). Treatment: the design's harshness (its mean block-0 punishment
   over its 500 games), controlling for our round-0 mean. Per 10 points of
   harshness, dn is -0.07 when we start 4+ ahead, -0.12 to -0.14 when
   within +-4, -0.07 when 4+ behind; the score effect is -0.04 when well
   ahead, -0.2 to -0.8 in the close bins, -0.37 well behind. The late
   contribution gain is +0.09 to +0.16 in every bin. (Splitting by the
   realised punishment instead is confounded by composition: a group with
   more low givers is both punished more and poorer later.) So the lift
   from a push is roughly the same everywhere, and the members it costs
   are about twice as many in a close race. A manager that pushes hard
   only when it can afford it in share terms should beat a fixed push.
5. **Drafted v6: a share guard on v5's dose.** Kept the graded dose
   min(30, k * max(0, thr - c_i)) * w^(t mod 4) for valid members while
   t <= t_stop, dropped d (effect 1.9, flat), and added a guard with one
   parameter m: each round the total punishment is cut (all members
   pro rata) so that our per-member share (1.6 sum c - sum p)/n, averaged
   over the current block's rounds up to this one, stays at least m above
   the other group's. Their punishment this round is unknown, so I take
   their last round's (0 in round 0). Allowed points =
   n_now * (sum of the block's share gaps so far - m * rounds so far).
   Very negative m switches the guard off (v5 without d); positive m
   keeps a safety lead. Checked with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/test_rule_v6.py: with k=6, thr=10,
   w=0.5, ah charging 3 a head, a group giving 0/4/7/11 against 10/10/12/8
   gets 30/30/18 with m=-100, nothing in rounds 0-4 with m=0, and 0 then
   2.5-3.8 a head with m=-5; a group giving 0/15/15/11 against 5/5/6/8
   keeps its full 30 on the 0-giver at m=0 or -5; equal groups get 10 a
   head at m=-5 in round 0 instead of 30.
6. **Rough replay: how much would the guard cut, and is the lift lost
   bigger than the members saved?**
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v6_guard_replay.py replays
   v5's block-0 gaps (part 1) at the block level. Near v5's peak
   (k 4-9, thr 9.3-11.3, w 0.25-0.75) m=-8 cuts in 21% of games
   (16 of 77 points), m=-4 in 33% (26), m=0 in 49% (40), m=+4 in 65%
   (54). Read off the empirical dn(gap) curve, the members saved are
   +0.04 / +0.18 / +0.61 / +0.99 at m = -8 / -4 / 0 / +4 if the cut
   punishment had bought no lift within the block, but only +0.006 /
   +0.03 / +0.12 / +0.24 if each point buys back 0.045 share points
   (v5's 0.15 contribution per round-0 point over rounds 1-3). So the
   guard's value hangs on how much of the share cost the lift repays
   inside the block, which this replay cannot settle.
7. **Marginal value of block-0 punishment at v5's peak level, by race
   state.** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-5/v6_race2.py: per bin
   of the round-0 contribution gap (exogenous), score, dn and late
   contribution on the design's harshness h (its mean block-0
   punishment) with a quadratic, slope read at h=77 points (v5's
   level), controlling for our round-0 mean. Per 10 points:
   - gap < -4: score -0.15 (se 0.10), dn -0.09
   - -4..-2: -0.51 (0.12), dn -0.14
   - -2..-0.5: -0.60 (0.13), dn -0.17
   - -0.5..0.5: -0.30 (0.16), dn -0.15
   - 0.5..2: +0.03 (0.14), dn -0.14
   - 2..4: +0.01 (0.13), dn -0.11
   - > 4: +0.20 (0.13), dn -0.05
   The late contribution gain is +0.14 to +0.19 in every bin. So at
   v5's dose the marginal push already loses where we start level or a
   little behind, and would still pay where we start well ahead. That is
   the state dependence a fixed dose cannot use, and it fits the guard:
   cut when the share race is close or slightly lost, allow (and with a
   higher k, push harder) when ahead. Over the bins this is worth roughly
   +1 score point if the guard cuts ~40 points in the ~45% of games that
   start level or behind, before any gain from a harder push when ahead.
   It is not a big lever on paper, but it is the first one with a sign
   that changes with something the rule can see.
8. **Set the ranges and validated.** m: [-15, 5]. At -15 the guard
   rarely binds (exploration 6: at -12 it cuts in ~15% of games), which
   is a v5-without-d baseline inside the design. At +5 it cuts in ~2/3
   of games. In round 0 the guard sees only contributions (ah's round-0
   punishment, ~3 share points, is not yet visible), so an even start
   allows 4*(-m) points: 20 at m=-5, 40 at m=-10. I expect the best m at
   -8 to -2. thr widened slightly to [6.5, 13.5] around v5's 10.3; k
   stays [2, 30] log because the guard may let the sweep push harder
   where it is ahead; w [0, 1] and t_stop [3, 23] as in v5. The validator
   passes on all 256 design points.

## Key findings

1. v5 (block decay d) scored 70.2 (se 1.1) on fresh seeds, below v4's
   71.3; d's effect was 1.9 points. Five versions of a fixed graded
   push sit on one plateau at 67-72 (exploration 1).
2. The first switch is a mass migration: our block-1 size is spread
   almost uniformly over 0-8, the score runs from 27 (n=0) to 96 (n=8),
   and mean size over blocks 1-5 explains 51% of the score's variance
   across games (exploration 2).
3. Migration at the round-3 decision follows the block-average
   per-member pool-share gap between the groups (R2 0.54, 0.19 members
   per share point), as a steep sigmoid: half the ~6-member swing lies
   within +-2 share points; punishment adds nothing once the gap is in
   (exploration 3).
4. The membership cost of block-0 punishment is about twice as large in
   a close race as in a won or lost one (dn -0.12 to -0.14 vs -0.07 per
   10 points of design harshness), while the late contribution lift is
   the same everywhere (exploration 4).
5. At v5's dose the marginal block-0 push is worth +0.20 score per 10
   points when we start 4+ contribution points ahead, ~0 when 0.5-4
   ahead, and -0.3 to -0.6 when level or 0.5-4 behind (exploration 7).
6. How much the guard gains depends on how much of a punishment's share
   cost its within-block lift repays: from +0.6 members at m=0 if none
   to +0.1 if 70% (exploration 6).

## Hypothesis

In this simulation the first block is a race for members on per-member
pool share, and the winner takes most of the population. Punishment lifts
contributions for good, but it is paid out of the share that decides the
race. So a good manager makes the push conditional on standing. When its
group is clearly ahead on share it pushes hard, because the punishment
costs no members there. When the race is close or slightly lost it holds
back, because there a few share points decide whether several players
leave or join. The rule keeps v5's graded, block-timed dose, drops d, and
caps each round's total by a share guard. The dose is
min(30, k * max(0, thr - c_i)) * w^(t mod 4) for valid members while
t <= t_stop. The round's total is scaled down to at most
n * (sum of block share gaps so far - m * rounds so far).

- `k` (float, [2, 30], log): punishment points per contribution point
  below thr, before the 30 cap. I expect 5-15, possibly above v5's 6,
  since the guard spares the close games.
- `thr` (float, [6.5, 13.5]): the contribution from which a member is
  safe. I expect 9.5-11.
- `w` (float, [0, 1]): the weight per round of distance from the block's
  first round. I expect 0.3-0.7, as in v5.
- `t_stop` (int, [3, 23]): the last punishing round. I expect 10-16.
- `m` (float, [-15, 5]): the share lead, in pool points per member, below
  which the guard cuts punishment. I expect -8 to -2.

### Justification

Findings 2 and 3 say members decide the score and the round-3 share gap
decides members. Every punishment point lowers our share, so the push has
a membership price that the earlier versions paid blindly. Finding 4
shows this price depends on the race state, and finding 5 shows the sign
of the marginal push flips with it. The fixed dose of v1-v5 is then too
hard in close or slightly lost games and too soft in won ones. That is
also why retuning it stalled (finding 1).

- `m` is the guard's single knob. Its range runs from almost never
  binding (-15, a v5-like baseline) to binding in two thirds of games
  (+5).
- I expect a negative best m. Finding 6 says the within-block lift
  repays part of the share cost, and ah's round-0 punishment, which the
  guard cannot yet see in round 0, gives us ~3 share points on average.
- `k`, `thr`, `w` and `t_stop` keep v5's ranges, centred on their
  interior peaks. k keeps room above v5's peak in case the guard lets a
  harder push pay where we lead.
- d is dropped, because its effect was within noise.

The hypothesis is contradicted in two cases:
- The sweep's best m sits at -15. Then the guard never helps, and the
  lift repays the share within the block.
- The score stays at ~70-71. Then the state dependence of finding 5 is
  too small to use with a hard cap.

I chose not to model these:
- An override that punishes in hopeless races. Finding 5 puts the push
  there at -0.15 per 10 points, so cutting it costs nothing.
- Different margins per block. Later decisions follow the gap with less
  than half the slope, and by then the dose is small.
- An explicit forecast of ah's punishment. Its last round is used, and m
  absorbs the round-0 bias.
- Any reallocation of the cut punishment to later rounds.
