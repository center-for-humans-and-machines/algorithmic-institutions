# pf-20261106-7-v5

## Explorations

1. **Where did v4 end?** Looked at the four earlier final sweep.json files and v3's and v4's landscapes.json. Final pools: v1 65.5, v2 66.4, v3 67.4, v4 66.8 (v4: 69.1 / 67.4 / 71.4 / 59.4 against opponents 1-4, the same pattern as v3). v4's saturation budget was flat (effect 0.8, peak 107), and its decay peaked at the 0.2 edge (effect 2.7): with saturation added, the sweep wanted later blocks punished even less than v3 did. c0 (6.7) and k (6.8) still carry the surface, peak c0 9, k 11.4. So the shortfall-times-timing family has been tuned four times and sits at 67 ± 1, with each version's change inside about two standard errors of the final (se 0.53). I don't expect another timing or saturation tweak to move it. Next: pool all 1024 design points of v1-v4 (converted with v2_arrays.py to /tmp/v1..v4sweep.npz) and ask where the pool differences between points come from.

2. **Members or contributions?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-7/v5_decomp.py: per design point and opponent (1024 points x 4), the pool split into members, contribution per valid member and punishment per member. The pool's sd across points is ~5.3 against every opponent. Linearised, the members part has sd 4.3-5.1, the contribution part 7.9-9.0 and the punishment part 3.8-4.5, but members and contribution per member are strongly anti-correlated (-0.62 to -0.72), as are members and punishment (-0.77 to -0.82). Punishment buys contributions and sells members. The top points (pool 70-71.5) all hold ~4.1-4.3 members at 11-12.5 contribution. Against opponent 4 even the mean point has fewer members (3.80) and lower contributions (9.85 vs 10.2-11.4). Next: does the best amount of punishment differ by opponent?

3. **Does the best punishment level depend on the opponent?** v5_opp_optimum.py: pool against my mean punishment per member (ppm) across all points, per opponent. The quadratic optimum is ppm 1.65-1.79 against all four, and the binned pools form a broad plateau from 0.6 to 2.2 (within ~1 point) against every opponent. Each 0.3 ppm costs ~0.06-0.1 members and buys ~0.4-0.5 contribution per member. Even at ppm < 0.3 my group holds only 3.99 members against opponent 4 (4.57 against opponent 2, which punishes cooperators too). Correlations of point pools between opponents are only 0.33-0.41, i.e. mostly noise (se ~4.5 per point and opponent). So the total amount is settled and opponent-independent. If anything is left, it is in *whom* the punishment hits and *when* in a game, not how much. Next: within games, which features of a game state make a point of punishment pay more, e.g. group size, the group's mean, or the other group's contributions.
4. **Does the response to a punishment depend on how far the player sits below the rest of the group?** v5_ah_gap.py on 240k games pooled from the v1-v4 sweeps: my group's valid members at c <= 12, within cells of (round, own c, opponent, rounded leave-one-out mean of the other valid members, group size), with the sqrt-point slope interacted with the gap (others' mean - own c, mean 2.4, sd 4.8) and with group size. In round 0 the lift at t+3 is +0.51 per sqrt point, plus +0.058 per point of gap (se 0.0007). At t+8 it is +0.72, plus +0.038 per gap point. The exit per sqrt point is 0.036, minus 0.0013 per gap point. So at the same own contribution, a player 7 below the others responds about twice as strongly as one level with them (0.78 vs 0.43 at t+3), and leaves slightly less. In rounds 4 and 8-12 the gap interaction is small (+0.010-0.012 at t+3, ~0 at t+8), and a bigger group lifts a little less (-0.02 per member). This is the first heterogeneity I have found that is as large as the saturation of v4, and it matters most in round 0, the round that carries the rule. Next: does punishment spill over to the unpunished members? If it does, the total matters more than the allocation.

5. **Spillover to unpunished members.** v5_ah_spill.py, same pooled sample: my unpunished valid members, within cells of (round, own c, opponent, rounded others' mean, group size). Regressors: the points dealt to the other members of my group, and how many of them were punished. In round 0, an unpunished member's contribution rises by +0.03-0.04 per point dealt to others at t+3 and by +0.048 at t+8, for low and high contributors alike. It is +0.02-0.027 in rounds 4 and 8-12. Each additional other punished, at the same total, costs -0.05 to -0.15, and their exit rises by only +0.001 per point. So the contribution model has a strong linear deterrence spillover. With three others, a point dealt in round 0 lifts the rest by ~0.14 at t+8, about as much as the direct marginal lift at a typical 12 points. That explains why the amount, set by c0 and k, has carried every sweep. It also says spreading the same total over more players does not help the spillover; concentrating it on a few does not hurt it.

6. **Would a relative target pay in round 0?** v5_round0_cf.py: on the 131k round-0 contribution profiles of v4's sweep (exogenous to any rule), I compare the target (1-w)*c0 + w*others' mean against v4's absolute c0, at the same mean punishment (k rescaled by bisection), with the responses of 4 and 5 plugged in. At v4's peak (c0 9) the predicted summed direct lift at t+8 rises from 7.42 to 7.70-7.75 at w = 0.5-1. At v3's (c0 10) it rises from 7.53 to 7.80-7.86 at w = 0.25-0.75. Spillover is unchanged (6.45) and exits fall slightly (0.312 to 0.302-0.306). That is about +0.3 contribution per round in my group from round 1, roughly +0.4 pool: small, but the only allocation gain left and it costs no punishment. The others' mean has sd 3.4 across round-0 groups, so the relative target also adapts to cooperative vs uncooperative groups, which a fixed c0 cannot. I expect the best w at 0.3-0.75, not 1. With an all-low group a fully relative target would leave everyone unpunished, and the absolute part still matters.
7. **Is the gap effect robust, or an artefact of the linear interaction?** v5_ah_gap_bins.py: all round-0 members of my group across the four sweeps (~1.5M player-games), with the sqrt-p slope estimated separately in bands of gap x own contribution. Within own c 5-8, the t+8 lift per sqrt point climbs steadily with the gap: 0.64 (gap < 0, i.e. above the others' mean), 0.75, 0.81, 0.87, 0.90, 0.91 (gap 9+). The exit per sqrt point falls from 0.039 to 0.023. Within own c 9-12 the lift is ~0.45-0.58 throughout, but the exit cost falls from 0.049 (gap < 0) to 0.017 (gap 6-9). So punishing a member who is already at or above the rest of the group buys the least and costs the most members. An absolute c0 of 9-10 does exactly that to a 7-9 contributor in a group of 5s, while it under-punishes a 9 among 15s. This confirms exploration 4 and makes me more confident in the relative target than the counterfactual's +0.4 alone suggests.
8. **Drafted the v5 rule.** punishment = k * decay^block * pos * max(0, target - c) for valid members of my group, with target = (1 - w) * c0 + w * (leave-one-out mean contribution of the other valid members of my group this round). A lone member is measured against c0. pos is v3's and v4's block shape (1 / follow / follow^2 / 0 before a decision, follow^2 in round 23). The changes from v4 and why:
   - New `w` [0, 1], from explorations 4, 6 and 7. w = 0 is v3 exactly (with nblocks 6), so the sweep can fall back.
   - I dropped v4's saturation `budget`: it was flat in v4's sweep (effect 0.8), and the spillover of exploration 5 says the total, not its spread over players, is what counts.
   - `decay` now runs [0.05, 1] log, because v4's peak sat at its 0.2 edge.
   - `follow` is narrowed to [0, 0.8]: it was flat both times, peaking at 0.35-0.38.
   - `k` runs [2, 25] log: the peaks were 6.9 (v3) and 11.4 (v4), and a relative target with w near 1 gives smaller shortfalls, so it needs a larger k for the same total (exploration 6: 9.4-9.7 at w = 1).

   A technical note: `.sum(-1, keepdim=True)` hits the same cold-exec `__import__` failure as float scalars did in v1 (validate-rule passes, a cold replay crashes), so the code uses `.sum(-1).unsqueeze(-1)`. Replayed cold on the human histories (v5_replay.py, mean punishment per member of group 0 per round): v4's peak at w = 0 gives 13.5 / 9.3 / 3.9 / 0, then 5.2 in round 4 and 0.7 in round 8. w = 0.5 and 1 at matched k give 13.0 and 12.2 in round 0, with the same shape. (c0 6, k 2, decay 0.05, follow 0) is a near non-punisher (1.8 in round 0 only). (c0 14, k 25, decay 1, follow 0.8) gives ~20-24 every non-decision round. validate-rule passes on all 256 points.

## Key findings

1. Four versions of the shortfall-times-timing family scored 65.5, 66.4, 67.4 and 66.8, each step within about two final standard errors (0.53). v4's saturation was flat (effect 0.8), and its decay peaked at the 0.2 edge (exploration 1).
2. Across all 1024 earlier design points, punishment trades members for contributions: members and contribution per member correlate -0.62 to -0.72. The pool's optimum in mean punishment per member is 1.65-1.79 against every opponent, on a broad plateau from 0.6 to 2.2 (explorations 2, 3).
3. In round 0, at the same own contribution, the lift per sqrt point of punishment grows with how far the player sits below the rest of their group. For own c 5-8 the lift at t+8 is 0.64 when the player is above the others' mean and 0.91 when 9+ below it, while the exit per sqrt point falls from 0.039 to 0.023. For own c 9-12 the exit falls from 0.049 to 0.017. Later rounds show only a weak gap effect (explorations 4, 7).
4. Punishment spills over linearly: each point dealt to others lifts an unpunished member by +0.048 at t+8 in round 0 (+0.02-0.027 later), with almost no exit (+0.001). Spreading the same total over more punished players slightly lowers it (exploration 5).
5. On the round-0 contribution profiles of v4's sweep, at the same total punishment, a target mixing c0 with the others' mean (w 0.25-0.75) raises the predicted summed lift at t+8 by ~0.3 contribution per round (7.42-7.53 to 7.70-7.86) and slightly lowers exits, with the spillover unchanged (exploration 6).

## Hypothesis

A good manager punishes free riders hard in the first round of the game, again at block starts with a shrinking size, and never right before a switch decision. That is the timing family the earlier versions tuned, and the total amount is settled near 1.7 per member per round on average. What remains is *whom* to punish. The simulated players judge themselves against their own group. A member far below the rest of the group responds about 40% more strongly to the same punishment and leaves less. A member who is level with or above the rest gets the least lift and is the most likely to walk. So the expected contribution should be partly relative: a mix of a fixed level and the group's own norm. Then the rule spares a 9 in a group of 5s, presses a 9 in a group of 15s, and concentrates on the members furthest below their group.

The rule: punishment = k * decay^block * pos * max(0, (1 - w) * c0 + w * others' mean - c) for valid members of my group. Here others' mean is the leave-one-out mean of this round's valid contributions in my group (c0 for a lone member). pos is 1 / follow / follow^2 / 0 over a block's four rounds, with follow^2 in round 23.
- `c0` (int, [6, 14]): the absolute part of the target. I expect 8-11 (peaks of 10 and 9 in v3 and v4). It matters less as w rises.
- `w` (float, [0, 1]): the weight of the group's own norm in the target. I expect 0.3-0.75 (exploration 6). 0 is v3/v4's absolute target; 1 is purely relative.
- `k` (float, [2, 25], log): points per point of shortfall in round 0. I expect 7-14 (v3 6.9, v4 11.4, higher with larger w). The range runs from a near non-punisher to the 30 cap for any shortfall above ~1.
- `decay` (float, [0.05, 1], log): the per-block shrink factor. I expect 0.1-0.45 (v3 0.42, v4 at its 0.2 edge).
- `follow` (float, [0, 0.8]): the second round's share of a block's punishment. I expect 0.2-0.5 (flat before, peaks 0.35-0.38).

### Justification

Finding 1 says the timing family is tuned out, so c0, k, decay and follow keep their v3/v4 meanings. Their ranges are centred on the earlier optima, and decay's floor is moved below v4's edge. Finding 2 says the total is settled and opponent-independent, so a new parameter should reallocate punishment rather than scale it. Findings 3 and 5 give that reallocation. The relative target moves punishment from members level with their group, who barely respond and leave readily, to members far below it, who respond best. It also adapts the target to a cooperative or uncooperative group, which no absolute c0 can do. Finding 4 is why the reallocation costs nothing in spillover: the spillover follows total points, not how they are spread. It is also why v4's per-player saturation (budget) was dropped: a near-flat parameter would only thin the 256-point design.

What would contradict the hypothesis: a best w near 0, meaning the round-0 heterogeneity does not survive the full game (finding 3 is within-cell but observational, from earlier rules' random parameters). A pool no higher than v3's 67.4 would say the same. The predicted gain is small (~0.4 pool), so a null result is plausible. A best w near 1 with c0 flat would mean the absolute part was never needed.

Not modelled:
- Conditioning on the other manager's punishment level. The optimum amount did not differ by opponent (finding 2).
- Per-player punishment history. v4's saturation was flat.
- A gap-dependent target in later blocks only. The gap effect is weak there, but a single w is simpler, and later punishment is small anyway at decay below 0.45.
- Group-size terms. The lift falls only ~0.02 per extra member.
