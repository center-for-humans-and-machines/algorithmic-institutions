# pf-20261104-12-v2

## Explorations

1. **What did version 1 find?** Read v1's notes, `landscapes.json`, `sweep.json`
   and `final/` of `pf-20261104-12_sweep`, and ran
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-12/v1_final.py on the final sims. v1
   (`slope*(thr-c)` for c below thr, a quiet window before each switch,
   no punishment in the last `stop` rounds) reached 70.3 (se 1.1, 4.23
   members) at thr 11.7, slope 2.83, quiet 2, stop 6, against 56.0 for `ah`
   and 60.5 for `zero`. The fitted surface rises with slope right to the top
   of its range (67.2 at 2.7, still climbing), peaks in thr at ~11.5, quiet
   2 beats 3 (67.2 vs 66.3) and 0 (64.9), and stop climbs to its edge 6
   (+0.9 over the range). So the sweep wanted *harsher* punishment than I
   expected: a free rider gets the full 30. Per round the picture is
   striking: in the punished rounds (0,1,4,5,...,16,17) the rule deals ~5-10
   points per member and its pool drops to 18-70, while in the quiet rounds
   it is 68-91; contributions climb steadily from 8.7 to 13.4 and do *not*
   fall in the unpunished rounds 20-23 (13.3). The punishment bill is
   ~27 pool points in each of 10 punished rounds, ~11 per round on average:
   the largest remaining lever. Next: what in the punishment buys the
   contribution climb (its size, its frequency, its timing), using the 256
   design points of v1's sweep as a natural experiment.
2. **How do members of the rule's group answer the punishment v1 dealt?**
   Cached v1's sweep (256 points x 500 episodes) as arrays with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-12/cache_sweep.py (checked against
   `sweep.json` pools with arr_check.py: exact) and ran arr_response.py.
   Across the design points, punishment at a fixed own contribution varies
   from 0 to 30, a cleaner experiment than `ah`'s observational sims. The
   contribution response is large and close to linear in the punishment,
   with no saturation: a 0-contributor rises +1.7 next round unpunished,
   +2.3 at 5-10, +3.4 at 15-20, +4.9 at 25-30; a 3-6 contributor +0.2 vs
   +1.4 (5-10) vs +3.4 (25-30); a 9-12 contributor -0.4 vs +0.6 (5-10).
   Three rounds on the gaps hold or grow (0-contributor +2.2 vs +5.9).
   That is ~0.12-0.19 contribution points per punishment point per later
   round, the mid contributors with moderate punishment at the top of
   that. Leaving rises with the block's punishment: 26% with none, 35% at
   10-20, 42% at 20-40, 52% at 40-60, 66% above 60 (low contributors), and
   steeply with punishment in the decision round itself (31% none, 61% at
   10-20) — which v1's quiet window already avoids. The first decision is
   the most sensitive (40% leave if punished in block 0 vs 23% if not;
   later blocks 26-29% vs 19-20%). So a punishment point buys ~0.2-0.3
   pool points per remaining round, and costs a share of a member. The
   balance depends on how many rounds remain. Next: v1's `stop` range: with
   quiet 2, stop 4, 5 and 6 all only drop round 20 (18-19 are quiet
   anyway), so the sweep never tested dropping the 16-17 punishment; does
   late punishment pay?
3. **Does late punishment pay, from the punished member's own account?**
   Ran scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-12/arr_late.py: for members of the
   rule's group at a block's first round t, by own contribution and the
   punishment received, their pool account over rounds t..23 (1.6 x their
   later contributions while in the group, minus all punishment they get).
   At t=0 the account barely moves with the punishment (0-contributor: 98
   at 1-8 vs 85 at 24-30; 1-4: 103-116 vs 102); by t=8 it falls with it
   (40 unpunished vs 8 at 24-30); at t=16 it is clearly negative (14 vs
   -26; 1-4: 30 vs 0). This ignores spill-overs onto other members and is
   confounded by later punishment, but the drift is clear: a punishment
   early has the rounds to pay back, one late does not. Next: check why the
   return falls over time.
4. **Is the response weaker late, or for repeat offenders, or with low-giving
   peers?** Ran arr_hetero.py on low contributors (c<=6) staying a round.
   All three. By round, the next-round rise at 20-30 punishment is +5.3
   in rounds 0-3 (unpunished +1.2), +4.7 in 4-7, +4.0, +3.8, +3.4 in 16-22
   (unpunished +0.55). By earlier punishments of the same player: +5.7 the
   first time (vs +1.2), +4.7 after 2-3, +3.1 after 7+ (vs +0.4). By the
   others' mean contribution: with others at 14-20 a low contributor rises
   +3.2 unpunished and +6.9 at 20-30; with others below 6, +0.3 and +2.8:
   herding is as strong as punishment. So punishment works best early,
   and the same punishment buys less every block. Next: the member cost
   when punishment is confined to the block's first two rounds.
5. **What does early-block punishment cost in members?** Ran arr_leave.py on
   points with quiet>=2 (punishment only in a block's first two rounds).
   Leaving still rises with it: low contributors 27% unpunished, 33% at
   10-20, 36% at 20-30, 45% at 45-60; high contributors (15-20) 18% -> 23%.
   Others' punishment spills over: an unpunished 10-15 contributor leaves
   at 19% when the others got nothing and 31% when they averaged 20+ in the
   block. Joining from `ah`'s group *rises* when the rule's group is mildly
   punished (mean 0-5 per member in the block: 48% of low contributors,
   30% of 10-15 join, vs 39% / 27% with none) and falls when it is harsh
   (20+: 27% / 16%). v1's finding that early-block punishment is nearly
   free was too strong: it is cheaper, not free. Losing a free rider costs
   the pool nothing, though; losing a high contributor costs ~20 a round.
6. **Drafted v2.** Kept v1's core, `slope*(thr-c)` for members below
   thr, and its quiet window before each decision, and changed three
   things. (a) A per-block `decay`: block b punishes with slope*decay^b.
   Explorations 3-4 say a punishment point buys less every block, and v1's
   rounds 20-23 show contributions holding (13.3) once punishment stops, so
   the punishment can taper as the game goes on. (b) No punishment in rounds
   20-23, fixed: v1's `stop` of 4-6 all mean exactly that under quiet 2,
   and they were its best values; a separate `stop` would just spend design
   points. (c) Wider ranges where v1 ran into an edge or its best values
   cluster: slope to [0.8, 10] log (v1's surface still climbed at 2.8;
   above ~3 a free rider is capped at 30 anyway and slope sets how hard
   near-threshold members are hit), quiet to [1, 3] (0 was clearly worst,
   -2.4). v1's best point (decay 1) lies inside the new space. I chose not
   to add repeat-offender forgiveness: the response weakens with repeats
   (exploration 4) but is still +2.7 above unpunished after 7+ punishments,
   and it overlaps with decay.
7. **Validated and checked the schedule.** `validate-rule` passes (256
   distinct design points). scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-12/check_schedule_v2.py
   on a toy game: thr 12, slope 2.5, decay 0.7, quiet 2 gives a
   0-contributor 30, 21, 14, 10, 7 in rounds 0-1, 4-5, 8-9, 12-13, 16-17
   (a 5-contributor 17, 12, 8, 6, 4) and nothing elsewhere; decay 1.4,
   quiet 1 rises to the cap and punishes rounds 0-2, 4-6, ..., 16-18.

## Key findings

1. v1's best rule scored 70.3 against `ah` (56.0 for `ah`, 60.5 for
   `zero`) by punishing free riders hard (slope 2.83: a 0-contributor gets
   the full 30) in the first two rounds of each block; its surface still
   climbed at the top of the slope range, and its punishment bill was ~11
   pool points a round (exploration 1).
2. In v1's sweep the next-round contribution rise is close to linear in the
   punishment and persists: a 0-contributor +1.7 unpunished vs +4.9 at
   25-30, and +2.2 vs +5.9 three rounds on (exploration 2).
3. The response weakens over the game and with repeated punishment: +5.3 vs
   +1.2 in rounds 0-3 but +3.4 vs +0.55 in rounds 16-22; +5.7 for a first
   punishment, +3.1 after 7+ (exploration 4).
4. From the punished member's own account, punishment roughly pays back at
   round 0 (98 vs 85 for a 0-contributor) and loses clearly at round 16 (14
   vs -26) (exploration 3).
5. Punishment early in the block still costs members: low contributors
   leave at 27% unpunished vs 45% at 45-60 in the block, others' punishment
   spills over onto unpunished high contributors (18% -> 26%), and harsh
   groups attract fewer joiners (27% vs 48% for mild) (exploration 5).
6. Contributions hold when punishment stops: v1's best is flat at ~13.3
   over the unpunished rounds 20-23 (exploration 1).

## Hypothesis

A good manager invests in contributions early and stops paying for them
late. Punishment of low contributors raises their contributions durably
and in proportion to its size, and the raised level holds once it stops,
but each punishment point buys less as the game goes on and has fewer
rounds left to pay back. Punishment also costs members, most of all near
a switch decision. So the manager punishes shortfalls below a threshold
hard at the start of the game, early in each block and never in the
rounds before a decision, and tapers the punishment block by block, with
none after the last decision.

The rule: `punishment = slope * decay^block * max(0, thr - c_t)` for members
who contributed this round, 0 in the last `quiet` rounds of each block
(up to and including the decision round) and in rounds 20-23.

- `thr` (float, [5, 18]): contribution below which a member is punished. v1
  peaked at ~11.7 with a fall-off on both sides; with harsher slopes the
  best may move a little lower, with tapering a little higher. I expect
  10-14.
- `slope` (float, [0.8, 10], log): punishment per point of shortfall in the
  first block. v1's best was 2.8 at the top of its range; I expect 2.5-5.
  Above ~3 a free rider is at the cap, so slope then sets how hard
  members just under thr are hit. Log because the effect is multiplicative.
- `decay` (float, [0.4, 1.5], log): per-block change of that rate. I expect
  0.6-0.9 (findings 3, 4); 1 is v1's flat schedule, and >1 lets the sweep
  find that late punishment pays after all.
- `quiet` (int, [1, 3]): rounds before and including each switch decision
  with no punishment. I expect 2, as in v1; 0 was clearly worst there.

### Justification

Findings 2 and 6 say punishment is an investment whose return persists after
it stops; findings 3 and 4 say the return falls over the game, which is
what `decay` models, and v1's surface rewarding the largest `stop` points
the same way. Finding 1 grounds the threshold and the wider slope range;
finding 5 (and v1's quiet result) grounds `quiet` and the fact that the
taper should also save members. Rounds 20-23 go unpunished because v1's best
`stop` values meant exactly that. What would contradict it: a sweep whose
best decay sits at or above 1 (late punishment still pays, perhaps because
it holds contributions up), or at 0.4 (only the first block matters, so a
single opening block of punishment would do), or a best slope at 10 (the
rule wants a pure step). Not modelled: repeat-offender forgiveness (the
response to repeats weakens but stays positive, and it overlaps with
decay), herding on the group's mean (finding 3's peer effect is real but a
threshold relative to the group would punish less exactly when peers are
low and herding is weakest), a separate first-block intensity (decay
already makes the first block the hardest), and newcomers. These readings
come from v1's design points, a cleaner experiment than `ah`'s sims but
still with later punishment confounded; the sweep is the test.
