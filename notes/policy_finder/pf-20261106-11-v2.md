## Explorations

1. **How did version 1 do, and where did its sweep point?** Read the v1 notes, rule, `sweep.json`, `landscapes.json` and `final/sweep.json` of plots/simulation/policy_finder/pf-20261106-11_sweep. The final result is 65.1 (se 0.56): 67.6, 66.6, 69.1 and 57.0 against opponents 1-4. The fitted surface peaks with `rate` at its top edge, 3.0, and `lam` at its bottom edge, 0. Pool rises steadily with log rate, from 59.9 at 0.1 to 63.8 at 3. `w` wants the relative norm (best 0.9-1.0), `anchor` is nearly flat (63.2-63.8 over 4-15) and `h` has no effect at all (effect 0.0002). Across the 256 points, rate correlates with pool at +0.31 (Spearman) but with group size at -0.80. So in the artificial humans, harsher punishment buys more contribution than it loses in members, which goes against my v1 expectation of 0.5-1.5. The range stopped short, and the next version must reach higher. Next I want to see what the opponents do.

2. **What do the four opponents do?** Looked at v1's final run per opponent, per block, and at punishment by contribution bin, with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-11/b1_final.py. Opponent 4 never punishes. Opponents 1 and 3 punish low contributors hard early (c 0-5: 6.8-7.9) and fade to 0 by round 16. Opponent 2 punishes mildly throughout (3.3 at c 0, 0.3 even at c 20, ~1 per head late). Against all of them my group has higher contributions (late 12.4-14.5 vs 10.1-13.3) but fewer members (4.2-4.3 vs 4.6-4.7 in non-empty rounds; 3.5-4.1 counting empty rounds). Opponent 4 is my worst pairing at 57.0: after block 1 it has 5.1 members to my 3.7. My rule still deals 1.2-3.4 per head late in the game, and the opponents deal ~0. Groups often empty out entirely: group 0 has 0 members in ~10% of rounds and all 8 in ~5%. Membership is very volatile, so herding matters. Next: fit the artificial humans' switching on the sweep's own games.

3. **What makes the artificial humans switch?** Turned the v1 sweep's 131072 games into arrays (b2_arrays.py) and fit a logit of the switch decision with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-11/b3_ah_switch.py. The switch rate is 27% (39% at the first decision). Per point of own mean punishment in the block, the log-odds go up 0.086, plus 0.047 per point of punishment in the decision round itself. The share gap (other group's 1.6*mean c minus own) adds 0.087 per point. Group size is the strongest term, -0.22 per member: from 48% leaving at size 1 to 16% at size 8. A harsh other group keeps players home (-0.07 per point of its mean punishment). So the decision-round penalty I modelled in v1 is real (lam 0 was best), and big groups hold members. Raising the group's contribution by 1 point is worth as much retention as removing 1 point of mean punishment. Next: how contributions respond to punishment in the artificial humans.

4. **How do the artificial humans' contributions respond to punishment?** Looked at next-round and two-round contribution changes of players who stay, by own contribution and punishment, in a 40000-game sample of the v1 sweep, with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-11/b4_ah_contrib.py. The response is monotone and does not saturate. At c = 0 the change is +1.6 unpunished, +2.7 at p 5-10, +3.4 at p 10-20 and +5.6 at p 20-30. At c 4-8 it is +0.1 unpunished and +3.9 at p 20-30. Relative to the others it holds even near the norm: c within 1 of the others' mean goes -0.06 unpunished and +0.85 at p 5-10. Only players well above the norm cut (c 16-20 by 1-3 at any p), and c 12-16 drops at p above 10. An OLS fit gives +0.15 per point of punishment, with the others' mean pulling at +0.21. So, unlike the humans' inverted U in v1 entry 3, harsh punishment of those below the norm keeps paying in the simulator. Next: does the gain last?

5. **How long does the gain last?** Regressed c[t+k] - c[t] on p[t] (players at or below their group's mean who stay k rounds), controlling for c, the others' mean and later punishments, with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-11/b5_persist.py. The effect per point is 0.08 at k=1, 0.13 at k=2-4 and 0.17 at k=6-8, so it persists and even grows. Meanwhile the others' mean pulls ever harder (0.21 at k=1, 0.52 at k=8), so a raised contributor also lifts the rest. A point of punishment in round t buys about 0.15 * 1.6 = 0.24 pool per remaining round, before spillover, against 1 point of cost. It pays back after ~4 rounds, so early punishment is worth much more than late, and rounds 21-23 hardly pay at all. Next: what punishment costs in members around the first switch.

6. **What decides membership at the first switch, per opponent?** Regressed my group's size after round 4 on my and the other manager's per-head punishment in rounds 0-3 and the contribution gap, per opponent, with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261106-11/b6_block1.py. Every per-head point I deal costs 0.045 members in round 0 and 0.06 in round 1, but 0.09 in round 2 and 0.08 in round 3. Each point the other manager deals gains me 0.04-0.10, and each point of contribution gap gains 0.32. A member at round 4 is worth 8 pool points per round over the game (members later rise 0.38 per member at round 4). Opponent 3 punishes only in rounds 0-1 of the block, and opponent 4 never punishes; against opponent 4 I keep only 3.56 members at round 4 and score 51.5 in the sweep. So timing within the block matters beyond the decision round itself: punish in the block's first two rounds, go quiet in its last two. My retention depends on the gap between my severity and the opponent's. Against a lenient opponent each point I deal costs more in relative terms.

7. **Where on rate should the next sweep look?** Restricted the v1 points to the good region (lam < 0.35, w > 0.4; 56 points) and binned by rate. 8. **Drafted the v2 rule.** I kept v1's core, punishing shortfalls below a target in proportion, and changed four things.
   - The target is now max(norm + `delta`, `floor`), with norm the others' mean. v1 wanted w ~0.9-1 and found anchor flat, so the norm leads. `delta` lets the sweep ask for a little more than the norm, since in entry 4 even players at the norm rose under punishment. `floor` keeps an absolute minimum for groups that sink.

Entry 6 shows that my retention depends on my severity relative to the opponent's (my points -0.05 to -0.09 members, theirs +0.04 to +0.10). kappa = 0 ignores the opponent. kappa = 2 against a never-punishing opponent cuts my severity to 1/7, and against one dealing 6 per member raises it by 1.9x.

## Key findings

1. Version 1 scored 65.1 and its surface peaked at the top edge of rate (3.0) and the bottom of lam (0). Pool climbed from 59.9 to 63.8 over rate 0.1-3, w preferred 0.9-1, and anchor and h were flat (entries 1, 7).
2. The artificial humans keep raising their contributions as punishment grows: c = 0 rises +1.6 unpunished and +5.6 at p 20-30. Even players at the group norm rise (+0.85 at p 5-10 vs -0.06). Only players well above the norm cut (entry 4).
3. The gain lasts and spreads: +0.08 per punishment point after 1 round, 0.13-0.17 after 2-8 rounds, while the others' mean pulls more over time (0.21 to 0.52). A point pays back after roughly 4 rounds, so early punishment is worth most and the last rounds' least (entry 5).
4. Switching in the simulator rises with own punishment (+0.086 log-odds per point of block mean, +0.047 more for the decision round) and with the share gap (+0.087 per point). It falls with group size (-0.22 per member) and with the other group's punishment (-0.07) (entry 3).
5. For membership after the first switch, punishment in the block's rounds 2-3 costs about twice as much as in rounds 0-1 (0.08-0.09 vs 0.045-0.06 members per per-head point). The other manager's punishment offsets mine at a similar rate (+0.04 to +0.10), and a member at round 4 is worth ~8 pool points per round (entry 6).
6. The four opponents differ: one never punishes, two punish low contributors hard early and fade to 0, one punishes mildly throughout. My worst pairing is the never-punishing one (57.0 in v1's final; 3.56 members at round 4), where rate barely mattered and lam mattered most (entries 2, 6, 7).

## Hypothesis

In this simulator, a good manager punishes members below the group norm hard and in proportion to the shortfall, because the contribution gain grows with punishment, lasts and spreads to the others. It times punishment so it costs few members. It punishes in the first rounds of each block, goes quiet in the rounds just before players choose groups, and stops at the end where the payback no longer comes. How hard it can afford to be depends on the other group. A harsh opponent pushes players to me, so I can punish more. A lenient one makes my punishment cost members, so I should ease off.

The rule: target = max(others' mean + delta, floor), punishment = rate * max(0, target - c) * w_pos * min(1, (23 - t)/3) * (1 + kappa*q)/(1 + kappa*3). Here w_pos is 0 in the decision rounds 3, 7, 11, 15, 19, `late` in rounds 2, 6, 10, 14, 18, and 1 otherwise, and q is the other manager's mean punishment per member so far.
- `rate` (float, [0.5, 12] log): points per point of shortfall. I expect 3-8.
- `delta` (float, [-4, 6]): target offset from the norm. I expect 0-3.
- `floor` (float, [0, 12]): absolute minimum target. I expect 4-10, with a weak effect.
- `late` (float, [0, 1]): multiplier in the round before each decision. I expect 0-0.5.
- `kappa` (float, [0, 2]): how much my severity follows the opponent's. I expect 0.2-1.

### Justification

Findings 1, 2 and 3 move `rate` up: v1's peak sat at the top of its range, and the simulator's response does not saturate even at p 20-30. The new range [0.5, 12] puts v1's best (3) in the middle on the log scale, and the punishment cap of 30 makes rates above ~10 nearly equivalent. `delta` comes from finding 2: players at the norm still rise under punishment. Its range lets the sweep choose between tolerating a bit below the norm and asking for more. `floor` replaces v1's anchor at lower weight, because v1 preferred the relative norm (finding 1). The hard zero in the decision round comes from v1's lam (finding 1) and finding 4's +0.047. `late` comes from finding 5: round 2 of the block costs members nearly as much as round 3, so the sweep can silence it or not. The fixed end-fade comes from finding 3, and from v1's h having no effect, so I fixed it instead of spending a dimension on it. `kappa` comes from findings 4, 5 and 6: my retention depends on my severity relative to the opponent's, and my worst pairing is with the lenient opponent.

Several sweep results would contradict this. A best `rate` at 12 would mean even the cap is not harsh enough, so the target or timing should carry more. `late` near 1 would mean the round before the decision is harmless. `kappa` at 2 would mean following the opponent matters more than I allow, and at 0 that it does not help. A large gain against opponent 4 without loss elsewhere would support kappa.

What I did not model:
- Scaling with group size. The switching slope is steeper in small groups, but it is a second-order term.
- Escalation for repeat free riders, and special treatment of newcomers.
- Recency in q. Opponents that fade late keep a positive q from their early rounds, which slightly over-licenses my late punishment. The end-fade and the shrinking shortfalls late in the game limit that.
I could not simulate here (no model weights), so the levels rest on the v1 sweep's games and on the human data.
