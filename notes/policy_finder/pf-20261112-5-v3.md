## Key findings

1. Neither earlier version beat never punishing in its sweep: v2's 256 points average 14.3 and
   every binned cell sits at 13-16, `zero` 13.97 in the reference sims; v2's final 18.43 +- 1.51
   is a high draw on that plateau (exploration 1).
2. Pushing free riders out at a decision does not pay: per point dealt at round 3 to a zero
   contributor the rest-of-game lead falls 0.47 +- 0.11, because they contribute once in `ah`'s
   group (`ah`'s pool +0.30 per point) (exploration 2).
3. Contribution habits are a commons shared through switching: my punishment raises my members'
   contribution about one point per point per member-round (v1: 8.45 -> 9.88 -> 11.08), but `ah`'s
   members' rises with it over the game (9.69 -> 10.15 -> 10.75) though not in rounds 0-3, before
   any switch (exploration 4); the block-1 gate shows the same, own +29 and other +28 pool points
   per game at doses 10-20 (exploration 6).
4. The AH switchers have no sticky types to exploit: base exit 18-25% and the dose's extra exits
   are the same whatever the member's switching history (exploration 5).
5. Punishment from round 8 on only loses, more the larger the dose (block 2 at doses 20-40: -59 +-
   14 per game; rounds 20-23: just the points dealt, -28 +- 2) (exploration 6).
6. Round-0 punishment raises rounds 1-3 contribution but shrinks my group at the first decision
   (dose 20-40: -0.23 +- 0.08 members) and the lead in rounds 0-3 (exploration 8).
7. Across all 512 points of v1 and v2, the only phase whose punishment goes with a higher lead is
   right after the first decision: rounds 4-7 +3.75 +- 1.38 per point per member-round with an
   optimum near 1.2, round 4 alone +1.12 +- 0.58, while rounds 0-3 (-1.79 +- 0.92) and 8-19
   (-3.39 +- 0.85) go with a lower one; across-point and collinear, so weak (exploration 9).

## Hypothesis

On this stack the score is mostly membership, and contribution habits diffuse between the groups
at every switch decision, so whatever a manager trains by punishing is shared with `ah`'s group
while the members it costs are its own. A good manager is therefore close to `zero`, and spends
punishment only where the trained contribution has the longest time to pay before it leaks and
where the punishment costs the fewest exits: right after the first decision (round 4), when the
biggest reshuffle is done, the next decision weighs that round least and 19 rounds remain; not
before the first decision (that is where the most players move) and not from the middle of the
game on. The rule: in the first `w` rounds of each of the blocks `first` .. `first+nblocks-1`
(never after round 19), each member of my group who timed in, is not alone and gave at most `hi`
gets `D` points.

- `D` (float, [2, 24] log): the flat dose. Expect 5-12.
- `hi` (int, 2..12): the highest contribution punished. Expect 5-8.
- `first` (int, 0..2): the first open block. Expect 1.
- `nblocks` (int, 1..3): how many blocks are open. Expect 1-2.
- `w` (int, 1..2): open rounds at the start of each open block. Expect 1.

### Justification

Findings 1 and 3 explain why two sweeps came out flat and set the frame: the gain in contribution
is real but shared through switching, the member cost is mine, so the rule stays light and
contains a near-`zero` manager (D=2, hi=2, nblocks=1 deals almost nothing). Findings 6 and 7 are
why the window became a parameter that can start after round 0 (`first`, expected 1); 0 is kept
so the sweep can compare against v2's own peak (first=0, nblocks=2), and 2 tests whether later is
better still. Finding 5 bounds the window: `first + nblocks` reaches at most block 4 and the code
stops at round 19, and I expect nblocks 1-2. `w` stays 1..2 from v2's evidence that pos 2-3 cost
5-8x the exits of pos 0; I expect 1. `D`'s range goes higher than v2's (to 24) because finding 7's
round-4 phase tolerated more punishment than any other, and its lower end of 2 is close to silent;
`hi` keeps v2's evidence that members above ~10 do not answer and that hi >= 10 lost 1-2 points
(range 2..12, expect 5-8). Finding 2 is why there is no floor: sparing or exiling zero contributors
does not help (v2's `lo` had an effect of 0.36). What would contradict it: a sweep peak at
first=0 (round-0 training pays after all) or at D near 2 or nblocks=1 with hi=2 (any punishment
loses), or `other` rising as fast as `own` at the peak (the leak eats the gain everywhere). Not
modelled: the group's size (no size pattern in exploration 7), per-player history (finding 4),
and the endgame (finding 5). The main uncertainty is finding 7's strength: it is the weakest piece
of evidence and the whole difference from v2 rests on it.
