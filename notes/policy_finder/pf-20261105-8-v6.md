   peak (656.8), mid falls 6 points at 9.5 and 13 at 3.3, free 7 at 20. The 4-d box free

   percentile 646.9 (exploration 4).

## Hypothesis

A good manager makes an expensive first impression and then stays cheap. For about the
first 7 rounds it punishes every player by their shortfall from full contribution, on a
steep curve: about 28 for a free rider, about 6 for a half contributor, a point at 15,
nothing at 18+. The clones' raise is persistent and spreads to the group, and it is
largest for the lowest contributors, so these early points repay several times over.
After round ~7, punishing mid contributors no longer repays in any group state. A player
who has dropped to (almost) nothing still repays a full punishment, so until round ~14
the manager punishes only those players, and after that nobody. This structure is
settled after five versions; v6 changes how the curve is set. Two numbers fix it, the free
rider's dose and the half contributor's dose. These are the directions the score
actually depends on, so the sweep's fitted peak should not slide along a ridge into a
corner.
The rule: base = free * (max(20 - c, 0) / 20)^log2(free / mid). Every valid player gets
it in rounds t < horizon; only players with c < floor get it in rounds horizon <= t < end.
- `free` (float, [22, 30]): the punishment of a zero contributor. I expect 26-30; 30 is
  the cap.
- `mid` (float, [4, 9.5]): the punishment of a player giving 10. It sets the
  steepness. I expect 5.5-7.5.
- `horizon` (int, 4..9): end of the opening phase. I expect 6-8.
- `end` (int, 10..19): first round with no punishment. I expect 12-16.
- `floor` (fixed at 2): the late phase hits only 0 and 1 contributors.

### Justification

Finding 1 says the family's shape is right and its weak step is the sweep's fitted peak.
Finding 2 locates the optimum in terms of the curve itself and says which directions
matter. Finding 4 turns this into two curve parameters with no trade-off between them:
`free` moves only the free rider's dose, and `mid` moves everything in between. It also
places each range so that its edges sit 3-6 fitted points below the peak. The one edge
at the optimum's side, free = 30, is the game's maximum punishment.
`horizon` and `end` keep v3-v5's staged stop. Finding 3 confirms the horizon's place:
the value of an opening round crosses zero at rounds 6-7, and it does so in nearly every
group state. The end is flat over 10-19 (finding 2), so its wide range costs little.
What would contradict this:
- a v6 landscape peaking at an edge of `mid` or `horizon` (the pooled fit mislocated the
  curve);
- a final clearly below ~652 (the plateau is lower than the re-checks suggest).

Not modelled:
- a horizon gated on the group's level (finding 3: about 1 point at most);
- a free zero point of the curve (exploration 4: flat over 20-25);
- a swept floor and a separate round-0 standard (v5: flat);
- relative standards, credit for rising and escalation (no gain in v1/v2's tests);
- caps on repeated late hits (v4: about 1 point);
- timeouts, beyond excluding timed-out players.
