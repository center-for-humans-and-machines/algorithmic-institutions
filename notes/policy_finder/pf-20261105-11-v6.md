2. **Do high-opening groups want a fine on their lowest?**

   games), and that is near the fitting noise. It matches f1's sign: in groups
   with no free rider, ask a little more and price it gently. Not a big lever.
   Next: since rounds 0-3 decide the game, is there a lever in rounds 1-3 among

## Key findings

1. The five finals' GP points play the same 2000 games, as do sweep points of
   the same index across versions, so their comparisons are paired. Against
   v4_gp (649.88): v2 -0.19 (se 0.48), v1 -1.11 (0.64), v5 -0.98 (0.44),
   v3 -4.22 (0.64) (5, 8).
2. Removing the per-index seed effect halves the sweep noise (residual sd 3.27 ->
   1.72, seed effects sd 3.05). The FE-adjusted plateau is the known one: a fine
   of ~3-7 at c=12, 9-11 at c=9 and 22-28 at c=0 (T 12-14, A 1.5-4.5,
   B ~1.5-2.4). Harsher schedules lose 4-8 (6).
3. v5's loss to v4 is its stop round. Mean c is equal up to round 8 and then
   +0.36 to +0.46 higher in every round 9-23 under S=8, for a round-8 bill of
   1.19 a player. The gain does not fade, so a fine's effect is a lasting level
   shift (8).
4. With S as categories and seed fixed effects, S=8 is the best stop round:
   S7 -0.3 to -0.6, S9 -0.3 to -0.5, S10 -0.6 to -0.9, S12 -1.6 to -2.3,
   S5-6 -1.1 to -1.5 (8).
5. The best target does not depend on the round-0 lineup. Every bin of the lowest
   opener peaks at T 12-14, while its level runs from 621 to 695 (3, 4).
6. An extra opening-offset dimension, or a different box, does not raise the
   expected value at the sweep's peak. The boxes are all within 0.35, and the
   offset costs 0.1-0.2 unless its effect is large (7, 9).

## Hypothesis

A good manager makes the group lock in during the opening rounds. It puts a
predictable price on contributing below a moderate standard: every shortfall gets
a tap, and the price rises steeply with the shortfall, so free riders feel it
most and onlookers see that shortfalls are not tolerated. A fine buys a lasting
rise in contribution. Its value therefore shrinks with the rounds left, while its
double cost does not. So the manager fines through round 8, the last round in
which the bill is still covered, and is silent after. The rule:
`punishment = A + B*(T - c)` for c < T in rounds t <= S, else 0.
- `T`, the target: no fine at or above it. Sweep [11.8, 15.0], uniform.
- `A`, the tap any shortfall gets. Sweep [0.8, 4.6], uniform.
- `B`, the price per point of shortfall. Sweep [1.35, 2.7], log-uniform.
- `S`, the last punished round. Fixed at 8.

### Justification

- The shape comes from five versions of real sweeps: the plateau in fine terms
  (finding 2), and the paired finals, where every GP point of this family ties
  except the misplaced ones (finding 1).
- `S` = 8 rests on findings 3 and 4. The paired v4/v5 games isolate round 8's
  fine as worth ~+1, and the seed-adjusted stop-round profile peaks at 8.
  Fixing it spares the GP a dimension that adds noise. That noise cost v5 its
  ~1 (S=7) and v3 its ~4 (N=3) (finding 6).
- `T`, `A` and `B` are left to the sweep. The ranges hold the FE-adjusted best
  cells (finding 2) and the GP points of v2, v4 and v5 with room on each side:
  - T 12-14.3 inside [11.8, 15];
  - A 1.5-4.5 inside [0.8, 4.6];
  - B 1.5-2.4 inside [1.35, 2.7].

  Their profiles fall off by ~2-5 at the ends (v5 entry 2). The mock sweeps show
  the box choice costs under 0.35 (finding 6). I expect the peak near T ~13,
  A ~2.5, B ~1.8.

What would contradict it:
- a GP peak at an edge of T, A or B, which would mean the plateau has moved;
- a paired final below v4's by more than ~1 at an interior peak, which would mean
  the plateau is narrower than the seed-adjusted fit says.

Not modelled:
- A lineup-adaptive target. It is null (finding 5).
- A separate opening target. It does not pay for its dimension (finding 6).
- Give-up counts, ratchets, relative or rising targets, and late maintenance.
  Earlier versions found them neutral or harmful, and finding 4 shows that later
  fines stop covering their cost.
- Timeouts. They are exogenous, and a timed-out player cannot be punished.

I expect a tie with v4's 649.9 within its paired se of ~0.5, not a clear gain.
