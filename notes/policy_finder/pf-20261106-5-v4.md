6. **Drafted v4: v3's block-start opening, re-parameterised where v3's sweep was blind.** A valid member of my group contributing `c` in block `b` (rounds 4b..4b+3) gets `slope * max(target - decay * b - c, 0)` (the manager clamps at 30) in the first `window_open` rounds of block 0 and the first `window_late` rounds of each later block, in blocks `b < n_blocks` only. Changes from v3 and why: (a) `cap` is gone: its profile rose monotonically to 30, the game's own maximum, so the clamp does the job and `slope` alone sets how threshold-like the rule is (range widened up to 25 so the sweep can reach a pure step; v3's slope profile was flat 5-8 and only fell slowly after). (b) One `window` became two: entry 5 says the opening needs its second round (and v3's window 2 beat 1 by a point), while in later blocks the cheapest round for members is the first one after the decision (v3 entry 3: a point in block round 0 costs a quarter to half the switching of a later round) and that is also where newcomers arrive; v3 forced the same window on both. (c) `n_open`, `target_late` and `t_end` (effects 0.76, 0.0, 1.0, two of them made irrelevant by the early stop) became `n_blocks` and `decay`: the stop now counts whole blocks, and later blocks' target is a smooth drop from the opening one, so every point of the design actually uses all its parameters. `n_blocks` reaches down to 1 (opening only), which v3's t_end >= 8 could not test though its profile was highest at its low edge. (d) No reading of the other manager: entry 4 found the same optimal dose against all four opponents. ## Key findings

1. v3's peak (target 8.8, slope 6.3, cap 30, punishing only rounds 0, 1, 4, 5, 8) is the best result so far, pool 68.52 (70.1 / 70.0 / 73.0 / 60.9 against opponents 1-4), +3.4 on v2; window 2 beat 1, 3 and 4 (67.3 vs 66.3 / 66.5 / 64.2), slope and target dominate the surface, cap peaked at the game's maximum 30, and n_open and target_late had no effect (exploration 1).
2. After that opening, contributions stay at 11.5-12.9 per member for 15 rounds without punishment, the late pool is 78-83 per round, and the five punishing rounds cost ~5.5 pool points per round of the game; against everyone but the non-punisher the group gains members at the first decision (4.19-4.48), against opponent_4 it drops to 3.63 (exploration 2).
3. The best dose is the same against every opponent: 1-4 punishment points per member-round in rounds 0-11, falling off above 5; members at round 4 fall steadily with dose against all of them (exploration 4; the per-opponent surfaces of exploration 3 were too noisy to say otherwise).
4. Round-0 punishment raises rounds 9-11 contributions most for the lowest contributors (c0 3-5: 6.9 unpunished vs 10.3 at 20-29 points) and hurts above 11; it works through the round-1 contribution (0.49 per point of c1), and a second hit adds +1.8 for those still at <= 3 but backfires for those who already rose to 9+ (exploration 5).

## Hypothesis

Against these artificial humans a manager sets a contribution norm once, early, with a hard and cheap-in-members hit, and the norm then carries the group: punish whoever is clearly below about 9 in the first round or two of the game, very hard (up to the 30 maximum), stop punishing before each switch decision, and in later blocks punish at most in the round right after the decision (when newcomers arrive and members care least), on a target that may relax as the group settles, for a few blocks at most. The rule: a valid member of my group contributing `c` in block `b` gets `slope * max(target - decay * b - c, 0)` (clamped to 30) in the first `window_open` rounds of block 0 or the first `window_late` rounds of a later block, in blocks `b < n_blocks`.

- `target` (float, [5.5, 12.5]): the opening norm. I expect 8.5-10 (v3's profile peaked at 8.6-9.3).
- `slope` (float, [1.5, 25], log): points per point of shortfall; large values make it a threshold rule dealing 30. I expect 5-15 (v3's peak 6.3 with cap 30 at its edge).
- `decay` (float, [-1, 3]): target drop per later block. I expect 0-1.5 (v3's n_open profile mildly preferred keeping the opening target, but late punishment of 9-11 contributors buys little); negative values let the sweep show a rising norm is better.
- `window_open` (int, [1, 4]): punishing rounds at the start of the game. I expect 2.
- `window_late` (int, [1, 3]): punishing rounds at the start of later blocks. I expect 1.
- `n_blocks` (int, [1, 5]): blocks with any punishment. I expect 2-3 (v3's peak: 3, with a flat profile).

### Justification

Finding 1 is why the block-start structure, the target near 9 and hard hits are kept, and why `cap` is dropped (its best was the game's own clamp) in favour of a wider `slope`. Finding 4 is why the opening keeps its second round (`window_open`, expected 2) and why the target is absolute: punish whoever is still low, never those who rose. Finding 2 shows the cost of the later punishing rounds (rounds 4, 5, 8: ~60-75 pool points) and that members are the price; together with v3's block-position switching result it motivates a separate, likely shorter `window_late`, a `decay` of the later target and an `n_blocks` that can go down to 1. Finding 3 is why the rule ignores the other manager: the best dose was the same against all four. What would contradict this: `window_late` best at 3 or `window_open` at 1 (the opening/later split is useless), `n_blocks` at 1 with flat `decay` (later blocks are pure cost), or a peak at the high edge of `slope` with a low `target` (only a pure threshold on near-zero contributors matters). Not modelled: per-player histories (a second hit helps only those still at the bottom, which the absolute target already handles), group size, and opponent-reading, to keep six readable parameters for 256 points.
