## Explorations

1. **Where do versions 1 and 2 leave me?** Read both notes, rules, `sweep.json`, `landscapes.json` and `final/sweep.json`. Both finals landed on "never punish" (v1 at base = slope = 0; v2 at fine 0.62, which floors to 0) and both scored the identical +16.76 +- 1.42 (same seeds, same behaviour): own 62.1 vs ah 45.4, members 4.63 vs 3.37. In v2's sweep the non-punishing points (fine < 1, 26 points) averaged 14.80, fines 2-3 averaged 14.88, fines 5-10 13.59; thr 2-4 averaged 14.65 over all fines. So v2's "show free-riders the door" was roughly a wash at small fines, and anything heavier lost. Also noted: across v2's points own pool rises slightly with the fine (60.3 -> 61.3) but ah's pool rises more (45.6 -> 47.7) via members. The bar for v3 is +16.76, set by doing nothing; v3 must keep "do nothing" in its design and find a lever that neither version tested. Candidates: fines only after the last switch (rounds 20-23, where they cannot cost members), fines only at the first round of a block (where v2 found own fines do not push exits), or conditioning on the state of the race (gap, group size). Also checked: no artificial-human weights in the container, so no local simulation; I can only mine the sweeps.

2. **Per-player value of a fine by block phase (dead end).** Regressed, in v1's sweep, a fined member's own future input to my pool (c if still in my group, else 0) and the round-t co-members' future input, k = 1..8 rounds ahead, on the fine, by phase t%4 and contribution band, with the controls of v2's entry 5, in scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-2/v3_value.py and v3_value_analyse.py. The own-input coefficients look sensible (+0.13-0.27 per point at lags 1-3 for low givers at phases 0-1, close to zero at phase 3, i.e. a fine at the decision round buys nothing because the target leaves or does not respond) but the spillover coefficients are absurd (+1 to +2.5 contribution from the co-members per point of fine on one player), which means the per-player fine still proxies for the design point's whole punishing history. I do not trust this regression for anything but the phase ordering of the own effect: phase 0 > 1 > 2 > 3. Next: use the design-level randomisation instead, starting with the endgame, where v1's t_stop gives a clean experiment.

3. **Do fines after the last switch pay? (no).** Built per point x seat x round means of both groups' pool, size, contributions and fines (scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-2/v3_point_rounds.py) and, in v1's sweep, compared points with t_stop 19..23 (identical play through round 19 given their other params) in scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-2/v3_endgame.py. Found my group's total contribution in rounds 20-23 minus 16-19 is unchanged by endgame fining (d_c -0.20, -0.10, +0.24, +0.21, -0.14 for 0..4 fined endgame rounds; regression slope on spend 0.00 +- 0.02), while my pool's change falls one-for-one with the spend (+4.52 -> +0.01 as endgame spend goes 0 -> 4.9). So endgame fines are pure cost: the artificial humans do not respond to a fine within the last 3 rounds at all. Also from v3_point_rounds: pools rise through the game (my group 50 -> 68 in v1), so later rounds weigh more in the per-round mean, and ah's spend is heaviest in rounds 0-3 (15 per round in total vs 3.3 at the end) which is where my lead is built (pool 50 vs 41 in round 0). Next: does anything early pay at the design level, especially fining only in the first rounds of the game or of a block.

4. **What is a point of spend worth, block by block, at the design level?** Regressed each design point's lead (mean over rounds of my pool minus ah's, seats pooled) on its spend per block divided by 24, so that a coefficient of -1 means "pure cost, nothing back", in scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-2/v3_block_value.py, for v1's and v2's sweeps. v1: rounds 0-3 -10.4 +- 1.3 per point, blocks 4-7 and 8-11 +6.6 and +1.2 (but these three block spends correlate 0.97-0.99, so only their sum, about -2.7, is identified), 12-15 -0.85 +- 0.75, 16-19 -1.31 +- 0.77, 20-23 -0.84 +- 0.90. v2: rounds 0-3 -2.6 +- 0.8, 4-7 +2.5 +- 1.0 (corr 0.8 with the first), later blocks -1.0, -0.8. Members fall most per point of spend in the first block (-0.27 members per point/24 in v1). So spend in the first block is far worse than its cost: it is what decides the first, largest switch (my group goes 4.0 -> 4.43 at round 4 while ah spends ~15 per round in rounds 0-3), and anything I spend then shrinks the per-capita gap that drives that inflow. Later spend is about pure cost: it buys back roughly as much contribution as members it loses. Nothing anywhere is clearly positive. Next: whether the return depends on whom the fine targets (zero-givers vs middling givers), using the design-level variation in thr/base/slope.

5. **Does the return depend on whom the fine targets? (yes: middling-low givers in mid-game are the one positive).** Per v1 design point, summed my spend by the target's contribution (0, 1-4, 5-9, 10+) and period (rounds 0-3, 4-19, 20-23) and regressed the lead on these spends / 24 (-1 = pure cost) in scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-2/v3_target_value.py and v3_target_value2.py (bootstrap over points). Coarse bands: rounds 0-3 any target -3.75 +- 0.62; rounds 4-19 on zero-givers -1.50 +- 0.88, on givers of 1-9 **+0.53 +- 0.41**, on givers of 10+ -3.22 +- 0.67; endgame -1.75 +- 0.78; intercept (no spend) 15.4. Finer: rounds 4-19 on givers of 1-4 +1.76 +- 1.17, of 5-9 +0.20 +- 0.50, zero-givers -2.43 +- 1.20. Bootstrap: P(givers-1-9 coefficient > -1) = 1.00, P(> 0) = 0.93. The band spends correlate 0.57-0.85, so they are separated, if not cleanly. So a fine on a member who gives a little but not nothing, between the first and the last switch, buys back more than it costs (it raises the lead by about 0.5 point per point spent, i.e. 1.5 points of pool per point of fine), while fines on zero-givers, on cooperators, in the first block or in the endgame lose 1.5-3.8 per point. Both v1 and v2 always fined zero-givers and always fined in the first block (v1 from round 0, v2 before the first decision), which hid this. My reading: zero-givers in the artificial humans are a type that does not respond (or responds by leaving, v1 entry 3), whereas low positive givers are conditional cooperators a fine nudges up; in the first block my fines erase the per-capita gap that draws ah's members over at round 4 (entry 4). Next: draft a rule on this.

6. **Does v2's sweep say the same, and does the phase in the block matter?** Repeated the target split on v2's sweep, where the window w separates decision-round fines (phase 3) from fines earlier in the block, in scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-2/v3_target_value_v2.py. Found rounds 4-19, givers of 1-9, phases 0-2: **+2.05 +- 0.78** per point (well above the -1 of pure cost); the same givers at the decision round: -3.74 +- 1.27; zero-givers at phases 0-2: -3.81 +- 1.52; zero-givers at the decision round +3.67 +- 2.96 (noisy); rounds 0-3 -1.65 +- 0.73. The decision-round zero-giver result is v2's own door-showing idea and is too noisy to build on (and v2's final settled on no fining at all). Next: draft the rule.

7. **Drafted the rule: "nudge the low contributors between switches".** A valid member of my group is fined a flat `fine` if lo <= c < thr this round, the round is among the first `w` rounds of its 4-round block (never the decision round), t >= 4*first, and t <= 19. Changes from v1/v2 and why: v1 and v2 both fined zero-givers and both fined in the first block, which entries 4-6 show are the costly parts; v2 fined at the decision round, which for contributing members is the worst timing (entry 6: -3.74). v3 fines exactly the cell that both sweeps show paying (+0.53 in v1, +2.05 in v2 per point), and leaves the other cells to parameters with a contrast value in their range: `lo` = 0 re-admits zero-givers, `first` = 0 re-admits the first block, so the surface can confirm the finding or overturn it. `fine` < 1 floors to no punishment (1/8 of the design), which keeps v1/v2's winner, never punishing, inside the design. Per v1 entry 8's experience I used `t - 4*floor(t/4)` rather than `th.remainder`. Static spend on `zero`-managed groups of the reference sims (inline check): with first = 1, the fined share of member-rounds is 2-10% at w = 1 and 7-30% at w = 3 for lo 1, thr 3-12, so a fine of 3 spends about 0.2-0.9 per member-round, v1's mid range, and the 8-point corner ~2.4, which is clearly bad and gives the surface room to bend down.

8. **Does it do what I meant?** Validated (`validate-rule`: valid, 256 distinct points) and ran it in a fresh process on a hand-made history with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261112-2/v3_rule_test.py (thr 8, lo 1, fine 3, w 2, first 1): it fines the givers of 1, 5 and 7 in rounds 4, 5 and 16, spares the zero-giver, the giver of 8 and of 20, the player in the other group and the timed-out player, and fines no one in rounds 0-3, 6-7, 18-20.

## Key findings

1. The bar is "never punish": both earlier finals settled there and scored +16.76 +- 1.42 (own 62.1, ah 45.4, members 4.63 vs 3.37); in v2's sweep non-punishing points averaged 14.8 and small fines on chronic free-riders 14.9 (exploration 1).
2. Fines after the last switch are pure cost: in v1, my group's contributions in rounds 20-23 do not move with endgame fines (slope 0.00 +- 0.02) while my pool drops one-for-one with them (exploration 3).
3. Spend in the first block (rounds 0-3) costs far more than itself, -10.4 (v1, collinear) / -3.75 / -1.65 per point on the -1 = pure-cost scale, because it erodes the per-capita gap that draws ah's members over at the first, biggest switch (4.0 -> 4.43 members) (explorations 4, 5, 6).
4. Who is fined matters: in rounds 4-19, fines on givers of 1-9 return more than they cost, +0.53 +- 0.41 (v1; +1.76 for givers of 1-4) and +2.05 +- 0.78 (v2, phases 0-2), while fines on zero-givers (-1.5 to -3.8) and on givers of 10+ (-3.2) lose (explorations 5, 6).
5. When in the block matters: the same low givers fined at the decision round lose -3.74 +- 1.27 per point (v2), in line with v2's finding that a fine at the decision round pushes its target out; the per-player own response is largest for fines at the block's first rounds (explorations 2, 6).
6. The per-player regression of a fine's value is confounded by each design point's whole punishing history (co-members' contributions +1 to +2.5 per point fined on someone else), so only design-level comparisons are trustworthy here (exploration 2).

## Hypothesis

Against this stack, punishment loses when it is broad, early or late, and the earlier versions' "never punish" peak comes from averaging those losing parts with one paying part. The paying part is narrow: a member who gives a little but not nothing is a conditional cooperator whose contribution a fine raises for several rounds, so fining him between switches, early in the block (so the fine does not push him out at the coming decision), buys back more pool than it costs. Zero-givers do not respond (or leave), cooperators are put off, fines in the first block erase the per-capita lead that pulls ah's members over at round 4, and fines after round 19 have nothing left to buy. So a good manager fines only low positive givers, only in the first rounds of blocks 1-4, and otherwise stays out of the way.

The rule (`configs/managers/rule_based/pf-20261112-2-v3.yml`): a valid member of my group is fined `fine` if lo <= c < thr this round, the round is among the first `w` rounds of its block, and 4*first <= t <= 19; otherwise 0.
- `thr` (float, [2, 12]): upper line of the fined band; cooperators at or above it are never fined. Expect 5-9.
- `lo` (int, [0, 2]): lower line; 1 spares the zero-givers, 0 fines them too (the contrast), 2 also spares givers of 1. Expect 1.
- `fine` (float, [0, 8]): flat fine; below 1 floors to no punishment, so "never punish" is in the design. Expect 2-5.
- `w` (int, [1, 3]): fining rounds per block from its first round; the decision round is never fined. Expect 2-3.
- `first` (int, [0, 3]): first fining block; 0 re-admits rounds 0-3 (the contrast), 1 starts after the first switch. Expect 1.

### Justification

Finding 4 is the core: two sweeps of different rules agree that rounds 4-19 fines on givers of 1-9 earn more than their cost while all other fines lose, hence the band [lo, thr) with `lo` around 1 and `thr` below 10; the range [2, 12] for `thr` straddles the 9/10 line where v1's coefficient turns negative, and `lo` [0, 2] puts the expected best (1) inside, with 0 as the known-bad contrast. Finding 5 sets `w` <= 3 (the decision round is excluded by construction, since both v2's -3.74 and v2's exit analysis condemn it); w = 3 fines most of the paying rounds, w = 1 only the block's first round, where the per-player response was largest. Finding 3 sets `first`: the expected best is 1, with 0 in the range as the contrast and 2-3 for the case that only late-middle blocks pay. Finding 2 fixes the end at round 19 with no parameter. `fine` [0, 8]: the design-level returns are roughly linear over v1's and v2's spend levels (fines of ~1.5-6), the floor below 1 gives a no-punishment region that holds the bar of finding 1, and the top (spend ~2.4 per member-round) should be visibly worse. It would be contradicted if the sweep's peak sat at fine < 1 again, at lo = 0 or first = 0, or at thr near 12. What I chose not to model: door-showing fines on zero-givers at the decision round (v2's +3.67 +- 2.96 is too noisy, and v2's own sweep of that idea peaked at no fining), a fine that scales with the shortfall (v1's band split shows the return falling for givers of 5-9 vs 1-4, which the flat fine with `thr` can capture), conditioning on the race's state (gap, group sizes), and a separate last fining round (the 4-19 coefficients are pooled and I have no clean evidence on late blocks beyond finding 2); each would thin a 256-point design for an effect I could not measure.
