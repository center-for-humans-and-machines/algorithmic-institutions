## Key findings

1. v6's final (18.85, lo 1, hi 5, amt 21.4 at round 4) beat v4/v5 (17.90/17.88) by about +1 on the same streams,
   and its sweep mean rose 15.29 -> 15.61, but its surface is flat in lo and amt: within the one-dose band the
   sweep has nothing left to find (exploration 1).
2. A first hit at round 8 on members not hit at round 4 is a wash: per point -0.079 (0.111) for 0-5 givers in
   the group at round 4, +0.004 (0.055) for round-8 newcomers, whose raised giving (+0.08 per round per point) is
   offset by members lost at round 12 (-0.009 per point) (exploration 2).
3. Pooled over four sweeps (325,000 games), a round-4 hit on a 1-2 giver gains +0.36 / +0.62 / +0.95 rest-of-game
   lead at 18 / 23 / 28 points, still rising at 28 (+0.072 per point, se 0.055), while on a 3-5 giver it peaks at
   +1.13 near 18 and falls to +0.77 at 28 (-0.071 per point, se 0.033); the v6-only slopes have the same signs
   (exploration 3).
4. The best dose falls with the contribution: 1 and 2 at the cap, 3 near 20, 4-5 near 17; 0-givers gain ~0 at
   every dose (their raised giving is cancelled by the members lost). Per game 0.54 members give 1-2 and 0.91
   give 3-5 at round 4 (exploration 4).
5. The tiers' optima hold in every own-size class (1-2 rising to 28-30 for n 1-4, 5-6 and 7-8; 3-5 peaking at
   13-20), and members lost at round 8 (-0.08 to -0.11 per hit member) are back by round 12 at every dose
   (exploration 3).

## Hypothesis

Membership is the score, so a good manager punishes once, at round 4, right after the first switch decision
and with 19 rounds left, and never again. It hits the members who give little, but how hard depends on how
little: near-free riders (1-2 points) move and stay only under the hardest hit, while low givers (3-5) respond
fully to a moderate one and leave more under a harder one. Zero-givers and members giving 6 or more are left
alone. The rule: at round 4 only, every valid member who gave lo-mid points gets amt_fr, every valid member who
gave mid+1-hi points gets amt.
- `lo` (int, fixed 1): lowest contribution punished; zero-givers are worth nothing at any dose.
- `mid` (int, fixed 2): top of the hard tier.
- `hi` (int, fixed 5): highest contribution punished.
- `amt_fr` (float, uniform [22, 30]): the hit on 1-2 givers. I expect 27-30; 30 is the cap.
- `amt` (float, uniform [12, 24]): the hit on 3-5 givers. I expect 16-20.

### Justification

Finding 1 is why v7 keeps v6's timing and band and changes only the doses: the band is right, but one dose for
it is a compromise. Finding 2 is why round 4 stays the only punishing round. Findings 3 and 4 give the two tiers,
the split after 2 and the ranges: amt_fr's range covers the rising part of the 1-2 curve up to the cap, amt's
centres on the 3-5 peak; finding 4 fixes lo at 1 and finding 5 says no size condition is needed. The predicted
gain over v6's peak is about +0.13 for the region on average and +0.37 at its best corner, so I expect v7's final
to match or beat 18.85 by a few tenths, within the noise of one final.

What would contradict it: a v7 sweep whose mean is not above v6's 15.61, or a surface falling in amt_fr toward
30. The second would mean the 1-2 curve's rise at 28 was noise (its slope is 1.3 se from zero) or that hits
near the cap cost members later than round 12.

Not modelled:
- Hitting zero-givers: their lead is ~0 at every dose, so lo is fixed at 1 rather than swept.
- A dose that falls continuously with contribution: two tiers capture the 1-2 vs 3-5 split, and the sweep could
  not resolve a slope within a tier.
- Own size or newcomer conditions: the tiers' optima hold in every size class, and v6 found newcomer rules no
  better.
- Any hit after round 4 (finding 2).
