## Key findings

1. v5's final point scored 17.51 on seed 1000, +0.75 (se 0.37) over the zero manager on the same games; over v5's whole sweep the paired gain is +0.82 per game (se 0.06), flat in both swept parameters (bar -0.004 per unit, amount -0.005 per point), so bar and amount are exhausted (explorations 1, 2).
2. The gain of an example depends on how cooperative the rest of the group is: per touched v5 game, others' window mean <= 6 gives -0.22, 8-10 +2.11, > 14 +5.49; the gap between the others' mean and the target's gives <= 4 -0.27, 6-8 +2.81, > 10 +6.48 (exploration 3).
3. Filtering v5's pushes by the gap adds little (+0.03 per game at a gap floor of 3-5), because the pushes it removes lose only a little (exploration 4, 6).
4. A high gap makes examples pay at size 6, where they lose on average: one-target size-6 pushes gain +2.75 per touched game at gap > 8 (n 445) and +4.3 to +15.5 at decision 7 for gaps 8-10 to > 12, against -0.5 to -5.7 below 8; sizes 2-5 and decision 19 lose at every gap (explorations 5, 6).
5. Under the zero manager a size-6 group with a lowest-member gap > 8 arises at round 7 in 2.4% of games, so the size-6 lever is worth about +0.1 on the score if its estimate holds (exploration 6).
6. The human data hold too few hard examples (24 group-decisions) to measure the effect; the human managers made them mostly where the gap was > 8 (18 of 24) (exploration 7).

## Hypothesis

The zero manager's members-first backbone stays, and the one punishment that pays is a single hard, visible example at a switch decision. It pays because the rest of the group answers it and ah's group, too small to attract, receives and punishes the example if he leaves. The answer comes from members who are themselves contributing: an example in a group of free riders gains nothing, while in a cooperative group with a clear free rider it gains several pool points per game. So the trigger should be relative, the gap between the other members and the lowest one, not an absolute bar. Because a large gap makes the example strong, it can also be made when my group holds 6 rather than 7-8, but only with a larger gap there. The rule: at decisions 7, 11 and 15, punish the lowest valid member (window mean at most `bar`) by `amount` if my group holds at least `n_full` members and his gap is at least `gap_full`, or holds `n_full` - 1 and his gap is at least `gap_six`. `gap_full` (float, swept [0, 7], expected best 3-5; 0 is v5), `gap_six` (float, swept [6, 20], expected best 8-12; near 20 means never), `n_full` (int, fixed 7), `bar` (int, fixed 12), `amount` (int, fixed 20), `n_push` (int, fixed 1), `last_dec` (int, fixed 3).

### Justification

Finding 1 says v5's two parameters have no slope left, so I fix them at v5's final region (bar 12, amount 20) and spend the sweep on the new dimension. Finding 2 is the basis of the relative trigger. Finding 3 says a gap floor at size 7-8 changes little, so `gap_full` ranges from 0 (exactly v5, checked in exploration 9) to 7, past where the what-if turns down (6+), and I expect 3-5. Findings 4 and 5 give `gap_six`: positive only above about 8, with large but noisy gains at 10+, from about 150-450 v2 games that were also contaminated by later pushes. The range [6, 20] covers "loses" (6-8), the expected best (8-12) and "never" (15-20, where the condition almost never holds), so the sweep can confirm or reject the size-6 lever. Expected gain: about +0.9 to +1.0 over zero, against v5's +0.82 sweep mean, i.e. a final near 17.6-17.7 on the shared seed, though one final run has an se of about 0.37 on the paired difference. What would contradict it: a sweep that is best at `gap_six` near 20 (size-6 examples do not pay when applied by a rule rather than seen in v2's mixed games), or best at `gap_full` near 0 with a fall above it (the relative trigger adds nothing at size 7-8). Not modelled: size-6 examples restricted to decision 7 (11 was mixed, 15 positive, all noisy, so one threshold for all decisions), several examples per decision at a high gap (two targets looked better at gap > 6, but across games that differ in more than the number of targets), a gap-dependent dose (the dose response is flat above 13), and examples at decision 19 or in groups of 5 or fewer (negative at every gap).
