## Explorations

1. **Setup: what can I run here, and what drives the AHs?** Read docs, `manager/rule.py`, the AH training configs. No trained AH weights are in the container (only copula params), so no local simulation; the design has to come from the human data. The contribution AH sees prev_contribution, prev_punishment, group onehot plus a per-group recurrent virtual node (own-group peers matter); the switch AH sees prev per-capita common good, prev_punishment, group. Score is the group's pool averaged over rounds, empty group = 0, so group size is a first-order lever: a group of 6 has 50% more pool than a group of 4 at equal per-capita contributions.
2. **How do contributions respond to punishment?** `explore1.py`, one copy per game, rows where the player stays in the group. Mean next-round change in contribution: contributors at 0-4 go +0.5 unpunished, +1.6 at p 3-5, +2.3 at p 6-10, +3.7 at p>10; contributors at 15-19 drop -1.5 unpunished and -3.3/-5.2 when punished 6-10/>10; full contributors punished at all crash (-5 to -16). OLS next_c = 0.65 c + 0.31 group mean + 0.07 p (per point) - 0.001 p^2: partial effect of punishment is small overall, and own-group mean is a strong pull (peer effect). Mean punishment by human managers falls from ~3.6 in rounds 0-3 to ~1.1 late; mean contribution is flat around 9. So: punish only low contributors, never the top; the gain per punishment point is maybe 0.2-0.3 contribution next round for low contributors, i.e. ~0.4 pool points per point spent in the next round, which only pays if it persists. Next: what makes players leave.
3. **What drives switching?** `explore2.py`, 2000 decisions (rounds 3,7,11,15,19). Switch rate 0.29 overall (0.44 at the first decision, ~0.24 later). By own punishment at the decision round: 0.22 unpunished, 0.29 at 1-3, 0.49 at 4-8, 0.67 above 8. By mean own punishment over the 4-round block: 0.19 at 0 up to 0.61 above 8. By own-minus-other per-capita pool quintile: 0.49 when ours is >10.7 lower, 0.10 when >12.3 higher. Logit: own pc pool -0.09, other pc pool +0.05, own block punishment +0.12, own group's mean punishment +0.17 and the *other* group's mean punishment -0.18 (players flee punishing managers, and stay put when the other one punishes), group size -0.16. Low contributors punished in the block leave at 0.43 vs 0.20 unpunished; high contributors barely react. So punishment costs twice: pool points directly, and members (each member is worth ~1.6 x ~9 = 14 pool points a round). A member lost is far costlier than the ~0.3 contribution gained per punishment point. Next: what did the human managers who ended with big pools do?
4. **Which human managers got the big pools?** `explore3.py`, 100 group-episodes, empty groups counted 0. Pool correlates +0.66 with mean group size and -0.31 with punishment per member. By quartile of punishment per member per round: 0.32 -> pool 55 (4.65 members, sum c 35), 1.21 -> pool 67 (4.39 members, sum c 45), 2.07 -> 47, 4.80 -> 38 (3.26 members). Within a game the less-punishing group has the larger pool (59.7 vs 44.1) in 56% of games. So the optimum is not zero but light: about 1 point per member per round, with ~28% of member-rounds punished, beat both no punishment and heavy punishment. Next: what the light punishers' rule looks like (who, how much, when).
5. **What rule did the best (second-lightest) quartile of human managers follow?** `explore4.py`, punishment by contribution bin and by gap = group mean - own contribution, per quartile of manager punishment. Q1 (pool 67): gap <=0 -> 0.2-0.3 mean (6-10% punished), gap 0-3 -> 1.3, 3-6 -> 3.9, 6-10 -> 7.8, >10 -> 14.0 (94% punished); full contributors never punished; front-loaded, 2.3 per member-round in rounds 0-3, ~0.9 in rounds 12-23. Q0 (almost none) gets fewer contributions; Q2 is similar in shape to Q1 but adds more on at-or-above-mean players (0.5-0.6) and is heavier early (3.8); Q3 punishes everyone incl. above-mean (2.0-2.3 at gap<=0, 40% punished). So the winning shape is a gap rule: p ~ 1.2-1.4 x (group mean - c) for below-mean players only, decaying over the game. Next: does the effect of punishment persist, and does the other group's punishment change how much we can afford?
6. **Does punishment's effect persist, and does the other manager's punishment change what we can afford?** `explore5.py`. For below-mean players staying in the group, the OLS coefficient of this round's punishment on contribution k rounds later is 0.125, 0.119, 0.120, 0.166 for k=1..4 (se ~0.01-0.02): the lift persists. For at/above-mean players it is -0.058 (punishing them backfires). Block-level switch logit: own block punishment +0.144/pt, own group's mean punishment +0.22, other group's mean punishment -0.255, no interaction (+0.005). Leaving rate of players punished >3: 0.48 when the other manager punished <=0.5 per member, 0.49 when >2; of those unpunished 0.22 vs 0.11; mildly punished (0-3) 0.31 vs 0.19. Rough accounting at round ~4: a point of punishment buys ~0.12 x 1.6 x ~12 remaining rounds ~ 2.3 pool points, costs 1 directly and about 1-1.5 in expected lost members (a member is worth ~12-14 pool points per round). So targeted punishment is a small net gain early and a loss late; the margin is thin, so the sweep has to find the level. The other manager's punishment cuts our leaving rates by a lot, so the rule should punish more freely when the other manager punishes, and less when it does not (then our punished players have a lenient alternative). Hypothesis taking shape: below-mean gap punishment with a dead zone, a per-player cap, linear decay to zero over the game, and a multiplier on the other manager's observed punishment per member.
7. **Drafted the rule.** gap = (mean valid in-group contribution this round - c - dead zone)_+; punishment = slope x gap x clamp(1 - t/horizon, 0, 1) x clamp(1 + adapt x other_p, 0, 4), capped at cap, zero in round 23 (no future left to influence, switches are over). other_p = mean punishment of players outside our group over rounds 0..t-1 (0 in round 0). Five params: slope, dead zone, cap, horizon, adapt.
8. **Validated, then replayed the rule open-loop on the human histories to calibrate the ranges.** `validate-rule` passes (256 distinct points). `replay.py` runs the rule as group 0's manager on the 50 human games (contributions as the humans made them, other manager = the human one). Note: in a fresh Python process a builtin-free `exec` of `th.clamp(..., min=)` raised `KeyError: '__import__'` (torch lazy import); after one warm-up call it works, and the validator and sim have torch warm, so I left the code as is. Mean punishment per member-round: slope 1, dead zone 1, cap 10, horizon 24, adapt 0 -> 0.42 (17% punished; 0.95 in rounds 0-3, 0.15 in 12-23); same with adapt 0.5 -> 0.78; slope 0.5, dead zone 2, cap 6, horizon 16 -> 0.07 (almost no punishment); slope 2, dead zone 0, cap 30, horizon 40, adapt 1 -> 4.35 (Q3-like heavy). So the box spans no punishment through the heavy human quartile, with the best human quartile (Q1, ~1.2 per member-round) near slope ~1.3, small dead zone, long horizon, in its interior. I expect the optimum somewhat lighter than Q1 humans because the AH switch model penalises own punishment, so a slope 0.5-1.5, dead zone 0-3, cap 5-15, horizon 12-30, adapt 0-0.6.

## Key findings

1. Group size is the first lever on the score: across the 100 human group-episodes the pool correlates +0.66 with mean members and -0.31 with punishment per member (exploration 4).
2. Punishment raises the contribution of below-mean players persistently, ~0.12-0.17 per point for at least 4 rounds, and lowers that of at/above-mean players (-0.06 per point); full contributors punished at all drop by 5-16 points (explorations 2, 6).
3. Punishment drives members out: switch rate 0.22 unpunished vs 0.49 at 4-8 points and 0.67 above 8 at the decision round; own block punishment +0.14 and own group's mean punishment +0.17-0.22 on the switch logit, own minus other per-capita pool gap from 0.49 to 0.10 switch rate across quintiles (exploration 3, 6).
4. The other manager's punishment shields us: its mean punishment per member enters the switch logit at -0.18 to -0.26, roughly halving the leaving rate of our unpunished and mildly punished members when it punishes >2 per member (exploration 6).
5. The best human managers punished lightly and only below the group mean: the second-lightest quartile (1.2 per member-round) had the largest pools (67 vs 55 for near-zero and 38 for the heaviest), punishing roughly 1.3 x the gap below the mean, nobody at or above it, and front-loaded (2.3 early, ~0.9 late) (explorations 4, 5).
6. Back-of-envelope, a punishment point early buys ~2.3 pool points of future contribution and costs ~1 directly plus ~1-1.5 in expected lost members; late in the game the gain vanishes, so punishment should decay to zero (exploration 6).

## Hypothesis

A good manager here is a light, targeted, front-loaded punisher that keeps its group attractive. It punishes only members contributing clearly below the group's current mean, in proportion to the shortfall and with a per-player cap (big single punishments make people leave), concentrates punishment early when there are many rounds left for the lift in contributions to pay back, and stops by the end. Since players leave a punishing manager mostly when the other group is a lenient alternative, it scales its punishment with what the other manager is seen to punish: against a punisher it can afford more, against a non-punisher it should hold back to keep its members.

Rule: `gap = (mean valid in-group contribution now - c - dead_zone)+`; `punishment = min(slope * gap * clamp(1 + adapt * other_p, 0, 4), cap) * clamp(1 - t/horizon, 0, 1)`, zero in round 23 and for non-members, where `other_p` is the mean punishment of players outside our group over rounds so far.

- `slope` (float, [0.1, 3.0] log): points of punishment per contribution point of shortfall. Expected 0.5-1.5 (human Q1 ~1.3).
- `dead_zone` (float, [0, 6]): shortfall tolerated before punishing. Expected 0-3.
- `cap` (float, [2, 30] log): largest punishment per member per round. Expected 5-15.
- `horizon` (float, [4, 40]): round at which punishment has decayed linearly to zero; 40 means still ~40% strength at the end. Expected 12-30.
- `adapt` (float, [-0.4, 1.0]): scaling with the other manager's observed mean punishment per player. Expected 0-0.6; negative values let the sweep say "be more lenient against a punisher, to win members".

### Justification

Finding 1 and 3 say members matter more than squeezed contributions, so the rule punishes few people (dead zone, below-mean only), caps single punishments, and has a corner (low slope / big dead zone / short horizon) that is effectively no punishment, which the sweep can choose if the AH switch model makes any punishment a net loss. Findings 2 and 5 give the shape: shortfall below the group mean, never the top contributors, slope range centred on the human Q1 value 1.3 but reaching much lower. Finding 6 gives the linear decay and the hard stop at round 23 (no future, no more switches). Finding 4 gives `adapt`; its range includes negative values because the opposite reading (outcompete a punisher on leniency to grow the group) is also plausible and I could not separate them in the human data. The replay (exploration 8) shows the box runs from ~0.07 to ~4.4 points per member-round, bracketing every human quartile. What would contradict it: a sweep optimum at slope's low end or dead zone's high end (punishment never pays in the AH world), or `adapt` pinned at an end. Not modelled: special treatment of newcomers, timing within the 4-round block (the decision-round punishment itself was not significant beyond the block mean, +0.03), group-size dependence of leniency (switch logit -0.16 per member) and the other group's per-capita pool, which the rule cannot act on directly; I left them out to keep five parameters with clear readings.
