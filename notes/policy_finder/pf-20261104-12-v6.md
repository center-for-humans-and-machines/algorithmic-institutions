## Key findings

1. v5's final, 73.29 (se 1.12, 4.37 members), is the best of five
   versions but within a standard error of v3 (73.0) and v4 (72.9); its
   surface has no edge and two dead dials (follow effect 0.0006, size
   0.21), so re-drawing the same ranges would not move it (exploration 1).
2. Using the sweeps' randomised dials against pre-treatment states, every
   punishment dial pays more in groups that already give a lot: own3 x
   decay +0.63 (se 0.10), own0 x slope +0.91 (se 0.13), own0 x follow
   +0.58, while the rival group's contributions moderate nothing (|g| <=
   0.32) and `peer` does not capture the pattern (own3 x peer -0.44)
   (exploration 2).
3. Within quintiles of the state, decay's effect on the later pool goes
   from -1.25 per sd (round-3 mean <= 6.5) to -0.26 (11.5+), and v4's
   best slope rises from 4.0 to 12.4 as round 0's group mean rises from
   ~4.5 to ~13: the right intensity grows roughly in proportion to the
   group's generosity (exploration 3).
4. Group size at round 4 moderates the dials only weakly (n4 x slope
   +0.65, n4 x follow +0.83, n4 x size 0.01), consistent with v5's dead
   size term (explorations 1, 2).

## Hypothesis

A good manager makes its group visibly more generous than the other early,
when member flows are set, and spends punishment where it is both
effective and cheap: on clearly low givers in groups whose other members
give a lot. There a point pulls the free rider towards a high herd and
keeps the common good, which every player weighs when switching, high
enough that few leave; in a group that is collapsing, the same point
mostly drives members out and leaves nobody worth following, so the
manager should ease off. It keeps the timing that five versions agree on:
hard at each block's first two rounds, quiet before each switch and after
the last, tapering block by block.

The rule: `punishment = slope * decay^block * w_pos * (m/10)^herd *
max(0, bar - c_t)`, `bar = (1-peer)*thr + peer*m`, m the mean contribution
this round of the member's current group-mates (lone member: bar thr,
factor 1), `w_pos` 1 at a block's first round, `follow` at its second, 0 at
its last two and in rounds 20-23.

- `thr` (float, [6, 11]): fixed part of the bar; expect 7.5-9.5 (v5 8.1).
- `peer` (float, [0.1, 0.9]): weight of the mates' mean in the bar; expect
  0.4-0.7 (v5 0.58).
- `slope` (float, [4, 30], log): punishment per shortfall point at round 0
  with mates giving 10; expect 8-15 (v5 9.7).
- `decay` (float, [0.2, 0.75], log): per-block factor; expect 0.3-0.5 (v5
  0.41).
- `follow` (float, [0.3, 1.4]): second-round punishment relative to the
  first; flat in v5, expect 0.7-1.3.
- `herd` (float, [-0.5, 2.5]): elasticity of the punishment to the mates'
  mean contribution; expect 0.7-1.5, 0 is v5.

### Justification

Finding 1 is why v6 changes the rule's structure instead of its ranges,
and why `size` goes: it did nothing in v5 (finding 4 agrees), and its slot
is better spent on a dial with support. Findings 2 and 3 are that support:
because the dials were drawn at random, the interactions with the
pre-treatment state are causal estimates of how the value of punishment
changes with the group's generosity, and they all point the same way, with
a proportional scaling (factor ~3 in slope for ~3 in the start) suggesting
herd near 1. They also fit what v3 found per member (gain 0.144 vs 0.096
per point, leaving +0.42 vs +0.95 pp with generous vs stingy mates) and
v5's finding that member flows flatten once the common good per member is
above ~15. `peer` stays because it answers a different question (what
counts as free riding) and the sweep keeps it near 0.5; `herd` answers how
hard to press. The ranges of thr, peer, slope, decay and follow are
re-centred on v5's peak and the per-bin peaks of exploration 3, with herd
0 inside the range so the sweep can recover v5. What would contradict the
hypothesis: herd's best near 0 or negative, or no gain over v5's 73.3
(the moderation was then already absorbed by the bar, or too small - the
interactions are ~1 pool point per sd - to show on a plateau whose noise is
of that size). Not modelled: the rival group's state (finding 2: it
moderates nothing), group size (finding 4), newcomers, forgiveness and
expulsion (dead ends of v4 and v5), and punishment at block positions 2-3
or after the last switch.
