## Key findings

1. v4's final point scored 17.22 against the zero manager's 16.76 on the same final seed (+0.46, twice v3's +0.23); v4's sweep games pair with v1-v3's by (seed, slot), and its 41215 punished games gained +1.76 each (se 0.12), +0.63 per design point on average with no losing region (explorations 1-3).
2. The bar was still rising at v4's top (bar 3 +0.45 to bar 7-8 +0.78 per point), mainly because a higher bar touches more games (29% -> 42%) at a steady gain per touched game; the reach of the bar saturates near 11-12 (46%) because my group holds 7+ in under half the games (explorations 3, 8).
3. The gain is a group-wide response to one visible hard punishment: my group's total contribution rises ~2 per round whether I punish one, two or three members, while the cost grows with each (1.3, 2.5, 3.7 per round); three targets lose (-0.22 per touched game), one gains most (+2.5); the target's own contribution to my group is unchanged; ah's pool falls ~1.0 (explorations 4, 5).
4. The response has a dose threshold near 13: one-target pushes at decision 7 gain +0.4 to +0.8 per touched game below 13 and +2.5 to +3.3 above it, with little dependence on the dose beyond (explorations 6).
5. Pushes pay only when my group holds 7-8: at 6 or fewer, and at decision 19 at any size, they gain nothing or lose; decision 7 pays most (+2.2), 11 and 15 less (+0.9, +0.8) (explorations 4, 7).
6. Zero givers are the weakest targets (+0.6 per touched game against +1.5 to +2.3 for window means 0.5-6) (exploration 4).

## Hypothesis

The zero manager's members-first backbone stays; the one punishment that pays on this stack is a single, hard, visible example made in a (nearly) full group at a switch decision. When my group holds 7 or 8 players, a dose of 13+ on its lowest contributor raises the whole group's contributions by about 2 per round for the rest of the game, and when the target leaves he lands in ah's nearly empty group where ah punishes him, sinking ah's pool. Since the response does not grow with the number punished but the cost does, one target per decision is enough, and since the bar mostly decides how many games get an example, it should be high. v5's rule: `n_full` (int, fixed 7: punish only when my group has at least 7 members), `n_push` (int, fixed 1: one member per decision, the lowest window mean), `last_dec` (int, fixed 3: decisions after rounds 7, 11 and 15, not 19), `bar` (int, [5, 12]: the member is eligible if his window mean is at most bar; expected best 8-11), `amount` (int, [12, 30]: the dose; expected best 14-22).

### Justification

Finding 1 shows the mechanism works and that v4's design was positive everywhere, so v5 refines rather than replaces it. Finding 3 fixes `n_push` at 1 (same response, lower cost; per point 1 was also v4's best). Finding 5 fixes `n_full` at 7 and `last_dec` at 3. Finding 2 moves `bar` to [5, 12]: v4's weakest values 3-4 dropped, the top extended to where the bar's reach saturates, so the sweep can find where the extra (more cooperative) groups stop paying. Finding 4 sets `amount` [12, 30]: starting just below the threshold so the sweep can see it, the top kept because larger doses still gained per touched game. With only two swept parameters the 256 points sample each (bar, amount) cell about 1.7 times, so the fitted surface is less noisy than v4's five-dimensional one. Expected: about +0.7 to +0.9 over zero on the score (v4's sweep mean +0.63, plus higher bars and single targets), i.e. a final near 17.5. What would contradict it: a paired effect that falls for bar above 8 (examples in cooperative groups backfiring) or for amounts near 12-13 being as good as 20 (no threshold). Not modelled: a floor sparing zero givers (finding 6; small expected gain, no data on the alternative target), a stricter size threshold for later decisions, and pushes in smaller groups or at decision 19 (finding 5).
