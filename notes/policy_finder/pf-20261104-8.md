# pf-20261104-8

## Explorations

1. **How do `ah` and `zero` do against each other in the reference sims?** Looked at
   plots/simulation/25_LEVIN_run1_ah_zero_pairings_batched/per_round.parquet with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-8/ref_sims.py (score = per round, group sum of
   1.6*c - p, averaged; `common_good` in the parquet is the per-member share, not the pool).
   Found `zero` beats `ah`: pool 60.5 vs 56.0 (members 4.57 vs 4.00 against ah). In
   zero_vs_ah the zero group grows to 5.24 members at the first switch and stays ~5.0;
   ah shrinks to 3.8. But per member ah is not worse: ah's members give ~9.5 and pay
   ~1.8 punishment (≈13.4 pool/member), zero's give ~8.0 (12.8/member), and in zero_vs_zero
   contributions decay 8.7 -> 7.7 while under ah_vs_ah they climb 8.8 -> 10.5.
   So punishment does lift contributions in the AH model, but costs members, and members
   dominate the score. Next: what drives the switching model (punishment received?
   payoff gap between groups?), and how contribution responds to punishment.

2. **What drives switching, in the switch model and in the human data?** Read
   configs/training/artificial_humans/switch_predictor/joint_exodus.yml: the switch
   model's inputs are `common_good` (in the sim the per-member share of the pool,
   environment.py), own `punishment`, group and round, with an edge model over the other
   players (so it can compare groups). Then looked at the human data (one copy per game,
   50 games) with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-8/human_switch.py: at the 5 decisions,
   switch rate is 0.29; by mean punishment received in the 4-round block: 0.19 with none,
   0.24 at (0,1], 0.36 at (1,3], 0.45 at (3,6], 0.58 above 6. By share gap own-minus-other
   group: 0.50 when 6+ points behind, 0.10 when 6+ ahead. Logit: +0.156 per point of mean
   punishment, -0.078 per point of share gap, later blocks less switching (-0.18/block).
   So a manager must keep the punishment each player *feels* low and the share high;
   punishing a player 1 pt/round costs about as much exit-propensity as being 2 share
   points behind the other group. Next: does punishment actually raise contributions in
   the human data, and by how much, to see if any punishment pays for itself.

3. **Does punishment raise contributions, in humans and in the AH stack?** Looked at
   next-round contribution change by contribution and punishment bins, humans
   (scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-8/human_response.py) and the ah-managed side of the
   reference sims (sim_response.py). Humans: +0.10 per punishment point (concave, -0.0026 p^2),
   controlling for deviation from the group mean (-0.31: strong pull toward the mean).
   Low contributors (<=5) punished 5-10 rise +2.5 vs +0.3 unpunished; high contributors
   (>15) punished *drop* (-7.6 at 5-10 vs -1.5). AH stack: same shape, +0.08/pt, low
   contributors +1.45 at 1-2 pts vs +0.93 unpunished, +2.3 at 5-10; high contributors fall.
   So punishment helps only on low/below-mean contributors and with diminishing returns:
   the first 1-3 points buy the most (~0.3-0.5 contribution/pt next round vs 1 pt of pool
   lost now). Human managers punished 1.85/player-round, mostly by deviation below the group
   mean (dev < -8: 10.9 avg, 80% punished). Across human groups, the light-punishing quartile
   (0.8-1.5/player-round) had the best pool (67.0) vs near-zero (55.2) and heavy (38.1,
   3.3 members). Switching in the sims mirrors humans: +0.118 logit per point of mean
   punishment in the block, -0.072 per share point of gap. So: light, targeted punishment of
   below-mean contributors, never of high contributors. Next: how long does a punishment's
   effect on contributions last (worth punishing late?), and which rounds of a block
   the switch decision is most sensitive to.

4. **How long does a punishment's effect last, and when in a block does it hurt
   retention most?** Looked at the ah-managed side of the reference sims with
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-8/sim_timing.py. Persistence: a punishment at a block's
   first round raises contribution 1, 2 and 3 rounds later by about the same amount
   (+0.066, +0.072, +0.071 per pt; below-mean players +0.16-0.18 per pt with -0.003 p^2), so
   the effect does not fade within a block: a point of punishment returns roughly
   1.6*0.17 = 0.27 pool per remaining round, and breaks even after ~4 rounds. Late
   punishment cannot pay back; round 23 punishment is pure loss. Switching vs punishment by
   position in the block (logit, with share and block): p0 +0.014, p1 +0.023, p2 +0.037,
   p3 +0.052 per pt: the switch model is strongly recency-weighted, the decision round
   counts ~4x the block's first round. So: punish early in each block, stay quiet in the
   last round(s) before a switch decision, and stop punishing some rounds before the end.

5. **Drafted the rule.** Punish a member in proportion to how far their contribution this
   round falls below the mean of the *other* members of my group (valid contributors),
   beyond a tolerance, capped; zero in the last `quiet` rounds before each switch decision
   (rounds 0-19 only; the last block has no decision), and zero from round `t_end` on.
   Five params: k, tol, cap, quiet, t_end. Above-mean and high contributors are never
   punished (their contributions fall when punished, exploration 3).

6. **How much would the draft punish, over its design?** Replayed the rule on 300
   zero_vs_zero episodes (group 0 as mine) with scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-8/replay.py
   (it calls the rule exactly as the manager does, then clamps and floors). First surprise:
   with k in [0.05, 2] the design's median was 0.05 punishment per member-round, because
   the manager *floors*: k*shortfall < 1 gives nothing. Changed the code to round k*shortfall
   to the nearest integer before the cap, so a shortfall of 0.5/k already earns 1 point.
   Shortfall below the others' mean in those histories: P(>0) 0.49, P(>2) 0.30, P(>4) 0.17,
   P(>6) 0.09, P(>8) 0.05, so a tolerance range up to 8 mostly meant "never punish"; narrowed
   tol to [0, 5]. t_end below 8 leaves at most one block of punishing, which exploration 4
   says is where punishment pays back best, but [4, 23] put a quarter of the design near
   zero; narrowed to [8, 23]. Moved k to [0.25, 6] log. Design now: punishment per
   member-round quantiles 0.02 / 0.08 (25%) / 0.14 (median) / 0.28 (75%) / 0.51 (90%) / 1.19
   (max), against ah's ~1.8 and the human light-punishing quartile's 0.8-1.5. That spans
   "nearly zero" (zero beats ah, exploration 1) to "light" (human best, exploration 3),
   with heavy punishment (ah level) outside on purpose. Validation passes: 256 distinct points.
   (Side note: running rule code in a fresh process fails with KeyError '__import__' until
   torch has called sum/clamp/remainder once outside the no-builtins scope - a lazy import;
   validate-rule and the sim warm torch up, so it only needed a warm-up line in replay.py.)

## Key findings

1. On the stack the rule faces, never punishing beats `ah`: pool 60.5 vs 56.0, because the
   zero group holds 4.57 members on average vs ah's 4.00 (exploration 1); per member, ah's
   group is no worse (~13.4 vs 12.8 pool/member, contributions climbing 8.8 -> 10.5 under ah
   vs decaying 8.7 -> 7.7 under zero).
2. Switching rises with the punishment a player received in the block (+0.118 logit/pt in
   the sims, +0.156 in humans) and falls with the own-minus-other share gap (-0.072 and
   -0.078 per share point) (exploration 2-3).
3. The switch model is recency-weighted: punishment in the decision round counts ~4x a
   block's first round (+0.052 vs +0.014 logit/pt) (exploration 4).
4. Punishment raises next contributions only of below-mean/low contributors (+0.16-0.18 per
   pt, concave), and lowers those of high contributors; humans and AH agree (exploration 3-4).
5. The contribution effect persists undiminished for at least 3 rounds (+0.066/0.072/0.071
   per pt at 1/2/3 rounds), so a point pays back ~0.27 pool per remaining round, breaking
   even after ~4 rounds; late punishment cannot pay back (exploration 4).
6. In human groups, light punishment (0.8-1.5 per member-round) had the best pool (67.0)
   vs near-zero (55.2) and heavy (38.1, with 3.3 members) (exploration 3).
7. The manager floors punishments, so a proportional rule with small slopes punishes
   nothing; rounding before the cap fixes it (exploration 6).

## Hypothesis

A good manager here punishes little, and only where it buys contributions without costing
members: a few points to members clearly below the others' contribution, never to average
or high contributors; early in each 4-round block, when the effect has rounds to pay back and
the switch model weighs it least, and silent in the rounds just before each switch decision;
and not at all in the last rounds, where no effect can repay the pool it costs. This keeps
the felt punishment well below `ah`'s (so members stay or arrive) while holding up
contributions that would otherwise decay as under `zero`.

The rule: shortfall = (mean contribution of the other valid members of my group this
round) - (member's contribution) - tol; punishment = min(cap, round(k * shortfall)) if
shortfall > 0, else 0; zero in the last `quiet` rounds of each of the first five blocks and
from round `t_end` on.

- `k` (float, [0.25, 6] log): points per point of shortfall. Expect 0.5-2.
- `tol` (float, [0, 5]): tolerated shortfall. Expect 1-3.
- `cap` (float, [1, 15] log): most punishment per member and round. Expect 2-6.
- `quiet` (int, 0-3): silent rounds before each switch decision. Expect 1-2.
- `t_end` (int, 8-23): first round with no punishment. Expect 16-21.

### Justification

Finding 1 says the score is driven by members more than by contributions per member, and
finding 2 that felt punishment and a low share push members out: hence low overall
punishment, and a range whose design mostly sits between near-zero and the human light
level (finding 6), not at ah's level. Finding 4 gives the targeting (shortfall below the
others' mean, with a tolerance `tol` so ordinary noise is not punished, and nothing for
high contributors) and, with its concavity, the `cap`; `k` sets how hard. Finding 3 grounds
`quiet`; finding 5 grounds `t_end` (late punishment cannot pay back; round 23 is pure loss,
so the range stops at 23). Finding 7 is why amounts are rounded. The ranges come from the
replay in exploration 6: the design spans 0.02-1.19 punishment per member-round with median
0.14; I expect the best near 0.3-0.8.

What would contradict it: a peak at k's or tol's low-punishment edge with t_end at 8 (the
best is simply zero, any punishment loses members), or at quiet = 0 (recency in the switch
model does not matter, or what I saw was confounded by contribution levels). A peak at the
high k / high cap edge would say the AH contributors respond more than their switching
penalises it.

Not modelled: punishing on a history of low contribution (one round's shortfall is noisy,
but the AH contribution model reacts to the last round's punishment and contribution, and a
history window would cost a parameter); absolute contribution targets (the group mean
adapts to the level the AH players settle at); reacting to the other group's share or to
group size (the rule cannot change the other group, and punishing less when the group is
small would be a sixth parameter); timeouts (timed-out players are charged nothing anyway,
and are left out of the others' mean).
