# pf-20261104-7-v4

## Explorations

1. **Where did v1-v3 end up?** Read the three notes, rules and final sweep.json files, and v3's
   landscapes.json. Finals: v1 70.5 (se 1.1), v2 69.6, v3 69.5 (threshold 9.3, rate 6.2,
   early_factor 0.97, t_end 5, shape 3: i.e. punish everyone below ~9 hard in rounds 0-4, then
   nothing). v3's surface killed my "wait for the first switch" idea: early_factor's profile rises
   from 63.0 at 0 to 70.8 at 1, monotone; t_end (effect 0.6) and shape (1.2) barely matter;
   threshold (effect 14, peak ~9.3) and rate (peak 5.5-7.7) do. Three different shapes all land at
   ~70, so the punishment-schedule family is exhausted; a fourth version has to add a dimension
   that conditions on the game state. Also checked: there is no switch model or `ah` artifact
   locally (artifacts/ has contribution models only), so I cannot simulate; data only.

2. **What exactly moves members at the first decision?** scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-7/
   v3_first_switch.py on v3's 256-point sweep (all episodes, ~128k), my group = agent_group 0
   (checked against the rule's punishments in the raw rows). common_good is per *contributing*
   member: (1.6 sum c - sum p) / valid members. Member change at round 3->4 by the round-3 gap
   cg_mine - cg_other is a clean sigmoid: -2.78 (gap < -15), -1.82 (-10..-5), -0.37 (-2..0), +0.17
   (0..2), +1.69 (5..10), +2.81 (> 15); my group is empty after round 3 in 40% of episodes with
   gap < -15 and 0% above +15; episode score 35 vs 108. Slope per gap point is steepest near 0
   (~0.27 members per point for |gap| < 3) and flattens beyond +-10 (0.06-0.14). OLS: dn = 0.067
   gap_round3 + 0.121 mean gap rounds 0-2 - 0.003 p_round3 - 0.02 mean p rounds 0-3 (+0.048 n3):
   once the gap is controlled, punishment has no direct effect. So punishment costs members only
   through the visible common good relative to the other group's. That suggests a state-dependent
   rule: punish freely while my group's common good stays ahead of the other's, and hold back
   when punishing would push it behind. Next: how big the gap is under v3's winner and whether
   such a cap would bind.

3. **How far behind is my group under v3's best points, and would a cap bind?**
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-7/v3_gap_levels.py and cap_check.py on v3's 15 best sweep
   points (rounds 0-7, non-empty groups). My group spends 6.7 per member in round 0 (5.6, 4.7, 4.0
   in rounds 1-3), `ah` 3.8 -> 2.9. Before punishment my cg is 14.5 vs `ah`'s 10.7 (after its
   punishment); after mine, 7.9: I push my group behind in 59% of episodes in round 0 (34% are
   behind before punishing), still 45% in round 3. A cap "post-punishment cg >= other's cg +
   margin" would bind in round 0 in 27% of episodes at margin -10 (removing 1.9 of the 6.7 per
   member), 42% at -5 (3.1), 59% at 0 (4.4), 68% at +3 (5.1). So a margin in [-20, 6] spans from
   "never binds" (v3's behaviour) to "almost never fall behind". The trade-off is real: v3's
   early_factor profile says early punishment pays on average (the level it buys persists), but
   it does not differentiate the states where a punishment point is cheap (well ahead or hopelessly
   behind, slope 0.06-0.14 members per gap point) from those where it is dear (gap near 0, 0.27).

4. **Drafted v4's rule.** For valid members of my group: raw = min(rate * max(threshold - c, 0),
   30) in rounds t < t_end, else 0 (v3's core without its fade and early factor, both flat or
   at 1 on v3's surface). New: a common-good cap. My group's pre-punishment cg = 1.6 * mean c of my
   valid members; the other group's estimated cg = 1.6 * mean c of its valid members this round -
   its mean punishment per valid member last round (0 in round 0, as I cannot see this round's).
   The budget per member is max(pre_cg - other_cg - margin, 0); if the raw punishments exceed
   budget x members they are scaled down proportionally. So the manager punishes freely while its
   group's visible common good stays ahead of the other's by `margin`, and holds back where
   punishing would put it behind, which is where the switch model moves members (exploration 2).
   Four parameters: threshold [6, 14], rate [2, 30] log, t_end int [4, 23], margin [-20, 6].
   t_end's range is wide again because with the cap later punishment may stop costing members.

5. **Validated and hand-checked the rule.** `validate-rule` passes (256 distinct design points).
   scripts/policy_finder/pf-20261104-7/test_v4.py on a hand-built round: with margin -1000 the
   rule gives v3's plain punishments (10 and 30 to the 8- and 4-contributors, 0 to the timed-out
   player and to the 12-contributor); with margin 0, my group's pre-punishment cg 12.8 is already
   below the other's 14.0 (16 - its last-round 2 per member) so nothing is dealt; with margin -5
   the budget is 3.8 per member and the punishments scale to 2.85 and 8.55 as computed by hand.
   One known bias: in round 0 there is no earlier punishment of the other group to subtract, so
   its cg is overestimated by `ah`'s round-0 spending (~3.8 per member, exploration 3); round 0
   behaves as if the margin were ~4 higher. The margin range absorbs that, and a fixed prior for
   the other manager's spending would not generalise, so I left it.

## Key findings

1. Three schedule-shaped rules (v1 70.5, v2 69.6, v3 69.5, se ~1.1) all converge on "punish
   everyone below ~9-12 hard in the first rounds, then little or nothing"; v3's surface shows
   full-strength early punishment is best (early_factor 63.0 at 0 to 70.8 at 1) and the timing of
   the stop barely matters (t_end effect 0.6) (exploration 1).
2. At the first switch, my group's member change is a sigmoid of the common-good gap (mine minus
   the other group's): -2.78 below -15, -0.37 at -2..0, +0.17 at 0..2, +2.81 above +15, with the
   group empty after round 3 in 40% vs 0% of episodes; steepest (~0.27 members per point) near a
   gap of 0, flat (0.06-0.14) beyond +-10 (exploration 2).
3. Once the gap is controlled, punishment has no direct effect on members (OLS: -0.003 per point
   in round 3, -0.02 for rounds 0-3 vs +0.067 per gap point in round 3 and +0.121 for the mean gap
   in rounds 0-2) (exploration 2).
4. Under v3's best points my group's cg goes from 14.5 before punishment to 7.9 after it in round
   0, behind `ah`'s 10.7; punishment puts the group behind in 59% of episodes in round 0 and 45% in
   round 3, while only 34% / 31% are behind before punishing (exploration 3).
5. A cap keeping the post-punishment cg at least `margin` above the other group's would bind in
   round 0 in 27% of episodes at margin -10 and 59% at 0, removing 1.9 to 4.4 of the 6.7 points
   per member spent (exploration 3).

## Hypothesis

On this stack punishment buys a contribution level that persists, so it pays to punish low
contributors hard early; its cost is members, and members move by the gap between the two groups'
visible common good per member, not by punishment as such. A good manager therefore punishes as
v3 does but spends only out of its group's lead: it keeps its group's common good after
punishment at least as high as the other group's (give or take a margin), punishing freely when
its group is ahead and holding back exactly where an extra point would tip the group behind,
where the switch model is most sensitive.

The rule, for valid members of my group in rounds t < t_end: raw = min(rate * max(threshold - c,
0), 30), scaled down proportionally so that the group's total punishment per valid member does
not exceed max(1.6 * mean c_mine - est_cg_other - margin, 0), with est_cg_other = 1.6 * mean c of
the other group's valid members this round - their mean punishment last round.

- `threshold` (float, [6, 14]): contribution below which a member is punished. Expected ~9-11
  (v3's peak 9.3, v1's 12); possibly a bit higher now, since the cap limits the cost of a broad bar.
- `rate` (float, [2, 30], log): punishment points per point of shortfall, capped at 30. Expected
  ~5-12 (v3's peak 5.5-7.7), maybe higher with the cap.
- `t_end` (int, [4, 23]): first round without punishment. v3 found it flat from 5 to 20; with the
  cap later punishment may become cheaper, so the full range; I expect 6-14.
- `margin` (float, [-20, 6]): the common-good lead over the other group the manager protects.
  -20 practically switches the cap off (v3's behaviour), 0 never lets punishment put the group
  behind. Expected ~-8 to -2: lead protection near the steep part of the sigmoid, but not so
  tight that low-contributing groups go unpunished.

### Justification

Finding 1 grounds the core (threshold, rate, t_end) and their ranges around earlier peaks;
dropping v3's early_factor and shape follows from their profiles (1 and flat). Findings 2 and 3
are the reason for `margin`: if members respond to the cg gap and not to punishment as such, the
right control variable for the member cost is the gap, and the sigmoid (finding 2) says a
punishment point costs 2-4 times more members near a gap of 0 than far from it, which a fixed
schedule cannot see. Findings 4 and 5 show the cap would bind often enough to matter, and that
the margin's range spans "off" to "strict". What would contradict it: a peak at margin -20 (the
contribution level is worth more than any members the gap costs, so the cap only takes away
punishment where it pays) or a pool no better than v3's at the peak. Not modelled: the
flattening of the sigmoid when hopelessly behind (punishing more there would be cheap, but it
would add a second margin and these are rare states), a separate margin for decision rounds
(round 3's gap weighs ~0.07 vs ~0.04 for each earlier round, but v1's switch-round factor bought
nothing measurable), a prior for the other manager's round-0 spending (would not generalise),
and allocation of a capped budget other than proportionally (the response is concave, so
proportional scaling already keeps small punishments small).
