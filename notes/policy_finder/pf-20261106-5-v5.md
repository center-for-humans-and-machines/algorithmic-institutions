## Key findings

1. v4's final (67.39) did not beat v3's (68.52); its fitted peak (68.0) and its best re-checked point (68.59) sit on the same ~68 plateau. Only the opening's target (effect 5.8), slope (5.4) and window (2.6; two rounds best) moved the pool; decay (0.0), window_late (0.6) and n_blocks (1.3) did not (exploration 1).
2. After the opening my group's contributions plateau at 11.7 / 10.3 / 12.5 / 9.6 per member against opponents 1-4, members at 4.40 / 4.78 / 4.34 / 4.43; the norm erodes against the non-punisher as its players come over (exploration 2).
3. On the punished player alone punishment never pays back within 12 rounds; a round-1 hit on someone already at 8-10 halves their staying (0.53 -> 0.29) and their value (86.6 -> 40.7), while for those still at <= 5 it is neutral (exploration 3).
4. The pay-off is with the others: unpunished high contributors give +0.7 per round per other member hit in round 0 (13.6 / 14.3 / 15.2 / 15.4 in rounds 9-11 with 0-3 others hit), still there in rounds 20-23, at a staying cost of 0.76 -> 0.63 (exploration 4).
5. Late punishment is harmful: in round 20 the unpunished members drop 0.3-3 points per round when others are hit (exploration 5).
6. At block starts 4, 8 and 12 a moderate hit (2-5 per member) gives the most pool over the rest of the game at every group contribution level (e.g. 94.5 -> 101.0 for 0-2 -> 2-5 at mean c 10.5-12), a heavy one (10+) always loses; the gain does not depend on whether the norm is eroding, so no trigger (exploration 6).

## Hypothesis

Against these artificial humans punishment works as a group-wide norm signal: hitting the members below the norm raises everyone's contributions for the rest of the game, mostly those not hit, while costing the hit player (and somewhat the group) in switching. So a good manager sets the norm in a two-round opening, hard on everyone clearly below about 10 in round 0 and, in round 1, only on those who still have not responded (sparing those who moved up, whom a second hit drives out), then keeps a moderate, lower maintenance norm in the single round right after each of the first few switch decisions (when newcomers arrive and punishment costs least in members), and never punishes otherwise, never after round 16. The rule: a valid member of my group contributing `c` gets `slope * max(target_r - c, 0)` (clamped to 30) with target_r = `target0` in round 0, `target1` in round 1, `target_late` in rounds 4, 8, ..., 4 * n_late, and nothing in any other round.

- `target0` (float, [7, 13]): the opening norm. I expect 9-11 (v3's peak 8.8, v4's 9.9, v4's s095 11.2; v3's profile fell off by 13.6).
- `target1` (float, [3, 13]): round 1's target. I expect 6-9, below target0 (finding 3); a best value near target0 would say the round-1 hit should be the same as v3/v4's.
- `slope` (float, [2, 25], log): points per point of shortfall. I expect 4-10 (v3 6.3, v4 5.8, profile flat beyond).
- `target_late` (float, [2, 12]): the maintenance norm at block starts. I expect 5-8, which deals the 2-5 per member of finding 6 (v4's peak used 6.9 in round 4 and 3.9 in round 8).
- `n_late` (int, [0, 4]): how many block starts after the opening are punished (rounds 4 ... 16). I expect 2-3 (v4's n_blocks profile peaked at 3 later blocks, flat); 0 lets the sweep drop maintenance.

### Justification

Finding 1 is why the structure is kept (absolute targets, steep slope, a two-round opening, block-start timing) and why v4's irrelevant parameters (decay, window_late, the window of the opening) are dropped or fixed, so the 256 points fall on the dimensions that moved the pool. Findings 3 and 4 together are the reason for `target1`: the opening's pay-off comes through the unpunished members, so round 0 should hit everyone clearly low, but in round 1 those who already moved up are the ones a second hit drives out, while hits on those still at the bottom keep the norm signal going. Findings 2 and 6 are the reason for `target_late` and `n_late` on their own, decoupled from the opening: moderate block-start maintenance pays at every level, and v4 could only set it through decay. Finding 5 (and v3's dead response after round 16) is why nothing is punished after round 16 and why `n_late` stops at 4. What would contradict this: `target1` best near or above `target0` (the round-1 split is useless; v3/v4's single target was right), `n_late` best at 0 (maintenance is pure cost), or `target_late` at its top edge (later blocks want the opening's norm). Not modelled: a trigger on an eroding norm (finding 6 found the gain the same whether or not the norm erodes), reading the other manager (v4: same optimal dose against every opponent), newcomer-specific targets (v2: they respond like incumbents) and group size, to keep five parameters the sweep can resolve.
