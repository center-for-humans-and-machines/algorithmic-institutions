3. **First vs repeat punishment.** OLS over v2's sweep (our members staying in the group from t-1

6. **Drafted v3.** Six parameters. A member's tenure is the number of consecutive rounds it has

## Key findings

1. v2's best rule (68.0 in its final; 70.1 / 68.9 / 73.2 / 59.7 against opponents 1-4) punishes
   only in rounds 0, 1 and 4; its surface falls steadily as punishment runs later (t_end 4-5:
   68.3, 14: 66.0, 24: 59.3), and quiet=0 is clearly worst (60.6 vs 68.3 at 2) (exploration 1).
2. Players who join after the opening are never punished by v2 and stay 1.5-2.7 points below the
   incumbents for the whole block (against opponent 4: 8.6 vs 11.3 at round 8, 8.7 vs 10.1 at
   16), about one newcomer per block (exploration 2).
3. A member's first punishment by us is worth 0.07-0.09 contribution points per punishment
   point at any round; a repeat much less (interaction -0.036 at round 1, -0.067 at round 12),
   so a first punishment repays in ~7 rounds and a late repeat in 15-20 (exploration 3).
4. The round-0 response is large and lasting (low contributors +2.0 at 13-18 points, +3.5 at
   25-30 eight rounds on; mid contributors +0.55 after 1-3 points); newcomers at later switches
   respond about half as much but clearly (+0.6 to +1.1 at 6-12 points for c>6, +0.9 to +1.5 at
   21-30 for c<=6), while previously punished low incumbents hardly respond (-0.1 to +0.1)
   (explorations 4, 5).
5. Punishing a newcomer up to ~12 points in its first block does not raise its leaving rate
   (0.25 vs 0.26 at round 8); 21-30 points raises it to 0.29-0.35 and >30 to 0.43-0.46
   (exploration 8).

## Hypothesis

In the artificial humans, what a punishment buys depends on who receives it more than on when:
a member's first punishment from us lifts its contribution lastingly, repeats on the same member
add little, and the group mean contribution drags on newcomers who arrive unpunished. A good
manager therefore treats punishment as an initiation: it punishes shortfalls from the group's
mean harshly but only during each member's first rounds with the group (all members at the start
of the game, each newcomer from the round it joins), keeps quiet in the rounds before a switch
decision, never re-punishes long-standing members, and stops initiating when too few rounds
remain to repay. This depends only on our own members' histories, so it should hold whatever
the other manager does; newcomers already disciplined elsewhere contribute near our mean and
draw little punishment automatically.

The rule: punishment = min(cap, k * max(0, mean_c - c_i - s0)) for valid members whose tenure
(consecutive rounds in our group before this one) is below `window`, zero in the `quiet` rounds
before each decision (rounds < 20) and from round `t_end` on.
- `k` (float, [1, 20], log): points per point of shortfall; I expect 3-6 (v2 peaked at 3.6).
- `s0` (float, [-3, 3]): tolerance below the mean; I expect -1.5 to 0 (v2 peaked at -1).
- `cap` (float, [10, 30]): per-member maximum per round; I expect 15-27 (v2 peaked at 25-27;
  finding 5 argues lower for newcomers).
- `window` (int, 1-8): rounds of tenure during which a member can be punished; I expect 2-5
  (2: the two rounds after arriving; 5 also catches the start of the next block, v2's
  round 4).
- `quiet` (int, 1-3): silent rounds before each decision; I expect 2 (v2's clear best).
- `t_end` (int, 8-24): first round with no punishment at all; I expect 14-20 (a first
  punishment needs ~7 rounds to repay, finding 3).

### Justification

Findings 1 and 3 explain v2's surface: late punishment there fell mostly on members already
punished, whose response is small (finding 4), so the cut-off belongs on the member's tenure
rather than on the calendar; the window does that, and window=5 with quiet=2 reproduces v2's
best for the original members, so the sweep can fall back on it. Findings 2 and 4 motivate
extending the opening shock to newcomers: they arrive below our mean and respond to a first
punishment, while in v2 they were never punished. Finding 5 bounds the cost in members and
motivates keeping cap in the sweep with a lower bound of 10. Finding 3's ~7-round payback
motivates `t_end` with its range starting at 8. `k`, `s0` and `quiet` keep v2's reading, with
ranges narrowed around v2's peaks (k 3.6, s0 -1, quiet 2; quiet=0 dropped as clearly worse).
It would be contradicted if the sweep's best window sat at 1 with t_end at 8 (then the opening
alone matters and newcomers are not worth it), or if pools against opponents 1 and 3, whose
newcomers arrive pre-punished, fell below v2's. I did not model repeat-free-rider targeting of
incumbents (their low response, finding 4), the other manager's behaviour directly (the
mean-relative shortfall already spares newcomers it disciplined), or returners separately (they
count as newcomers, having spent time with the other manager).
