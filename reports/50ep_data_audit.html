50ep Training Data Audit

Why is prev_punishment uninformative? — issue #97

Motivation

  • PR #96: 50ep contribution AH is exploitable — no decay at p=0, wrong-direction elasticity at p=5,10.
  • PR #84: prev_contribution dominates the model (+1.86 delta log_loss); prev_punishment effectively unused (+0.004).
  • PR #92: ICC 0.64 (50ep) vs 0.55 (legacy); marginal ΔR² from punishment 5.6× smaller; ATT ≈ half.

Model-side picture is settled. This audit asks the data-side question: why is the punishment signal so weak in 50ep, and what specifically changed vs the legacy 4-player dataset?

In 50ep the avg and sum criteria pick largely disjoint sets — only 2 episodes (ep 7, ep 31) clear both bars. Eight excel on welfare without growing their group; eight bulk their group at lower per-agent payoff.

Takeaway. It's harder to beat the no-punishment baseline in 50ep than in legacy — or equivalently, human managers (50ep) are worse at it than rule-based ones (legacy includes random_1, the rule-based protocol). The gap reflects how the experiments were performed, not just manager skill.

Verdict so far

  • No-decay-at-p=0 is mostly faithful behaviour in the operational regime (mid-c starting state). Strong decay regime exists in 50ep data only at c=20 — a contribution level the AH can't reach.
  • Wrong-direction elasticity at p=5,10 is the more telling divergence — data shows mild decay; model shows contribution rising.
  • Cooperative ceiling collapsed. (c=20, p=0): 31% legacy → 12% 50ep. No dense stay-at-top steady state for the AH to draw on.
  • Manager elasticity weakened. 50ep human managers were ~2× more lenient on free-riders; conditional gradient halved (54pp → 30pp).
