The joint exodus head: switching as waves, not coin flips

structured-head installed by PR #171 + #172 · switch bay of both machines (head plus the conditional-Bernoulli picker)

The problem

Every fourth round, players may switch groups. The stock predictor gave each player an independent stay-or-leave coin, but real regroup rounds come in waves: several players abandon a group at once, and a busy exodus from one group coincides with quiet in the other (the groups' leaver counts are anti-correlated at -0.47). Independent coins cannot produce lopsided groups, so the group-size row (SC) sat above 2 -- and an oracle test (PR #169) had proved that no fix inside a single group could ever close it.

The change

On decision rounds the per-player coins are bypassed (the dashed box on the drawing). One round-level head predicts the pair of leaver counts for the two groups jointly, capturing the opposition between them; a conditional-Bernoulli step then picks which members actually leave, weighted by each player's own predicted probability -- so individual tendencies are respected within the drawn counts.

The maths, in plain English

  1. The baseline: every fourth round, each player gets a probability p of leaving their group, and a coin is flipped per player, independently. Independent coins cannot produce coordinated waves -- that is a mathematical limit, not a fitting problem.
  2. Step A predicts HOW MANY leave, jointly for both groups. Take the per-player internal representations the network already computes, average them within each group, and append two plain numbers per group: its size and the round number.
  3. A small extra network maps that summary to a 9 x 9 table of probabilities -- one cell for every combination of 'm0 players leave group 0 AND m1 players leave group 1' (each count 0..8) -- normalised by a softmax over the 81 cells. It is trained by cross-entropy: each real decision round says which cell actually happened, and the table learns to give that cell probability.
  4. Because the table is over the PAIR of counts, it can put extra probability on lopsided combinations -- a big exodus from one group in the same round as calm in the other -- which is exactly the anti-correlation (-0.47) that independent coins can never express.
  5. One safety detail: the gradient from this new head is blocked from flowing back into the shared network (the input is 'detached'). The head is a pure add-on reader -- the per-player predictions everyone else relies on cannot be bent by it.
  6. Step B decides WHO leaves, given the drawn counts. If m of a group's k members must leave, the probability that a particular set of m members is the one that leaves is proportional to the product of those members' individual odds p/(1-p). So the players the per-player head already considered restless are the likeliest picks -- individual character survives the group-level draw. This is the textbook 'conditional Bernoulli' distribution.
  7. With at most 8 members per group there are at most 70 possible sets for any count, so the sampler simply lists all of them and computes each one exactly -- no approximation. (Products of odds are computed as sums of log-odds so probabilities near 0 or 1 don't overflow.)
