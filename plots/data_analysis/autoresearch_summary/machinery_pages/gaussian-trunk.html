The Gaussian-MLP trunk: bending the response curves

nonlinear-emission installed by PR #167 · contribution bay of the gaussian-MLP machine (trunk plus the feature plate)

The problem

A linear model can only move contributions up and down in straight lines, but human reactions bend -- the response to punishment flattens near the extremes, and the reaction-by-round-type row (RCA) sat beyond 5. An earlier plain MLP attempt (PR #151) fit individuals better but wrecked the group dynamics, because nothing forced it to look at the group.

The change

A small 2-layer network predicts both the mean and the spread of each member's next contribution, and its inputs are hand-forced to include the member's own-group context (the green 'group core forced' plate on the intake) -- so the extra flexibility works with the group signal instead of around it.

The maths, in plain English

  1. The model's claim: given what a member currently sees (their features x), their next contribution is a bell curve -- centre mu(x), spread sigma(x) -- and BOTH depend on the situation. A calm regular gets a narrow curve; an unpredictable member in a chaotic round gets a wide one.
  2. The network computing mu and sigma is deliberately tiny: the features pass through one hidden layer of 8 units with a tanh squashing function, then a plain linear layer outputs two numbers -- mu directly, and log(sigma) (predicting the log is the standard way to guarantee sigma stays positive without any constraints).
  3. Training maximises the likelihood of the real next contributions under those curves (the Gaussian negative-log-likelihood loss). This rewards two things at once: putting the centre in the right place, and being honestly wide when people genuinely are unpredictable -- a model that pretends to be sure gets punished by the same loss.
  4. In the simulation, a member's contribution is one draw from their curve, rounded to the nearest whole number in 0..20, with anything beyond the ends clipped to 0 or 20.
  5. The 'forced core' is a rule imposed on feature selection, not on the network. Feature sets are normally chosen freely by cross-validation -- and an earlier attempt (#151) showed a flexible network then buys individual accuracy by ignoring the group. Here every candidate feature set MUST contain the member's own-group signals (like the group's average contribution last round), so the extra flexibility has to work with the group context.
  6. Where it plugs in: this IS the trunk of the second stack -- the group copula (#170), the exodus head (#172, #174) and the inflated emission (#177) all attach to it.

What it bought

RCA moved out of the worst band (> 5 down to 2-5) with the mean easing 1.631 -> 1.615. Modest on its own -- but this trunk is what the whole second machine is built on: the group copula (#170), the joint exodus head (#172, #174) and the inflated emission (#177) all bolt onto it.
